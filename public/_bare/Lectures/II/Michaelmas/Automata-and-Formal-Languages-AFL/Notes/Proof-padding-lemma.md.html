Just keep adding irrelevant states!

Let $M = (Σ, Q, P)$ be a register machine. Introduce a new state $\hat q\not\in Q$. $\hat q$ does not show up in any instructions in the range of $P$.

Define $\hat M = (Σ, Q\cup \set{\hat q}, \hat P)$ where $\hat P|_Q = P$ and $\hat P(\hat q) = ?(0,ε,\hat q,\hat q)$.

If $C$ is a configuration with state in $Q$, then $\hat M$ and $M$ transform $C$ in the same way. By induction, the computation sequences of $\hat M$ are precisely the computation sequences of $M$ since we start from $q_\mathrm{S}\in Q$.

Thus $M\mapsto M^+$ produces a strongly equivalent register machine with strictly bigger state set. Iterate this to produce infinitely many distinct strongly equivalent machines.
