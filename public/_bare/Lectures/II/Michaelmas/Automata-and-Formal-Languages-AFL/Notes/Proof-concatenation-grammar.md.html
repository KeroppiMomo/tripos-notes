$\mathcal{L}(G)\, \mathcal{L}(G') \subseteq \mathcal{L}(H)$

Let $vw\in \mathcal{L}(G)\, \mathcal{L}(G')$ with $v\in \mathcal{L}(G)$ and $w\in \mathcal{L}(G')$. We have a $G$-derivation $(σ_0, σ_1, \ldots, σ_n)$ of $v$ and a $G'$-derivation $(τ_0, τ_1, \ldots, τ_m)$ of $w$. Then $$ \begin{align*} T \xrightarrow{H}_1 S S' = σ_0 S' \xrightarrow{H}_1 σ_1 S' \xrightarrow{H}_1 \ldots \xrightarrow{H}_1 σ_n S' &= vS' \\ &= vτ_0 \xrightarrow{H}_1 vτ_1 \xrightarrow{H}_1 \ldots\xrightarrow{H}_1 vτ_m = vw \end{align*} $$

$\mathcal{L}(H) \subseteq \mathcal{L}(G)\, \mathcal{L}(G')$ when $G, G'$ do not share variables and are variable-based

Let $T = σ_0 \xrightarrow{H}_1 σ_1 \xrightarrow{H}_1 \ldots \xrightarrow{H}_1 σ_n = u$ be any $H$-derivation. We will define recursively what it means that a symbol in $σ_i$ belongs to the first half in $σ_i$, in a way such that the first half of $σ_i$ forms an initial segment of $σ_i$ containing variables only from $V$ and the other half contains variables only from $V'$.

  • For $i=1$, $σ_1 = SS'$ since there is only one rule involving $T$. Say $S$ belongs to the first half of $σ_1$ and $S'$ does not. Note that $S\in V$ and $S' \in V'$.

  • Suppose $σ_i = αγβ \xrightarrow{H}_1 αδβ = σ_{i+1}$ is an application of the rule $γ\to δ$. A symbol instance in $α$ or $β$ already occurs in $σ_i$, so say it belongs to the first half of $σ_{i+1}$ iff it belongs to the first half of $σ_i$.

    Since $G, G'$ are variable-based, $γ$ consists entirely of variables. As $V\cap V'=\emptyset$, we have two cases:

    • $γ\in V^\ast$. By induction hypothesis that variables from $V$ are all in the first half, $γ$ lies entirely in the first half of $σ_i$. Say $δ\in V^\ast$ lies entirely in the first half of $σ_{i+1}$. Also say that this production step belongs to the first half.

    • $γ\in V'^\ast$. We also have $γ$ lies entirely in the second half of $σ_i$. Say $δ\in V'^\ast$ lies entirely in the second half of $σ_{i+1}$. Also say that this production step belongs to the second half.

Therefore $u = vw$ where $v$ is the subword of letters belonging to the first half. Now observe the production steps that belong to the first half form a $G$-derivation of $v$ from $S$. Similarly all production steps that belong to the second half form a $G'$-derivation of $w$ from $S'$. This shows $u = vw \in \mathcal{L}(G) \, \mathcal{L}(G')$.
