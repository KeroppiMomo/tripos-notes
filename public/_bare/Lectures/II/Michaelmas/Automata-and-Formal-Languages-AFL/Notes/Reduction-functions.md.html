Definition (many-one reducible, equivalent) open_in_new

Let $L, L'\subseteq \mathbb{W}$ be languages.

A total computable function $f: \mathbb{W}\to \mathbb{W}$ is called a reduction of $L$ to $L'$ if for all $w\in \mathbb{W}$, $$ \begin{align*} w\in L \iff f(w) \in L'. \end{align*} $$

We say $L$ is many-one reducible to $L'$ (denoted as $L\leq_m L'$) if there is a reduction from $L$ to $L'$.

We say $L$ is many-one equivalent to $L'$ (denoted as $L\equiv_m L'$) if $L\leq_m L'$ and $L'\leq_m L$.

  • $\leq_m$ is a partial preorder, i.e. it is reflexive ($L\leq_m L$ via $f=\id_\mathbb{W}$) and transitive (if $L_1 \leq_m L_2$ and $L_2 \leq_m L_3$, then $L_1 \leq_m L_3$ by concatenation of reductions).

  • $\equiv_m$ is an equivalence relation.

  • Note $L\leq_m L'\iff \mathbb{W}\setminus L \leq_m \mathbb{W}\setminus L'$. Thus $L$ and $\mathbb{W}\setminus L$ is either many-one equivalent or incomparable by $\leq_m$.

Proposition (many-one reducible to computable or computably enumerable languages) open_in_new

Let $L,L'\subseteq \mathbb{W}$. Suppose $L$${}\leq_m{}$$L'$.

  1. If $L'$ is computable, then so is $L$.

  2. If $L'$ is computably enumerable, then so is $L$.

Proof

  • Interpret $L\leq_m L'$ as "$L$ is at most as complicated as $L'$".

  • $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbb{W}\setminus \mathbf{K}$ are incomparable in $\leq_m$: otherwise $\mathbb{W}\setminus \mathbf{K}\equiv_m \mathbf{K}$ and in particular $\mathbb{W}\setminus \mathbf{K}\leq_m \mathbf{K}$, contradicting with $\mathbf{K}$ being $Σ_1$ but not $Π_1$.

Definition (hardness and completeness) open_in_new

Let $\mathcal{C}\subseteq \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{W})$ be a class of languages and $L\subseteq \mathbb{W}$ be a language.

Say $L$ is $\mathcal{C}$-hard if $X$${}\leq_m{}$$L$ for all $X\in \mathcal{C}$.

Say $L$ is $\mathcal{C}$-complete if $L$ is $\mathcal{C}$-hard and $L\in \mathcal{C}$.
