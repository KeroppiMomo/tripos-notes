Let $G = (Σ, V, P, S)$ and $G' = (Σ, V', P', S')$ be regular grammars with disjoint sets of variables.

The regular concatenation grammar of $G$ and $G'$ $$ \begin{align*} H = \bigl(Σ, \quad V \cup V', \quad P' \cup (P \setminus \sb{A \to a}{A \to a \in P}) \cup \sb{A \to aS'}{A \to a \in P}, \quad S\bigr) \end{align*} $$ produces $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{L}(H) = \mathcal{L}(G) \, \mathcal{L}(G'). \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • This shows that the class of regular languages is closed under concatenation.
