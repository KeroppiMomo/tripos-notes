For $σ\in Ω^\ast$ a string, write $v(σ)$ for the number of variables in $σ$. Call a rule of the form $A\to BC$ binary and a rule of the form $A \to a$ unary.

A binary rule increases $|σ|$ by 1 and $v(σ)$ by 1. A unary rule keeps $|σ|$ the same and decreases $v(σ)$ by 1.

Since $|S| = v(S) = 1$, there are $n-1$ applications of a binary rules to reach length $n$. They increase the number of variables by $n-1$ to $n$. Since $v(w) = 0$, we need $n$ applications of a unary rule. Thus in total any $G$-derivation consists of $n+(n-1) = 2n-1$ rule applications.
