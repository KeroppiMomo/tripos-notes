Let $α\in Ω^\ast$ and $S \xrightarrow{G} α$ be a deduction of length $n$.

Induct on $n$: $S$ is of the form $wA$. Given some $wA$, applying a terminal rule $A\to a$ produces $wa\in \mathbb{W}$, and applying a nonterminal rule $A\to aB$ produces $w'B$ for some $w'\in \mathbb{W}$ and $B\in V$.

Nonterminal rules keep the number of variables the same and increases the length; terminal rules reduce the number of variables by one and keeps the length the same. Thus for $α\in \mathbb{W}$, as the deduction starts from $S$ (one variable, length 1), we must have $|α|-1$ nonterminal rules before 1 terminal rule.
