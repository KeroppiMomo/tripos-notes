We have shown that $L$ is accepted by a deterministic automaton $D = (Σ, Q, δ, q_0, F)$. We will show that $L$ satisfies the pumping lemma with pumping number $n=|Q|$.

Suppose $w\in L = \mathcal{L}(D)$ with $|w|\geq n$. Write $w = a_0 \ldots a_{n-1} v$ for some $v\in \mathbb{W}$. Let $q_0, q_1, \ldots, q_n\in Q$ be the state sequence of the computation of $a_0 \ldots a_{n-1}$. Since the state sequence has $n+1$ elements but $Q$ has $n$ only, the pigeonhole principle implies that one of the states must occur twice in the sequence. Let $0\leq i<j\leq n$ be such that $q_i = q_j$.

Take $$ \begin{align*} x &= a_0 \ldots a_{i-1}, \\ y &= a_i \ldots a_{j-1}, \\ z &= a_{j} \ldots a_n v. \end{align*} $$ They satisfy $|y|>0$ and $|xy|\leq n$. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} \hat δ(q_0, x) &= q_i, \\ \hat δ(q_i, y) &= q_j = q_i, \\ \hat δ(q_i, z) &= \hat δ(q_j, z) \in F. \end{align*} $$

Since $\hat δ(q_0, xy^{k+1}) = \hat δ(\hat δ(q_0, xy^{k}), y)$, by induction we have $\hat δ(q_0, xy^{k}) = q_i$ for all $k\in \N$. Hence $\hat δ(q_0, xy^k z) = \hat δ(q_i, z) \in F$ and so $xy^k z \in F$.
