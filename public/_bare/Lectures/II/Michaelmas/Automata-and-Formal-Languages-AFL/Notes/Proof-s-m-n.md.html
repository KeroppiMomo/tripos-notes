Since $g$ is computable, let $M$ be a register machine that computes it, i.e. $f_{M,k+1}(\v w, v) = g(\v w, v)$. For any fixed $v\in \mathbb{W}$, let $M_v$ be the register machine that performs the operation $\v w \mapsto (\v w, v)$, i.e. writing $v$ to register $k$. Note $$ \begin{align*} g(\v w, v) = f_{M, k+1}(f_{M_v, k}(\v w)) = f_{M\circ M_v, k}(\v w) \end{align*} $$ so it suffices to show the operation $v\mapsto \op{code}(M$${}\circ{}$$M_v)$ is performed by some register machine.

Break it down into concatenating three register machines: $$ \begin{align*} v \mapsto \op{code}(M_v) \mapsto (\op{code}(M_v), \op{code}(M)) \mapsto \op{code}(M \circ M_v). \end{align*} $$

The first two operations $v\mapsto \op{code}(M_v)$ and $w\mapsto (w, \op{code}(M))$ are clear. For the last one, note the proof of the concatenation lemma provides a concrete algorithm of how to perform the concatenation operation.
