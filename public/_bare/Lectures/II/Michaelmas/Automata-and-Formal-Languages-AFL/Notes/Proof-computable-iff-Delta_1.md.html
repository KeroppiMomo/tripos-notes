Computable $\implies\Delta_1$

If a set $X\subseteq \mathbb{W}^k$ is computable, then so is $\mathbb{W}^k \setminus X$, so it suffices to show $X$ is $Σ_1$.

Define $Y = \sb{(\v w, v)\in \mathbb{W}^{k+1}}{\v w\in X}$. $Y$ is computable: ignore $v$ and do the computation to check if $\v w\in X$, so $X$ is $Σ_1$.

$Δ_1\implies$ computable

Suppose $X\subseteq \mathbb{W}^k$ and $\mathbb{W}^k \setminus X$ are $Σ_1$ thus computably enumerable, i.e. there exists register machines $M, M'$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \v w\in X &\iff \exists v (t_{M,k}(\v w, v) = a), \\ \v w\not\in X &\iff \exists v (t_{M',k}(\v w, v) = a) \end{align*} $$ where $t_{M,k}$ is the truncated computation function.

Splitting the word $v$ into $v_{(0)}$ and $v_{(1)}$, define the computable total function $$ \begin{align*} f(\v w,v) = \begin{cases} t_{M,k}(\v w, v_{(1)}) &\text{if $\#(v_{(0)})$ is even}, \\ t_{M',k}(\v w, v_{(1)}) &\text{if $\#(v_{(0)})$ is odd}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ Minimise this function to obtain $h$ which finds the least $v$ such that $f(\v w, v) = a$. For each $\v w$, since either $\v w\in X$ or $\v w\not\in X$, such a $v$ must exist, so $h$ is a total function. We finally have $$ \begin{align*} χ_X(\v w) = \begin{cases} a &\text{if $\#(h(\v w)_{(0)})$ is even}, \\ ε &\text{if $\#(h(\v w)_{(0)})$ is odd}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$
