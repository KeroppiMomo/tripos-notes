Given a deterministic automaton $D = (Q, Σ, δ, q_0, F)$, the nondeterministic automaton $N = (Q, Σ, δ^\mathrm{n}, q_0, F)$ where $$ \begin{align*} δ^\mathrm{n}(q, a) = \set{δ(q,a)} \end{align*} $$ has $\hat δ^\mathrm{n}(q, w) = \set{\hat δ(q, w)}$ by induction, so $\mathcal{L}(D) = \mathcal{L}(N)$.

Conversely, given a nondeterministic automaton $N = (Q, Σ, δ, q_0, F)$, we perform the subset construction: define a deterministic automaton $D = (\mathcal{P}(Q), Σ, Δ, \set{q_0}, G)$ where $$ \begin{align*} Δ(X,a) &= \bigcup \sb{δ(q,a)}{q\in X}, \\ G &= \sb{X\in \mathcal{P}(Q)}{X\cap F \neq \emptyset}. \end{align*} $$

Note that fixing a word $w = a_0 a_1 \ldots a_n \in Σ^\ast$, the state sequence $(X_0, \ldots, X_{n+1})$ corresponding to $w$ in $D$ equals the state set sequence $(Y_0, \ldots, Y_{n+1})$ corresponding to $w$ in $N$, i.e. $X_i = Y_i$, because by induction $$ \begin{align*} \hat Δ(\set{q_0}, ε) ={}& \set{q_0} = \hat δ(q_0, ε) &&\text{by definition}, \\ \hat Δ(\set{q_0}, wa) ={}& Δ(\hat Δ(\set{q_0}, w), a) &&\text{by definition} \\ ={}& Δ(\hat δ(q_0, w), a) &&\text{induction hypothesis} \\ ={}& \bigcup_{p\in \hat δ(q_0, w)} δ(p,a) &&\text{by definition} \\ ={}& \hat δ(q_0, wa) &&\text{by definition}. \end{align*} $$ Thus $$ \begin{align*} w\in \mathcal{L}(D) \iff X_{n+1}\in G \iff Y_{n+1} \cap F \neq \emptyset \iff w\in \mathcal{L}(N). \end{align*} $$

(If $N$ has $n$ states, then $D$ has $2^n$ states.)

For an $ε$-NFA, take instead $D = (\mathcal{P}(Q), Σ, Δ, \op{ecl}(q_0), G)$ with $$ \begin{align*} Δ(X,a) := \op{ecl}\left(\bigcup \sb{δ(q,a)}{q\in X}\right) \end{align*} $$ and the argument is the same.
