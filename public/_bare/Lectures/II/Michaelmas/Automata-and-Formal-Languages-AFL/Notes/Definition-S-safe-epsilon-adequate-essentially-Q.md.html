A rewrite rule $α\to β$ is called $S$-safe if $β$ does not contain $S$.

A grammar $G = (Σ, V, P, S)$ is called $ε$-adequate if all its rewrite rules are $S$-safe.

Let $\mathsf{Q}$ denote "noncontracting", "context-sensitive", "context-free" or "regular". $G$ is called essentially $\mathsf{Q}$ if all of its rewrite rules either have property $\mathsf{Q}$ or are the basic $ε$-rule $S \to ε$.

A language is called essentially $\mathsf{Q}$ if it is produced by an essentially $\mathsf{Q}$ grammar.
