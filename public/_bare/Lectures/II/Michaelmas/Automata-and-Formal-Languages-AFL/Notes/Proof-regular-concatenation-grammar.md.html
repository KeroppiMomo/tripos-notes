$\mathcal{L}(H) \supseteq \mathcal{L}(G) \, \mathcal{L}(G')$

Suppose $vw\in \mathcal{L}(G) \mathcal{L}(G')$ with $S \xrightarrow{G} v$ and $S' \xrightarrow{G'} w$. We know the final production in the derivation of $v$ is a terminal rule, say $A\to a$. Replacing the last step with an application of $A \to aS'$, we have $S \xrightarrow{H} vS'$. Moreover, $S'\xrightarrow{G'}w$ implies $S' \xrightarrow{H} w$, so $S \xrightarrow{H} vw$.

$\mathcal{L}(H) \subseteq \mathcal{L}(G) \, \mathcal{L}(G')$

Let $S = σ_0 \xrightarrow{H}_1 σ_1 \xrightarrow{H}_1 \ldots \xrightarrow{H}_1 σ_n = u\in \mathbb{W}$ be an $H$-derivation. Again we know all strings $σ_0, σ_1 ,\ldots, σ_{n-1}$ contain exactly one variable. Rules of $H$ ensure a non-decreasing number of variables in $V'$, so if $σ_i$ contains a variable in $V'$, then this remains true for all $σ_{i+1}, σ_{i+2}, \ldots, σ_{n-1}$. Therefore strings with variables from $V$ form an initial segment of the derivation.

Let $m$ be the unique number such that $σ_m$ has a variable from $V$ and $σ_{m+1}$ has a variable from $V'$. This must be an application of an $H$-rule of the form $A \to aS'$, so $σ_m = xA$ and $σ_{m+1} = xaS'$. All rules applied before $m$ are $G$-rules (since they do not involve variables from $V'$) and all rules applied after $m+1$ are $G'$-rules (since they do not involve variables from $V$). Thus $S \xrightarrow{G} xA$ and using $A \to a\in P$, $S\xrightarrow{G} xa$. Writing $v = xa$ and $u = vw$, we have $S' \xrightarrow{G'} w$.
