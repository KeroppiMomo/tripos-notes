Since $X$ is infinite, take $i: \N \to X$ to be an injection. Let $π: \N \to X^\ast$ be a function, and we want to show $π$ is not a surjection.

Perform a diagonal argument: we claim that $$ \begin{align*} D = \sb{i(n)}{n\in \N, i(n)\not\in π(n)} \end{align*} $$ is not in the range of $π$. Indeed, for any $d\in \N$, the definition says $i(d) \in D$ iff $i(d)\not\in π(d)$ so $D \neq π(d)$.
