If $L\neq \emptyset$ satisfies regular pumping lemma with pumping number $n$, then there is a word $w\in L$ with $|w|<n$

Since $L\neq \emptyset$, there exists a word $w\in L$ that has minimal length. If $|w|\geq n$, then it can be pumped down to produce a word of smaller length in $L$, which contradicts with minimality. Thus $|w|<n$.

The algorithm

Construct, as in the proofs here and here, a deterministic automaton $D$ such that $\mathcal{L}(G) = \mathcal{L}(D)$ and the number of states of $D$ is $2^{m+1}$ where $m = |V|$ is the number of nonterminal symbols of $G$.

Check all (finitely many) words $w$ of length at most $2^{m+1}$ whether $w\in \mathcal{L}(D)$ by running the automaton.

If such $w$ exists, then $\mathcal{L}(G)\neq \emptyset$. Otherwise, since $\mathcal{L}(D)$ satisfies the pumping lemma with pumping number $2^{m+1}$, $\mathcal{L}(G) = \emptyset$ by the above.
