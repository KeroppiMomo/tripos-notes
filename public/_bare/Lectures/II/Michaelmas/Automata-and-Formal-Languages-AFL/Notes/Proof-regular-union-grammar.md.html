$\mathcal{L}(H) \supseteq \mathcal{L}(G) \cup \mathcal{L}(G')$

For any derivation $S\xrightarrow{G} w$ or $S' \xrightarrow{G'} w$, replace the first rule application $S\to α$ or $S'\to α$ with $T \to α$ to make $T \xrightarrow{H} w$.

$\mathcal{L}(H) \subseteq \mathcal{L}(G) \cup \mathcal{L}(G')$

Suppose $T\xrightarrow{H} w$. If $T\xrightarrow{H}_1 w$, then this is an application of $T\to w$. By definition, either $S\to w\in P$ or $S'\to w\in P'$, so $w\in \mathcal{L}(G) \cup \mathcal{L}(G')$.

Otherwise, we have $T\xrightarrow{H}_1 α \xrightarrow{H} w$. As above, either $S\to α\in P$ or $S'\to α\in P'$. In the first case, all variables in $α$ are in $V$. Since $G, G'$ (and therefore $H$) are context-sensitive, every production rule needs a variable to be rewritten. Therefore by induction, all variables occurring in the derivation of $α\xrightarrow{H} w$ will also be in $V$, and rules applied in the derivation come from $P$. Hence $S \xrightarrow{G}_1 α\xrightarrow{G} w$. Similarly for the second case.
