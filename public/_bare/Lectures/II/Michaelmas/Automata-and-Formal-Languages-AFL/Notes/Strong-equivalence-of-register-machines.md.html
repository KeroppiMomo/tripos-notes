Definition (strongly equivalent register machines) open_in_new

Two $Σ$-register machines $M = (Σ, Q, P)$ and $M' = (Σ, Q', P')$ are said to be strongly equivalent if for all $k\in \N$ and $\v w\in \mathbb{W}$, letting $C$$(k,M,\v w) = (q,\v v)$ and $C(k,M',\v w) = (q', \v v')$,

  • we have $\v v = \v v'$; and

  • $q = q_H$ iff $q' = q_H'$.


  • If $|Q| = |Q'|$, then for each register machine $M=(Σ,Q,P)$, we can make a strongly equivalent register machine $M'=(Σ,Q',P')$ by relabelling.

Proposition (countably many register machines) open_in_new

Fix $Σ$. There are only countably many $Σ$-register machines up to strong equivalence.

Proposition (padding lemma) open_in_new
