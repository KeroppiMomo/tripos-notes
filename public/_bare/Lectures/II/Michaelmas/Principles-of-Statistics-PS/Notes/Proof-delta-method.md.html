Taylor expand to give $$ \begin{align*} (Φ(\hat θ_n) - Φ(θ_0))_i = \pdfd{Φ_i}{θ^T}(θ^{(i)})\cdot (\hat θ_n - θ_0) \end{align*} $$ for some $θ^{(i)}$ on the line segment between $θ_0$ and $\hat θ_n$.

We claim $\hat θ_n \xrightarrow{p} θ_0$. Fix $ε>0$ and $δ>0$. Since $r_n(\hat θ_n - θ_0)$ converges in distribution, it is bounded in probability, so there exists $M>0$ such that $\prob(|r_n (\hat θ_n - θ_0)| < M) < δ$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Since $M/r_n < ε$ for all sufficiently large $n$, we get $\prob(|\hat θ_n - θ_0|<ε)<δ$ for all sufficiently large $n$, as claimed.

Therefore, $θ^{(i)}\xrightarrow{p}θ_0$ as well, and by the continuous mapping theorem, $\pdfd{Φ_i}{θ}(θ^{(i)}) \xrightarrow{p} \pdfd{Φ_i}{θ}(θ_0)$. Hence by Slutsky's, $$ \begin{align*} r_n(Φ(\hat θ_n) - Φ(θ_0))_i = \pdfd{Φ_i}{θ^T}(θ^{(i)}) \cdot r_n(\hat θ_n - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} \pdfd{Φ_i}{θ^T}(θ^i) \cdot Z. \end{align*} $$
