The Markov kernel is $$ \begin{align*} K(t,B) ={}& \prob[X_{n+1}\in B \mid X_n = t] \\ ={}&\prob[X_{n+1}\in B, A_{n}=1\mid X_n = t] + \prob[X_{n+1}\in B, A_n = 0\mid X_n = t] \\ ={}& \int_{B} a(t,s) \,q(t,s) \d s + \mathbb{1}_{\set{t\in B}} \int_{\mathcal{X}} (1-a(t,s)) \, q(t,s) \d s \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \int_{\mathcal{X}} K(t,B) f(t) \d t ={}& \int_{\mathcal{X}} \int_{B} a(t,s) q(t,s) f(t) \d s \d t + \int_{B} \int_{\mathcal{X}} (1-a(t,s)) q(t,s) f(t) \d s \d t. \end{align*} $$

We compute the first term $$ \begin{align*} \int_{\mathcal{X}} \int_{B} a(t,s)q(t,s) f(t)\d s \d t={}& \int_{\mathcal{X}} \int_{B} \min\set{q(t,s)f(t), q(s,t)f(s)} \d s \d t \\ ={}& \int_{\mathcal{X}} \int_{B} a(s,t) q(s,t) f(s) \d s \d t \\ ={}& \int_{B} \int_{\mathcal{X}} a(s,t) q(s,t) f(s) \d t \d s. \end{align*} $$ Renaming $t$ as $s$ and $s$ as $t$, we get $$ \begin{align*} \int_{\mathcal{X}} K(t,B) f(t)\d t ={}& \int_{B} \int_{\mathcal{X}} q(t,s)f(t) \d s \d t \\ ={}&\int_{B} f(t) \d t \end{align*} $$ as required.
