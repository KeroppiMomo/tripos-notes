Note $\overline{X_n}$ has constant risk: $$ \begin{align*} R(\overline{X_n}, θ) = \expect_θ[(\overline{X_n} - θ)^2] = \var_θ[\overline{X_n}] = 1/n \end{align*} $$ so admissible implies minimax.

Let $δ$ be another estimator, and suppose $R(δ, θ) \leq 1/n$ for all $θ\in Θ$. By bias-variance decomposition, $$ \begin{align*} R(δ,θ) = \expect_θ[(δ - θ)^2] = B(θ)^2 + \var_θ[δ(X)] \end{align*} $$ where $B(θ) := \expect_θ[δ(X)] - θ$ is the bias. And by Cramér-Rao, $$ \begin{align*} \var_θ[δ(X)] \geq \frac{(\dd{θ}\expect[δ(X)])^2}{n \,I(θ)} = \frac{(B'(θ) + 1)^2}{n\, I(θ)}. \end{align*} $$ Computing the Fisher information $I(θ)$ to be $$ \begin{align*} I(θ) ={}& -\expect_θ\left[\pdd[2]{θ}\left(-\log \sqrt{2π} - \frac{1}{2}(X - θ)^2\right)\right] = 1, \end{align*} $$ we combine all of the above to get $$ \begin{align*} B(θ)^2 + \frac{1}{n}(B'(θ) + 1)^2 \leq \frac{1}{n}. \end{align*} $$

Now we analyse this inequality:

  • $B'(θ) \leq 0$ so $B$ is decreasing.

  • $B$ is bounded.

Therefore there exists $θ_n \nearrow \infty$ and $\tilde θ_n\searrow \infty$ such that $B'(θ_n)\to 0$ and $B'(\tilde θ_n)\to 0$. Then $B(θ_n) \to 0$ and $B(θ_n) \to 0$ by the inequality, so $B \equiv 0$ by monotonicity.

Hence $R(δ,θ) = 1/n = R(\overline{X_n}, θ)$, and so $\overline{X_n}$ is admissible.
