Let $H_0: θ \in Θ_0 = \set{θ_0}$ and $Θ\subseteq \R^d$. Assume the usual regularity conditions, we want to show $$ \begin{align*} 2 \log Λ = 2 l_n(θ_0) - 2 l_n(\hat θ_n)\xrightarrow{d} χ_d^2, \end{align*} $$ where $l_n(θ) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log f(X_i, θ)$ and $\hat θ_n$ is the maximum likelihood estimator based on $X_1, \ldots, X_n$.

Let $ε>0$ be sufficiently small such that regularity conditions hold on $\sb{θ}{\|θ - θ_0\| < ε}$, and let $E_n$ be the event where $\|\hat θ_n - θ_0\|<ε$. Since $\hat θ_n \xrightarrow{p} θ_0$, $\prob(E_n) \to 1$, so it suffices to show the distributional limit of $2\log Λ$ in $E_n$.

As $l_n$ is $C^2$, we Taylor expand $$ \begin{align*} 2 l_n(θ_0) - 2l_n(\hat θ_n) = 2\pdfd{l_n}{θ^T}(\hat θ_n)\cdot (\hat θ_n - θ_0) - (\hat θ_n - θ_0)^T \pdfxd{l_n}{θ}{θ^T}(\overline{θ_n}) (\hat θ_n - θ_0) \end{align*} $$ for some $\overline{θ_n}$ on the line segment between $θ_0$ and $\hat θ_n$.

Since $\hat θ_n$ maximises $l_n$, we have $\pdfd{l_n}{θ}(\hat θ_n) = 0$. Moreover, asymptotic normality shows $\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_n - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,I(θ_0\inv))$. It suffices to show $-\frac{1}{n}\pdfxd{l_n}{θ}{θ^T}(\overline{θ_n}) \xrightarrow{p} I(θ_0)$, then we are done by the result of Wald statistics.

We apply the same technique as with the observed Fisher information: define $$ \begin{align*} \tilde J(θ) := \expect_{θ_0}\left[\pdd{θ}\pdd{θ^T}\log f(X, θ)\right] \end{align*} $$ and noting $\tilde J(θ_0) = -I(θ_0)$, decompose $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{n} \pdfxd{l_n}{θ}{θ^T}(\overline{θ_n}) - \tilde J(θ_0) ={}& \left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfxd{\log f(X,θ)}{θ}{θ^T}(\overline{θ_n}) - \tilde J(\overline{θ_n})\right) + (\tilde J(\overline{θ_n}) - \tilde J(θ_0)). \end{align*} $$ Upper bound the first term by $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{θ\in K} \left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfxd{\log f(X,θ)}{θ}{θ^T}(θ) - \tilde J(θ)\right| \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0 \end{align*} $$ using a uniform law of large numbers. For the second term, since $\hat θ_n\xrightarrow{p}θ_0$, we also have $\overline{θ_n} \xrightarrow{p}θ_0$, and then apply the continuous mapping to $\tilde J$.
