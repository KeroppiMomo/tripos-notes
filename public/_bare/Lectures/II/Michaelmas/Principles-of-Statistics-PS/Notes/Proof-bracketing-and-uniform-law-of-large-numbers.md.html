(Non-examinable)

Take any $ε>0$. Take $N(ε/3)$ brackets $[\underline{h_j}, \overline{h_j}]$ covering $\mathcal{H}$. Then on an event $A$ with $\prob(A) = 1$, for all $ω\in A$, there exists $n_0(ω)$ such that for all $n>n_0(ω_{0})$, $$ \begin{align*} \max_{g\in \set{\underline{h_1}, \overline{h_1}, \ldots, \underline{h_N}, \overline{h_N}}} \left|\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} g(X_i(ω)) - \expect g(X)\right| \xrightarrow{n\to \infty} 0. \end{align*} $$

Take $h\in \mathcal{H}$ arbitrary. On the event $A$, there exists $j$ such that $n>n_0$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} h(X_i) - \expect h(X) \leq{}& \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \overline{h_j}(X_i) - \expect h(X) \\ ={}& \underbrace{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \overline{h_j}(X_i) - \expect \overline{h_j}(X)}_{<ε/3} + \underbrace{\expect \overline{h_j}(X) - \expect h(X)}_{<ε/3 \text{ by hypothesis 3}} \\ <{}& ε. \end{align*} $$ A similar argument but using $\underline{h_j}$ yields that $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i}^{n} h(X_i) - \expect h(X) > -ε. \end{align*} $$ So we conclude that with probability 1, $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{h\in \mathcal{H}} \left|\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} h(X_i) - \expect h(X)\right| \xrightarrow{n\to \infty}0. \end{align*} $$
