Let $ε>0$ be such that $B_ε(θ_0)$ is contained within $K$. Let $E_n$ be the event where $\|\hat θ_n - θ_0\|<ε$. By consistency, $\prob(E_n) \to 1$ as $n\to \infty$. Therefore, it suffices to prove the distributional limit of $\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_n - θ_0)$ in $E_n$.

As $θ \mapsto f(x,θ)$ is twice continuously differentiable at $θ_0$ and $\hat θ_n$ maximises $\overline{l_n}(θ) := \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \log f(X_i, θ)$, we have $\pdfd{\overline{l_n}}{θ}(\hat θ_n) = 0$. Applying Taylor's theorem, $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \pdfd{\overline{l_n}}{θ}(θ_0) + \overline{A_n}(\hat θ_n - θ_0) \implies \sqrt{n} (\hat θ_n - θ_0) = -\overline{A_n}\inv \left(\sqrt{n}\pdfd{\overline{l_n}}{θ}(θ_0)\right) \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} (\overline{A_n})_{ij} = \pdfxd{\overline{l_n}}{θ_i}{θ_j} (θ^{(i)}) \end{align*} $$ for some $θ^{(i)}$ on the line segment between $θ_0$ and $\hat θ_n$.

We will show $\overline{A_n}\xrightarrow{p} -I(θ_0)$ and $\sqrt{n} \pdfd{\overline{l_n}}{θ}(θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, I(θ_0))$. By the continuous mapping theorem (working in an event where $\overline{A_n}$ is invertible), $\overline{A_n}\inv \xrightarrow{p} -I(θ_0)\inv$, so by Slutsky's lemma, $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{n}(\hat θ_n - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} I(θ_0)\inv \cdot N(0, I(θ_0)) = N(0, I(θ_0)\inv). \end{align*} $$

$\sqrt{n} \pdfd{\overline{l_n}}{θ}(θ_0)$

Using that $\pdfd{\log f(X, θ)}{θ}(θ_0)$ has mean $0$ and variance $I(θ_0)$, $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{n} \pdfd{\overline{l_n}}{θ}(θ_0) &= \sqrt{n}\biggl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfd{\log f(X_i, θ)}{θ}(θ_0) - \underbrace{\expect_{θ_0}\left[\pdfd{\log f(X,θ)}{θ}(θ_0)\right]}_{0}\biggr) \\ &\xrightarrow{d} N\biggl(0, \var\Bigl(\pdfd{\log f(X,θ)}{θ}(θ_0)\Bigr)\biggr) \\ &= N(0, I(θ_0)). \end{align*} $$

$\overline{A_n}$

Estimate using $-I(θ_0)$ by $$ \begin{align*} (\overline{A_n})_{ij} ={}& \left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfxd{\log f(X_i, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ^{(i)}) - \expect_{θ_0}\biggl[\pdfxd{\log f(X, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ^{(i)}) \;\bigg|\; θ^{(i)}\biggr]\right) \\ &+\left(\expect_{θ_0}\biggl[\pdfxd{\log f(X, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ^{(i)}) \;\bigg|\; θ^{(i)}\biggr] - \expect_{θ_0}\biggl[\pdfxd{\log f(X, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ_0)\biggr]\right) \\ &+ \expect_{θ_0}\biggl[\pdfxd{\log f(X, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ_0)\biggr]. \end{align*} $$

For the first term $(I)$, we eliminate the dependence on $θ^{(i)}$ by bounding by $$ \begin{align*} |(I)| \leq \sup_{θ\in K} \left|\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfxd{\log f(X_i, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ) - \expect_{θ_0}\left[\pdfxd{\log f(X, θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ)\right]\right| \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0 \end{align*} $$ and apply a uniform law of large numbers.

For the second term, write $g(θ) = \expect_{θ_0}[\pdfxd{\log f(X,θ)}{θ_i}{θ_j}(θ)]$. By dominated convergence, $g$ is continuous. Moreover since $\hat θ_n \xrightarrow{p} θ_0$, also $θ^{(i)} \xrightarrow{p}θ_0$, so by the continuous mapping theorem, $g(\hat θ_n) \xrightarrow{p} g(θ_0)$, showing the second term $\xrightarrow{p} 0$.

Finally, the third term equals $-I(θ_0)_{ij}$.
