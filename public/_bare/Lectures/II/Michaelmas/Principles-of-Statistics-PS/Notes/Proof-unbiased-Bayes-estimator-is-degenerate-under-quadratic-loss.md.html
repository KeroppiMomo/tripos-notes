We know $$ \begin{align*} δ(x) = \expect[θ\mid x], \qquad \expect[δ(X)\mid θ] = θ. \end{align*} $$ It suffices to show $$ \begin{align*} \expect[(δ(X) - θ)^2] = 0. \end{align*} $$

Expand $$ \begin{align*} \expect[(δ(X) - θ)^2] = \expect[θ^2] - 2\expect[δ(X) θ] + \expect[δ(X)^2]. \end{align*} $$ Mess around with it: $$ \begin{align*} \expect[δ(X)^2] ={}& \expect[δ(X) \cdot \expect[θ\mid X]] &&\text{$π$-Bayes rule}\\ ={}& \expect[\expect[δ(X) θ\mid X]] \\ ={}& \expect[δ(X)θ] &&\text{total expectation} \end{align*} $$ and $$ \begin{align*} \expect[δ(X) θ] ={}& \expect[\expect[δ(X) θ \mid θ]] &&\text{total expectation} \\ ={}& \expect[\expect[δ(X) \expect[δ(X)\mid θ]\mid θ]] &&\text{unbiasedness} \\ ={}& \expect[\expect[δ(X) \mid θ]^2] \\ ={}& \expect[θ^2] &&\text{unbiasedness}. \end{align*} $$
