Assumption 4 allows us to define $$ \begin{align*} l(θ) := \expect_{θ_0} [\log f(X, θ)]. \end{align*} $$

Taking $g(x,θ) = \log f(x,θ)$ and $G(x) = \sup_{θ\in Θ} |\log f(x,θ)|$, assumptions 1, 2, 4 gives a uniform law of large numbers $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{θ\in Θ} \left|\overline{l_n}(θ) - l(θ)\right| \xrightarrow{p} 0 \tag{1} \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} \overline{l_n}(θ) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \log f(X_i, θ). \end{align*} $$

By assumptions 4 and 5, $θ_0$ is the unique maximiser of $l$. Moreover assumption 2 together with dominated convergence applied to assumption 4 implies $l$ is continuous.

Note $\hat θ_n$ is a maximiser of $\overline{l_n}(θ)$. Since $\overline{l_n}$ is continuous, $f>0$ and $Θ$ is compact, a maximiser for $\overline{l_n}$ always exists.

To show $\hat θ_n \xrightarrow{p} θ_0$, we need to show for all $ε>0$, $$ \begin{align*} \prob[\hat θ_n \in Θ_ε] \xrightarrow{n\to \infty} 0 \end{align*} $$ where $Θ_ε = \sb{θ\in Θ}{\|θ - θ_0\| \geq ε}$.

Fix $ε>0$. Since $Θ_ε$ is compact and $l$ is continuous, there exists a maximiser $θ_ε\in Θ_ε$ of $l$, and $l(θ_ε) < l(θ_0)$ as $θ_0$ is the unique maximiser of $l$. Pick any $δ>0$ such that $δ < \frac{1}{2}(l(θ_0) - l(θ_ε))$. Let $A_{n,ε}$ be the event where $\sup_{θ\in Θ} |\overline{l_n}(θ) - l(θ)| < δ$, which satisfies $\prob(A_{n,ε}) \to 1$ by $(1)$. In the event $A_{n,ε}$, $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{θ\in Θ_ε}\overline{l_n}(θ) \leq{}& \sup_{θ\in Θ_ε} l(θ) + \sup_{θ\in Θ_ε} |\overline{l_n}(θ) - l(θ)| \\ <{} & l(θ_ε) + δ \\ <{}& l(θ_0) - δ \\ <{}& \sup_{θ\in Θ} l(θ) - \sup_{θ\in Θ}|\overline{l_n}(θ) - l(θ)| \\ \leq{}& \sup_{θ\in Θ} \overline{l_n}(θ) \end{align*} $$ so $\hat θ_n$ cannot lie in $Θ_ε$. Therefore $\prob[\hat θ_n \in Θ_ε] \leq 1 - \prob(A_{n,ε}) \to 0$.
