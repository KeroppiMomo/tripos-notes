Define for $θ\in Θ$, $$ \begin{align*} \tilde I(θ) = \expect_{θ_0} [\underbrace{∇_θ \log f(X, θ) ∇_θ \log f(X, θ)^T}_{q(X,θ)}]. \end{align*} $$ Note $\tilde I(θ_0) = I(θ_0)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \hat \imath_n - I(θ_0) = \underbrace{\bigl(\hat\imath_n(\hat θ_\text{MLE}) - \tilde I(\hat θ_{\text{MLE}})\bigr)}_{(A)} + \underbrace{\bigl(\tilde I(\hat θ_\text{MLE}) - \tilde I(θ_0)\bigr)}_{(B)} . \end{align*} $$

In $(A)$, we get rid of the dependence on the random variable $\hat θ_{\text{MLE}}$: $$ \begin{align*} |(A)| \leq \sup_{θ\in Θ} \Bigl|\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} q(X,θ) - \expect_{θ_0} q(X,θ)\Bigr| \xrightarrow{P_{θ_0}} 0 \end{align*} $$ by a uniform law of large number.

Finally, note $\hat θ_\text{MLE} \xrightarrow{p} θ_0$, and by the regularity conditions, $\tilde I$ is continuous, so the continuous mapping theorem yields $(B) \xrightarrow{p} 0$.

The case with $\hat \jmath$ is similar.
