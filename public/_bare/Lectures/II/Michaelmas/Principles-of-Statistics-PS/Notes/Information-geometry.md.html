Definition (Kullback-Leibler divergence) open_in_new

Let $P_{θ_0}, P_θ$ be two laws on $\mathcal{X}$ with densities $f(\cdot , θ_0)$ and $f(\cdot , θ)$. Suppose $\expect_{θ_0}|\log(X, θ')| < \infty$ with $X \sim P_{θ_0}$ for $θ' = θ_0$ and $θ$.

The Kullback-Leibler divergence is $$ \begin{align*} \op{KL}(P_{θ_0}, P_θ) := l(θ_0) - l(θ) = \int_{\mathcal{X}} f(x, θ_0) \log \frac{f(x, θ_0)}{f(x,θ)} \d x \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} l(θ') := \expect_{θ_0}[\log f(X, θ')] \qquad (X \sim P_{θ_0}). \end{align*} $$

Proposition (true parameter maximises expectation of log likelihood) open_in_new

Let $\sb{f(\cdot ,θ)}{θ\in Θ}$ be a statistical model, with $X \sim f(\cdot , θ_0)$.

Suppose $\expect_{θ_0}|\log (X, θ)|<\infty$ for all $θ\in Θ$. Then the true parameter $θ_0$ maximises $$ \begin{align*} l(θ) := \expect_{θ_0}\log(X, θ) \end{align*} $$ and hence minimises $\op{KL}(P_{θ_0}, P_θ)$.

Moreover if $θ$ is identifiable (i.e. $f(\cdot, θ) = f(\cdot, θ') \iff θ = θ'$), then $θ_0$ is the unique maximiser of $l$ and hence the unique minimiser of $\op{KL}(P_{θ_0}, P_θ)$.

Proof

Definition (score function) open_in_new

Let $\sb{f(\cdot ,θ)}{θ\in Θ}$ be a statistical model, $Θ\subseteq \R^d$ and $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be iid copies of $X$.

The score function is a random vector $$ \begin{align*} S_n(θ) := \pdd{θ} l_n(θ) ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdd{θ} \log f(X_i, θ), \qquad θ \in \op{int}(Θ) \end{align*} $$ where $l_n(θ) := \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log f(X_i, θ)$ is the log-likelihood function.

The maximum likelihood estimator is a zero of $S_n$.

Under regularity conditions (namely $\int_{\mathcal{X}} \pdfd{f}{θ} \d x = \pdd{θ} \int_{\mathcal{X}} f \d x$), $$ \begin{align*} \expect_θ S_n(θ) = 0. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

Definition (Fisher information) open_in_new

Let $\sb{f(\cdot ,θ)}{θ\in Θ}$ be a statistical model, $Θ\subseteq \R^d$ and $X\sim f(\cdot, θ)$ be a random variable.

The Fisher information matrix is $$ \begin{align*} I(θ) := \expect_θ\left[\pdd{θ}\log f(X, θ) \; \pdd{θ^T} \log f(X, θ)\right] = \expect_θ[S(θ) \; S(θ)^T], \qquad θ\in \op{int}(Θ) \end{align*} $$ where $S(θ) = \pdd{θ}\log f(X, θ)$ is the score.

Under regularity conditions, (if $\int_{\mathcal{X}} \pdfd{f}{θ} \d x = \pdd{θ} \int_{\mathcal{X}} f \d x$ then) $$ \begin{align*} I(θ) = \var(S(θ)) \end{align*} $$ (Derivation) and (if $\int_{\mathcal{X}} \pdfxd{f}{θ_i}{θ_j} \d x = \pdxd{θ_i}{θ_j} \int_{\mathcal{X}} f \d x$ then) $$ \begin{align*} I(θ) = -\expect_θ\left[\pdd{θ} \pdd{θ^T} \log f(X, θ)\right]. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

If $X = (X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ and $X_i \sim f(\cdot, θ)$ are iid, then under regularity conditions (if $\int_{\mathcal{X}} \pdfd{f}{θ} \d x = \pdd{θ} \int_{\mathcal{X}} f \d x$ then) $$ \begin{align*} I_n(θ) := \expect_θ[S_n(θ) S_n(θ)^T] = n \, I(θ) \end{align*} $$ where $S_n(θ) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdd{θ} \log f(X_i, θ)$. (Derivation)

Theorem (Cramér–Rao) open_in_new

Let $\sb{f(\cdot ,θ)}{θ\in Θ}$ be a statistical model, $Θ\subseteq \R^d$, $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be iid random variables, and $\tilde Φ = \tilde Φ(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ be an estimator of $Φ = Φ(θ)\in \R^k$, a differentiable function $Θ\to \R^k$.

Under regularity conditions, $$ \begin{align*} \var_θ(\tilde Φ) \geq \frac{1}{n} \pdfd{\expect_θ[\tilde Φ]}{θ}(θ)^T \, I(θ)\inv \, \pdfd{\expect_θ[\tilde Φ]}{θ}(θ). \end{align*} $$

In particular, if $\tilde Φ$ is unbiased, then $$ \begin{align*} \var_θ(\tilde Φ) \geq \frac{1}{n} \pdfd{Φ}{θ}(θ)^T \, I(θ)\inv \, \pdfd{Φ}{θ}(θ). \end{align*} $$

Proof

However, non-regular estimators can have better asymptotic variance. For example, for $\hat θ_n$ the maximum likelihood estimator in a regular parameter model, define the Hodge's estimator $$ \begin{align*} \tilde θ_n = \begin{cases} \hat θ_n, &|\hat θ_n| \geq n^{-1/4}, \\ 0, &|\hat θ_n|<n^{-1/4}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

  • For $θ_0 \neq 0$, $\tilde θ_n - \hat θ_n \xrightarrow{p} 0$ so $\sqrt{n}(\tilde θ_n - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, I\inv(θ_0))$.

  • For $θ_0 = 0$, $\tilde θ_n \xrightarrow{d} δ_0$.
