Using Bayes' theorem $f(x\mid θ) π(θ) = π(θ\mid x) m(x)$ where $m(x) = \int_Θ f(x\mid θ) π(θ) \d θ$ is the marginal distribution, expand the $π$-Bayes risk as $$ \begin{align*} R_π(δ) ={}& \int_{Θ} \int_{\mathcal{X}} L(δ(x), θ) f(x\mid θ) π(θ) \d x \d θ \\ ={}& \int_{\mathcal{X}} \int_{Θ} L(δ(x), θ) π(θ\mid x) \d θ \; m(x) \d x \\ ={}& \int_{\mathcal{X}} \expect_π[L(δ(x), θ)\mid x] \, \underbrace{m(x)}_{\geq 0} \d x. \end{align*} $$

Hypothesis says $\expect_π[L(δ(x), θ)\mid x] \leq \expect_π[L(δ'(x), θ)\mid x]$ for any $x\in \mathcal{X}$ and any decision rule $δ'$, so $R_π(δ)\leq R_π(δ')$.
