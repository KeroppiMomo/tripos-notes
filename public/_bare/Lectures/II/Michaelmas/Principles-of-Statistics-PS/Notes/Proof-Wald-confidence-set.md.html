Confidence interval

Asymptotic normality implies $\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_{n} - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, I(θ_0)\inv)$, so $$\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_{n,j}- θ_{0,j}) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, (I(θ_0)\inv)_{jj}).$$

By the continuous mapping theorem, $(\hat I_n\inv)_{jj} \xrightarrow{p} (I(θ_0)\inv)_{jj}$, so by Slutsky, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_{n,j} - θ_{0,j})}{\sqrt{(\hat I_n\inv)_{jj}}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1). \end{align*} $$

Confidence set

Decompose $$ \begin{align*} n(\hat θ_n - θ_0)^T \hat I_n(\hat θ_n - θ_0) ={}& n(\hat θ_n - θ_0)^T I(θ_0) (\hat θ_n - θ_0) + n(\hat θ_n - θ_0)^T (\hat I_n - I(θ_0)) (\hat θ_n - θ_0). \end{align*} $$ We claim the first term $\xrightarrow{d} χ_p^2$ and the second term $\xrightarrow{p}0$. By Slutsky, we get $n(\hat θ_n - θ_0)^T \hat I_n (\hat θ_n - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d}χ_p^2$.

For the first term, by continuous mapping applied to $x \mapsto x^T I(θ_0) x$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{n}(\hat θ_n -θ_0)^T I(θ_0) \sqrt{n}(\hat θ_n - θ_0) \xrightarrow{d} Z^T \cdot I(θ_0) \cdot Z, &&Z \sim N(0,I(θ_0)\inv). \end{align*} $$ Note $\sqrt{I(θ_0)} Z \sim N(0, I_p)$, so $(\sqrt{I(θ_0) Z})^T (\sqrt{I(θ_0)}Z)$ is a sum of $p$ independent $N(0,1)$ variables, and hence follows $χ_p^2$.

For the second term, since $\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_n - θ_0)$ converges in distribution and $\hat I_n - I(θ_0)\xrightarrow{p} 0$, by Slutsky, the term converges to 0 in probability.
