Definition (correlation) open_in_new

The correlation of two square-integrable random variables $X,Y$ is $$ \begin{align*} \op{corr}(X,Y) = \frac{\cov(X,Y)}{\sqrt{\var(X)}\sqrt{\var(Y)}}. \end{align*} $$

Definition (sample variance, sample correlation) open_in_new

Let $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be iid copies of a square integrable random vector $X = (X^{(1)}, \ldots, X^{(p)})$ in $\R^p$. Form the matrix $\mathbf{X} = \pmat{X_1^T \\ \vdots \\ X_n^T}$.

Define the sample variance $$ \begin{align*} \hat Σ := \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (X_i - \overline{X_n})(X_i - \overline{X_n})^T = \frac{1}{n}((I - P)\mathbf{X})^T((I-P)\mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{X}^T (I-P) \mathbf{X} \end{align*} $$ where $P = \frac{1}{n} \v 1 \v 1^T$ is the projection matrix onto $\mathbf{1} = (1, \ldots, 1)^T\in \R^p$.

Define the sample correlation $$ \begin{align*} \hat ρ_{jk} := \frac{\hat Σ_{jk}}{\sqrt{\hat Σ_{jj} \hat Σ_{kk}}} = \frac{((I-P)\mathbf{X}^{(j)})^T((I-P) \mathbf{X}^{(k)})}{\|(I-P) \mathbf{X}^{(j)}\| \|(I-P) \mathbf{X}^{(k)}\|} = \frac{\sum_{i} (X_i^{(j)} - \overline{X_n}^{(j)})(X_i^{(k)} - \overline{X_n}^{(k)})}{\sqrt{\sum_{i} (X_i^{(j)} - \overline{X_n}^{(j)})^2 \cdot \sum_{i} (X_i^{(k)} - \overline{X_n}^{(k)})^2}}. \end{align*} $$

In a model $X \sim N(μ, Σ)$, $\hat Σ$ is the maximum likelihood estimator of $Σ$, and $\hat ρ_{jk}$ is the maximum likelihood estimator of $\op{corr}(X^{(j)}, X^{(k)})$.

In general, $\hat Σ \xrightarrow{p} \var(X)$ by the strong law of large numbers, and $\sqrt{n}(\hat Σ - \var(X))$ converges in distribution to a normal by the central limit theorem.

Definition (conditional independence, partial correlation) open_in_new

Given random vectors $X, Y, Z$, we say that $X$ and $Y$ are conditionally independent given $Z$ (or $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$) if $$ \begin{align*} f_{XY\mid Z}(x,y\mid z) = f_{X\mid Z}(x \mid z) f_{Y\mid Z}(y\mid z). \end{align*} $$ Equivalently, $$ \begin{align*} X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z \iff f_{X\mid Y, Z}(x\mid y,z) = m(x,z) \end{align*} $$ for some function $m$, whenever $f_{Y,Z}(y,z) > 0$.

The partial correlation of $X$ and $Y$ given $Z$ is the correlation of $X$ and $Y$ in the conditional density $f_{X,Y\mid Z}(\cdot ,\cdot \mid z)$: $$ \begin{align*} ρ_{X,Y\mid Z}(z) = \frac{\cov(X,Y\mid Z = z)}{\sqrt{\var(X\mid Z=z) \var(Y\mid Z = z)}}. \end{align*} $$

If $X,Y$ are conditionally independent given $Z$, then $ρ_{X,Y\mid Z} \equiv 0$.

Definition (sample conditional variance, sample partial correlation) open_in_new

Let $(W_i, Z_i)_{i=1}^n$ be iid copies of $(W, Z)$, with $W\in \R^m, Z \in \R^n$. Form the matrices $\mathbf{W} = \pmat{W_1^T \\ \vdots \\ W_n^T}, \mathbf{Z} = \pmat{Z_1^T \\ \vdots \\Z_n^T}$.

The sample conditional variance of $W$ given $Z$ is $$ \begin{align*} \hat Ω = \frac{1}{n}((I - P) \mathbf{W})^T ((I - P) \mathbf{W}) = \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{W}^T (I-P) \mathbf{W} \end{align*} $$ where $P$ is the projection matrix onto the columns space of $\pmat{\v 1 & \mathbf{Z}}$.

The sample partial correlation of $W^{(j)}$ and $W^{(k)}$ given $Z$ is $$ \begin{align*} \hat ρ_{W^{(j)}, W^{(k)}\mid Z} = \frac{\hat Ω_{jk}}{\sqrt{\hat Ω_{jj} \hat Ω_{kk}}} = \frac{((I - P)\mathbf{W}^{(j)})^T ((I-P) \mathbf{W}^{(k)})}{\|(I-P)\mathbf{W}^{(j)}\| \|(I - P) \mathbf{W}^{(k)}\|}. \end{align*} $$

If $(W,Z)$ is jointly Gaussian, then $\hat Ω$ is the maximum likelihood estimator of $\var(W\mid Z)$ and $\hat ρ_{W^{(j)}, W^{(k)}\mid Z}$ is the maximum likelihood estimator of $ρ_{W^{(j)},W^{(k)}\mid Z}$. (Proof)
