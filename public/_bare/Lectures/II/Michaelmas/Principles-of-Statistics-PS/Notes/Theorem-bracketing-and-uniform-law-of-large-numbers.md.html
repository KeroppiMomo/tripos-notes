Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be iid copies of $X$ in $\mathcal{X}\subseteq \R^k$, and let $\mathcal{H}$ be a set of measurable functions $\mathcal{X} \to \R$.

Suppose for all $ε>0$, there exists finitely many measurable functions $\overline{h_1}, \underline{h_1}, \ldots, \overline{h_N}, \underline{h_N}: \mathcal{X}\to \R$ such that

  1. the brackets $\sb{[\underline{h_j}, \overline{h_j}]}{j=1,\ldots,N}$ covers $\mathcal{H}$, i.e. for all $h\in \mathcal{H}$, there exists $j$ such that $\underline{h_j}(x) \leq h(x) \leq \overline{h_j}(x)$ for all $x\in \mathcal{X}$;

  2. $\expect|\underline{h_j}(X)|, \expect|\overline{h_j}(X)| < \infty$ for all $j$; and

  3. $\expect[\overline{h_j}(X) - \underline{h_j}(X)] < ε$ for all $j$.

Then $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{h\in \mathcal{H}} \left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} h(X_i) - \expect h(X)\right| \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0 \qquad \text{as $n\to \infty$}. \end{align*} $$

Proof (non-examinable)
