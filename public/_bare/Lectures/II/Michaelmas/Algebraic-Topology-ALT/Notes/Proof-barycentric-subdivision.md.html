$\set{\hat σ_0, \hat σ_1, \ldots, \hat σ_p}$ is affinely independent

Suppose $σ_0 < σ_1 < \ldots < σ_p$, $\sum_{i=0}^{p} t_i \hat σ_i = 0$ and $\sum_{i=0}^{p} t_i = 0$. If not all $t_i=0$, find the maximal $j$ such that $t_j \neq 0$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \hat σ_j = -\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} \frac{t_i}{t_j} \hat σ_i \in \langle \hat σ_0, \ldots, \hat σ_{j-1}\rangle \subseteq σ_{j-1} < σ_j \end{align*} $$ but $\hat σ_j$ is the interior of $σ_j$, contradiction.

$K'$ is a simplicial complex

Faces

For any $\langle \hat σ_0, \ldots, \hat σ_p\rangle\in K'$ where $σ_0 < \ldots < σ_p$ are simplices in $K$, any subset of vertices $\set{\hat σ_{i_0}, \ldots, \hat σ_{i_q}}$ (where $i_0 < \ldots < i_q$) still results in a sequence $σ_{i_0} < \ldots < σ_{i_q}$.

Intersection

Let $σ' = \langle \hat σ_0, \ldots, \hat σ_p\rangle$ and $τ'=\langle \hat τ_0, \ldots, \hat τ_q\rangle$ where $σ_0 < \ldots < σ_p$ and $τ_0 < \ldots < τ_q$ are simplices in $K$. Note $σ' \subseteq σ_p$ and $τ'\subseteq τ_q$, so $$σ' \cap τ' \subseteq σ_p \cap τ_q := δ,$$ which is a common face of $σ_p$ and $τ_q$. Observe also that because $δ\leq σ_p$ and $σ'\subseteq σ_p$, we have (convince yourself using barycentric coordinates) $$ \begin{align*} σ' \cap δ &= \langle \hat σ_0, \ldots, \hat σ_{p'}\rangle &&\text{where $p'$ is maximal such that $σ_{p'}\leq δ$}, \\ τ' \cap δ &= \langle \hat τ_0, \ldots, \hat τ_{q'}\rangle && \text{where $q'$ is maximal such that $σ_{q'}\leq δ$}. \end{align*} $$ Therefore by replacing $σ'$ with $σ'\cap δ$ and $τ'$ with $τ'\cap δ$, we can WLOG assume $σ_p, τ_q$ are faces of a common simplex $δ\in K$.

Induct on the dimension of $δ$ to show $σ'\cap τ'\in K'$. Clear if $δ$ is a 0-simplex. Two cases:

  • If $σ'$ or $τ'$ does not contain $\hat δ$, say $σ_p < δ$, then $σ'\cap τ'\subseteq σ_p$ lie in a smaller dimensional simplex, so induction hypothesis can be applied.

  • Otherwise if both $σ'$ and $τ'$ contain $\hat δ$, i.e. $σ_p = δ = τ_q$, then letting $σ'' := \langle \hat σ_0, \ldots, \hat σ_{p-1}\rangle, τ'' := \langle \hat τ_0, \ldots, \hat τ_{q-1}\rangle\in K'$, we observe $σ'\cap τ'$ is spanned by $σ''\cap τ''$ and $\hat δ$.

    (Indeed for any $x\in σ'\cap τ' \setminus \set{\hat δ}$, make a ray from $\hat δ$ to $x$, which must intersect $\partial δ$ at say $y$. Now $y\in \partial δ\cap σ' = σ''$ and $y\in \partial δ\cap τ' = τ''$, so $x$ is a convex combination of $y\in σ''\cap τ''$ and $\hat δ$.)

    Now $σ''\cap τ''\subseteq σ_{p-1}<δ$ lie in a smaller dimensional simplex, so by induction hypothesis is in $K'$. Thus $σ'\cap τ'$ is also in $K'$.

$|K'| = |K|$

Since $\langle \hat σ_0, \ldots, \hat σ_p\rangle \subseteq σ_p$, it is clear that $|K'| \subseteq |K|$.

Let $x\in σ = \langle a_0, \ldots, a_p\rangle\in K$, and write its barycentric coordinates as $x = \sum_{i=0}^{p} t_i a_i$. By reordering the vertices, assume $t_0 \geq t_1 \geq \ldots \geq t_p$. Then $$ \begin{align*} x ={}& (t_0 - t_1) \underbrace{a_0}_{\widehat{\langle a_0\rangle}} + 2(t_1 - t_2) \underbrace{\frac{a_0 + a_1}{2}}_{\widehat{\langle a_0, a_1\rangle}} + 3(t_2 - t_3) \underbrace{\frac{a_0 + a_1 + a_2}{3}}_{\widehat{\langle a_0, a_1, a_2\rangle}} + \ldots + (p+1)t_p \underbrace{\frac{a_0 + \ldots + a_p}{p+1}}_{\widehat{\langle a_0, \ldots, a_p\rangle}} \end{align*} $$ shows $x\in \langle \widehat{\langle a_0\rangle}, \widehat {\langle a_0, a_1\rangle}, \ldots, \widehat{\langle a_0, \ldots, a_p\rangle}\rangle\in K'$. Hence $|K| \subseteq |K'|$.
