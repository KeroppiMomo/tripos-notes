Simplicial approximation

Let $a: K\to K'$ be a simplicial approximation of the identity map. We will prove the claim by double induction on the dimension of $K$ and the number of simplexes of $K$ of maximal dimension.

For the base case, consider when $K$ is a simplex $σ$ and all of its faces. $K'$ is a cone with cone point $\hat σ$, and $K$ is a cone with any vertex as the cone point. Thus their homology groups are trivial in strictly positive degrees, and isomorphic to $\Z$ in degree 0, generated by any vertex. Then it is clear that $a_\ast$ is an isomorphism.

For the induction step, let $σ$ be a simplex of $K$ of maximal dimension. Then it is not a proper face of any simplex, and thus $L := K \setminus \set{σ}$ is a subcomplex. Let $S$ be the subcomplex given by $σ$ and all of its faces, and $T = S\cap L$. Note that $a$ sends $L'$ into $L$, $S'$ into $S$, and $T'$ into $T$: if $\hat τ\in V_{L'}$, then the unique simplex $τ$ whose interior contains $\hat τ$ is in $L$, and so any simplicial approximation to the identity maps $\hat τ$ to an element of $V_{τ}\subseteq V_L$; similarly for $S$ and $T$.

A diagram chase shows that $a_\ast$ must also be an isomorphism.

Any two simplicial approximations $K' \to K$ to the identity map are contiguous to each other and so induce the same isomorphism $H_*(K') \to H_*(K)$.

Chain map

First let's check $s_\bullet$ is indeed a chain map: by induction assume $d_{n-1} \circ s_{n-1} = s_{n-2} \circ d_{n-1}$. Then $$ \begin{align*} d_n \circ s_n(σ) ={}& d_n([\hat σ, s_{n-1}\circ d_n(σ)]) \\ ={}& s_{n-1}\circ d_n(σ) - [\hat σ, d_{n-1} \circ s_{n-1} \circ d_n(σ)] \\ ={}& s_{n-1}\circ d_n(σ) - [\hat σ, s_{n-2} \circ \underbrace{d_{n-1} \circ d_n}_{0}(σ)] \\ ={}& s_{n-1}\circ d_n(σ). \end{align*} $$

Let $\prec$ be a total order of $V_K$, and let $a: K' \to K$ be a simplicial approximation to the identity, sending $\hat σ$ to the smallest vertex of $σ$ with respect to $\prec$. We will show $a_n \circ s_n: C_n(K) \to C_n(K)$ is the identity by induction on $n$. Certainly $a_0 \circ s_0$ is. For $σ = [v_0, \ldots, v_n]\in C_n(K)$ with $v_0\prec \ldots \prec v_n$, assuming $a_{n-1} \circ s_{n-1} = \id_{C_{n-1}(K)}$, we have $$ \begin{align*} a_n(s_n(σ)) ={}& a_n([\hat σ, s_{n-1}\circ d_n(σ)]) \\ ={}& [v_0, a_{n-1} \circ s_{n-1} \circ d_n(σ)] \\ ={}& [v_0, d_n(σ)] \\ ={}& \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^i [v_0, v_0, \ldots, v_{i-1}, v_{i+1}, \ldots, v_n] \\ ={}& [v_0, \ldots, v_n] \end{align*} $$ since duplicated $v_0$'s appear in terms $i\neq 0$. Hence $\id_{H_n(K)} = a_\ast \circ s_\ast = ν_K \circ s_\ast$.
