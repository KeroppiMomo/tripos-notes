Definition (homotopy) open_in_new

Let $f, g: X \to Y$ be maps.

A homotopy from $f$ to $g$ is a map $H: X \times [0,1] \to Y$ such that $$ \begin{align*} H(x, 0) ={}& f(x), \\ H(x,1) ={}& g(x). \end{align*} $$

Say $f$ is homotopic to $g$ if such a map exists, written as $f\simeq g$ or $f \simeq_H g$ if we wish to specify the homotopy $H$.

For a subset $A\subseteq X$, say a homotopy $H$ is relative to $A$ if $$ \begin{align*} f(a) = H(a,t) = g(a) \qquad \text{for all $a\in A, t\in [0,1]$}. \end{align*} $$ We write $f\simeq g \rel A$.

"Homotopic relative to $A$" is an equivalence relation on the set of maps $X\to Y$. (Proof)

Homotopic maps are preserved by composition: if $f_0, f_1: X\to Y$ are homotopic and $g_0, g_1: Y\to Z$ are homotopic, then so are $g_0\circ f_0, g_1\circ f_1: X\to Z$. (Proof)

Definition (homotopy equivalence) open_in_new

A map $f: X\to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence if there exists a homotopy inverse, i.e. a map $g: Y\to X$ such that $g\circ f$ $\simeq$ $\id_X$ and $f\circ g\simeq \id_Y$.

Two topological spaces $X, Y$ are said to be homotopy equivalent (written as $X \simeq Y$) if a homotopy equivalence $f: X\to Y$ exists.

The relation of homotopy equivalence is reflexive, symmetric and transitive. (Proof)

Definition (contractible space) open_in_new

A space $X$ is contractible if it is homotopy equivalent to the one-point space: $X \simeq \set{*}$.

Expanding the definition: $X$ is contractible iff a homotopy $X \times [0,1]\to X$ from $\id_X$ to constant.

Definition (retraction, deformation retraction) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a space and $A\subseteq X$ be a subspace.

A retraction of $X$ to $A$ is a map $r: X\to A$ such that $r|_A = \id_A$.

A deformation retraction of $X$ to $A$ is a map $r: X\to A$ that is a retraction and satisfies $i\circ r$ $\simeq$ $\id_X$, where $i: A\hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion map.

A strong deformation retraction of $X$ to $A$ is a deformation retraction $r: X\to A$ with $i\circ r\simeq\id_X \rel A$.

A deformation retraction is a homotopy equivalence with homotopy inverse $i$.

Definition (path, loop, concatenation, inverse, constant path) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space and $x_0, x_1\in X$ be points.

A path from $x0$ to $x1$ is a map $γ: [0,1]\to X$ such such that $γ(0) = x_0$ and $γ(1) = x_1$. We write $γ: x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_1$.

If $x_0 = x_1$, then we call $γ$ a loop based at $x0$_.$ For paths $γ: x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_1$ and $γ': x_1 \rightsquigarrow x_2$, define the concatenation of $γ$ and $γ'$ as the path $(γ\cdot γ'): x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_2$ defined by $$ \begin{align*} (γ\cdot γ')(t) = \begin{cases} γ(2t), &0\leq t\leq \frac12, \\ γ'(2t-1), &\frac12\leq t\leq 1. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Define the inverse of $γ$ as the path $γ\inv: x_1\rightsquigarrow x_0$ defined by $γ\inv(t) = γ(1-t)$.

For any $x_0\in X$, define the constant path at $x_0$ as $c_{x_0}(t) = x_0$.

The concatenation is continuous by the gluing lemma.

The relation $\sim$ on $X$ given by $x\sim y \iff$ (there exists a path from $x$ to $y$) is an equivalence relation by the above constructions.

Definition (zeroth homotopy group) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space.

The set of path components is denoted $π_0(X)$.

Proposition (map descends on path components) open_in_new

For any map $f: X\to Y$, there is a well-defined associated function $$ \begin{align*} π_0(f) : π_0(X) &\to π_0(Y) \\ [x] &\mapsto [f(x)] \end{align*} $$ where $X\to{}$$π_0(X)$${}: x\mapsto [x]$ etc sends a point to its path component. (Proof)

In fact $π_0$ is a functor from the homotopy category to the category of sets, namely:

  • If $f$${}\simeq{}$$g$, then $π_0(f) = π_0(g)$. (Proof)

  • $π_0$ preserves composition: $π_0(g\circ f) = π_0(g) \circ π_0(f)$ for maps $f: X\to Y$ and $g: Y\to Z$.

  • $π_0(\id_X) = \id_{π_0(X)}$.

If $f$ is a homotopy equivalence, then $π_0(f)$ is a bijection. (Proof)

Definition (homotopic as paths) open_in_new

Two paths $γ, γ': [0,1]\to X$ from $x_0\in X$ to $x_1\in X$ are homotopic as paths if they are homotopic relative to $\set{0,1}$. We write $γ\simeq γ'$ as paths from $x_0$ to $x_1$.

Concatenation preserves this property: if $γ_0 \simeq γ_1$ as paths from $x_0$ to $x_1$ and $γ_0' \simeq γ_1'$ as paths from $x_1$ to $x_2$, then $γ_0 \cdot γ_0'\simeq γ_1\cdot γ_1'$ as paths from $x_0$ to $x_2$. (Proof)

Let $γ_0: x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_1$, $γ_1: x_1\rightsquigarrow x_2$, $γ_2: x_2 \rightsquigarrow x_3$ be paths in $X$. Then

  • $(γ_0 \cdot γ_1) \cdot γ_2 \simeq γ_0 \cdot (γ_1\cdot γ_2)$ as paths from $x_0$ to $x_3$; (Proof)

  • $c_{x_0}\cdot γ_0 \simeq γ_0 \simeq γ_0 \cdot c_{x_1}$ as paths from $x_0$ to $x_1$; (Proof)

  • $γ_0 \cdot γ_0\inv \simeq c_{x_0}$ as paths from $x_0$ to $x_0$. (Proof)

Theorem (fundamental group) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a space and $x_0\in X$ be a point.

Let $π_1(X, x_0)$ denote the set of homotopy classes of loops in $X$ based at $x_0$. Then $π_1(X, x_0)$ is a group with operation $[γ] \cdot [γ'] = [γ\cdot γ']$ and identity element $[c_{x_0}]$.

Proof: immediate from properties here.

Definition (based space, based map, based homotopy) open_in_new

A based space is a pair $(X, x_0)$ of a topological space $X$ and a basepoint $x_0\in X$.

A based map $f: (X, x_0) \to (Y, y_0)$ is a continuous function $f: X\to Y$ such that $f(x_0) = y_0$.

A based homotopy a homotopy relative to $\set{x_0}$.

Proposition (based maps descend to homomorphisms between fundamental groups) open_in_new

For any based map $f: (X, x_0) \to (Y, y_0)$, there is a well-defined group homomorphism on fundamental groups $$ \begin{align*} f_\ast \equiv π_1(f) : π_1(X, x_0) &\to π_1(Y, y_0) \\ [γ] &\mapsto [f\circ γ]. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

In fact $π_1$ is a functor from the homotopy category of based space to the category of groups, namely

  • If $f$ is based homotopic to $f'$, then $π_1(f) = π_1(f')$.

  • $π_1$ preserves composition: $π_1(g\circ f) = π_1(g)\circ π_1(f)$ for based maps $f: (X,x_0)\to (Y,y_0), g: (Y,y_0)\to (Z,z_0)$.

  • $π_1(\id_X) = \id_{π_1(X,x_0)}$.

Definition (simply connected) open_in_new

A topological space $X$ is simply connected if

  • $X$ is path-connected; and

  • its fundamental group $π_1(X, x_0)$ is trivial for some (and thus every) basepoint $x_0$.

If $X$ is contractible, then $X$ is simply connected.

Lemma (simply connected iff unique homotopy class of paths) open_in_new

A space $X$ is simply connected iff for each pair of points $x_0, x_1\in X$, there exists a unique homotopy class of paths from $x_0$ to $x_1$.
