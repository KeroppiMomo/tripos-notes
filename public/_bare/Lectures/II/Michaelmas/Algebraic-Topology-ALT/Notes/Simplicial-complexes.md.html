Definition (affinely independent) open_in_new

A finite set of points $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n\in \R^m$ is said to be affinely independent if for all $t_0, \ldots, t_n \in \R$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i a_i = 0 \quad \text{and}\quad \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i = 0 \quad \iff \quad t_0 = \ldots = t_n = 0. \end{align*} $$

  • $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n$ are affinely independent iff $a_1 - a_0, a_2 - a_0, \ldots, a_n - a_0$ are linearly independent. (Proof)

Definition (simplex) open_in_new

Suppose $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n\in \R^m$ are affinely independent.

They are said to span an $n$-simplex $$ \begin{align*} σ = \langle a_0, \ldots, a_n\rangle = \sb{\sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i a_i}{t_i\geq 0, \; \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i = 1}. \end{align*} $$ The $a_i$'s are the vertices of $σ$.

Any points $x\in σ$ can be written uniquely (by affine independence) as $x = \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_ia_i$. $(t_0, \ldots, t_n)$ are the barycentric coordinates of $x$.

A face of $σ$ is a simplex $τ$ spanned by a subset of $\set{a_0, \ldots, a_n}$. We write $τ\leq σ$, and $τ<σ$ if $τ$ is a proper face (i.e. $τ\neq σ$).

The boundary $\partial σ$ of $σ$ is the union of all of its proper faces. The interior $\mathring{σ}$ of $σ$ is $σ\setminus \partial σ$.

  • These notions of boundary and interior differ from the topological ones.

Definition (simplicial complex) open_in_new

A (geometric) simplicial complex in $\R^m$ is a finite set $K$ of simplices in $\R^m$ satisfying that

  1. if $σ\in K$ and $τ\leq σ$, then $τ\in K$; and

  2. if $σ, τ\in K$, then either $σ\cap τ$ is empty or is a face of both $σ$ and $τ$.

The vertices $V_K$ of $K$ is the set of all 0-simplices of $K$.

The dimension of $K$ is the largest $n$ for which $K$ contains an $n$-simplex.

The polyhedron $|K|\subseteq \R^m$ associated to $K$ is the union of all simplices in $K$.

The $d$-skeleton $K_{(d)}$ of $K$ is the sub-simplicial complex given by all simplices of $K$ of dimension at most $d$.

  • The standard $n$-simplex $Δ^{n} \subseteq \R^{n+1}$ is the simplex spanned by the standard basis vectors $e_1, \ldots, e_{n+1}$ of $\R^{n+1}$. The set of all its faces defines a simplicial complex.

  • The simplicial $(n-1)$-sphere is the simplicial complex given by the proper faces of $Δ^n$.

Lemma (point on polyhedron lies in interior of unique simplex) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a simplicial complex.

Any point on the polyhedron $|K|$ lies in the interior $\mathring{σ}$ of some unique simplex $σ\in K$.

Proof

Definition (simplicial map) open_in_new

Let $K$ and $L$ be simplicial complexes.

A simplicial map is a function $f: V_K \to V_L$ such that if $σ = \langle a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_p\rangle\in K$, then the set $\set{f(a_0), \ldots f(a_p)}$ (which may contain repeated vertices) spans a simplex of $K$, denoted as $f(σ)$.

We write $f: K\to L$ for a simplicial map.

  • Each simplicial map $f: K\to L$ indices a continuous map $|f|: |K|\to |L|$ between polyhedra. We also have $|f|\circ |g| = |f\circ g|$. (Proof)

Definition (star, link) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a simplicial complex and $x\in K$ be a point.

  • The (open) star of $x$ is the union of the interior of simplices in $K$ which contain $x$: $$ \begin{align*} \op{St}_K(x) = \bigcup_{x\in σ\in K} \mathring σ. \end{align*} $$

  • The link $\op{Lk}_K(x)$ of $x$ is the union of the simplices in $K$ which do not contain $x$, but are a face of a simplex which contains $x$.

Lemma (simplicial approximation) open_in_new

Let $K$ and $L$ be simplicial complexes, and $f: |K|\to |L|$ be a continuous map.

Let $g: V_K\to V_L$ be a simplicial approximation to $f$, meaning for each $v\in V_K$, $$ \begin{align*} f(\op{St}_K(v)) \subseteq \op{St}_L(g(v)). \end{align*} $$

Then $g$ defines a simplicial map, and $f$ is homotopic to $|g|$ via $$ \begin{align*} H: |K| \times [0,1] &\to |L| \\ (x,t) &\mapsto (1-t) \cdot f(x) + t\cdot g(t). \end{align*} $$ In particular $H$ is relative to the subspace $\sb{x\in |K|}{f(x) = |g|(x)}$ where $f$ and $|g|$ agree.

Proof

Definition (barycentric subdivision) open_in_new

The barycentre of a simplex $σ = \langle a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_p\rangle$ is the point $$ \begin{align*} \hat σ = \frac{a_0 + \ldots + a_p}{p+1}. \end{align*} $$

The (first) barycentric subdivision of a simplicial complex $K$ is the simplicial complex $$ \begin{align*} K' = \sb{\set{\hat σ_0, \hat σ_1, \ldots, \hat σ_p}}{σ_i\in K\text{ and } σ_0 < σ_1 < \ldots < σ_p}. \end{align*} $$

Define the $r$-th barycentric subdivision $K^{(r)}$ inductively as $(K^{(r-1)})'$.

  • $K'$ is indeed a simplicial simplex and $|K'| = |K|$.

Definition (mesh of simplicial complex) open_in_new

The mesh of a simplicial complex $K$ is $$ \begin{align*} μ(K) = \max \sb{\|v_0 - v_1\|}{\langle v_0, v_1\rangle\in K}. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (mesh of barycentric subdivisions tend to zero) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a simplicial complex of dimension $n$. Then $$ \begin{align*} μ(K') \leq \Bigl(\frac{n}{n+1}\Bigr) \cdot μ(K). \end{align*} $$ In particular, $μ$$(K^{(r)}) \to 0$ as $r\to \infty$.

Proof
