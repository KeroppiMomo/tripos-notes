Definition (covering space) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space.

A covering space of $X$ is a pair $(\tilde X, p)$ of a space $\tilde X$ and a map $p: \tilde X \to X$ satisfying that for each $x\in X$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U\subseteq X$ of $x$ which is evenly covered by $p$; i.e. $p\inv(U) = \bigsqcup_{α\in I} V_α$ is a disjoint union of open sets $\set{V_α}_{α\in I}$ in $Y$ such that each $p|_{V_{α}}: V_α \to U$ is a homeomorphism.

We call $p$ a covering map.

  • A homeomorphism is a covering map.

Definition (lifting) open_in_new

Let $p: \tilde X\to X$ be a covering space and $f: Y\to X$ be a map.

A lift of $f$ along $p$ is a map $\tilde f: Y\to \tilde X$ such that $p\circ \tilde f = f$.

Lemma (uniqueness of lifts) open_in_new

Let $p: \tilde X \to X$ be a covering space or a Hausdorff local homeomorphism, $f: Y\to X$ be a map, and $\tilde f_0, \tilde f_1: Y\to \tilde X$ be two lifts of $f$. Then $$ \begin{align*} S = \sb{y\in Y}{\tilde f_0(y) = \tilde f_1(y)} \end{align*} $$ is both open and closed.

Proof

  • Thus if $Y$ is connected, then either $S = \emptyset$ or $S = Y$.

Theorem (lifting theorem) open_in_new

Let $p: (\tilde X, \tilde x_0)\to (X, x_0)$ be a based covering map, and $f: (Y, y_0)\to (X, x_0)$ be a based map.

Assume $Y$ is path-connected and locally path-connected. Then there is a lift $\tilde f: (Y, y_0) \to (\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$ of $f$ iff $f_\ast$$(π_1(Y, y_0)) \subseteq p_\ast(π_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0))$.

Proof: Bredon Chapter 3 Theorem 4.1.

Note if $Y$ is simply connected, then we always get a lift.

Lemma (homotopy lifting lemma, monodromy theorem) open_in_new

Let $p: \tilde X \to X$ be a covering space, $H: Y\times [0,1]\to X$ be a homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$.

For a lift $\tilde f_0$ of $f_0$, there exists a unique homotopy $\tilde H: Y \times [0,1] \to \tilde X$ such that

  • $\tilde H(y, 0) = \tilde f_0(y)$ for all $y\in Y$; and

  • $p\circ \tilde H = H$.

Alternatively, we can take $p: \tilde X\to X$ to be a local homeomorphism and $\tilde X$ to be Hausdorff, and assume any path from $H(y, 0)$ to $H(y,1)$ has a lift for all $y\in Y$.

Proof

  • This is known as the monodromy theorem in Riemann surfaces.

  • Taking $Y = \set{*}$, we see that given a path $γ: [0,1] \to X$ and a starting point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(γ(0))$, there exists a unique lift $\tilde γ: [0,1] \to \tilde X$ of $γ$ starting at $\tilde x_0$.

  • If $γ\simeq γ'$ as paths from $x_0$ and $x_1$, and $\tilde γ,\tilde γ'$ are their lifts starting at a common point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(x_0)$, then $\tilde γ\simeq \tilde γ'$ as paths. (Proof)

  • If $x_0\in X$ and $\tilde x_0\in p\inv(x_0)$, then $p_\ast$${}: π_{1}(\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to π_1(X, x_0)$ is an injection. (Proof)

Corollary (path induces bijection between point preimages under covering map) open_in_new

Let $p: \tilde X \to X$ be a covering space.

If there exists a path $γ: x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_1$ on $X$, then it induces a bijection $$ \begin{align*} γ_\ast : p\inv(x_0) &\to p\inv(x_1) \\ y_0 &\mapsto \tilde γ_{y_0}(1) \end{align*} $$ where $\tilde γ_{y_0}$ is the unique lift of $γ$ starting at $y_0$.

Proof

  • If $γ \simeq γ'$ as paths, then $\tilde γ_{y_0} = \widetilde{(γ')}_{y_0}$ as paths so $γ_\ast = (γ')_\ast$.

Definition (n-sheeted cover) open_in_new

Say a covering map $p: \tilde X \to X$ is an $n$-sheeted cover (for $n\in \N \cup \set{\infty}$) if each $p\inv(x)$ has cardinality $n$.

  • If $X$ is path-connected, then all $p\inv(x)$ has the same cardinality.

Galois correspondence open_in_new

Informally, for a path-connected, locally path-connected and semi-locally simply connected space $X$ and $x_0\in X$, there are bijections $$ \biggl\{\begin{gather}\text{path-connected based covering map}\\ p: (\tilde X, \tilde x_0)\to (X, x_0)\end{gather}\biggr\} \,\Big/ \,\text{homeomorphism} \longleftrightarrow \set{\text{subgroups of $π_1(X, x_0)$}} $$ and $$ \biggl\{\begin{gather}\text{path-connected covering map}\\ p: \tilde X\to X\end{gather}\biggr\} \,\Big/ \,\text{homeomorphism} \longleftrightarrow \set{\text{conjugacy classes of subgroups of $π_1(X, x_0)$}}. $$

Definition (locally path-connected) open_in_new

A topological space $X$ is called locally path-connected if for every point $x\in X$ and neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, there exists a path-connected neighbourhood $V\subseteq U$ of $x$.

Definition (semi-locally simply connected) open_in_new

A topological space $X$ is called semi-locally simply connected if for all points $x_0\in X$, there exists a neighbourhood $U\subseteq X$ of $x_0$ such that any loop in $U$ based at $x_0$ is contractible (i.e. homotopic to $c_{x_0}$ as paths) in $X$. In other words, the homeomorphism $i_\ast$${}: π_1(U, x_0)\to π_1(X, x_0)$ induced by inclusion $i: U \to X$ is trivial.

Theorem (construction of universal cover) open_in_new

Suppose a space $X$ is path-connected and locally path-connected. Then $X$ is semi-locally simply connected iff there exists a universal cover $p: \tilde X \to X$.

One such universal cover is given by, fixing some $x_0\in X$, $$ \begin{align*} \tilde X ={}& \set{\text{homotopy classes of paths in $X$ starting at $x_0$}}, \\ p([γ]) ={}& γ(1). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Note $π_1(X,x_0)$ acts on $\tilde X$ by homeomorphism $γ\bullet [α] = [γ \bullet α]$, and $p$ induces a homeomorphism $π_1(X,x_0) \backslash \tilde X\to X$. This action is free and properly discontinuous (Proof).
