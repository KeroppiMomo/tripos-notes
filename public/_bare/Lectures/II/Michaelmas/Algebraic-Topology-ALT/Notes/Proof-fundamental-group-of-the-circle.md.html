Universal cover for $S^1$

We will show $p: \R \to S^1$ given by $p(t) = e^{2πit}$ is a covering map.

For the set $U_{y>0} = \sb{x+iy\in S^1}{y>0}$, its preimage $$ \begin{align*} p\inv(U_{y>0}) = \bigsqcup_{j\in \Z} (j, j+\tfrac{1}{2}) \end{align*} $$ is the disjoint union of open intervals. On each interval, $$ \begin{align*} p|_{(j, j+\frac{1}{2})}: (j, j+\tfrac{1}{2}) \to U_{y>0} \end{align*} $$ is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse $$ \begin{align*} p|_{(j, j+\frac{1}{2})}(x+iy) = j + \frac{1}{2π}\cos\inv(x). \end{align*} $$

Similarly with $U_{y<0}, U_{x>0}$ and $U_{x<0}$. These four open sets cover $S^1$, so $p$ is a covering map.

Also $\R$ is contractible so simply connected. Thus $p$ is a universal cover.

Group isomorphism

Choose the basepoint $\tilde x_0 = 0 \in \Z = p\inv(1)$. Acting on this basepoint gives a bijection $$ \begin{align*} l: π_1(S^1, 1) \to \Z. \end{align*} $$

For any $n\in \Z$, define a path $\tilde u_n: [0,1] \to \R$ given by $t \mapsto nt$ from $0$ to $n$, and let $u_n = p\circ \tilde u_n.$ Note that since $\R$ is simply connected, there is a unique homotopy class of paths between each pair of points. So for any $[γ]\in π_1(S^1, 1)$, if the lift $\tilde γ$ of $γ$ starting at 0 ends at $n\in \Z = p\inv(1)$, then $[\tilde γ] = [\tilde u_n]$ and thus $[γ] = [u_n]$.

Now we verify $l$ is a homomorphism: for any two elements $[u_n], [u_m] \in π_1(S^1, 1)$, since $\tilde u_n \cdot \tilde u_m$ and $\tilde u_{n+m}$ are both paths in $\R$ from 0 to $n+m$, they are homotopic as paths, so $[u_n] \cdot [u_m] = [u_{n+m}]$. Hence $$ \begin{align*} l([u_n] \cdot [u_m]) = l([u_{n+m}]) = n+m = l([u_n]) + l([u_m]). \end{align*} $$
