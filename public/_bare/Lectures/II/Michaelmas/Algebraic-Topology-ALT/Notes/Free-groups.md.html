Definition (free group) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a set (the alphabet) and suppose $S\inv = \sb{s\inv}{s\in S}$ is disjoint from $S$.

A word in $S$ is a (possibly empty) finite sequence $(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ of elements in $S$.

An elementary reduction of a word removes a subword $(s, s\inv)$ or $(s\inv, s)$. A word is reduced if it does not admit any elementary reductions.

The free group $F(S)$ on the alphabet $S$ is the set of reduced words in $S$, with the operation being concatenation and then applying elementary reduction until the word is reduced.

Define a function $ι: S \to F(S)$ sending $s \mapsto (s)$.

Proof

  • $F(S)$ is the unique group (up to isomorphism) satisfying the condition of this lemma.

Definition (presentation) open_in_new

Let $S$ be an alphabet and $R \subseteq{}$$F(S)$ be a subset.

The group with generators $S$ and relations $R$ is defined as the quotient $$ \begin{align*} \langle S \mid R\rangle = F(S)\; /\; \langle \langle R\rangle\rangle \end{align*} $$ where $\langle \langle R\rangle\rangle$ is the largest normal subgroup containing $R$: $$ \begin{align*} \langle \langle R\rangle\rangle = \sb{\prod_{i=1}^{n} g_i\, r_i^{ε_i}\, g_i\inv}{g_i\in F(S), \; r_i\in R, \; ε_i \in \set{\pm 1}}. \end{align*} $$ The data $(S,R)$ is called a presentation of the group.

If $S$ and $R$ are finite, we call it a finite presentation.

Lemma (universal property of group presentations) open_in_new

For any group $H$ and group presentation $\langle S \mid R\rangle$, $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{group homomorphisms } \langle S\mid R\rangle \to H} &\to \sb{\text{functions }ϕ: S\to H}{\varphi(r)=1 \;\forall r\in R} \\ ψ &\mapsto q\circ ι \circ ψ \end{align*} $$ is a bijection, where $q: F(S) \to \langle S\mid R\rangle$ is the quotient map and $\varphi: F(S)\to H$ is given by the universal property of free groups.

That is, for any (set) function $ϕ: S\to H$ such that $ϕ(s_1)^{ε_1} \ldots ϕ(s_n)^{ε_n} = 1$ for all $(s_1^{ε_1}, \ldots, s_n^{ε_n})\in R$, there exists a unique group homomorphism $ψ: \langle S\mid R\rangle\to H$ such that this diagram commutes:

Proof

  • Any group $G$ has a canonical presentation: the identity $\id_G: G\to G$ induces a surjective homomorphism $\varphi : F(G) \to G$. This further induces $ψ: \langle G\mid \ker \varphi\rangle \to G$, which is an isomorphism by the first isomorphism theorem.

Lemma (free product with amalgamation) open_in_new

Define the free product as $$ \begin{align*} G_1 * G_2 = \langle S_1 \cup S_2 \mid R_1 \cup R_2\rangle. \end{align*} $$

The inclusions $S_i\to S_1 \cup S_2 \to F(S_1 \cup S_2)\to G_1 * G_2$ induce canonical homomorphisms $G_1 \xrightarrow{j_1} G_1 * G_2 \xleftarrow{j_2} G_2$.
