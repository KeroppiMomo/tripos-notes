The inclusion map $i: \set{v_0}\to K$ is a simplicial map and so induces an inclusion chain map $i_\bullet: C_\bullet(\set{v_0})\to C_\bullet(K)$. The only map $r: K\to \set{v_0}$ is also simplicial and induces a chain map $r_\bullet: C_\bullet(K) \to C_\bullet(\set{v_0})$. We will show that $r_\bullet$ is a chain homotopy inverse to $i_\bullet$.

One direction is easy: $r_\bullet\circ i_\bullet = \id_{C_\bullet(\set{v_0})}$.

We claim that $$ \begin{align*} h_n: C_n(K) &\to C_{n+1}(K) \\ [[a_0, \ldots, a_n]] &\mapsto [v_0, a_0, \ldots, a_n] \end{align*} $$ is a chain homotopy from $i_\bullet \circ r_\bullet$ to $\id_{C_\bullet(K)}$.

It is well-defined: since $K$ is a cone, $\{v_0, a_0, \ldots, a_n\}$ spans a simplex of $K$. Moreover $[a_0, \ldots, a_n] \mapsto [v_0, a_0, \ldots, a_n]$ sends $T_n(K)$ into $T_{n+1}(K)$.

Now we check $$ \begin{align*} h_{n-1}\circ d_n + d_{n+1} \circ h_n = \id_{C_\bullet(K)} - i_\bullet\circ r_\bullet. \end{align*} $$ For $n>0$, noting $r_n$ is trivial, we have $$ \begin{align*} (h_{n-1}\circ d_n + d_{n+1} \circ h_n)([[a_0, \ldots, a_n]]) ={}& \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^i [[v_0, a_0, \ldots, a_{i-1}, a_{i+1}, \ldots, a_n]] \\ &+ [[a_0, \ldots, a_n]] \\ &+ \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^{i+1} [[v_0, a_0, \ldots, a_{i-1}, a_{i+1}, \ldots, a_n]] \\ ={}& [[a_0, \ldots, a_n]] \\ ={}& (\id - i_n \circ r_n)([[a_0, \ldots, a_n]]), \end{align*} $$ For $n=0$, $$ \begin{align*} (h_{-1}\circ d_0 + d_{1} \circ h_0)([[a_0]]) ={}& d_1([[v_0, a_0]]) \\ ={}& [[a_0]] - [[v_0]] \\ ={}& (\id - i_n \circ r_n)([[a_0]]). \end{align*} $$
