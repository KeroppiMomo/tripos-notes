Define a relation on $\tilde X$ by $$ \begin{align*} [γ] \sim [δ] \iff γ(1) = δ(1) \text{ and } [γ\cdot δ\inv] \in H. \end{align*} $$ Check that it is in fact an equivalence relation because $H$ is a subgroup. Note also that $p$ agrees on $\sim$-equivalence classes. Therefore we can define the quotient space $\tilde X_H = \tilde X / {\sim}$, the quotient map $q: \tilde X \to \tilde X_H$ and the induced map $p_H: \tilde X_H \to X$.

Covering map

Notice if $[γ]\in U_{[α]}$ and $[γ']\in U_{[β]}$ have $[γ] \sim[γ']$, then any element $[γ\cdot η] \in U_{[α]}$ is identified under $\sim$ with $[γ'\cdot η]\in U_{[β]}$ and vice versa, so $q(U_{[α]}) = q(U_{[β]})$. This means the images of $U_{[α]}$ and $U_{[β]}$ under $q$ are either disjoint or equal.

If $q([γ]) \in q(U_{[α]})$, then $q(U_{[γ]})$ and $q(U_{[α]})$ are not disjoint and thus equal. Hence $[γ] \in U_{[γ]} \subseteq q\inv(q(U_{[α]}))$, which shows that $q(U_{[α]})$ is open in $\tilde X_H$.

We have shown that, for $U\in \mathcal{U}$, $q\inv(p_H\inv(U)) = p\inv(U)$ is a disjoint union of sets of the form $U_{[α]}$, so $p_H\inv(U) = q(p\inv(U))$ is a union of sets of the form $q(U_{[α]})$. They are open and disjoint as we argued, so $p_H$ is a covering map.

$(p_H)_\ast(π_1(\tilde X_H, q([c_{x_0}]))) = H$

For any $[γ]\in H$, the lift $\tilde γ$ of $γ$ to $\tilde X$ starting at $[c_{x_0}]$ ends at $[γ]$. Then $q\circ \tilde γ$ is a lift of $γ$ to $\tilde X_H$ starting at $q([c_{x_0}])$ and ending at $q([γ])$. But $[γ] \sim [c_{x_0}]$, so $q\circ \tilde γ\in π_1(\tilde X_H, q([c_{x_0}]))$.

Conversely for any $[\tilde γ_{H}]\in π_1(\tilde X_H, q([c_{x_0}]))$, letting $[γ] = p_H([\tilde γ_H])$, the lift $\tilde γ$ of $γ$ to $\tilde X$ starting at $[c_{x_0}]$ ends at $[γ]$. But $p_H \circ \tilde γ_H \simeq γ = p_H \circ q \circ \tilde γ$, so $q \circ \tilde γ_H \simeq \tilde γ$ is a loop in $\tilde X_H$. Therefore $[γ] \sim [c_{x_0}]$.
