Let $p: \tilde X \to X$ be a covering space, $H: Y\times [0,1]\to X$ be a homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$.

For a lift $\tilde f_0$ of $f_0$, there exists a unique homotopy $\tilde H: Y \times [0,1] \to \tilde X$ such that

  • $\tilde H(y, 0) = \tilde f_0(y)$ for all $y\in Y$; and

  • $p\circ \tilde H = H$.

Alternatively, we can take $p: \tilde X\to X$ to be a local homeomorphism and $\tilde X$ to be Hausdorff, and assume any path from $H(y, 0)$ to $H(y,1)$ has a lift for all $y\in Y$.

Proof

  • This is known as the monodromy theorem in Riemann surfaces.

  • Taking $Y = \set{*}$, we see that given a path $γ: [0,1] \to X$ and a starting point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(γ(0))$, there exists a unique lift $\tilde γ: [0,1] \to \tilde X$ of $γ$ starting at $\tilde x_0$.

  • If $γ\simeq γ'$ as paths from $x_0$ and $x_1$, and $\tilde γ,\tilde γ'$ are their lifts starting at a common point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(x_0)$, then $\tilde γ\simeq \tilde γ'$ as paths. (Proof)

  • If $x_0\in X$ and $\tilde x_0\in p\inv(x_0)$, then $p_\ast$${}: π_{1}(\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to π_1(X, x_0)$ is an injection. (Proof)
