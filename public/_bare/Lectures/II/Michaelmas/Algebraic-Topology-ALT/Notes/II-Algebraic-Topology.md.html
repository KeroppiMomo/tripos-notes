Homotopy open_in_new

Definition (homotopy) open_in_new

Let $f, g: X \to Y$ be maps.

A homotopy from $f$ to $g$ is a map $H: X \times [0,1] \to Y$ such that $$ \begin{align*} H(x, 0) ={}& f(x), \\ H(x,1) ={}& g(x). \end{align*} $$

Say $f$ is homotopic to $g$ if such a map exists, written as $f\simeq g$ or $f \simeq_H g$ if we wish to specify the homotopy $H$.

For a subset $A\subseteq X$, say a homotopy $H$ is relative to $A$ if $$ \begin{align*} f(a) = H(a,t) = g(a) \qquad \text{for all $a\in A, t\in [0,1]$}. \end{align*} $$ We write $f\simeq g \rel A$.

"Homotopic relative to $A$" is an equivalence relation on the set of maps $X\to Y$. (Proof)

Homotopic maps are preserved by composition: if $f_0, f_1: X\to Y$ are homotopic and $g_0, g_1: Y\to Z$ are homotopic, then so are $g_0\circ f_0, g_1\circ f_1: X\to Z$. (Proof)

Definition (homotopy equivalence) open_in_new

A map $f: X\to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence if there exists a homotopy inverse, i.e. a map $g: Y\to X$ such that $g\circ f$ $\simeq$ $\id_X$ and $f\circ g\simeq \id_Y$.

Two topological spaces $X, Y$ are said to be homotopy equivalent (written as $X \simeq Y$) if a homotopy equivalence $f: X\to Y$ exists.

The relation of homotopy equivalence is reflexive, symmetric and transitive. (Proof)

Definition (contractible space) open_in_new

A space $X$ is contractible if it is homotopy equivalent to the one-point space: $X \simeq \set{*}$.

Expanding the definition: $X$ is contractible iff a homotopy $X \times [0,1]\to X$ from $\id_X$ to constant.

Definition (retraction, deformation retraction) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a space and $A\subseteq X$ be a subspace.

A retraction of $X$ to $A$ is a map $r: X\to A$ such that $r|_A = \id_A$.

A deformation retraction of $X$ to $A$ is a map $r: X\to A$ that is a retraction and satisfies $i\circ r$ $\simeq$ $\id_X$, where $i: A\hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion map.

A strong deformation retraction of $X$ to $A$ is a deformation retraction $r: X\to A$ with $i\circ r\simeq\id_X \rel A$.

A deformation retraction is a homotopy equivalence with homotopy inverse $i$.

Definition (path, loop, concatenation, inverse, constant path) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space and $x_0, x_1\in X$ be points.

A path from $x0$ to $x1$ is a map $γ: [0,1]\to X$ such such that $γ(0) = x_0$ and $γ(1) = x_1$. We write $γ: x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_1$.

If $x_0 = x_1$, then we call $γ$ a loop based at $x0$_.$ For paths $γ: x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_1$ and $γ': x_1 \rightsquigarrow x_2$, define the concatenation of $γ$ and $γ'$ as the path $(γ\cdot γ'): x_0 \rightsquigarrow x_2$ defined by $$ \begin{align*} (γ\cdot γ')(t) = \begin{cases} γ(2t), &0\leq t\leq \frac12, \\ γ'(2t-1), &\frac12\leq t\leq 1. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Define the inverse of $γ$ as the path $γ\inv: x_1\rightsquigarrow x_0$ defined by $γ\inv(t) = γ(1-t)$.

For any $x_0\in X$, define the constant path at $x_0$ as $c_{x_0}(t) = x_0$.

The concatenation is continuous by the gluing lemma.

The relation $\sim$ on $X$ given by $x\sim y \iff$ (there exists a path from $x$ to $y$) is an equivalence relation by the above constructions.

Definition (covering space) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space.

A covering space of $X$ is a pair $(\tilde X, p)$ of a space $\tilde X$ and a map $p: \tilde X \to X$ satisfying that for each $x\in X$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U\subseteq X$ of $x$ which is evenly covered by $p$; i.e. $p\inv(U) = \bigsqcup_{α\in I} V_α$ is a disjoint union of open sets $\set{V_α}_{α\in I}$ in $Y$ such that each $p|_{V_{α}}: V_α \to U$ is a homeomorphism.

We call $p$ a covering map.

A homeomorphism is a covering map.

Lemma (homotopy lifting lemma, monodromy theorem) open_in_new

Let $p: \tilde X \to X$ be a covering space, $H: Y\times [0,1]\to X$ be a homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$.

  • $\tilde H(y, 0) = \tilde f_0(y)$ for all $y\in Y$; and

  • $p\circ \tilde H = H$.

Taking $Y = \set{*}$, we see that given a path $γ: [0,1] \to X$ and a starting point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(γ(0))$, there exists a unique lift $\tilde γ: [0,1] \to \tilde X$ of $γ$ starting at $\tilde x_0$.

Definition (free group) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a set (the alphabet) and suppose $S\inv = \sb{s\inv}{s\in S}$ is disjoint from $S$.

A word in $S$ is a (possibly empty) finite sequence $(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ of elements in $S\cup S\inv$.

An elementary reduction of a word removes a subword $(s, s\inv)$ or $(s\inv, s)$. A word is reduced if it does not admit any elementary reductions.

The free group $F(S)$ on the alphabet $S$ is the set of reduced words in $S$, with the operation being concatenation and then applying elementary reduction until the word is reduced.

Define a function $ι: S \to F(S)$ sending $s \mapsto (s)$.

Definition (presentation) open_in_new

Inclusion maps induce homomorphisms between fundamental groups, and the universal property of amalgamated free product gives a group homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} Φ: π_1(A, x_0) *_{π_1(A\cap B, x_0)} π_1(B, x_0) \to π_1(X, x_0). \end{align*} $$

If

  1. $A,B$ are open subsets;

  2. $A\cup B = X$; and

  3. $A\cap B$ is path-connected,

then $Φ$ is an isomorphism.

Proof

Definition (cell complex) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space and $f: S^{n-1}\to X$ be a map.

The space obtained by attaching an $n$-cell to $X$ along $f$ is the quotient space $$ \begin{align*} X \cup_f D^n := (X\sqcup D^n)/{\sim} \end{align*} $$ where $\sim$ is the equivalence relation identifying $x \sim f(x)$ for all $x\in S^{n-1} = \partial D^n$, and an subset of $X \sqcup D^n$ is open iff its intersections with $X$ and with $D^n$ are open.

A finite cell complex is a space obtained with finitely many steps as follows:

  • Step 0: start with a finite set $X^0$ of discrete points.

  • Step $i\geq 1$: attach finitely many $i$-cells to $X^{i-1}$ to form $X^i$.

Taking $X = X^n$, we say $n$ is the dimension of $X$. $X^i$ is called the $i$-skeleton of $X$.

Definition (affinely independent) open_in_new

A finite set of points $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n\in \R^m$ is said to be affinely independent if for all $t_0, \ldots, t_n \in \R$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i a_i = 0 \quad \text{and}\quad \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i = 0 \quad \iff \quad t_0 = \ldots = t_n = 0. \end{align*} $$

$a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n$ are affinely independent iff $a_1 - a_0, a_2 - a_0, \ldots, a_n - a_0$ are linearly independent. (Proof)

Definition (simplex) open_in_new

Suppose $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n\in \R^m$ are affinely independent.

They are said to span an $n$-simplex $$ \begin{align*} σ = \langle a_0, \ldots, a_n\rangle = \sb{\sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i a_i}{t_i\geq 0, \; \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i = 1}. \end{align*} $$ The $a_i$'s are the vertices of $σ$.

Any points $x\in σ$ can be written uniquely (by affine independence) as $x = \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_ia_i$. $(t_0, \ldots, t_n)$ are the barycentric coordinates of $x$.

A face of $σ$ is a simplex $τ$ spanned by a subset of $\set{a_0, \ldots, a_n}$. We write $τ\leq σ$, and $τ<σ$ if $τ$ is a proper face (i.e. $τ\neq σ$).

The boundary $\partial σ$ of $σ$ is the union of all of its proper faces. The interior $\mathring{σ}$ of $σ$ is $σ\setminus \partial σ$.

These notions of boundary and interior differ from the topological ones.

Definition (simplicial complex) open_in_new

A (geometric) simplicial complex in $\R^m$ is a finite set $K$ of simplices in $\R^m$ satisfying that

  1. if $σ\in K$ and $τ\leq σ$, then $τ\in K$; and

  2. if $σ, τ\in K$, then either $σ\cap τ$ is empty or is a face of both $σ$ and $τ$.

The vertices $V_K$ of $K$ is the set of all 0-simplices of $K$.

The dimension of $K$ is the largest $n$ for which $K$ contains an $n$-simplex.

The polyhedron $|K|\subseteq \R^m$ associated to $K$ is the union of all simplices in $K$.

The $d$-skeleton $K_{(d)}$ of $K$ is the sub-simplicial complex given by all simplices of $K$ of dimension at most $d$.

  • The standard $n$-simplex $Δ^{n} \subseteq \R^{n+1}$ is the simplex spanned by the standard basis vectors $e_1, \ldots, e_{n+1}$ of $\R^{n+1}$. The set of all its faces defines a simplicial complex.

  • The simplicial $(n-1)$-sphere is the simplicial complex given by the proper faces of $Δ^n$.

Definition (simplicial map) open_in_new

Let $K$ and $L$ be simplicial complexes.

A simplicial map is a function $f: V_K \to V_L$ such that if $σ = \langle a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_p\rangle\in K$, then the set $\set{f(a_0), \ldots f(a_p)}$ (which may contain repeated vertices) spans a simplex of $K$, denoted as $f(σ)$.

Definition (star, link) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a simplicial complex and $x\in K$ be a point.

Lemma (simplicial approximation) open_in_new

Let $K$ and $L$ be simplicial complexes, and $f: |K|\to |L|$ be a continuous map.

Let $g: V_K\to V_L$ be a simplicial approximation to $f$, meaning for each $v\in V_K$, $$ \begin{align*} f(\op{St}_K(v)) \subseteq \op{St}_L(g(v)). \end{align*} $$

Then $g$ defines a simplicial map, and $f$ is homotopic to $|g|$ via $$ \begin{align*} H: |K| \times [0,1] &\to |L| \\ (x,t) &\mapsto (1-t) \cdot f(x) + t\cdot |g|(t). \end{align*} $$ In particular $H$ is relative to the subspace $\sb{x\in |K|}{f(x) = |g|(x)}$ where $f$ and $|g|$ agree.

Proof

Definition (barycentric subdivision) open_in_new

The (first) barycentric subdivision of a simplicial complex $K$ is the simplicial complex $$ \begin{align*} K' = \sb{\langle\hat σ_0, \hat σ_1, \ldots, \hat σ_p\rangle}{σ_i\in K\text{ and } σ_0 < σ_1 < \ldots < σ_p}. \end{align*} $$

$K'$ is indeed a simplicial simplex and $|K'| = |K|$. (Proof)

Definition (exact sequence) open_in_new

We say a pair of homomorphisms of abelian groups $$ \begin{align*} A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \end{align*} $$ is exact at $B$ if $\im f = \ker g$.

Say a collection of homomorphisms $$ \begin{align*} \ldots \to A_i \to A_{i+1} \to A_{i+2} \to \ldots \end{align*} $$ is exact if it is exact at $A_i$ whenever there is a map entering and a map exiting $A_i$.

A short exact sequence of abelian groups is an exact sequence $$ \begin{align*} 0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0, \end{align*} $$ i.e. $f$ is injective, $g$ is surjective, and $\im f = \ker g$.

Chain maps $A_\bullet \xrightarrow{i_\bullet} B_\bullet \xrightarrow{j_\bullet} C_\bullet$ form a short exact sequence of chain complexes if for each $n$, $0\to A_n \xrightarrow{i_n} B_n \xrightarrow{j_n} C_n\to 0$ is a short exact sequence of abelian groups.
