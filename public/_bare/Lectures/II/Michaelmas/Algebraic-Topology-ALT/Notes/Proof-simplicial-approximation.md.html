Simplicial map

Let $σ = \langle a_0, \ldots, a_p\rangle\in K$. Taking any $x\in \mathring σ$, we have $$ \begin{align*} &&x&\in \bigcap_{i=0}^{p} \op{St}_K(a_i) \\ \implies &&f(x)&\in \bigcap_{i=0}^{p} f(\op{St}_K(a_i)) \subseteq \bigcap_{i=0}^{p} \op{St}_K(g(a_i)). \end{align*} $$

This means $f(x)$ is in the interior of some simplex which has $g(a_i)$ as a vertex for each $i=0,\ldots,p$. Hence the unique simplex $τ\in L$ whose interior contains $f(x)$ has vertices $g(a_i)$. Thus $\set{g(a_0), \ldots, g(a_p)}$ spans a face of $τ$, so spans a simplex of $L$.

Homotopy

We now check that $(1-t)\cdot f(x) + t\cdot |g|(x)\in |L|$ for all $(x,t)\in |K|\times [0,1]$. Let $σ = \langle a_0, \ldots, a_p\rangle\in K$ and $τ\in L$ such that $x\in \mathring σ$ and $f(x)\in \mathring τ$. Letting $x = \sum_{i=0}^{p} t_i a_i$, the definition of $|g|$ gives $$ \begin{align*} |g|(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{p} t_i \, g(a_i) \end{align*} $$ From the above argument, $g(a_i)$ are vertices of $τ$, so $|g|(x)\in τ$. Thus $(1-t)\cdot f(x) + t\cdot |g|(x)\in τ$ by convexity.

The defined map $H$ is a linear interpolation and so continuous.
