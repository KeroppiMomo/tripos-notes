Universal cover $\implies$ semi-locally simply connected

Suppose $p: \tilde X\to X$ is a universal cover and $x_0\in X$ is a point. Let $U\subseteq X$ be an open neighbourhood of $x_0$ which is evenly covered. Pick some $\tilde x_0\in p\inv(x_0)$, and take some open neighbourhood $V\subseteq \tilde X$ of $\tilde x_0$ such that $p|_V : V \to U$ is a homeomorphism.

For any loop $γ$ in $U$ based at $x_0$, there is a lift $\tilde γ = p|_V\inv\circ γ$ in $V$, which is a loop based at $\tilde x_0$. Thus the homeomorphism $p_\ast: π_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0)\to π_1(X, x_0)$ sends $[\tilde γ]$ to $[γ]$. However $\tilde X$ is simply connected, so $[\tilde γ] = [c_{\tilde x_0}]$ and hence $[γ] = [c_{x_0}]$.

Path-connected, locally path-connected, semi-locally simply connected $\implies$ universal cover

(Non-examinable)

Motivation: > Suppose there is a universal cover $p: \tilde X\to X$. Pick basepoints $x_0\in X$ and $\tilde x_0\in p\inv(x_0)\subseteq \tilde X$. We know that since $\tilde X$ is simply connected, for each point $\tilde x\in \tilde X$, there is a unique homotopy class of paths from $x_0$ to $x$. This gives a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \tilde X \longleftrightarrow \set{\text{paths in $\tilde X$ starting from $\tilde x_0$}} \,\big/\, {\simeq}. \end{align*} $$ > Moreover path lifting leads to a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{paths in $\tilde X$ starting from $\tilde x_0$}} \longleftrightarrow \set{\text{paths in $X$ starting from $x_0$}}, \end{align*} $$ and homotopy lifting says paths in $X$ are homotopic iff their lifts in $\tilde X$ are homotopic. > Thus there is a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \tilde X \longleftrightarrow \set{\text{paths in $X$ starting from $x_0$}} \,\big/\, {\simeq}. \end{align*} $$ We will define $\tilde X$ this way.

Define $\tilde X$ and $p$ as mentioned.

Base for the topology on $X$

We claim that $$ \mathcal{U} = \left\{U\subseteq X\ \middle|\ \begin{align*} &\text{$U$ is open in $X$, is path-connected,} \\ &\text{and $π_1(U, x) \to π_1(X, x)$ is trivial for all $x\in U$} \end{align*}\right\} $$ is a base for the topology of $X$. (Here $i_\ast$${}: π_1(U,x)\to π_1(X,x)$ refers to the homeomorphism induced by inclusion $i: U\to X$.)

It suffices to show that for all open neighbourhood $V\subseteq X$ of a point $x\in X$, there exists a neighbourhood $U\in \mathcal{U}$ of $x$ that is contained within $V$.

Since $X$ is semi-locally simply connected, there exists a neighbourhood $U'\subseteq V$ of $x$ where $π_1(U', x) \to π_1(X,x)$ is trivial. Then by local path-connectedness, find a path-connected neighbourhood $U\subseteq U'$ of $x$. Note $π_1(U, x)\to π_1(X,x)$ has a factorisation $π_1(U, x) \to π_1(U', x) \xrightarrow{\text{trivial}} π_1(X,x)$ so is trivial. In addition for any $y\in U$, choose a path $u: y \rightsquigarrow x$ in $U$. The commutative square

shows that $π_1(U, y) \to π_1(X, y)$ is also trivial. Hence $U\in \mathcal{U}$.

Basis for the topology on $\tilde X$

We will show that there is a topology on $\tilde X$ which admits the base $$ \begin{align*} \tilde{\mathcal{U}} = \sb{U_{[α]}}{U\in \mathcal{U}, \; [α]\in \tilde X, \; α(1)\in U} \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} U_{[α]} = \sb{[α\cdot α']}{\text{path $α'$ in $U$ starting at $α(1)$}}. \end{align*} $$

The covering property is clear as $[α] \in U_{[α]}$, so it suffices to check that if $[γ]\in U_{[α]}\cap V_{[β]}$, then there exists a basic neighbourhood $W\in \mathcal{U}$ of $γ(1)$ such that $W_{[γ]}\subseteq U_{[α]} \cap V_{[β]}$.

We can write $[α\cdot α'] = [γ] = [β\cdot β']$ for paths $α'$ in $U$ and $β'$ in $V$. Take $W \in \mathcal{U}$ so that $γ(1) \in W\subseteq U\cap V$, which is possible as $\mathcal{U}$ is a base. For any path $γ'$ in $U\cap V$, we see $$ \begin{align*} [α \cdot \underbrace{(α'\cdot γ')}_{\text{path in $U$}}] = [γ\cdot γ'] = [β \cdot \underbrace{(β'\cdot γ')}_{\text{path in $V$}}], \end{align*} $$ so $[γ\cdot γ'] \in U_{[α]} \cap V_{[β]}$.

$p$ is continuous

It suffices to show that $p\inv(U)$ is open in $\tilde X$ for all $U\in \mathcal{U}$. For any $[γ] \in p\inv(U)$, the neighbourhood $U_{[γ]}\in \tilde{\mathcal{U}}$ of $[γ]\in \tilde X$ is contained within $p\inv(U)$.

$p|_{U_{[α]}}: U_{[α]} \to U$ is a homeomorphism

  • Surjectivity: since $U$ is path-connected, for any $x\in U$, choose a path $α': α(1) \rightsquigarrow x$ in $U$. Then $p$ sends $[α\cdot α']\in U_{[α]}$ to $x$.

  • Injectivity: for $[α\cdot α'], [α\cdot α'']\in U_{[α]}$ ending at the same point, $α'\cdot α''\inv$ is a loop in $U$ based at $α(1)$. Since $π_1(U,α(1)) \to π_1(X,α(1))$ is trivial, we have $[α'\cdot α''\inv] = [c_{α(1)}]$ and thus $[α\cdot α'] = [α\cdot α'']$.

  • Open map: our argument for surjectivity shows that $p(V_{[β]}) = V$, so $p$ maps all base elements to open sets.

$p$ is a covering map

We claim that $U\in\mathcal{U}$ is evenly covered by sets of the form $U_{[α]}\in \mathcal{U}$. It is clear that they cover $p\inv(U)$, so we want to show any two $U_{[α]}, U_{[β]}$ are either disjoint or equal.

Suppose $[γ]\in U_{[α]}\cap U_{[β]}$, so we can write $[α\cdot α'] = [γ] = [β\cdot β']$ for paths $α',β'$ in $U$. For any $[α\cdot α'']\in U_{[α]}$, observe $[α\cdot α''] = [β\cdot (β'\cdot α'\inv\cdot α'')]\in U_{[β]}$, so $U_{[α]}\subseteq U_{[β]}$. The reverse inclusion is similar.

$\tilde X$ is simply connected

The key observation is that for any path $γ: [0,1]\to X$ starting from $x_0$, its lift to $\tilde X$ starting from $[c_{x_0}]$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} \tilde γ : [0,1] &\to \tilde X \\ s &\mapsto [ t \mapsto γ(st) ] \end{align*} $$ and ends at $[γ]$. This shows that $\tilde X$ is path-connected.

Moreover any loop $\tilde γ$ in $\tilde X$ based at $[c_{x_0}]$ is the lift of a loop $γ = p\circ \tilde γ$ in $X$ based at $x_0$. However from the above, $\tilde γ$ must end at $[γ]$, so we have $[γ] = [c_{x_0}]$. Hence $p_\ast: π_1(\tilde X, [c_{x_0}]) \to π_1(X, x_0)$ is trivial. But $p_\ast$ is injective, so $π_1(\tilde X, [c_{x_0}])$ is trivial.
