Let $x\in |K| = \bigcup_{σ\in K} |σ|$. It lies in some simplex, say $σ = \langle a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n\rangle$, so $x = \sum_{i=0}^{n} t_i a_i$ for some coefficients $t_i\geq 0$. Taking $\set{b_0, \ldots, b_m} = \sb{a_i}{t_i>0}$, we remove zero terms to get $x = \sum_{j=0}^{m} s_j b_j$ where all $s_j > 0$, so $x$ lies in the interior of a simplex $\langle b_0, \ldots, b_m\rangle \leq σ$.

Suppose $x\in \mathring σ\cap \mathring σ'$. Then $σ\cap σ' \neq \emptyset$ and thus is a face of both $σ$ and $σ'$. However it contains an interior point $x$ of either simplex, so it cannot be a proper face of either. Hence $σ = σ\cap σ' = σ'$.
