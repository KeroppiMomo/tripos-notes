Let $(E,\mathcal{A},μ)$ be a measure space.

We say a measurable function $f: E\to \R$ is integrable if the integral of its absolute value is finite: $$ \begin{align*} \int |f| \d μ < \infty. \end{align*} $$

Write $\mathcal{L}^1(E,A,μ)$ for the space of integrable functions.

If this holds, then define its integral as $$ \begin{align*} \int f \d μ := \int f^+ \d μ - \int f^- \d μ \end{align*} $$ where $f^+ = \max\set{f,0}$ and $f^- = \max\set{-f, 0}$.

Since $\int f^{\pm}\leq \int |f|< \infty$, we don't get $\infty-\infty$ in $\int f^+ - \int f^-$.

This is consistent with our definition for non-negative measurable functions.

We can allow $f$ to take values in $\overline{\R}$, but integrability implies $f\inv(\set{\pm \infty})$ is negligible.
