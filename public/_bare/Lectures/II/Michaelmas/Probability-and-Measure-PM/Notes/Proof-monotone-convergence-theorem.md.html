Let $f = \lim_{n\to \infty} f_n = \sup_{n\to\infty} f_n$. $f$ is non-negative, and we have shown $f$ is measurable.

$\lim_{n\to \infty} \int f_n \leq \int f$

Since $f_n \leq f$ as $f_n$ is increasing, we have $\int f_n \leq \int f$. Taking the limit gives $\lim_{n\to \infty} \int f_n \leq \int f$.

$\lim_{n\to \infty} \int f_n \geq \int f$

Let $h\leq f$ be a simple non-negative function, and fix some $ε>0$, so $(1-ε)h < f$. The sets $$ \begin{align*} E_n := \sb{x\in E}{(1-ε)h(x) \leq f_n(x)}. \end{align*} $$

  • is increasing because $f_n$ is increasing; and

  • satisfies that $\bigcup_{n\in \N} E_n = E$, because for each $x\in X$, since $f_n(x) \nearrow f(x) > (1-ε)h(x)$, there exists some $N$ such that $(1-ε)h(x) \leq f_n(x)$ for all $n\geq N$.

Therefore we can approximate $$ \begin{align*} f_n \geq (1-ε)h \cdot \mathbb{1}_{E_n}. \end{align*} $$ Writing $h = \sum_{k=1}^{K} α_k \mathbb{1}_{A_k}$ in the canonical representation, we have $$ \begin{align*} h \,\mathbb{1}_{E_n} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} α_k \mathbb{1}_{A_k \cap E_n}, \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{n\to \infty} \int f_n \geq{} & \sup_{n\to \infty} \int (1-ε) h \, \mathbb{1}_{E_n} \\ \geq{} & \sup_{n\to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^{K} (1-ε)α_k\, μ(A_k \cap E_n) \\ ={}& (1-ε)\sum_{k=1}^{K} α_k\sup_{n\to \infty} μ(A_k \cap E_n) \\ ={}& (1-ε)\sum_{k=1}^{K} α_k \,μ\Bigl(A_k \cap \underbrace{\bigcup_{n\in \N} E_n}_{E}\Bigr) \tag{*}\\ ={}& (1-ε) \int h \end{align*} $$ where $(*)$ has applied the continuity of $μ$. This holds for all $ε$, so $$ \begin{align*} \limup_{n\to \infty} \int f_n \geq \int h \d μ \end{align*} $$ which holds for all $h$.
