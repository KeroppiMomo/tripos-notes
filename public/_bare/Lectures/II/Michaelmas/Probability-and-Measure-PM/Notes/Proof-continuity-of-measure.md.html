Increasing sequence

Take $$ \begin{align*} C_0 ={}& A_0, \\ C_n ={}& A_n \setminus A_{n-1}, \qquad n\geq 1 \end{align*} $$ which satisfies $A_n = \bigsqcup_{i=0}^{n} C_i$. So $$ \begin{align*} μ\biggl(\bigcup_{n\geq 0} A_n\biggr) ={}& μ\biggl(\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0} C_n\biggr) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} \sum_{i=0}^{n} μ(C_i) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} μ\biggl(\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{n} μ(C_i)\biggr) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} μ(A_n). \end{align*} $$

Decresaing sequence

For a decreasing sequence $(A_n)$, the complements $(B_n = A_{0} \setminus A_n)_{n\geq 0}$ is increasing. Also $μ(A_n)\leq μ(A_0)<\infty$ and $μ(\bigcap_{n\in \N} A_n) \leq μ(A_0) < \infty$, so $$ \begin{align*} μ(A_0) - μ\biggl(\bigcap_{n\geq 0} A_n\biggr) ={}& μ\biggl(A_0 \setminus \bigcap_{n\geq 0} A_n\biggr) \\ ={}& μ\biggl(\bigcup_{n\geq 0} B_n\biggr) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} μ(B_n) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} \bigl(μ(A_0) - μ(A_n)\bigr) \\ ={}& μ(A_0) - \lim_{n\to \infty} μ(A_n). \end{align*} $$
