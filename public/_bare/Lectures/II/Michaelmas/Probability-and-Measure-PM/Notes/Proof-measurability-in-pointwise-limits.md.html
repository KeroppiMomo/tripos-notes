Measurability of inf, sup, lim inf, lim sup

Let $f := \inf_{n\in \N} f_n$. Since $\mathcal{B}(\overline{\R}) = σ\sb{[-\infty, a)}{a\in \R}$, it suffices to check $f\inv([-\infty, a))\in \mathcal{A}$ for all $a\in \R$.

Note if $f(x) < a$, then there exists some $n\in \N$ such that $f_n(x) < a$. Thus $$ \begin{align*} f\inv([-\infty, a)) = \bigcup_{n\in \N} \underbrace{f_n\inv([-\infty, a))}_{\in \mathcal{A}} \in \mathcal{A}, \end{align*} $$ so $f$ is measurable.

$\sup_{n\in \N} f_n = -\inf_{n\in \N} (-f_n)$ so is measurable. Then it follows that $\liminf f_n$ and $\limsup f_n$ are measurable.

Measurablility of limits

If $f_n\to f$ pointwise, then $f = \liminf f_n = \limsup f_n$, so is measurable.

Measurability of subset where limit exists

Since for each $x\in E$, $f_n(x)$ converges iff $\limsup f_n(x) = \liminf f_n(x)$ as $n\to \infty$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \sb{x\in E}{f_n(x) \text{ has a limit}} = (\limsup f_n - \liminf f_n)\inv(\set{0}), \end{align*} $$ which is measurable because $\limsup f_n - \liminf f_n$ is measurable.
