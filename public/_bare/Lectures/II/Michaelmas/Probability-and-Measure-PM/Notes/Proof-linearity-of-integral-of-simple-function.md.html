Indeed $af + bg$ is simple: suppose the canonical representations of $f$ and $g$ are $$ \begin{align*} f = \sum_{i=1}^{n} α_i \mathbb{1}_{A_i}, \qquad g = \sum_{j=1}^{m} β_j \mathbb{1}_{B_j}. \end{align*} $$ Letting $$ \begin{align*} C_{ij} = A_i \cap B_j\in \mathcal{A}, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n, \; j = 1, \ldots, m, \end{align*} $$ since $A_i = \bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m} C_{ij}$ and $B_j = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n} C_{ij}$, we obtain a partition $\set{C_{ij}}_{i=1,\ldots,n, \, j=1,\ldots,m}$ of $E$. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} f = \sum_{i,j} α_i \mathbb{1}_{C_{ij}}, \qquad g = \sum_{i,j} β_j \mathbb{1}_{C_{ij}} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} af + bg = \sum_{i,j} (aα_i + bβ_j) \mathbb{1}_{C_{ij}}. \end{align*} $$

Then $af + bg$ is simple and non-negative, so $$ \begin{align*} \int af + bg \d μ ={}&\sum_{i,j} (aα_i + bβ_j) μ(C_{ij}) \\ ={}& a \sum_{i} α_i\sum_{j} μ(C_{ij}) + b \sum_{j} β_j \sum_{i} μ(C_{ij}) \\ ={}& a \sum_{i} α_i μ(A_i) + b \sum_{j} β_j μ(B_j) \\ ={}& a \int f \d μ + b \int g \d μ. \end{align*} $$
