Let $f = f_1 \times f_2$.

$f_1, f_2$ measurable $\implies$ $f$ measurable

Since $\mathcal{B}_1 \otimes \mathcal{B}_2 = σ\sb{B_1 \times B_2}{B_1\in \mathcal{B}_1, B_2\in \mathcal{B}_2}$, it suffices to check $f\inv(B_1 \times B_2)\in \mathcal{A}$ for all $B_1\in \mathcal{B}_1, B_2\in \mathcal{B}_2$:

$$ \begin{align*} f\inv(B_1 \times B_2) ={}& f\inv((B_1 \times F_2) \cap (F_1 \times B_2)) \\ ={}& f\inv(B_1 \times F_2) \cap f\inv(F_1 \times B_2) \\ ={}& \underbrace{f_1\inv(B_1)}_{\in \mathcal{A}} \cap \underbrace{f_2\inv(B_2)}_{\in \mathcal{A}} \in \mathcal{A}. \end{align*} $$

$f$ measurable $\implies$ $f_1, f_2$ measurable

The map $$ \begin{align*} π_1: (F_1 \times F_2, \mathcal{B}_1 \otimes \mathcal{B}_2) &\to (F_1, \mathcal{B}_1) \\ (x,y) &\mapsto x \end{align*} $$ is measurable because for all $B_1\in \mathcal{B}_1$, $$ \begin{align*} π_1\inv(B_1) = B_1 \times F_2 \in \mathcal{B}_1 \otimes \mathcal{B}_2. \end{align*} $$

Then $f_1 = π_1 \circ f$ is a composition of measurable functions so measurable. Similar with $f_2 = π_2 \circ f$.
