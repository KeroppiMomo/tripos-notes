One direction is trivial: any $σ$-algebra on $E$ contains $E$ and is stable by set difference, countable union and countable intersection.

Suppose $\mathcal{M}$ is a monotone class, stable by finite intersections. It suffices to show $\mathcal{M}$ is stable by countable unions.

Let $(A_n)_{n\in \N}$ be a sequence in $\mathcal{M}$. Construct $$ \begin{align*} B_n := \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} A_i = \left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n} A_i^\complement \right)^\complement \in \mathcal{M}, \end{align*} $$ which is an increasing sequence in $\mathcal{M}$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i = \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} B_i \in \mathcal{M}. \end{align*} $$
