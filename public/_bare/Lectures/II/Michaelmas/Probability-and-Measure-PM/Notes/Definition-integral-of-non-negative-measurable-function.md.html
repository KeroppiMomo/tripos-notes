Let $(E,A,μ)$ be a measure space.

For a non-negative measurable function $f: (E,\mathcal{A}) \to [0,\infty] \subseteq{}$$\overline{\R}$, the integral of $f$ with respect to $μ$ is $$ \begin{align*} \int f \d μ = \sup_{\substack{h\in \mathcal{E}_+ \\h\leq f}} \int h \d μ \end{align*} $$ where $\mathcal{E}_+$ is the set of non-negative simple functions $(E,\mathcal{A}) \to [0,\infty)$.

This is consistent with the definition of integrals for simple functions because if $f\leq g$ are non-negative simple, then $\int f \d μ \leq \int g \d μ$.

If $f\leq g$ are non-negative measurable, then $\int f \d μ \leq \int g \d μ$ by definition.
