Let $V$ and $W$ be representations of $G$ over $k$.

Suppose $V$ and $W$ are irreducible and $ϕ: V\to W$ is $G$-linear.

  1. $ϕ$ is either zero or an isomorphism.

  2. If $W = V$ is finite-dimensional and $k$ is algebraically closed, then $ϕ = λ\,\id_V$ for some $λ\in k$.

Proof

If $k$ is algebraically closed, then $$ \begin{align*} \dim \Hom_G(V, W) = \begin{cases} 1, &\text{if $V,W$ are $G$-isomorphic}, \\ 0, &\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

If $ρ: G\to \GL(V)$ is irreducible and $z\in{}$$Z(G)$, then $ρ(z)$ is $G$-linear (because $ρ(z)ρ(g) = ρ(g)ρ(z)$) so $ρ(z) = λ\, \id_V$ for some $λ\in k$.

If $G$ is abelian, then any irreducible representation of $G$ is one-dimensional. (Proof)
