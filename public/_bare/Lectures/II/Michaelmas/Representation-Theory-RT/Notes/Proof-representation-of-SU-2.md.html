$V_n$ is irreducible

Let $W\leq V_n$ be a subrepresentation. We want to show $W = 0$ or $V_n$.

If $\sum_{k=0}^{n} r_k x^{n-k} y^k \in W$ and $r_i\neq 0$, then $x^{n-i}y^i\in W$

We prove this claim by induction on $\# \sb{k}{r_k\neq 0}$. Clear if this is 1, as $W$ is a complex vector space.

Suppose $r_i\neq 0$, and pick any $z\in \C$ with $z^n, z^{n-2}, \ldots, z^{2-n}, z^{-n}$ all distinct and $|z| = 1$. Since $W$ is a $\op{SU}(2)$-invariant subspace, $$ \begin{align*} W \ni \pmat{z \\ &z\inv} * \sum_{k=0}^{n} r_k x^{n-k} y^k = \sum_{k=0}^{n} r_k z^{n-2k} x^{n-k} y^k. \end{align*} $$ Since $W$ is a complex vector space, $$ \begin{align*} W \ni z^{n-2i} \sum_{k=0}^{n} r_k x^{n-k} y^k. \end{align*} $$ Subtracting, $$ \begin{align*} W \ni \sum_{k=0}^{n} \underbrace{r_k(z^{n-2k} - z^{n-2i})}_{\tilde r_k} x^{n-k} y^k \end{align*} $$ with $\tilde r_k = 0$ iff $r_k = 0$ or $k = i$. By induction hypothesis, $x^{n-k}y^k\in W$ for all $k\neq i$ with $r_k \neq 0$. Thus we conclude $x^{n-i} y^i\in W$ using $\sum_{k=0}^{n} r_k x^{n-k} y^k\in W$.

Applying the claim

Suppose $w\in W\setminus \set{0}$. Then $w$ contains some non-zero term $x^{n-i} y^i$, and by the claim above, we have $x^{n-i} y^i\in W$.

Letting $\pmat{a & b \\ -\bar b & \bar a}\in \op{SU}(2)$ act on $x^{n-i} y^i$ for any $a,b\neq 0$, we see $(ax - \bar b y)^{n-i} (bx - \bar ay)^i \in W$, the $x^n$ term of which has non-zero coefficient. Therefore $x^n \in W$.

Finally letting $\pmat{a & b \\ -\bar b & \bar a}\in \op{SU}(2)$ act again, we have $(ax - \bar b y)^n\in W$, where every term $x^{n-k} y^k$ has non-zero coefficient. Thus $x^{n-k} y^k\in W$ for all $k$. Hence $W = V_n$.

$V_n$ exhausts all irreducible representation

Let $V$ be an irreducible representation of $\op{SU}(2)$. By our previous results, $χ_V(z)\in \Z_{\geq 0}[z, z\inv]^{\text{even}}$ is an even Laurent polynomial of non-negative integer coefficients, and $$ \begin{align*} χ_{V_0}(z) ={}& 1, \\ χ_{V_1}(z) ={}& z + z\inv, \\ χ_{V_2}(z) ={}& z^2 + 1 + z^{-2}, \\ χ_{V_3}(z) ={}& z^3 + z + z\inv + z^{-3}, \\ \vdots \end{align*} $$ is a $\Q$-basis for $\Q[z, z\inv]^{\text{even}}$ (even Laurent polynomial of rational coefficients). Therefore $$ \begin{align*} χ_{V}(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{N} a_n \, χ_{V_n}(z) \end{align*} $$ for some $a_n\in \Q$. Clearing denominators and moving negative terms to the other side, $$ \begin{align*} m \,χ_V(z) + \sum_{n=0}^{N} b_n\, χ_{V_n}(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{N} c_n\, χ_{V_n}(z) \end{align*} $$ for some $m>1$ and $b_n, c_n\geq 0$. Both sides are characters of representations, so $$ \begin{align*} m V \oplus \bigoplus_{n=0}^N b_n V_n \simeq \bigoplus_{n=0}^N c_n V_n. \end{align*} $$

Since $V$ and $V_n$ are irreducible, we must have $V = V_n$ for some $n$.
