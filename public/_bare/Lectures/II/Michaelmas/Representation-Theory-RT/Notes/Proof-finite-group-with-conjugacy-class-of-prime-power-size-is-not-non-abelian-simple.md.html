Let $\mathcal{C}\neq \set{1}$ be conjugacy class of size $p^r$ where $p$ is prime and $r\in \Z_{\geq 0}$. Suppose on the contrary $G$ is a non-abelian simple group.

Since $Z(G)$ is a normal subgroup and $Z(G) \neq G$ (since $G$ is not abelian), $Z(G) = 1$, so $r\geq 1$ .

Let $ρ_1 = \mathbb{1}, ρ_2, \ldots, ρ_k$ be all the irreducible representations of $G$, and let $χ_1 = \mathbb{1}, χ_2, \ldots, χ_k$ be their characters. For $ρ_i \neq \mathbb{1}$, because $G$ is simple, $ρ_i$ is faithful. Since $Z(G)=1$, no $g\in G\setminus \set{1}$ acts by $λ\,\id$ and so has $|χ_i(g)| = χ_i(1)$ (sheet 2 Q1). Our previous result then says either $χ_i(g) = 0$ or $p\mid \deg χ_i$.

Fix any $g\in \mathcal{C}$. By column orthogonality, $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{k} χ_i(g) \overline{χ_i(1)} \\ ={}& 1 + p \sum_{i=2}^{k} \frac{\deg χ_i}{p} χ_i(ρ) \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} -\frac{1}{p} = \sum_{i=2}^{k} \underbrace{\frac{\deg χ_i}{p}}_{\text{either $\in \Z$}} \underbrace{χ_i(ρ)}_{\text{or }0} \in \Z \end{align*} $$ which is absurd.
