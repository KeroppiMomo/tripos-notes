Consider the minimal polynomial $m_g(t)$ of an element $g\in \SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$. If two elements of $\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ have different minimal polynomial, then they are not conjugate. There are several cases:

$m_g(t) = t-λ$

Since $1 = \det g = λ^2$, we get $λ = \pm 1$. Then $g = \pm I$.

Let's compute the order of $\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$. For $\pmat{a&b\\c&d}\in \SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$, if $a\neq 0$, then $b,c$ can be taken to be any $\mathbb{F}_q$ and $d = a\inv(1 + bc)$. If $a = 0$, then $c = b\inv\in \mathbb{F}_q^\times$ and $d$ can be any $\mathbb{F}_q$. Therefore $\# \SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q) = (q-1)q^2 + (q-1)q = (q-1)q(q+1)$.

$m_g(t) = (t - λ)(t - μ)$ for some distinct $λ,μ\in \mathbb{F}_q$

Since $\det g = 1$, we have $μ = λ\inv$. Letting $\pmat{a\\b}$ and $\pmat{c\\d}$ be the corresponding eigenvectors (which are orthogonal), we get $$ \begin{align*} g = \pmat{a & ηc \\ b & ηd}\pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv}\pmat{a & ηc \\ b & ηd}\inv \end{align*} $$ where $η = (ad - bc)\inv$. This shows $g$ is conjugate to $\pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv}$ in $\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$.

Compute its centraliser: $\pmat{a & b \\ c & d}\pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv} = \pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv} \pmat{a & b \\ c &d}$ iff $b=c=0$ since $λ\neq λ\inv$. Therefore the centraliser is $T$.

$T\isom \mathbb{F}_q^\times$, so its order is $q-1$. By orbit-stabiliser, the size of the conjugacy class is $\#\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)/(q-1) = q(q+1)$.

Note $\pmat{λ \\& λ\inv} = \pmat{&1 \\ -1} \pmat{λ\inv \\ &λ}\pmat{&1 \\ -1}\inv$. Moreover conjugate matrices have equal trace. Therefore there are in total $\frac{1}{2}(q-3)$ such conjugacy classes (the $3$ comes from $λ\neq 0, \pm 1$).

$m_g(t)$ is quadratic and irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_q$

It splits into $(t - λ)(t - λ\inv)$ for some $λ = a+b\sqrt{ε}\in \mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ where $b\neq 0$. Since $$\mathbb{F}_q \ni \tr(g) = λ + λ\inv = a + b\sqrt{ε} + \frac{a - b\sqrt{ε}}{a^2 - b^2ε},$$ we have $$ \begin{align*} b\left(1 - \frac{1}{a^2 - b^2 ε}\right) = 0. \end{align*} $$ As $b \neq 0$, we get $a^2 - b^2 ε = 1$. (Or $λ, λ\inv$ are roots of a polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_q$, so $λ\inv$ is the Galois conjugate of $λ$, namely $a - b\sqrt{ε}$.)

$g$ is conjugate to $\pmat{a & bε \\ b & a}$ in $\GL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$: since they both have the minimal polynomial $t^2 - 2\tr(g) t + 1 = t^2 - 2at+ 1$, they have the same rational canonical form (because degree of minimal polynomial equals 2), so are conjugate in $\op{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$.

We showed $\sb{\pmat{c&dε \\ d &c}}{c,d\in \mathbb{F}_q, (c,d)\neq (0,0)}\leq \GL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ is cyclic and $\det$ restricted to it is surjective. Therefore letting $g = h\pmat{a & bε \\ b & a}h\inv$ for $h\in \GL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$, finding $k = \pmat{c & dε\\d& c}$ with determinant $(\det h)\inv$, we get $g = kh\pmat{a & bε \\ b & a} h\inv k\inv$, so $g$ is conjugate to $\pmat{a & bε \\ b &a}$ in $\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$.

Routine computation of the centraliser shows it is $T_{ns}$. Moreover by the above argument but using $λ\inv$ instead, $\pmat{a & bε \\ b & a}$ is conjugate to $\pmat{a & -bε \\ -b & a}$ in $\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$. Conjugate matrices have equal trace. Therefore there are in total $\frac{1}{2}(q+1-2)$ such conjugacy classes (the $2$ comes from $a = \pm 1$).

$m_g(t) = (t - λ)^2$

Then $1 = \det g = λ^2$ so $λ = \pm 1$. Choosing an eigenvector as a basis vector, $g$ is conjugate to $\pmat{λ & μ \\ &λ}$.

Note for any $x\in \mathbb{F}_q^\times$, $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{x \\ &x\inv}\pmat{λ & μ \\ &λ} \pmat{x\inv \\ &x} = \pmat{λ & x^2μ \\ &λ} \end{align*} $$ so $g$ is conjugate to one of $\pmat{λ & 1 \\ &λ}$ (if $μ$ is square) or $\pmat{λ & ε \\ &λ}$ (if otherwise). Routine calculation shows these two are not conjugate in $\SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$.

Computation of centraliser is an exercise to you, my dear reader.
