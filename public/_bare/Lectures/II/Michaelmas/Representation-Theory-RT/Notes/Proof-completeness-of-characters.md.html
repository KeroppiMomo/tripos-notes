Let $χ_1, \ldots, χ_k$ be all the irreducible characters, afforded by representations $ρ_1: G\to \GL(V_1), \ldots, ρ_k: G\to \GL(V_k)$. We already showed $χ_1, \ldots, χ_k$ are orthonormal, so it remains to show all class functions can be written as a linear combination of them. It suffices to show the orthogonal complement of $\op{\C-span}\set{χ_1, \ldots, χ_k}$ is $\set{0}$.

Suppose $f\in \mathcal{C}_G$ such that $\langle f, χ_i\rangle = 0$ for all $i$. Consider $$ \begin{align*} f_i := \frac{1}{\#G} \sum_{g\in G} \overline{f(g)} ρ_i(g). \end{align*} $$ $f_{i}$ is a $G$-linear endomorphism of $V_i$, so by Schur's lemma, $f_i = λ\, \id$. However $\tr f_i = \frac{1}{\# G} \sum_{g\in G} \overline{f(g)} χ_i(g) = \langle χ_i, f\rangle = 0$, so $f_i = 0$.

Then for any representation $ρ$, by complete reducibility, we can write $ρ \isom \bigoplus_i n_i ρ_i$, so $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{g\in G} \overline{f(g)}ρ(g) = 0. \tag{*} \end{align*} $$

Take $ρ = ρ_\text{reg}$ to be the regular representation and let $(*)$ act on the identity $1\in \C[G]$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{g\in G} \overline{f(g)} \, g = 0 \qquad \text{in $\C[G]$} \end{align*} $$ so $f(g) = 0$ for all $g\in G$.
