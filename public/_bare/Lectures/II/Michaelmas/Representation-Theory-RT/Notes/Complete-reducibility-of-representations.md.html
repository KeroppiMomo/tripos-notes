Definition (direct sum of representations) open_in_new

Let $ρ_U: G\to \GL(U), ρ_W: G\to \GL(W)$ be subrepresentations of $ρ: G\to \GL(V)$.

Say $ρ$ is an (internal) direct sum of $ρ_U$ and $ρ_W$, written $ρ = ρ_U \oplus ρ_W$, if $V = U$${}\oplus{}$$W$, i.e. $V = U + W$ and $U \cap W = \set{0}$. In this case we say $ρ$ is decomposable.

In matrix representation, $ρ = ρ_U \oplus ρ_W$ iff in a basis $\set{u_1, \ldots, u_k, w_1, \ldots, w_l}$ for $V$ containing a basis $\set{u_1, \ldots, u_k}$ for $U$ and a basis $\set{w_1, \ldots, w_l}$ for $W$, the matrix of $ρ(g)$ for any $g\in G$ has the form $\pmat{A & 0 \\ 0 & B}$, where $A$ is the matrix for $U$ and $B$ is the matrix for $W$.

Theorem (Weyl's unitary trick) open_in_new

Let $ρ: G\to \GL(V)$ be a complex representation.

There exists an inner product $\langle,\rangle$ on $V$ which is $G$-invariant, i.e. $\langle gv, gw\rangle = \langle v,w\rangle$ for all $v,w\in V$.

Therefore $ρ(G)$ is conjugate to a subgroup of the unitary group $U(V)$.

Theorem (Maschke, complete reducibility) open_in_new

Let $ρ: G\to \GL(V)$ be a representation over a field $k$, and $U$ be a subrepresentation.

If $G$ is finite, and $\op{char}{}$$k = 0$ or $\op{char} k\not\mid |G|$, then there exists another subrepresentation $U$ such that $V = U$${}\oplus{}$$W$ as representations.

Therefore by induction, $ρ$ is completely reducible, i.e. is a direct sum of irreducible representations.
