Let $λ$ be a generator of the cyclic group $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ of order $q-1$. The subgroup $B$ of upper triangular matrices have linear representations $$ \begin{align*} ρ_i: \qquad \qquad B &\to \C^\times \\ \pmat{λ^k & b \\ &λ^{-k}} &\mapsto ω^{ik} \end{align*} $$ where $ω$ is any primitive $(q-1)$-th root of unity.

To compute $\op{Ind}^{G}_B ρ_i$, we claim $G = B \sqcup BsB$, where $s = \pmat{& 1 \\ -1}\not\in B$. We compute $$ \begin{align*} s\pmat{a & b \\ & a\inv} s\inv = \pmat{a\inv \\ -b & a}. \end{align*} $$ This shows $sBs\inv \cap B = T$, and orbit-stabiliser implies $$ \begin{align*} \# BsB = \frac{\# B \cdot \# B}{\#(B \cap sBs\inv)} = \frac{((q-1)q)^2}{q-1} = q^2(q-1) = \# G - \#B \end{align*} $$ as claimed. Therefore by Mackey, $$ \begin{align*} \langle \op{Ind}_B^{G} ρ_i, \op{Ind}_B^{G} ρ_i\rangle ={}& \langle ρ_i, ρ_i\rangle + \langle \op{Res}^B_T ρ_i, \prescript{s\inv}{}{\op{Res}^B_T}ρ_i\rangle \\ ={}& \underbrace{\langle ρ_i, ρ_i\rangle}_{1} + \langle \op{Res}^B_T ρ_i, \op{Res}^B_T ρ_{-i}\rangle \end{align*} $$ because $\prescript{s\inv}{}{\op{Res}^B_T ρ_i}(g) = ρ_i(sgs\inv) = ρ_i(g\inv) = ρ_{-i}(g)$.

Therefore $\op{Ind}_B^{G} ρ_i$ is irreducible iff $ρ_i = ρ_{-i}$, i.e. $i = 0$ or $\frac{1}{2}(q-1)$ mod $q-1$.

Last four chracters

Recall facts about the character table.

We have found $1 + 1 + \frac{q-3}{2} + \frac{q-1}{2} = q$ distinct irreducible characters: $\mathbb{1}, \mathrm{St}, θ_i, ϕ_j$, so four remains.

We consider $\sum_{\text{irred }χ} (\deg χ)^2 = \#G$. The remaining four contribute to the sum $$ \begin{align*} \# G - (\deg \mathbb{1})^2 - (\deg \mathrm{St})^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{(q-3)/2} (\deg θ_i)^2 - \sum_{j=1}^{(q-1)/2} (\deg ϕ_j)^2 = q^2 + 2. \end{align*} $$ Computing $\langle χ, ϕ_{\frac{1}{2}(q+1)}\rangle = 0$ for $χ = \mathbb{1}, \mathrm{St}, θ_i, ϕ_j$ (for $1\leq i\leq \frac{q-1}{2}$, $1\leq j\leq \frac{q-1}{2}$) shows $χ_3, χ_4$ are not one of the found characters. Therefore we cannot have $ϕ_{\frac{1}{2}(q+1)} = χ_3 - χ_4$: otherwise $\deg χ_3 \geq q$ and with three others characters, the sum $\sum_{\text{irred }χ} (\deg χ)^2$ is too big. Therefore $ϕ_{\frac{1}{2}(q+1)} = χ_3 + χ_4$, and $χ_1, χ_2, χ_3, χ_4$ are the four remaining characters.

Since $\deg χ_1 + \deg χ_2 = q + 1$ and $\deg χ_3 + \deg χ_4 = q-1$, we have $$ \begin{align*} q^2 + 2 ={}& (\deg χ_1)^2 + (q+1-\deg χ_1)^2 + (\deg χ_3)^2 + (q-1-\deg χ_2)^2 \\ ={}& 2(\deg χ_1 - \tfrac{1}{2}(q+1))^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(q+1)^2 + 2(\deg χ_3 - \tfrac{1}{2}(q-1))^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(q-1)^2 \\ \geq{}& q^2 + 1 \\ \end{align*} $$ so we must have $\deg χ_1 = \frac{1}{2}(q+1) = \deg χ_2$ and $\deg χ_3 = \frac{1}{2}(q-1) = \deg χ_4$.

The conjugacy classes $\pmat{λ\\&λ\inv}$ and $\pmat{a&bε \\ b&a}$ are self-inverses, so the character values are real.

This implies all $e_\pm, f_\pm, g_\pm, h_\pm$ are real. For $(q-1)/2$ even, then $-1$ is not a square, so $\pmat{\pm 1 & 1 \\ &\pm 1}$ is inverse to $\pmat{\pm 1 & ε \\ &\pm 1}$. This implies all $e_\pm = \bar g_{\pm}, f_\pm = \bar h_\pm$.
