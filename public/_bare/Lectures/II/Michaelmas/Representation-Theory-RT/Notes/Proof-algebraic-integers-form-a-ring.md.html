(James and Lieback 22.3)

We use the formulation that algebraic integers are precisely eigenvalues of integer matrices.

Fix algebraic integers $λ,μ$, and let $Av = λv$ and $Bw = μw$, where $A\in \Z^{m\times m}, B\in \Z^{n\times n}, 0\neq v\in \C^{m}, 0\neq w\in \C^n$.

In the tensor product space $\C^{m} \otimes \C^n \isom \C^{mn}$, form the tensor product matrix $A \otimes B\in \Z^{mn \times mn}$. Then $$ \begin{align*} (A \otimes B)(v \otimes w) = (Av) \otimes (Bw) = (λv) \otimes (μw) = (λμ)(v \otimes w) \end{align*} $$ with $v\otimes w\neq 0$, so $λμ$ is an eigenvalue of an integer matrix $A \otimes B$, thus an algebraic integer.

Moreover $$ \begin{align*} (A \otimes I_n + I_m \otimes B)(v \otimes w) = (Av) \otimes w + v \otimes (Bw) = (λ+μ)(v \otimes w). \end{align*} $$ Hence $λ+μ$ is also an algebraic integer.
