Definition (algebraic integer) open_in_new

A complex number $α\in \C$ is called an algebraic integer if it is the root of a monic polynomial in $\Z[X]$.

We write $$ \begin{align*} \Z[α] = \bigcap_{\substack{R\leq \C \\ α\in R}} R, \end{align*} $$ or equivalently, $$ \begin{align*} \Z[α] ={}& \im(e_α), & e_α : \Z[X] &\to \C \\ && f &\mapsto f(α). \end{align*} $$

Proposition (algebraic integer iff eigenvalue of integer matrix) open_in_new

Let $α\in \C$. $α$ is an algebraic integer iff it is an eigenvalue of some square matrix, all of whose entries are integers.

Proof

Theorem (algebraic integers form a ring) open_in_new

The algebraic integers form a subring of $\C$. That is, if $λ,μ$ are algebraic integers, so are $λ+μ$ and $λμ$.

Proof

Proposition (rational algebraic integer is an integer) open_in_new

Let $α\in \C$ be an algebraic integer.

If $α\in \Q$, then $α\in \Z$.

Proof

Proposition (subring of C finitely generated as Z-module comprises algebraic integers) open_in_new

Any subring of $\C$ which is finitely generated as a $\Z$-module contains only algebraic integers.

Proof

Proposition (character values are algebraic integers) open_in_new

If $χ$ is a character of a finite group $G$, then $χ(g)$ is an algebraic integer for all $g\in G$.

Proof

Definition (group algebra) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite group.

The group algebra of $G$ is the $\C$-algebra $$ \begin{align*} \C[G] ={}& \sb{\sum_{g\in G} α_g e_g}{α_g \in \C}, \end{align*} $$ of formal $\C$-linear combinations of $G$ (with multiplication rule $e_g e_h = e_{gh}$).

Its centre is $Z(\C[G]) := \sb{x\in \C[G]}{xy = yx\; \forall y\in \C[G]}$, which is a subalgebra.

Lemma (class sums form basis for centre of group algebra) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite group. Let $\mathcal{C}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{C}_k$ be all the conjugacy classes of $G$, and define the class sum $C_i = \sum_{g\in \mathcal{C}_i} e_g\in{}$$\C[G]$.

$\set{C_1, \ldots, C_k}$ is a basis for the centre $Z(\C[G])$.

Moreover, $C_i C_j = \sum_{l=1}^{k} a_{ijl} C_k$ for some $a_{ijl}\in \Z_{\geq 0}$ called the structure constants for $\Z(\C[G])$.

Proof

Lemma (central character of group algebra) open_in_new

Let $ρ: G\to \GL(V)$ be a complex representation of a finite group $G$, and extend linearly to $ρ:{}$$\C[G]$${}\to \op{End}(V)$ a representation of algebras.

If $ρ$ is irreducible, then there exists an algebra homomorphism $ω_V: Z(\C[G]) \to \C$ such that $$ \begin{align*} ρ(z) = ω_V(z)\,\id_V \qquad \forall z\in Z(\C[G]). \end{align*} $$

If furthermore $\mathcal{C} = \prescript{G}{}g$ is the conjugacy class of $g\in G$ and $C = \sum_{x\in \mathcal{C}} e_x$ is its class sum, then $$ \begin{align*} ω_V(C) = \frac{\#\mathcal{C}\cdot χ_ρ(g)}{\deg ρ} \end{align*} $$ and is an algebraic integer.

Proof

Theorem (degree of irreducible representation divides group order) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite group.

If $ρ$ is an irreducible complex representation of $G$, then $\deg ρ \mid \#G/\#$$Z(G)$.

Proof

Proposition (irreducible character at conjugacy class of size coprime to degree) open_in_new

Let $χ$ be a character of $G$ and $\mathcal{C} = \prescript{G}{}g$ is the conjugacy class of $g\in G$.

If $χ$ is irreducible and $\# \mathcal{C}$ and $\deg χ$ are coprime, then $χ(g) = 0$ or $|χ(g)| = \deg χ$.

Proof

Theorem (finite group with conjugacy class of prime power size is not non-abelian simple) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite group. If $G$ has a conjugacy class which is not $\set{1}$ and whose size is a prime power, then $G$ cannot be a non-abelian simple group.

Proof

If a group $G$ has order $p^a q^b$ where $p,q$ are primes, $a,b\in \Z_{\geq 0}$ and $a+b\geq 2$, then $G$ is not simple.
