Let $k\in K\setminus \set{1}$.

Consider $gkg\inv$. Note for all $g'\in G$, $kg\inv g' H \neq g\inv g'H$ so $gkg\inv g'H \neq g'H$. Moreover $gkg\inv\neq 1$, so $gkg\inv \not\in K$.

Let $x\in G\setminus K$. By definition there exists a unique $gH\in G/H$ such that $xgH = gH$. Since $k$ does not fix any element of $G/H$, $kgH \neq gH$, so by uniquely of $gH$, $$ \begin{align*} xkgH \neq kgH = kxgH. \end{align*} $$ Thus $xk \neq kx$.
