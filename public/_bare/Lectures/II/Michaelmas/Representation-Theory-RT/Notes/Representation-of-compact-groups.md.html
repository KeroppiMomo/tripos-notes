Definition (topological group) open_in_new

A topological group is a topological space which is also a group, such that the group operation $\cdot: G\times G\to G$ and the inverse $\inv: G\to G$ are continuous.

Definition (representation of topological group) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\C$.

A representation of a topological group $G$ on $V$ is a continuous group homomorphism $ρ: G \to{}$$\GL$$(V)$ (where $\GL(V)$ has topology inherited from $\op{End}(V) \isom \C^{n\times n}$).

Theorem (Haar measure) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a compact topological group.

There exists a unique Borel measure $μ$ on $G$ satisfying

  1. translation invariance: $μ(S) = μ(gS)$ for all $S\subseteq G$ Borel, $g\in G$;

  2. $\int_G μ(\d g) = 1$.

Let $G,H$ be compact topological groups. Replacing $\frac{1}{\# G}\sum_{g\in G}$ with the Harr measure $\int \d g$, we prove the following results analogously:

  • Schur's lemma.

  • For irreducible representations $ρ, ρ'$, $\dim \op{Hom}_\C(ρ, ρ') = 1$ if $ρ$ and $ρ'$ are isomorphic and 0 otherwise.

  • Complete reducibility: every (finite-dimensional) representation of $G$ is a direct sum of irreducible representations.

  • Characters are now continuous class functions $G \to \C$.

  • $\langle χ_ρ, χ_ρ\rangle = 1$ iff $ρ$ is irreducible.

  • Every irreducible representation of an abelian group is one-dimensional.

  • All irreducible representations of $G\times H$ are of the form $V \otimes W$, where $V$, $W$ are irreducible representations of $G$, $H$ respectively.

Theorem (representation of S1) open_in_new

The irreducible representations of the compact topological group $S^1 ={}$$U$$(1)$ are $$ \begin{align*} ρ_n: S^1&\to \GL(\C) = \C^\times, &&n\in \Z \\ z &\mapsto z^n. \end{align*} $$ Therefore any character of a (finite-dimensional) representation of $S^1$ is identified with a Laurent polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients $\sum_{n=-N}^{N} a_n z^n\in \Z_{\geq 0}[z,z\inv]$ (where $a_n\in \Z_{\geq 0}$).

Theorem (conjugacy of SU(2)) open_in_new

The special unitary group of degree 2, $\op{SU}(2)$, is the group of unitary $2\times 2$ complex matrices of determinant 1.

  1. $\op{SU}(2) = \sb{\pmat{a & b \\ -\bar b & \bar a}}{a,b\in \C, \; |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1}$, which is homeomorphic to $S^3$. (Proof)

  2. $Z$$(\op{SU}(2)) = \set{\pm I}$. (Proof)

Define the maximal torus $T = \sb{\pmat{a \\&a\inv}}{|a|^2 = 1}\leq \op{SU}(2)$, which is homeomorphic to $S^1$, and $s = \pmat{&1 \\ -1}$.

  1. $s\pmat{a \\ &a\inv}s\inv = \pmat{a\inv \\ &a}$ and $N$$(T) = T \cup sT = \set{\pmat{a \\ &a\inv}, \pmat{& b\\ -b\inv}}$. (Proof)

  2. There is a well-defined bijection $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{conjugacy classes of $\op{SU}(2)$}} &\leftrightarrow [-1, 1] \\ \prescript{\op{SU}(2)}{}g &\mapsto \frac{1}{2}\tr(g). \end{align*} $$ In particular, every conjugacy class intersects $T$ at $\set{x, x\inv}$. (Proof)

  3. $Z_{\op{SU}(2)}$$(\pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv}) = T$ for $λ\neq \pm 1$. (Proof)

  4. Each conjugacy class, except $\set{I}$ and $\set{-I}$, is homeomorphic to $S^2$. (Proof)

Theorem (representation of SU(2)) open_in_new

Let $V_n$ be the $(n+1)$-dimensional complex vector space of degree $n$ homogeneous polynomials in two variables $x,y$: $$ \begin{align*} V_n = \sb{r_0 x^n + r_1 x^{n-1} y + \ldots + r_{n-1} x y^{n-1} + r_n y^n}{r_i\in \C}, \end{align*} $$ and define a representation of $\op{GL}_2(\C)$ on $V_n$ by $$ \begin{align*} \Bigl(\pmat{a & b \\ c & d} * f\Bigr)(x,y) := f\Bigl((x,y)\pmat{a & b \\ c & d}\Bigr) \equiv f(ax + cy, bx + dy). \end{align*} $$

  1. The restriction of a representation $ρ$ of $\op{SU}(2)$ to $T$ determines $ρ$ uniquely (up to isomorphism). Proof

  2. The character of a representation $ρ$ of $\op{SU}(2)$ correspond to an even Laurent polynomial of non-negative integer coefficients: $$ \begin{align*} χ_ρ(z) \equiv χ_ρ\left(\pmat{z \\ &z\inv}\right) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} a_n z^n, \qquad a_n\in \Z_{\geq 0}, \; a_n = a_{-n}. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

  3. The character of $V_n$ is $$ \begin{align*} χ_{V_n}(z) = z^n + z^{n-2} + \ldots + z^{2-n} + z^{-n} = \frac{z^{n+1} - z^{-(n+1)}}{z - z\inv}. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

  4. $V_0, V_1, V_2, \ldots$ are all the irreducible representations of $\op{SU}(2)$. (Proof)

Lemma (Clebsch-Gordan rule for SU(2)) open_in_new

Let $V_n$ be the unique irreducible representation of $\op{SU}(2)$ of dimension $(n+1)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} V_n \otimes V_m \isom V_{n+m} \oplus V_{n+m-2} \oplus \ldots \oplus V_{|n-m|} \end{align*} $$ where $\otimes$ is the tensor product. This is known as the Clebsch-Gordan rule.
