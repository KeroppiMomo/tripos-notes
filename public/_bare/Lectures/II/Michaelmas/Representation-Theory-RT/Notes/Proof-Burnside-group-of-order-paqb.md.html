All the abelian simple groups are $C_p$, so $G$ cannot be simple abelian.

We've seen if $\# G = p^a$ with $a\geq 1$, then $1 \neq Z(G) \lhd G$. Thus if $a\geq 2$, then $G$ cannot be simple.

Assume $a\geq 1, b\geq 1$. Let $Q$ be a Sylow-$q$ subgroup, i.e. of order $q^b$. $Z(Q)\neq 1$, so let $g\in Z(Q) \setminus \set{1}$. This implies $Z_G(g) \geq Q$, and thus $\#\prescript{G}{}g = \# G / \#Z_G(g)$ is a power of $p$. The theorem follows from our previous result.
