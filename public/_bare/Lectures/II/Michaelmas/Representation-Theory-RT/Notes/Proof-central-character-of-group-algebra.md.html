Let $z\in Z(\C[G])$. For any $g\in G$, since $ze_g = e_g z$, we have $ρ(z) ρ(g) = ρ(g) ρ(z)$, so $ρ(z)$ is a $G$-linear endomorphism of $V$. By Schur's lemma, $ρ(z) = ω_V(z)\, \id_V$ for some $ω_V(z)\in \C$.

Since $ρ$ is an algebra homomorphism, it is clear that $ω_V$ is an algebra homomorphism: $ω_V(zw) \id = ρ(zw) = ρ(z)ρ(w) = ω_V(z) ω_V(w) \id$ so $ω_V(zw) = ω_V(z) ω_V(w)$ for all $z,w\in Z(\C[G])$.

Next, let $\mathcal{C}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{C}_k$ be all the conjugacy classes of $G$ with representatives $g_1, \ldots, g_k\in G$. We showed $C_i\in Z(\C[G])$, so $$ \begin{align*} \#\mathcal{C}_i \cdot χ_ρ(g_i) ={}& \sum_{x\in \mathcal{C}_i} \tr ρ(x) &&\text{$χ_ρ$ is a class function} \\ ={}& \tr ρ(C_i) \\ ={}& \tr (ω_V(C_i) \,\id_V) \\ ={}& \dim V \cdot ω_V(C_i). \end{align*} $$

We also showed $C_i C_j = \sum_{l=1}^{k} a_{ijl} C_l$ for $a_{ijl}\in \Z$. Since $ω_V$ is an algebra homomorphism, $ω_V(C_i) ω_V(C_j) = \sum_{l=1}^{k} a_{ijl} ω_V(C_l)$. Hence the $\Z$-span of $\set{ω_V(C_1), \ldots, ω_V(C_k)}$ is a finitely generated subalgebra of $\C$, thus are all algebraic integers.
