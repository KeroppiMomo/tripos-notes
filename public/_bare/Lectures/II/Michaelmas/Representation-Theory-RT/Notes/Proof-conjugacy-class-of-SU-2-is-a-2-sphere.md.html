Under the homeomorphism $$ \begin{align*} \op{SU}(2) &\to S^3 := \sb{(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4)\in \R^4}{x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 + x_4^2 = 1}\\ \pmat{a & b \\ -\bar b & \bar a} &\mapsto (\Re a, \Im a, \Re b, \Im b), \end{align*} $$ a conjugacy class $O = \sb{g\in \op{SU}(2)}{\frac{1}{2}\tr g = t}$ corresponds to $\Re a = t$, which is a $S^2$ for $t \neq \pm 1$.

Another way of seeing this: by orbit-stabiliser, $\op{SU(2)}/T = \op{SU(2)}/Z_{\op{SU}(2)}(\pmat{λ \\&-λ})$ is homeomorphic $\prescript{\op{SU}(2)}{}{\pmat{λ \\ &-λ}}$ (not fully justified). We consider another action: $\op{SU}(2)\actson \mathbb{P}^1 = \C\cup \set{\infty}$ by Möbius transformation $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{a & b \\ -\bar b & \bar a} * z = \frac{az + b}{-\bar b z + \bar a}. \end{align*} $$

Note the stabiliser of $\infty$ is $T$ and the action is transitive. Therefore by orbit-stabiliser, $\op{SU}(2)/T \isom \mathbb{P}^1 \isom S^2$.
