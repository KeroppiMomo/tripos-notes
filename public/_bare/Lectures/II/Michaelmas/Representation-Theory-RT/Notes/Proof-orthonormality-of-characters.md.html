Let $ρ: G\to \GL(V)$ and $ρ': G\to \GL(V')$ be irreducible complex representations.

Schur's lemma implies $$ \begin{align*} \dim \Hom_G(V', V) = \begin{cases} 1, &\text{if $ρ, ρ'$ are $G$-isomorphic}, \\ 0, &\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Using $\Hom_k(V', V) \isom V'^\ast \otimes V$ as vector spaces, define a $G$-action on $\Hom_k(V', V)$ such that this is an isomorphism as $G$-spaces, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} (g * θ)(v) = ρ(g) \, θ(ρ'(g)\inv v). \end{align*} $$ Then the characters of tensor product and dual representation tell us $$ \begin{align*} χ_{\Hom_k(V', V)} = χ_V \, \overline{χ_{V'}}. \end{align*} $$

Moreover $\Hom_G(V', V) = (\Hom_k(V', V))^G \equiv \sb{θ\in \Hom_k(V', V)}{gθ = θ\; \forall g\in G}$ is the isotypical component of the trivial representation in $\Hom_k(V', V)$. Hence $$ \begin{align*} \langle χ_V \overline{χ_{V'}}, \mathbb{1}\rangle = \langle χ_{\Hom_k(V', V)}, \mathbb{1}\rangle = \dim \Hom_G(V', V). \end{align*} $$

Thus $$ \begin{align*} \langle χ_V, χ_{V'}\rangle = \frac{1}{\#G} \sum_{g\in G} χ_V(g) \overline{χ_{V'}(g)} = \langle χ_V \overline{χ_{V'}}, \mathbb{1}\rangle = \begin{cases} 1, \text{if $ρ, ρ'$ are $G$-isomorphic}, \\ 0, \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$
