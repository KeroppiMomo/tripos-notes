By Bézout, there exists $a,b\in \Z$ such that $a\,\# \mathcal{C} + b\deg χ = 1$. Then $$ \begin{align*} α := \frac{χ(g)}{\deg χ} = a \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\# \mathcal{C}\cdot χ(g)}{\deg χ}}_{ω_V(g)} + b\cdot χ(g) \end{align*} $$ is an algebraic integer because it is a $\Z$-linear combination of $ω_V(g)$ and $χ(g)$.

Let $d = \deg χ$. $χ(g)$ is a sum of $d$ many $d$-th roots of unity, and we'll now use Galois theory to show $α=0$ or $|α| = 1$:

Note $α \in K := \Q[e^{2πi/d}]$. The norm of $α$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} N(α) = \prod_{σ\in \Gal(K/\Q)} σ(α). \end{align*} $$ Notice

  1. $N(α) \in \Q$; and

  2. since $σ\in \Gal(K/\Q)$ preserves roots of polynomials, as $α$ is an algebraic integer, so is $α$ and hence $N(α)$.

Therefore $N(α) \in \Z$. However since $σ(α)$ is still a mean of roots of unity, we have $|σ(α)| \leq 1$, and so $|N(α)| \leq 1$. If $N(α) = 0$, then $α = 0$. If $|N(α)| = 1$, then each factor in the product has unit modulus, so in particular $|α| = 1$.
