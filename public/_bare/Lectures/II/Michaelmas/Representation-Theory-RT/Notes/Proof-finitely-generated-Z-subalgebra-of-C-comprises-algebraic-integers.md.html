Let $A$ be a $\Z$-subalgebra of $\C$ finitely generated by $s_1, \ldots, s_n\in A$, meaning every element of $A$ is a $\Z$-linear combination of $s_1, \ldots, s_n$.

Fix $a\in A$. Since $as_i\in A$, we can express as $as_i = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij} s_j$ for some $a_{ij}\in \Z$. In matrix form, $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{a_{11} & \ldots & a_{1n} \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ a_{n1} & \ldots & a_{nn}}\pmat{s_1 \\ \vdots \\ s_n} = a \pmat{s_1 \\ \vdots \\ s_n}. \end{align*} $$

If $s_1 = \ldots = s_n = 0$, then $A = \set{0}$ so the claim is trivial. Otherwise $a$ is an eigenvalue of an integer matrix, so is an algebraic integer.
