Using linear algbera

Hypothesis says $α$ is a root of the polynomial $p(X) = X^m - 1 = \prod_{j=0}^{m-1} (X - e^{2πij/m})$, which is a product of distinct linear factors. Then apply this.

Using representation theory

Any representation $ρ: C_m\to \GL(W)$ of the cyclic group $C_m = \langle g\rangle$ is uniquely determined by $ρ(g)$. $ρ(g)$ always has an eigenvector $v$ (as $\C$ is algebraically closed), so $\C v$ is an invariant subspace of $ρ(g)$, thus of $G$. Hence $ρ$ is irreducible iff it is one-dimensional.

Consider the complex representation of $C_m = \langle g\rangle$, given by $g^k \mapsto α^k$. It is completely decomposable $V = \bigoplus_{i} V_i$ into irreducible representations, which we argued to be all linear. Thus $α$ is diagonalisable.
