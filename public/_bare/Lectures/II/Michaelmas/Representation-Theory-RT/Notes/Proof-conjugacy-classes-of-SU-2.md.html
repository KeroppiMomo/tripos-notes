Let $g\in \op{SU}(2)$. By spectral theorem, every unitary matrix is diagonalisable via a unitary matrix $U(2)$, i.e. $g = u\pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv} u\inv$ for some $u\in U(2)$. Letting $ε = \sqrt{\det u}$, $u/ε\in \op{SU}(2)$ satisfy $g = (u/ε) \pmat{λ \\ &λ\inv} (u/ε)\inv$. Therefore each elements of $\op{SU}(2)$ is conjugate to an element in $T$.

Conjugate elements have the same eigenvalues, and $\tr g = λ + λ\inv$, so the stated map is well-defined. If $\tr g = \tr g'$, then since $\det g = \det g'$, the eigenvalues of $g$ and $g'$ are equal. As they are diagonalisable, they are conjugate. Finally $\tr\pmat{e^{iθ} \\ &e^{-iθ}}$ attains every value in $[-1,1]$.
