Define $$ \begin{align*} q := \frac{1}{\#G} \sum_{g\in G} ρ(g). \end{align*} $$

Check that $q$ is a linear projection of $V$ onto $V^G$:

  1. $q: V\to V^G$ because for any $h\in G$, $$ \begin{align*} ρ(h)\,q = \frac{1}{\#G} \sum_{g\in G} ρ(hg) = \frac{1}{\# G} \sum_{g'\in G} ρ(g') = q \end{align*} $$ where $g' = hg$.

  2. If $v\in V^G$, then $ρ(g) v = v$, so $qv = v$.

  3. $q$ is clearly $\C$-linear. (In fact $q$ is also $G$-linear.)

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \dim V^G = \tr q = \frac{1}{\#G} \sum_{g\in G} χ_ρ(g) = \langle χ_ρ, \mathbb{1}\rangle. \end{align*} $$
