$(θ, w) \mapsto (v \mapsto θ(v) w)$ is clearly bilinear, so $Φ$ is linear by the universal property of tensor products.

Let $v_1, \ldots, v_d$ be a basis for $V$, and $v_1^\ast, \ldots, v_d^\ast$ be the dual basis for $V^\ast$. Construct $$ \begin{align*} \tilde Φ: \Hom_k(V, W) &\to V^\ast \otimes W \\ ϕ &\mapsto \sum_{i=1}^{d} v_i^\ast \otimes ϕ(v_i). \end{align*} $$ Clearly linear.

Check that $Φ$ and $\tilde Φ$ are inverse of each other. If $ϕ = Φ(θ \otimes w): v \mapsto θ(v)\, w$ for $θ\in V^\ast$ and $w\in W$, then $$ \begin{align*} \tilde Φ(ϕ) = \sum_{i=1}^{d} v_i^\ast \otimes (θ(v_i) w) = \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d} θ(v_i) v_i^\ast\Bigr) \otimes w = θ \otimes w. \end{align*} $$ Conversely given $ϕ\in \Hom_k(V, W)$, $$ \begin{align*} Φ(\tilde Φ(ϕ))(v_j) = \sum_{i=1}^{d} Φ(v_i^\ast \otimes ϕ(v_i))(v_j) = \sum_{i=1}^{d} v_i^\ast(v_j)\, ϕ(v_i) = ϕ(v_j) \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} Φ(\tilde Φ(ϕ)) = \sum_{j=1}^{d} ϕ(v_j) v_j^\ast = ϕ. \end{align*} $$
