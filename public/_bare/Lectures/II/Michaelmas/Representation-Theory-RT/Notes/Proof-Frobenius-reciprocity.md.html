Class function

Expand: $$ \begin{align*} \langle ϕ, \op{Ind}^G_H ψ\rangle_G ={}& \frac{1}{\#G\cdot \# H} \sum_{g\in G} ϕ(g) \sum_{\substack{x\in G: \\xgx\inv\in H}} \overline{ψ(xgx\inv)} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\#G \cdot \# H} \sum_{\substack{(g,x)\in G\times G:\\ xgx\inv\in H}} ϕ(g) \overline{ψ(xgx\inv)} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\#G \cdot \# H} \sum_{h\in H} \sum_{x\in G} ϕ(x\inv h x) \overline{ψ(h)} &&h = xgx\inv \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\#G \cdot \# H} \sum_{h\in H} \sum_{x\in G} ϕ(h) \overline{ψ(h)} &&ϕ\in \mathcal{C}_G \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\# H} \sum_{h\in H} ϕ(h) \overline{ψ(h)} \\ ={}& \langle \op{Res}^G_H ϕ, ψ\rangle_H. \end{align*} $$

Vector spaces

(Existence of isomorphism follows from $\langle χ_U,χ_V\rangle_G = \dim \Hom_G(V, U)$.)

Consider $$ \begin{align*} ψ: \Hom_G(V, \Hom_H(\C[G], W)) &\to \Hom_H(V, W) \\ α &\mapsto (v \mapsto α(v)(e_1)). \end{align*} $$

Let's spell out what $α$ means: for $v\in V$ and $x\in \C[G]$, if $g\in G$, then $$ \begin{align*} α(gv)(x) ={}& (g * α(v))(x) &&\text{$α$ is $G$-linear} \\ ={}& α(v)(xg) &&\text{$G$-action on $\Hom_H(\C[G], W)$}, \end{align*} $$ and if $h\in H$, then $$ \begin{align*} h \,α(v)(x) ={}& α(v)(hx) &&\text{$α(v)$ is $H$-linear}. \end{align*} $$

  • $ψ$ is well-defined: for $h\in H$, $$ \begin{align*} ψ(α)(hv) ={}& α(hv)(e_1) \\ ={}& α(v)(e_{1}\cdot h) \\ ={}& α(v)(h\cdot e_1) \\ ={}& h\, α(v)(e_1) \\ ={}& h\, ψ(α)(v). \end{align*} $$

  • $ψ$ is $\C$-linear.

  • $ψ$ is injective: if $α(v)(e_1) = 0$ for all $v\in V$, then for all $g\in G$ and $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} α(v)(e_g) ={}& α(v)(e_1 \cdot g) = α(gv)(e_1) = 0 \end{align*} $$ so $α = 0$.

  • $ψ$ is surjective: given $θ\in \Hom_H(V,W)$, let $$ \begin{align*} α(v)(e_{hg}) = h\, θ(gv), \qquad \forall h\in H, g\in G, \end{align*} $$ and extend $α(v)$ linearly. This is well-defined, as if $hg = h'g'$ for $h,h'\in H$, $g,g'\in G$, then $h'\, θ(g' v) = h'\, θ(h'\inv h g) = h'h'\inv h\, θ(g) = h\,θ(g)$ since $θ$ is $H$-linear. $α(v)$ is $H$-linear, as $α(v)(h'\cdot e_{hg}) = α(v)(e_{h'hg}) = h'h\,θ(gv) = h'\,α(v)(e_{hg})$. $α$ is clearly $\C$-linear, and is $G$-linear because $α(g'v)(e_{hg}) = h\,θ(gg'v) = α(v)(e_{hgg'}) = α(v)(e_{hg}\cdot g') = (g' * α(v))(e_{hg})$. Finally $θ = ψ(α)$.
