Normal $\implies$ splitting field

Because $L/K$ is finite, let $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$, and let $f_i$ be the minimal polynomial of $α_i$ over $K$. Define $$ \begin{align*} f = \prod_{i=1}^{n} f_i\in K[X]. \end{align*} $$ Since $L$ is normal, $f$ splits over $L$. Moreover $L$ is generated by roots of $f$, so $L$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K$.

Splitting field $\implies$ normal

Let $L$ be a splitting field of $f\in K[X]$ over $K$. Fix $α\in L$, and we want to show its minimal polynomial $g\in K[X]$ over $K$ splits over $L$.

Let $β\in M$ be a root of $g$ over some splitting field $M$ of $g$ over $L$. It suffices to show $β\in L$.

Since $α$ and $β$ have the same minimal polynomial $g$, we have $K(α) \isom K[X]/(g) \isom K(β)$. Moreover, $L = L(α)$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K(α)$, and $L(β)$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K(β)$. By the uniqueness of splitting fields, $L \isom L(β)$. But $L(β) \supseteq L$, so $[L(β):K] = [L:K]$ and hence $L(β) = L$ by the tower law. Thus $β\in L$.
