Theorem (all finite fields) open_in_new

Let $p$ be a prime number and $q = p^n$ for $n\geq 1$.

Any field with order $q$ is a splitting field of the separable polynomial $X^q - X$ over $\mathbb{F}_p = \Z/p\Z$ (so all its elements are the distinct roots of $X^q - X$). Such a field exists, and any two such fields are isomorphic.

Write $\mathbb{F}_q$ for a field of order $q$. All finite fields are of this form.

Proof

Theorem (Galois theory of finite fields) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be finite fields.

$L/K$ is Galois, and $\Gal(L/K)$ is a cyclic group generated by the $\#K$-power Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^{\#K}$.

Proof

By correspondence, $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ has a unique subfield of order $p^m$ for all $m\mid n$ and no others. (Proof)
