Existence

Let $L$ be a splitting field of $f(X) = X^q - X$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and define $$ \begin{align*} K := \sb{α\in L}{α^q - α = 0}, \end{align*} $$ which is the fixed field of $ϕ^n: x \mapsto x^q$. Clearly $K \subseteq L$ by definition. $K$ contains all roots of $f$ in $L$, so $K$ is a field where $f$ splits into linear factors. As $L$ is a splitting field, $L \subseteq K$. Thus $L = K$.

As $f$ has degree $q$, $\#K \leq q$. But $f$ is separable because $f'(X) = qX^{q-1} - 1 = -1$ is coprime with $f$, so roots of $f$ are distinct. Hence $\#K = q$.

Any field of order $q$ is a splitting field of $X^q - X$

If $K$ is a field of order $q$, we showed that $K^\times$ is cyclic of order $q-1$, so any element $α\in K$ satisfies $α(α^{q-1} - 1) = 0$ i.e. $α^q - α = 0$. This gives $q$-many roots to $X^q - X$, so $X^q - q$ splits into distinct linear factors in $K$. $K$ is generated by the roots of $X^q - q$, so $K$ is a splitting field of $X^q - X$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

Uniqueness is by the result of splitting fields.

Exhausting all finite fields

If $K$ is a finite field of characteristic $p$, then $\mathbb{F}_p$ is a subfield of $K$, so $K$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space. Thus as vector spaces, $K \isom {\mathbb{F}_p}^n$ and hence $\# K = p^n$.
