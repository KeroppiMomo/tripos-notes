Let $K = L^G$.

$L/K$ is algebraic and separable

Consider $α\in L$. Then $$ \begin{align*} f(X) := \prod_{β\in Gα} (X - β) \end{align*} $$ satisfies $σ(f) = f$ for all $f\in G$, so $f\in L^G[X] = K[X]$. Since $α$ is a root of $f$, $α$ is algebraic and separable over $K$, and $$ \begin{align*} [K(α) : K] \leq \deg f = \# Gα \leq \# G. \tag{1} \end{align*} $$

$L/K$ is finite

Choose $α\in L$ such that $[K(α):K]$ is maximal, possibly by $(1)$. We claim $K(α) = L$. Pick any $β\in L$. Since $K(α,β)$ is separably and finitely generated, it is separable and finite over $K$, so by the primitive element theorem, there exists $θ\in L$ such that $$ \begin{align*} K(α,β) = K(θ). \end{align*} $$ By construction of $α$, $$ \begin{align*} [K(θ): K] \leq [K(α) : K] \leq [K(α,β):K] = [K(θ): K] \end{align*} $$ so $[K(α):K] = [K(θ):K]$, and by the tower law, $K(α) = K(θ)$, and thus $β\in K(α)$.

Hence $L/K$ is finite.

$L/K$ is Galois

Version 1

Since $G \subseteq \Aut(L/K)$, $$ \begin{align*} K \subseteq L^{\Aut(L/K)} \subseteq L^G = K \end{align*} $$ so $L^{\Aut(L/K)} = K$. As $L/K$ is algebraic, $L/K$ is Galois. Moreover as $L/K$ is finite, fundamental theorem applies, so $L^{\Aut(L/K)} = L^G$ implies $G = \Aut(L/K)$.

Version 2

Now $$ \begin{align*} \#\Aut(L/K) \leq [L:K] = [K(α) : K] \leq \# G \end{align*} $$ but $G \subseteq \Aut(L/K)$, so $G = \Aut(L/K)$ and $[L:K] = \#\Aut(L/K)$. By classification of finite Galois extensions, $L/K$ is Galois.
