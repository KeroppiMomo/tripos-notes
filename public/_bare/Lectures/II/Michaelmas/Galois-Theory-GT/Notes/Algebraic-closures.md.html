Definition (algebraically closed) open_in_new

Let $K$, $L$ be fields.

$K$ is algebraically closed if every non-constant polynomial in $K[X]$ splits into linear factors in $K$.

$L$ is an algebraic closure of $K$ if $L$ is algebraically closed and $L/K$ is an algebraic extension.

The following are equivalent:

  1. $K$ is algebraically closed;

  2. the only irreducible polynomials in $K[X]$ are linear;

  3. if $L/K$ is an extension and $α\in L$ is algebraic over $K$, then $α\in K$;

  4. if $L/K$ is an algebraic extension, then $L = K$;

  5. if $L/K$ is a finite extension, then $L = K$.

If $L/K$ is algebraic, the following are equivalent:

  1. every non-constant polynomial in $L[X]$ splits in $L$, i.e. $L$ is an algebraic closure of $K$;

  2. every non-constant polynomial in $K[X]$ splits in $L$.

Proof

Proposition (existence of maximal ideal) open_in_new

Assume the axiom of choice.

  1. Any non-zero ring has a maximal ideal.

  2. If $I$ is an ideal of a ring $R$ and $I \subsetneq R$, then there is a maximal ideal of $R$ containing $I$.

Proof

Theorem (existence of algebraic closure) open_in_new

Assume the axiom of choice.

Any field $K$ has an algebraic closure.

If $K$ is contained in an algebraically closed field or $K$ is countable, we don't require the axiom of choice.

Proof

Proposition (uniqueness of algebraic closure) open_in_new

Assume the axiom of choice. Let $K$ be a field.

Let $L/K$ be an algebraic extension, and $σ: K\hookrightarrow M$ be a field embedding where $M$ is algebraically closed.

  1. There exists a $σ$-embedding $L\hookrightarrow M$.

  2. If $L$ and $M$ are algebraic closures of $K$, then any $σ$-embedding $L \to M$ is an isomorphism.
