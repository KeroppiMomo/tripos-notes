Existence of $σ$-embedding

Induct on $[L:K]$. If $[L:K]=1$, then $L = K$ so there is nothing to prove.

Otherwise, $\deg f>1$. Let $g\in K[X]$ be an irreducible factor of $f$, and let $α\in L$ be a root of $g$ in $L$. Since $σ(f)$ splits in $M$, pick any root $β\in M$ of $σ(g)$ in $M$. Then there exists a $σ$-embedding $$ \begin{align*} σ_1: K(α)&\to M \\ α &\mapsto β. \end{align*} $$

Because $[L:K(α)] = [L:K]/[K(α):K] < [L:K]$, by induction hypothesis, there exists a $σ_1$-embedding $τ: L\to M$, so a $σ$-embedding.

$M$ is a splitting field

Let $τ: L\to M$ be a $σ$-embedding, and let $α_1, \ldots, α_n\in L$ be the roots of $f$ in $L$, where $n=\deg f$. Then $τ(α_1), \ldots, τ(α_n) \in M$ are all the roots of $τ(f) = σ(f)$ in $M$. As $M$ is a splitting field, $M = σ(K)(τ(α_1), \ldots, τ(α_n)) = τ(K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)) = τ(L)$. Hence $τ$ is surjective, thus an isomorphism.
