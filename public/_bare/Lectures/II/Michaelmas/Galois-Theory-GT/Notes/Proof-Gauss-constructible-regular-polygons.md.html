Constructible $n$-gon $\iff$ $ϕ(n)$ is a power of two

A regular $n$-gon is constructible $\iff$ $\cos(2π/n)$ is a constructible number $\iff$ $\Q(\cos(2π/n))$ is a constructible subfield of $\R$.

Let $ξ_n = e^{2πi/n}$ and $α = ξ_n + ξ_n\inv = 2\cos(2π/n)$. By the theory of cyclotomic extensions, $\Q(ξ_n)/\Q$ is Galois and $\Gal(\Q(ξ_n)/\Q) \isom (\Z/n\Z)^\times$, so $$ \begin{align*} [\Q(ξ_n) : \Q] = ϕ(n). \end{align*} $$ Since $[\Q(ξ_n):\Q(α)] = 2$ (as $n\geq 3$), if $\Q(α)$ is constructible, then $[\Q(α) : \Q]$ is a power of two, and hence so is $[\Q(ξ_n) : \Q]$ by the tower law. Hence $ϕ(n)$ is a power of two.

Conversely if $ϕ(n)$ is a power of two, then since $\Gal(\Q(ξ_n)/\Q) \isom (\Z/n\Z)^\times$ is abelian and of order a power of two, $\Q(α)/\Q$ is Galois whose Galois group is also abelian and of order a power of two. By classification of finite abelian groups (or Chinese remainder theorem), there exist subgroups $1 = G_0 \subset G_1 \subset \ldots \subset G_m = \Gal(\Q(α)/\Q)$ where $|G_i:G_{i-1}| = 2$. By Galois correspondence, this corresponds to subfields $\Q(α) = \Q(α)^{G_0} \supset \Q(α)^{G_1} \supset \ldots \supset \Q(α)^{G_m} = \Q$ where consecutive degrees are 2. Hence $\Q(α)$ is constructible.

$ϕ(n)$ is a power of two $\iff$ $n$ is a product of powers of two and distinct Fermat primes

Factorise $n = \prod_{i} p_i^{a_i}$ into prime factors. Then $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(n) = \prod_{i} (p_i-1) p_i^{a_i-1}. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $ϕ(n)$ is a power of two iff $n$ is a product of powers of two and distinct primes of the form $2^m + 1$.

If $2^m + 1$ is a prime, then $m$ is a power of two. Indeed if $m = ab$ with $b>1$ odd, then $$ \begin{align*} 2^{ab} + 1 = (2^a)^b + 1 = (2^a + 1)((2^a)^{b-1} - (2^a)^{b-2} + \ldots - 2^a + 1) \end{align*} $$ is a non-trivial factorisation.
