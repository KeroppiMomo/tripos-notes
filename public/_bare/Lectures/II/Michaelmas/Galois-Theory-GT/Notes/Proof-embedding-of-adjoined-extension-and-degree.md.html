Let $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$. Induct on $n$. The case $n=1$ is handled here.

For the induction step, define $K_1 := K(α_1)$. By the $n=1$ case, $$ \begin{align*} \# \op{Hom}_σ(K_1, M) \leq [K_1: K] \tag{1} \end{align*} $$ with equality iff $σ(f_1)$ splits into distinct linear factors in $M$. By induction hypothesis, as $L = K_1(α_2, \ldots, α_n)$, for each $σ$-embedding $τ: K_1\to M$, $$ \begin{align*} \# \op{Hom}_τ(L, M) \leq [L: K_1]. \tag{2} \end{align*} $$

Because each $σ$-embedding $ϕ: L\to M$ is a $τ$-embedding where $τ = σ|_{K_1}$, the total number of $σ$-embeddings $L\to M$ is $$ \begin{align*} \# \op{Hom}_σ(L,M) = \sum_{τ\in \op{Hom}_σ(K_1, M)} \# \op{Hom}_τ(L, M) \leq [K_1:K] [L: K_1] = [L:K] \end{align*} $$ by tower law.

If equality holds, then equality holds at $(1)$, so $σ(f_1)$ splits into distinct linear factors over $M$. Swapping the roles of $f_i$ shows the same for $f_2, \ldots, f_n$.

If $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ all split into distinct linear factors over $M$, then equality holds at $(1)$. Moreover the minimal polynomial of $α_i$ is a factor of $f_i$, so still splits into distinct linear factors over $M$. Therefore equality holds at $(2)$.
