Since $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$ by finiteness, it suffices to show any $L = K(α, β)$ equals $K(θ)$ for some $θ\in L$, and induct.

We wish to find $c\in K$ such that $$ \begin{align*} L \equiv K(α,β) = K(θ), \qquad θ = α + cβ. \end{align*} $$

Let $f,g\in K[X]$ be the minimal polynomials of $α$ and $β$ over $K$ respectively, and let $α_1 = α, α_2, \ldots, α_n$ be roots of $f$, and $β_1 = β, β_2, \ldots, β_m$ be roots of $g$ in some splitting field $M$ of $fg$ over $L$. Since $L/K$ is separable, all $β_i$'s are distinct.

Consider $$ \begin{align*} h(X) := f(θ - cX) \in K(θ)[X]. \end{align*} $$ Note $h(β) = f(α) = 0$. If $h$ and $g$ share no roots other than $β$, then $\op{gcd}(g,h) = X - β$ in $M[X]$. But $g,h\in K(θ)[X]$, so $X - β\in K(θ)[X]$. Hence $β\in K(θ)$, and thus $α = θ - cβ \in K(θ)$. This shows $K(θ) = K(α,β)$ as required.

Therefore we need to find $c\in K$ so that $β_2, \ldots, β_m$ are not roots of $h$. Since $f$ has roots $α_1, \ldots, α_n$, this is equivalent to saying choosing $c$ such that $$ \begin{align*} θ - cβ_j \neq α_i \iff c \neq \frac{α_j - α}{β - β_i} \quad \text{for all $i=2, \ldots, m$, $j=1, \ldots, n$}. \end{align*} $$

If $K$ is infinite, this is possible. If $K$ is finite, then we showed $K^\times$ is cyclic, so taking $θ$ to be its generator works.
