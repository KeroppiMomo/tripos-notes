$M/K$ is finite

$L$ is a $K$-vector subspace of $M$, so $L$ is finite-dimensional.

$M$ has a finite basis over $K$, which is also a finite basis over $L$.

$L/K$ and $M/L$ are finite

Let $\set{v_1, \ldots, v_n}$ be a $K$-basis for $L$ and $\set{w_1, \ldots, w_m}$ be a $L$-basis for $M$.

We claim that $\set{v_i w_j}_{1\leq i\leq n, 1\leq j\leq m}$ is a $K$-basis for $M$.

  • Spanning: for any $x\in M$, we can write $x = \sum_{j=1}^{m} λ_j w_j$ for $λ_j\in L$. We further write $λ_j = \sum_{i=1}^{n} μ_{ij} v_i$ for $μ_{ij} \in K$. Thus $x = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} μ_{ij} (v_i w_j)$.

  • Linear independence: suppose $\sum_{i,j} μ_{ij} (v_i w_j) = 0$ for $μ_{ij}\in K$. Letting $λ_j = \sum_{i=1}^{n} μ_{ij} v_i\in L$, we get $\sum_{j} λ_j w_j = 0$. But $\set{w_1, \ldots, w_m}$ is linearly independent, so $λ_j = 0$ for all $j$. Then since $\set{v_1, \ldots, v_n}$ is linearly independent, all $μ_{ij} = 0$.
