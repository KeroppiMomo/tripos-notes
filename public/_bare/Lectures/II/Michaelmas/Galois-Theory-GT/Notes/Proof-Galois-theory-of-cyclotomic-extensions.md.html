Cyclotomic character

Fix any primitive $n$-th root of unity $ξ_n$. Any $σ\in \Gal(K(ξ_n)/K)$ maps roots of $X^n-1$ to roots of $X^n-1$, so there is a well-defined $a\in \Z/n\Z$ such that $σ(ξ_n) = ξ_n^a$. Moreover $σ(ξ) = ξ^a$ for all $ξ\in μ_n$ powers of $ξ_n$.

For $σ,τ\in \Gal(K(ξ_n)/K)$, $$ \begin{align*} (στ)(ξ_n) = σ(ξ_n^{χ(τ)}) = σ(ξ_n)^{χ(τ)} = ξ_n^{χ(σ) χ(τ)} \end{align*} $$ so $χ(στ) = χ(σ) χ(τ)$. Taking $τ = σ\inv$ shows $χ(σ) \in (\Z/n\Z)^\times$, and $χ$ is a group homomorphism.

Any $K$-automorphism $K(ξ_n) \to K(ξ_n)$ is uniquely determined by its action of $ξ_n$, so $χ$ is injective.

$K = \mathbb{F}_p$

Proof 1

Let $m\geq 1$ be the order of $p$ in $(\Z/n\Z)^\times$, i.e. $m$ is the smallest positive integer such that $n\mid p^m-1$. Let $L = \mathbb{F}_{p^m}$. Since $n\mid \# L^\times$ and $L^\times$ is cyclic, there exists a subgroup of $L^\times$ of order $n$, which is the roots of unity. Let $ξ_n\in L^\times$ be a generator.

Since $n\not\mid p^k-1$ for all $k<m$, no proper subfield $\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$ of $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ (for $k<m$) contains $ξ_n$, so $\mathbb{F}_{p^m} = \mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n)$. Hence $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ is a splitting field of $X^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and $[\mathbb{F}_{p^m} : \mathbb{F}_p] = m$.

Proof 2

Since $\mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n)$ is a finite field, we showed that $\Gal(\mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n) / \mathbb{F}_p)$ is cyclic and generated by the Frobenius map $ϕ: x\mapsto x^p$. Note $χ(ϕ) = p \pmod n$ and $χ$ is an injection, so $\Gal(\mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n)/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is isomorphic to the subgroup of $(\Z/n\Z)^\times$ generated by $p$. Thus $[\mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n)/\mathbb{F}_p]$ is the order of $p$ in $(\Z/n\Z)^\times$.

$K = \Q$

It suffices to show $p\pmod n \in \im(χ)$ for all prime $p\not\mid n$, because then for all $m\in (\Z/n\Z)^\times$, all its prime factors are in $\im(χ)$, and thus so is $m$.

We want to show $ξ_n$ and $ξ_n^p$ have the same minimal polynomial over $\Q$, since then there is a $\Q$-automorphism $\Q(ξ_n) \to \Q(ξ_n)$ sending $ξ_n \mapsto ξ_n^p$.

Let $f,g\in \Q[X]$ be the minimal polynomials of $ξ_n$, $ξ_n^p$ respectively. Clear dominators to assume $f,g$ are primitive irreducible polynomials in $\Z[X]$, and suppose $f\neq g$. Since $f,g\mid X^n - 1$ in $\Q[X]$, so in $\Z[X]$ by Gauss, by irreducibility of $f$ and $g$, we have $$ \begin{align*} fg \mid X^n - 1 \quad \text{in $\Z[X]$}. \end{align*} $$

Let $\bar f, \bar g$ be their reductions mod $p$. Still $$ \begin{align*} \bar f \bar g \mid X^n - 1 \quad \text{in $(\Z/p\Z)[X]$}, \end{align*} $$ and since $\dd{X}(X^n - 1) = nX^{n-1}\neq 0$ is coprime with $X^n - 1$, $X^n-1$ is separable, and thus so are $\bar f$ and $\bar g$.

Now note $ξ_n$ is a root of $g(X^p)$, so $$ \begin{align*} f(X)\mid g(X^p) \quad \text{in $\Z[X]$}. \end{align*} $$ Reducing mod $p$, and using the Frobenius homomorphism, $$ \begin{align*} \bar f(X) \mid \bar g(X^p) = \bar g(X)^p \quad \text{in $(\Z/p\Z)[X]$}. \end{align*} $$ Since $\bar f$ is separable, $$ \begin{align*} \bar f \mid \bar g \quad \text{in $(\Z/p\Z)[X]$}. \end{align*} $$ Hence $$ \begin{align*} \bar f^2\mid \bar f \bar g\mid X^n - 1 \quad \text{in $(\Z/p\Z)[X]$} \end{align*} $$ which contradicts with the separability of $X^n - 1$.
