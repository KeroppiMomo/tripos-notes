Induct on $n$. The case $n=1$ follows from injectivity of field embeddings.

Suppose for $n\geq 2$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^{n} λ_i σ_i(x) = 0 \qquad \forall x\in K. \tag{1} \end{align*} $$ Since $σ_1 \neq σ_2$, let $y\in K$ such that $σ_1(y) \neq σ_2(y)$. Substitute $x \mapsto xy$ to see $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^{n} λ_i σ_i(x) σ_i(y) = 0 \qquad \forall x\in K. \tag{2} \end{align*} $$

Take $(1)\cdot σ_1(y) - (2)$ to see $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=2}^{n} λ_i(σ_1(y) - σ_i(y)) \; σ_i(x) = 0 \qquad \forall x\in K. \end{align*} $$ By induction hypothesis, $λ_2(σ_1(y) - σ_2(y)) = 0$. By construction $σ_1(y) - σ_2(y) \neq 0$, so $λ_2 = 0$.

Now $(1)$ only has $n-1$ non-zero terms, so apply induction hypothesis again to conclude $λ_1 = \ldots = λ_n = 0$.
