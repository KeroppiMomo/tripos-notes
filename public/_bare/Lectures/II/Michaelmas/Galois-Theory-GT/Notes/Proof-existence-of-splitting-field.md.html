Adjoin roots of $f$ one by one.

Induct on $\deg f$. If $\deg f \leq 1$, then $K$ is already a splitting field of $f$ over $K$.

Suppose all polynomials of degree $<\deg f$ have a splitting field. Let $g\in K[X]$ be an irreducible factor of $f$. Then $L := K[Y]/(g)$ is a field extension of $K$, with $α := Y+(g)\in L$ being a root of $g$, thus of $f$. Therefore $f(X) = (X-α) h(X)$ for some $h\in L[X]$. By induction hypothesis, there is a splitting field $M$ of $h$ over $L$.

We claim $M$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K$. Since $h$ splits into linear factors in $M$, $f(X) = (X -α)h(X)$ also splits into linear factors in $M$. Moreover we have $L = K(α)$ and $M = L(α_2, \ldots, α_n)$ where $α_2, \ldots, α_n$ are roots of $h$ in $M$, so $M = K(α, α_2 ,\ldots, α_n)$ is generated by roots of $f$.
