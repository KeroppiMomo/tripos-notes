A finite group $G$ is solvable if there exist subgroups $$ \begin{align*} 1 = G_0 \subseteq G_1 \subseteq \ldots \subseteq G_m \end{align*} $$ such that for all $i=1,\ldots, m$,

  • $G_{i-1}$ is a normal subgroup of $G_i$;

  • $G_i/G_{i-1}$ is abelian.

We can replace "abelian" with "cyclic" by classification of finite abelian groups.

If $G$ is solvable, any subgroup and quotient of $G$ is solvable.

If a normal subgroup $N$ and quotient $G/N$ are solvable, then $G$ is solvable.

Any non-abelian simple group is not solvable, e.g. $A_n$ for $n\geq 5$.
