Since the roots of $Φ_n$ are the primitive $n$-roots of unity in $\C$, $\Gal(\Q(ξ_n)/\Q)$ preserves roots of $Φ_n$, so fixes $Φ_n$. Therefore $Φ_n\in \Q[X]$.

Induct on $n$ to show $Φ_n\in \Z[X]$. For $n = 1$, $Φ_1(X) = X - 1$. Since $$ \begin{align*} X^n - 1 = \prod_{d\mid n} Φ_d(X) = Φ_n(X) \cdot \prod_{\substack{d\mid n: \\d<n}} Φ_d(X) \end{align*} $$ and $\prod_{d\mid n: d<n} Φ_d\in \Z[X]$ for $d<n$ by induction hypothesis, Gauss lemma implies $Φ_n\in \Z[X]$.

$Φ_n$ is a degree-$ϕ(n)$ polynomial which has $ξ_n$ as a root. Since $\Gal(\Q(ξ_n)/\Q) \isom (\Z/n\Z)^\times$ has order $ϕ(n)$, $Φ_n$ is the minimal polynomial of $ξ_n$ over $\Q$. Therefore $Φ_n$ is irreducible over $\Q$, so over $\Z$.
