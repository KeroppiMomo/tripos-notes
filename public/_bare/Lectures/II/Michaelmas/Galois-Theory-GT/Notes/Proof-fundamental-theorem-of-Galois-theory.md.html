$L/F$ is Galois

Follows from classification:

Since $L/K$ is finite Galois, $L$ is a splitting field of a separable polynomial $f$ over $K$. Then $L$ is also a splitting field of $f$ over $F$, and $f$ remains separable in $F[X]$. Since $L/F$ is finite, $L/F$ is Galois.

Correspondence

It follows from definition that $\Gal(L/F)$ is a subgroup of $\Gal(L/K)$, and that the functions are inclusion reversing.

Check the given maps are inverses of each other:

  • $L^{\Gal(L/F)} = F$ because $L/F$ is Galois.

  • To see $H = \Gal(L/L^H)$ for any subgroup $H \leq \Gal(L/K)$, note from definition $H \subseteq \Gal(L/L^H)$, so it suffices to show $\# H \geq \# \Gal(L/L^H)$.

    By the primitive element theorem, $L = L^H(α)$ for some $α\in L$. Observe $$ \begin{align*} f(X) := \prod_{σ\in H} (X - σ(α)) \in L[X] \end{align*} $$ has $α$ as a root, and $σ(f) = f$ for all $σ\in H$ so $f\in L^H[X]$. Thus $$ \begin{align*} [L:L^H] \leq \deg f = \# H. \end{align*} $$ As $L/L^H$ is Galois, $[L:L^H] = \#\Gal(L/L^H)$, so we're done.

$F/K$ Galois $\implies$ $σ(F) = F$ for all $σ\in \Gal(L/K)$

To show $σ(F) \subseteq F$, fix $α\in L$ and let $f\in K[X]$ be the minimal polynomial of $α$. Since $f$ has $σ(α)$ as a root and $F/K$ is normal, $σ(α)\in F$.

Since $F \isom σ(F)$, $[F:K] = [σ(F):K]$ so by the tower law, $F = σ(F)$.

$σ(F) = F$ for all $σ\in \Gal(L/K)$ $\implies$ $F/K$ Galois

Using the fixed field definition

Clearly $K \subseteq F^{\Gal(F/K)}$. If $α\in F \setminus K = F \setminus L^{\Gal(L/K)}$, there exists $σ\in \Gal(L/K)$ such that $σ(α) \neq α$. But $σ(F) = F$, so $σ$ restricts to an automorphism $σ|_F: F\to F$. Hence $α\not\in F^{\Gal(F/K)}$.

Using classification as normal separable extension

Fix $α\in F$, and let $f\in K[X]$ be its minimal polynomial over $K$. We want to show $f$ splits in $F$ and is separable.

As before, roots of $f$ are $σ(α)$ for $σ\in \Gal(L/K)$. Because $σ(F) = F$, $σ(α) \in F$, so $f$ splits in $F$. Also $L/K$ is separable, so $f$ is separable.

$σ(F) = F$ iff $\Gal(L/F)$ is normal in $\Gal(L/K)$

Let $H = \Gal(L/F)$. Note $$ \begin{align*} L^{σHσ\inv} ={}& \sb{α\in L}{στσ\inv(α) = α \quad \text{for all }τ\in H} \\ ={}& \sb{α\in L}{τσ\inv(α) = σ\inv(α) \quad \text{for all }τ\in H} \\ ={}& σ(L^H) = σ(F) \end{align*} $$ Therefore by correspondence, $$ \begin{align*} σ(F) = F \iff L^{σHσ\inv} = L^H \iff σHσ\inv = H. \end{align*} $$

Restriction homomorphism

Consider the group homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} \op{res} : \Gal(L/K) &\to \Gal(F/K) \\ σ &\mapsto σ|_F. \end{align*} $$ Clearly its kernel is $\Gal(L/F)$. First isomorphism theorem then implies $$ \begin{align*} \Gal(L/K)\; / \; \Gal(L/F) \isom \im \op{res} \leq \Gal(F/K). \end{align*} $$

But comparing orders, $$ \begin{align*} \#(\Gal(L/K)/\Gal(L/F)) ={}& \#\Gal(L/K) / \#\Gal(L/F) \\ ={}& [L:K] / [L:F] \\ ={}& [F:K] = \#\Gal(F/K) \end{align*} $$ so $\im \op{res} = \Gal(F/K)$.
