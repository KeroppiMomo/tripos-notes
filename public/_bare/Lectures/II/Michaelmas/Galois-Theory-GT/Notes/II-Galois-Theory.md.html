Proposition (Frobenius map) open_in_new

Definition (field extension) open_in_new

Let $L$ be a field and $K\subseteq L$ a subfield, i.e. $K$ is a field under the same operations $+, \times$.

We say $L$ is an extension of $K$, written $L/K$.

Definition (finite extension, degree) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a field extension. Then $L$ is a $K$-vector space with the same operations $+$ and $\times$.

We say $L/K$ is finite if $L$ is a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space, in which case the degree $[L:K]$ of $L/K$ is the dimension.

Otherwise $L/K$ is infinite and $[L:K] = \infty$.

Call $L/K$ quadratic, cubic etc if $[L:K] = 2,3$ etc.

Definition (field adjunction, simple extension) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a field extension, and $α_1, \ldots, α_n\in L$.

  • Define $K[α_1, \ldots, α_n] = \sb{f(α_1, \ldots, α_n)}{f\in K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]}$ as the smallest subring of $L$ containing $K$ and $α_1, \ldots, α_n$.

    It is the image of the evaluation homomorphism $K[X_1, \ldots, X_n] \to L: X_i \mapsto α_i$.

  • Define $K(α_1, \ldots, α_n) = \sb{\frac{f(α_1, \ldots, α_n)}{g(α_1, \ldots, α_n)}}{f,g\in K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]}$ as the smallest subfield of $L$ containing $K$ and $α_1, \ldots, α_n$.

    It is the field of fractions of $K[α_1, \ldots, α_n]$.

  • $L/K$ is simple if $L = K(α)$ for some $α\in L$.

Definition (algebraic element, minimal polynomial, transcendental element, algebraic extension) open_in_new

Suppose $L/K$ is a field extension, and $α\in L$.

Let $ϕ: K[X] \to L$ be the evaluation homomorphism $ϕ(\sum_{i} c_iX^i) = \sum_{i} c_i α^i$. (Recall $K[X]$ is a PID.)

  • Say $α$ is algebraic over $K$ if $\ker ϕ\neq (0)$.

    In this case, the minimal polynomial of $α$ over $K$ is the unique monic polynomial $f$ such that $\ker ϕ = (f)$.

    Say $α$ is transcendental over $K$ if $\ker ϕ = (0)$.

  • Say $L/K$ is algebraic if every $α\in L$ is algebraic over $K$.

If $α$ is algebraic and $f$ is its minimal polynomial over $K$, then the first isomorphism theorem implies $$ \begin{align*} K(α) = K[α] \isom K[X]/(f). \end{align*} $$ so $K[α]$ is a field.

Moreover the basis $\set{1 + (f), X + (f), \ldots, X^{\deg f - 1} + (f)}$ of $K[X]/(f)$ shows that $$ \begin{align*} [K(α) : K] = \deg f. \end{align*} $$

If $α$ is transcendental, then $K[α] \isom K[X]$ and $K(α) \isom K(X)$.

Definition (K-homomorphism, K-embedding) open_in_new

  • Let $L/K$ and $M/K$ be field extensions.

    A $K$-homomorphism or $K$-embedding of $L$ into $M$ is a ring homomorphism $L\to M$ which is constant on $K$.

    Write $\op{Hom}_K(L,M)$ for the set of all $K$-embeddings.

  • Let $L/K$ and $M/K'$ be field extensions, and $σ:K\to K'$ be a field embedding.

    A $σ$-homomorphism or $σ$-embedding of $L$ into $M$ is a ring homomorphism $L\to M$ which is $σ$ on $K$.

    Write $\op{Hom}_σ(L,M)$ for the set of all $σ$-embeddings.

Theorem (embedding of simple extension) open_in_new

Let $L = K(α)$ be a simple extension, where $α\in L$ is algebraic over $K$ with a minimal polynomial $f$.

For any field embedding $σ: K \hookrightarrow K'$ and any field extension $M/K'$, there is a bijection between $σ$-embeddings and roots of $σ(f)$: $$ \begin{align*} \op{Hom}_σ(L,M) &\to \set{\text{roots of $σ(f)$ of $M$}} \\ τ &\mapsto τ(α) \end{align*} $$ where $σ(f)\in K'[X]$ is the polynomial obtained by applying $σ$ to coefficients of $f$. In particular $$ \begin{align*} \# \op{Hom}_σ(L,M) = \#\set{\text{roots of $σ(f)$ of $M$}} \leq \deg f. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (splitting field) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field and $f\in K[X]^\times$ be a non-zero polynomial.

An extension $L/K$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K$ if

  1. $f$ splits into linear factors in $L$; and

  2. $L ={}$$K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$ where $α_1, \ldots, α_n$ are the roots of $f$ in $L$.

Therefore splitting fields are finite extensions.

Algebraic closures open_in_new

Definition (algebraically closed) open_in_new

Let $K$, $L$ be fields.

$K$ is algebraically closed if every non-constant polynomial in $K[X]$ splits into linear factors in $K$.

$L$ is an algebraic closure of $K$ if $L$ is algebraically closed and $L/K$ is an algebraic extension.

The following are equivalent:

  1. $K$ is algebraically closed;

  2. the only irreducible polynomials in $K[X]$ are linear;

  3. if $L/K$ is an extension and $α\in L$ is algebraic over $K$, then $α\in K$;

  4. if $L/K$ is an algebraic extension, then $L = K$;

  5. if $L/K$ is a finite extension, then $L = K$.

If $L/K$ is algebraic, the following are equivalent:

  1. every non-constant polynomial in $L[X]$ splits in $L$, i.e. $L$ is an algebraic closure of $K$;

  2. every non-constant polynomial in $K[X]$ splits in $L$.

Proof

  2. If $I$ is an ideal of a ring $R$ and $I \subsetneq R$, then there is a maximal ideal of $R$ containing $I$.

Proof

A field extension $L/K$ is normal if it is algebraic, and for all $x\in L$, the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $K$ splits into linear factors over $L$.

Let $M/L/K$ be extensions and $L/K$ be normal. Then any $K$-automorphism of $M$ restricts to a $K$-automorphism of $L$. (Proof)

Theorem (finite normal extension) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a finite extension.

$L/K$ is normal iff $L$ is the splitting field of some (possibly reducible) polynomial in $K[X]$ over $K$.

Proof

Given any finite extension $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$ of $K$ with $f_i$ the minimal polynomial of $α_i$, the normal closure of $L/K$ is any splitting field $M$ of $\prod_{i=1}^{n} f_i$ over $L$, which is the unique extension (up to isomorphism) satisfying that if $M/M'/L$ where $M'$ is normal, then $M' = M$.

Definition (separable polynomial, extension) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a field extension.

A polynomial $f\in k[X]$ is separable if it splits into distinct linear factors over some (equivalently all) splitting fields of $f$.

An element $α\in L$ is separable over $K$ if $α$ is algebraic over $K$, and its minimal polynomial is separable over $K$.

$L/K$ is separable if every $α\in L$ is separable over $K$.

If $L/K$ is separable, then $L/K$ is algebraic.

$f$ is separable iff $\op{gcd}$$(f,f') = 1$. Note $\op{gcd}$ is independent of which base field you take. (Proof)

If $f$ is irreducible, then $f$ is separable iff $\op{char}K =: p >0$ and $f(X) = g(X^p)$ for some $g\in k[X]$. (Proof)

Galois extensions open_in_new

Definition (field automorphism) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a field extension.

An automorphism of $L$ is a bijective ring homomorphism $L\to L$. The automorphism group $\Aut(L)$ is the set of all automorphisms of $L$, with the group operation being composition.

A $K$-automorphism of $L$ is an automorphism $σ: L\to L$ such that $L|_K = \id_K$. They form a subgroup $\Aut(L/K)$ of $\Aut(L)$.

Check the inverse of a bijective field homomorphism is a field homomorphism.

$\Aut(L) = \Aut(L/K)$ for $K$ the prime subfield $\Q$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$ of $L$.

If $L/K$ is finite, then $\Aut(L/K) = \op{Hom}_K(L,L)$ because any embedding $L\to L$ is surjective by rank-nullity. Therefore $\# \Aut(L/K) \leq [L:K]$.

Definition (fixed field) open_in_new

Let $L$ be a field.

For any subset $S \subseteq \Aut(L)$ of automorphisms, the fixed field of $S$ is the subfield $$ \begin{align*} L^S := \sb{x\in L}{σ(x) = x \; \text{ for all $σ\in S$}}. \end{align*} $$

$S \subseteq \Aut(L/L^S)$ by definition. For $G = \Aut(L/K)$, we have $L/L^G/K$ and $G = \Aut(L/L^G)$, because $G \subseteq \Aut(L/L^G) \subseteq \Aut(L/K) = G$.

Definition (Galois extension) open_in_new

A field extension $L/K$ is Galois if

  1. $L/K$ is algebraic;

  2. $K ={}$$L^{\Aut(L/K)}$.

In this case, we write $\Gal(L/K) = \Aut(L/K)$.

Theorem (classification of finite Galois extension) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a finite field extension. The following are equivalent:

  1. $L/K$ is Galois;

  2. $L/K$ is separable and normal;

  3. $L$ is a splitting field of a (possibly reducible) separable polynomial over $K$;

  4. $\#\Aut(L/K) = [L/K]$.

In this case, for $α\in L$, the minimal polynomial of $α$ over $K$ is $$ \begin{align*} \prod_{β\in (\Aut(L/K))(α)} (X - β) \in K[X] \end{align*} $$ where $(\Aut(L/K))(α) \equiv \sb{σ(α)}{σ\in \Aut(L/K)}$ (and we take product over distinct elements).

Proof

Theorem (fundamental theorem of Galois theory) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension.

There are inclusion-reversing bijections $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{intermediate subfield }L/F/K} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{subgroups of $\Gal(L/K)$}} \\ \sb{\text{intermediate subfield $L/F/K$}}{\text{$F/K$ is Galois}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{normal subgroups of $\Gal(L/K)$}} \\ F &\mapsto \Gal(L/F) \\ L^H &\mapsfrom H. \end{align*} $$

For any intermediate subfield $F$, $L/F$ is a Galois extension. The following are equivalent:

  1. $F/K$ is Galois;

  2. $σ(F) = F$ for all $σ\in \Gal(L/K)$;

  3. $\Gal(L/F)$ is a normal subgroup of $\Gal(L/K)$.

In this case, restriction induces a group isomorphism $$ \begin{align*} {\Gal(L/K)}\; /\; {\Gal(L/F)} &\xrightarrow{\sim} \Gal(F/K) \\ σ + \Gal(L/F) &\mapsto σ|_F. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (compositum) open_in_new

Let $L_1, L_2$ be subfields of $M$.

The compositum $L_1 L_2$ is the smallest subfield of $M$ containing $L_1$ and $L_2$.

If $f\in{}$$\Aut$$(L_1 L_2)$ is such that $f|_{L_1} = \id_{L_1}$ and $f|_{L_2} = \id_{L_2}$, then $f = \id_{L_1L_2}$, because $(L_1L_2)^f$ is a subfield containing $L_1$ and $L_2$.

Theorem (Galois theory of compositum) open_in_new

Let $M/K$ be a finite extension with intermediate subfields $L_1, L_2$.

  1. If $L_1/K$ is Galois, then so is $L_1L_2$$/L_2$. There is an injective group homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} \Gal(L_{1}L_2/L_2) &\hookrightarrow \Gal(L_1/K) \\ σ &\mapsto σ|_{L_1}, \end{align*} $$ which is surjective iff $L_1 \cap L_2 = K$.

  2. If both $L_1/K$ and $L_2/K$ are Galois, then so is $L_1L_2/K$. There is an injective group homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} \Gal(L_1L_2/K) &\hookrightarrow \Gal(L_1/K) \times \Gal(L_2/K) \\ σ &\mapsto (σ|_{L_1}, σ|_{L_2}), \end{align*} $$ which is surjective iff $L_1 \cap L_2 = K$.

Proof

Trace and norm open_in_new

Definition (trace, norm) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a finite field extension.

For $α\in L$, let $m_α: L\to L: x \mapsto αx$ be the $K$-linear map given by multiplication by $α$.

The trace and norm maps are $$ \begin{align*} \tr_{L/K}: L&\to K, &N_{L/K}: L&\to K \\ α &\mapsto \tr(m_α), & α &\mapsto \det(m_α). \end{align*} $$ ($\tr$, $\det$)

$\tr_{L/K}$ is $K$-linear.

$N_{L/K}$ is multiplicative, i.e. $N_{L/K}(αβ) = N_{L/K}(α) \cdot N_{L/K}(β)$.

$N_{L/K}(α) = 0$ $\iff$ $m_α$ is singular $\iff$ $α = 0$.

If $M/L/K$ and $α\in L$, then $$ \begin{align*} \tr_{M/K}(α) &= [M:L] \tr_{L/K}(α), & N_{M/K}(α) &= (N_{L/K}(α))^{[M:L]}. \end{align*} $$ In particular, if $α\in K$, then $\tr_{L/K}(α) = [L:K]α$ and $N_{L/K}(α) = α^{[L:K]}$. (Proof)

If $α\in L$ has a minimal polynomial $f(X) = X^n + a_{n-1} X^{n-1} + \ldots + a_1 X + a_0\in K[X]$ over $K$, then $$ \begin{align*} \tr_{L/K}(α) &= -[L:K(α)] a_{n-1} = [L:K(α)] \cdot (\text{sum of roots of $f$}), \\ N_{L/K}(α) &= ((-1)^n a_0)^{[L:K(α)]} = (\text{product of roots of $f$})^{[L:K(α)]}. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

Trace and norm are transitive, i.e. if $M/L/K$ and $α\in M$, then $$ \begin{align*} \tr_{M/K}(α) ={}& \tr_{L/K}(\tr_{M/L}(α)), & N_{M/K}(α) ={}& N_{L/K}(N_{M/L}(α)). \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

Theorem (trace and norm in terms of embeddings) open_in_new

Let $M/L/K$ be field extensions, with $L/K$ finite separable and $M/K$ normal. Let $\{σ_1, \ldots, σ_d\}=\Hom_K(L,M)$ (so $[L:K] = d$). Then for all $α\in L$, $$ \begin{align*} \tr_{L/K}(α) &= \sum_{i=1}^{d} σ_i(α), & N_{L/K}(α) &= \prod_{i=1}^{d} σ_i(α). \end{align*} $$ ($\tr$, $N$)

Proof

Definition (symmetric polynomial) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a ring.

A polynomial $f\in R[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ is symmetric if for all permutation $σ\in S_n$, $$ \begin{align*} f(X_{σ(1)}, \ldots, X_{σ(n)}) = f(X_1, \ldots, X_n). \end{align*} $$

Write $R[X_1, \ldots, X_n]^{S_n}$ to denote all symmetric polynomials.

Theorem (fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials) open_in_new

The elementary symmetric polynomials in $n$ variables are $s_1, \ldots, s_n$ given by $$ \begin{align*} s_r(X_1, \ldots, X_n) = \sum_{1\leq i_1 < \ldots < i_r \leq n} X_{i_1} \ldots X_{i_r}. \end{align*} $$ Equivalently, they satisfy $$ \begin{align*} \prod_{i=1}^{n} (X_i + T) = T^n + s_1 T^{n-1} + \ldots + s_{n-1} T + s_n. \end{align*} $$

There is a ring isomorphism given by $$ \begin{align*} R[Y_1, \ldots, Y_n] &\to R[X_1, \ldots, X_n]^{S_n} \\ Y_i &\mapsto s_i(X_1, \ldots, X_n). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (Galois group of polynomial) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field.

For a separable polynomial $f\in K[X]$, let $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$ be the its splitting field of $f$ over $K$ and $α_1, \ldots, α_n$ be the roots of $f$ in $L$ (so $\deg f = n$). The Galois group $\Gal(L/K)$ acts on $\set{α_1, \ldots, α_n}$ faithfully, determining an injective group homomorphism $\Gal(L/K) \hookrightarrow S_n$.

The Galois group $\Gal(f/K) \leq S_n$ of $f$ over $K$ is the image of the homomorphism, defined up to conjugacy.

$f$ is irreducible iff $\Gal(f/K)$ is transitive. (Proof)

Definition (discriminant) open_in_new

Let $f\in K[X]$ be a monic separable polynomial, and let $α_1, \ldots, α_n$ be its roots in some splitting field.

Define $$ \begin{align*} Δ &:= \prod_{i<j} (α_i - α_j), \\ \op{Disc}(f) &:= Δ^2 = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} \prod_{i\neq j} (α_i - α_j). \end{align*} $$ $\op{Disc}(f)$ is called the discriminant of $f$.

$\op{Disc}(f) \in K$. (Proof)

We have a formula $$ \begin{align*} \op{Disc}(X^n + bX + c) ={}& (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\bigl((1-n)^{n-1} b^n + n^n c^{n-1}\bigr). \end{align*} $$ (sheet 3 Q7). In particular for $n=2,3$, $$ \begin{align*} \op{Disc}(X^2 + bX + c) &= b^2 - 4c, \\ \op{Disc}(X^3 + bX + c) &= -4b^3 - 27c. \end{align*} $$

Definition (resolvent cubic) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field and $f\in k[X]$ be a monic quartic (degree 4) polynomial.

Letting $α_1, α_2, α_3, α_4$ be the roots of $f$ in some splitting field, define $$ \begin{align*} β_1 ={}&(α_1 + α_2)(α_3 + α_4) \\ β_2 ={}& (α_1 + α_3)(α_2 + α_4) \\ β_3 ={}& (α_1 + α_4)(α_2 + α_3). \end{align*} $$

The resolvent cubic of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} g(X) = \prod_{i=1}^{3} (X - β_i). \end{align*} $$

$g\in k[X]$. (Proof)

Definition (cyclotomic extension) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field with characteristic $p$. Let $n\in \Z_{\geq 1}$. Assume $p=0$ or $p\not\mid n$.

Let $L$ be the splitting field of $X^n - 1$ over $K$. The group of $n$-th roots of unity $$ \begin{align*} μ_n := \sb{α\in L}{α^n = 1} \end{align*} $$ is cyclic, and any generator is called a primitive $n$-th root of unity, denoted $ξ_n$.

Then $L = K(ξ_n)$ is called a cyclotomic extension of $K$.

$K(ξ_n)/K$ is Galois, because $\dd{X}(X^n - 1) = nX^{n-1} \neq 0$ is coprime with $X^n - 1$, so $X^n - 1$ is separable.

Theorem (Galois theory of cyclotomic extensions) open_in_new

Let $K(ξ_n)/K$ be a cyclotomic extension.

  1. There is an injective group homomorphism, known as the cyclotomic character, $$ \begin{align*} χ: \Gal(K(ξ_n)/K) &\hookrightarrow (\Z/n\Z)^\times \\ \end{align*} $$ given by $$ \begin{align*} σ(ξ) = ξ^{χ(σ)} \end{align*} $$ for all $n$-th roots of unity $ξ\in μ_n$, where $(\Z/n\Z)^\times = \sb{a\in \Z/n\Z}{\op{gcd}(a,n) = 1}$ has order $ϕ$$(n)$.

  2. If $K = \mathbb{F}_p$, then $$ \begin{align*} [\mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n) : \mathbb{F}_p] = \text{order of $p$ in $(\Z/n\Z)^\times$} \end{align*} $$ and $\Gal$$(\mathbb{F}_p(ξ_n)/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is cyclic.

  3. If $K = \Q$, then $χ$ is an isomorphism, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \Gal(\Q(ξ_n)/\Q) \isom (\Z/n\Z)^\times. \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} [\Q(ξ_n):\Q] = ϕ(n). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (Kummer extension) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $p$, $n\in \Z_{\geq 1}$.

Assume $p=0$ or $p\not\mid n$, and $K$ contains all roots of unity $μ_n = \langle ξ_n\rangle$.

For $a\in K^\times$, let $L$ be the splitting field of $X^n - a$ over $K$. Letting $\sqrt[n]{a}\in L$ be a root of $X^n - a$, all its roots are $\sqrt[n]{a}, ξ_n \sqrt[n]{a}, \ldots, ξ_{n}^{n-1} \sqrt[n]{a}$.

We call $L = K(\sqrt[n]{a})$ a Kummer extension of $K$.

$K(\sqrt[n]{a})/K$ is Galois, because $\dd{X}(X^n - a) = nX^{n-1} \neq 0$ is coprime with $X^n - a$, so $X^n - a$ is separable.

Theorem (Galois group of Kummer extensions) open_in_new

Let $K(\sqrt[n]{a})/K$ be a Kummer extension.

  1. There is an injective group homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} θ: \Gal(K(\sqrt[n]{a})/K) &\hookrightarrow μ_n \\ σ &\mapsto \frac{σ(\sqrt[n]{a})}{\sqrt[n]{a}} \end{align*} $$ where $μ_n$ is the group of $n$-th roots of unity.

    In particular, $\Gal$$(K(\sqrt[n]{a})/K)$ is cyclic of order dividing $n$.

  2. $[K(\sqrt[n]{a}) : K]$ is the least $m\geq 1$ such that $(\sqrt[n]{a})^m\in K^\times$.

  3. $X^n - a$ is irreducible over $K$ iff $a$ is not a $d$-th power in $K$ for any $1<d\mid n$.

Proof

Ruler and compass constructions open_in_new

Definition (constructible number) open_in_new

For a finite set of points $S \subseteq \R^2$, consider the three operations:

  1. draw a straight line through two distinct points $P,Q\in S$;

  2. draw a circle centred at some $P\in S$ through some $Q\in S$;

  3. enlarge $S$ by adjoining any point of intersection of two distinct lines or circles constructed using 1 and 2.

A point $(x,y)\in \R^2$ is constructible from $S$ if we can enlarge $S$ to include $(x,y)$ using a finite sequence of operations 1, 2, 3.

A number $x\in \R$ is constructible if $(x,0)$ is constructible from $\set{(0,0), (1,0)}$.

Definition (constructible field) open_in_new

Let $K\subseteq \R$ be a subfield.

Say $K$ is constructible if there exist fields $$ \begin{align*} \Q = L_0 \subseteq L_1 \subseteq \ldots \subseteq L_m \end{align*} $$ such that $[L_i:L_{i-1}] = 2$ and $K \subseteq L_m$.

$[K:\Q]$ is a power of 2 by the tower law.

A number $x\in \R$ is constructible iff $\Q(x)$ is a constructible field.

Definition (solvable by radicals) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field.

An irreducible polynomial $f\in K[X]$ is solvable by radicals over $K$ if there exist fields $$ \begin{align*} K = K_0 \subseteq K_1 \subseteq \ldots \subseteq K_m \end{align*} $$ such that

  • $f$ has a root in $K_m$; and

  • $K_i ={}$$K_{i-1}(α_i)$ for some $α_i^{n_i} \in K_{i-1}$, $n_i\geq 1$ for all $i=1,\ldots,m$.

Definition (solvable group) open_in_new

A finite group $G$ is solvable if there exist subgroups $$ \begin{align*} 1 = G_0 \subseteq G_1 \subseteq \ldots \subseteq G_m \end{align*} $$ such that for all $i=1,\ldots, m$,

  • $G_{i-1}$ is a normal subgroup of $G_i$;

  • $G_i/G_{i-1}$ is abelian.

We can replace "abelian" with "cyclic" by classification of finite abelian groups.

If $G$ is solvable, any subgroup and quotient of $G$ is solvable.

If a normal subgroup $N$ and quotient $G/N$ are solvable, then $G$ is solvable.

Any non-abelian simple group is not solvable, e.g. $A_n$ for $n\geq 5$.

Theorem (solvable by radicals iff solvable Galois group) open_in_new

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic 0, and $f\in K[X]$ be an irreducible polynomial.

$f$ is solvable by radicals over $K$ iff $\Gal(f/K)$ is a solvable group.
