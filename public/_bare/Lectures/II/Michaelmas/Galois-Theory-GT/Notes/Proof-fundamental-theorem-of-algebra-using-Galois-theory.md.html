We want to show $\C$ is algebraically closed.

Suppose $L/\C$ is a proper finite extension. By taking its Galois closure over $\R$ (noting $\op{char}(\R) = 0$), WLOG assume $L/\R$ is Galois.

$\Gal(L/\R)$ is a 2-group

Suppose $\Gal(L/\R)$ is not a 2-group, and let $H\neq \Gal(L/\R)$ be a Sylow 2-subgroup of the finite group $\Gal(L/\R)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} 2\not\mid \#G/\#H = [L:\R]/[L:L^H] = [L^H : \R]. \end{align*} $$ By correspondence, $L^H \neq \R$, so let $α\in L^H \setminus \R$ and $f\in \R[X]$ be its minimal polynomial over $\R$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \deg f = [\R(α):\R] \mid [L^H :\R]. \end{align*} $$ So $\deg f$ is odd and $\geq 3$. But by the intermediate value theorem, $f$ has a real root, contradicting with irreducibility of $f$.

Quadratic extension is impossible

Now $\Gal(L/\C)$ is a subgroup of $\Gal(L/\R)$, so is also a 2-group. By the theory of $p$-groups, $\Gal(L/\C)$ has a subgroup $G$ of index 2. Then $$ \begin{align*} [L^G : \C] = [L:\C]/[L:L^G] = \#\Gal(L/\C) / \# G = 2. \end{align*} $$

However $\C$ has no quadratic extension: roots of any quadratic polynomial are given by the quadratic formula, so are complex.
