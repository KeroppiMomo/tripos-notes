Suppose $S\subseteq \R^2$ is a finite set of points with coordinates lying in a constructible field $K$. It suffices to show that if we adjoin $(x,y)\in \R^2$ using operation 3, then $K(x,y)$ is also constructible.

$K$ is constructible, so there a consecutive sequence of quadratic extensions $\Q = F_0 \subseteq F_1 \subseteq F_n, [F_i : F_{i-1}] = 2$ and $K \subseteq F_n$.

The lines and circles constructed in operations 1 and 2 have equations of the form $$ \begin{align*} aX + bY ={}& c, \\ (X-a)^2 + (Y-b)^2 ={}& c \end{align*} $$ with $a,b,c\in K$.

If $(x,y)$ is a point of intersection of two such lines or circles, then $x=r+s \sqrt{v}, y = t+u\sqrt{v}$ where $r,s,t,u,v\in K$. Thus $x,y\in K(\sqrt{v}) \subseteq F_n(\sqrt{v})$. Since $[F_n(\sqrt{v}) : F_n] = 1$ or $2$, it follows that $K(x,y)$ is constructible.
