$K(X_1,\ldots, X_n)^{S_n}$

Clearly $K[s_1, \ldots, s_n] \subseteq K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]^{S_n}$.

Elementary proof

Recall $K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ is a UFD.

Suppose $f/g\in K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)^{S_n}$, and WLOG take $f,g\in K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ to be coprime. This means $f/g = σ(f)/σ(g)$, so $$ \begin{align*} σ(g)\cdot f = σ(f) \cdot g \qquad \text{in $K[X_1 ,\ldots, X_n]$}. \end{align*} $$

Since $f \mid σ(f) \cdot g$ and $f,g$ are coprime, we have $f\mid σ(f)$. This argument also shows $g \mid σ(g)$. Moreover, $σ(f)$ and $σ(g)$ are coprime as well, so we have $σ(f)\mid f$ and $σ(g)\mid g$.

Hence $f,σ(f)$ and $g,σ(f)$ differ by a unit in $K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$, i.e. a constant. As $σ(g) f = σ(f)g$, the constants are the same, so $$ \begin{align*} σ(f) = cf, \qquad σ(g) = cg \end{align*} $$ for some $c\in K^\times$.

Note $σ$ has order dividing $\# S_n = n!$, so $$ \begin{align*} c^{n!} f = σ^{n!}(f) = f \implies c^{n!} = 1 \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} fg^{n!-1}, \;g^{n!} \in K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]^{S_n}. \end{align*} $$ By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomial, $K[X_1, \ldots, X_n]^{S_n} = K[s_1, \ldots, s_n]$. Hence $f/g \in K(s_1, \ldots, s_n)$.

Using Artin's theorem

By Artin, $[K(X_1, \ldots, X_n) : K(X_1,\ldots, X_n)^{S_n}] = \# S_n = n!$, so it suffices to show $[K(X_1, \ldots, X_n): K(s_1, \ldots, s_n)] \leq n!$.

Consider $$ \begin{align*} f(T) := \prod_{i=1}^{n} (T - X_i) = T^n - s_1 T^{n-1} + s_2 T^{n-2} - \ldots + (-1)^n s_n \end{align*} $$ a polynomial with coefficients in $K(s_1, \ldots, s_n)$. $K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K(s_1, \ldots, s_n)$, so by sheet 1 Q12, $$ \begin{align*} [K(X_1, \ldots, X_n) : K(s_1, \ldots, s_n)] \leq (\deg f)! = n!. \end{align*} $$

$K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)^{A_n}$

By Artin's theorem, $[K(X_1, \ldots, X_n) : K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)^{A_n}] = \# A_n = \frac{1}{2}n!$, so by the tower law, $$ \begin{align*} [K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)^{A_n} : K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)^{S_n}] = 2. \end{align*} $$ It suffices to show $δ$ is fixed by $A_n$ but not $S_n$. Indeed as $\sign(σ)$ equals the number of inversions of $σ$, $$ \begin{align*} σ(δ) = \sign(σ) δ \end{align*} $$ for all $σ\in S_n$. Since $δ\neq 0$ and $+1 \neq -1$ in $K$, $σ(δ) = δ$ if $σ\in A_n$ and $σ(δ) \neq δ$ if $σ\not\in A_n$.
