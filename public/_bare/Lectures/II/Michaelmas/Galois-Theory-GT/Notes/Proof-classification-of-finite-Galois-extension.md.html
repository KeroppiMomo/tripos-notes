Galois $\implies$ normal and separable

Note $\#\Aut(L/K) \leq [L:K]<\infty$. We will show that if $L/K$ is Galois, then any $α\in L$ has minimal polynomial $$ \begin{align*} f(X) = \prod_{β\in (\Aut(L/K))(α)} (X - β). \end{align*} $$

A priori $f\in L[X]$. However for any $σ\in \Aut(L/K)$, $$ \begin{align*} σ(f) = \prod_{β\in (\Aut(L/K))(α)} (X - σ(β)) = \prod_{β'\in (\Aut(L/K))(α)} (X - β') = f \end{align*} $$ with $β \mapsto β' := σ(β)$ a permutation of $(\Aut(L/K))(α)$, so coefficients of $f$ are in $L^{\Aut(L/K)} = K$. Hence $f\in K[X]$.

We need to show if $g\in K[X]$ has $α$ as a root, then $f\mid g$ in $K[X]$. Indeed because $$ \begin{align*} 0 = σ(g(α)) = g(σ(α)) \end{align*} $$ for any $σ\in \Aut(L/K)$, $(\Aut(L/K))(α)$ are roots of $g$. Therefore $f\mid g$ in $L[X]$, hence in $K[X]$.

Since $f$ splits into distinct linear factors over $L$, $L/K$ is normal and separable.

Normal and separable $\implies$ splitting field of separable polynomial

Let $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$ and $f_i$ be the minimal polynomial of $α_i$. Let $f = \op{lcm}(f_1, \ldots, f_n)\in K[X]$. Since $L$ is normal, $f$ splits in $L$, and because $X - α_i \mid f_i \mid f$ in $L[X]$, $f$ has roots $α_1, \ldots, α_n$. Therefore $L$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $K$.

We claim $f$ is separable. Since $L$ is separable, $f_i$ is separable. Moreover if $f_i\neq f_j$, then $\op{gcd}(f_i, f_j) = 1$ in $K[X]$ thus in $L[X]$ because $f_i$, $f_j$ are irreducible, so $f_i, f_j$ share no roots. Thus $f$ splits into distinct linear factors in $L$.

Splitting field of separable polynomial $\implies$ $\# \Aut(L/K) = [L:K]$

Let $L = K(α_1, \ldots, α_n)$ be a splitting field of a separable $f$ over $K$, where $α_1, \ldots, α_n$ are roots of $f$. Since the minimal polynomial $f_i$ of $α_i$ divides $f$, $f_i$ is also separable. We noted $\Aut(L/K) = \Hom_K(L,L)$. Since $f_i$ splits into distinct linear factors in $L$, we showed $\#\Hom_k(L,L) = [L:K]$.

$\# \Aut(L/K) = [L:K]$ $\implies$ Galois

Note the tower $$ \begin{align*} L/L^{\Aut(L/K)}/K. \end{align*} $$

Using $\Aut(L/K) = \Aut(L/L^{\Aut(L/K)})$, we get $$ \begin{align*} [L:K] = \#\Aut(L/K) = \# \Aut(L/L^{\Aut(L/K)}) \leq [L:L^{\Aut(L/K)}]. \end{align*} $$ So by the tower law, $[K:L^{\Aut(L/K)}] = 1$, hence $K = L^{\Aut(L/K)}$
