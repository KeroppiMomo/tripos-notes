$r(T)$ is well-defined, because $σ(T)\neq \emptyset$ and $σ(T) \subseteq \sb{z\in \C}{|z|\leq \|T\|}$.

$r(T) \leq \inf_{n\in \N} \|T^n\|^{1/n}$

Suppose $λ\in σ(T)$. Spectral mapping theorem implies $λ^n\in σ(T^n)$, so $|λ^n| \leq \|T^n\|$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} |λ| \leq \|T^n\|^{1/n}. \end{align*} $$ Take sup on the left and inf on the right to get $r(T) \leq \inf_{n\in \N}\|T^n\|^{1/n}$.

$r(T) \geq \limsup_{n\to \infty} \|T^n\|^{1/n}$

(Non-examinable)

Recall for $λ>\|T\|$, $$ \begin{align*} R_T(λ) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{T^n}{λ^{n+1}} \end{align*} $$ with $R_T$ a holomorphic function. This is a Laurent series, so has a radius of convergence $\limsup_{n\to \infty} \|T^n\|^{1/n}$. But $R_T$ is defined on $ρ(T) \supseteq \sb{z\in \C}{\|z\| > r(T)}$. so $$ \begin{align*} r(T) \geq \limsup_{n\to \infty}\|T^n\|^{1/n}. \end{align*} $$
