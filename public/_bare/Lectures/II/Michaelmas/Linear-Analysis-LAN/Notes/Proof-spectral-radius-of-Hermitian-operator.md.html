Proof 1: using formula

Note $\|T^2\| = \|T^\ast T\|$${}={}$$\|T\|^2$, so by induction $\|T^{2^n}\| = \|T\|^{2^n}$.

Hence the spectral radius formula says $$ \begin{align*} r(T) = \lim_{n\to \infty} \|T^{2^n}\|^{1/2^n} = \|T\|. \end{align*} $$

Proof 2: using approximate eigenvalues

WLOG assume $\|T\| = 1$ by replacing $T$ with $T/\|T\|$. We want to show either $+1$ or $-1$ is an approximate eigenvalue of $T$.

From the definition of $\|T\| = 1$, take $(x_n)_{n\in \N}$ in $H$ such that $\|x_n\| = 1$ and $\|Tx_n\| \to \|T\| = 1$.

Now $$ \begin{align*} \|(\id - T^2)x_n\|^2 ={}& \langle (\id - T^2)x_n, (\id - T^2)x_n\rangle \\ ={}& \|x_n\|^2 - \langle x_n, T^2 x_n\rangle - \langle T^2 x_n, x_n\rangle + \|T^2 x_n\|^2 \\ \leq{}& 1 - \langle Tx_n, Tx_n\rangle - \langle Tx_n, Tx_n\rangle + \|T\|^2 \|Tx_n\|^2 \\ ={}& 1 - \|Tx_n\|^2 \to 0 \end{align*} $$ so $(\id - T)(\id + T)x_n = (\id - T^2) x_n \to 0$.

Two cases:

  • If $(\id + T)x_n\to 0$, then $-1$ is an approximate eigenvalue of $T$.

  • Otherwise, there exists $ε>0$ and a subsequence $n_1 < n_2 < \ldots$ such that $\|(\id + T)x_{n_k}\| > ε$ for all $k\in \N$.

    Define $$ \begin{align*} y_k := \frac{(\id + T)x_{n_k}}{\|(\id + T)x_{n_k}\|}. \end{align*} $$ Then $\|y_k\| = 1$ and $$ \begin{align*} \left\|(\id - T)y_k\right\| ={}& \frac{\|(\id - T)(\id + T)x_{n_k}\|}{\|(\id + T)x_{n_k}\|} < \frac{1}{ε} \|(\id - T)(\id + T)x_{n_k}\| \to 0 \end{align*} $$ so $+1$ is an approximate eigenvalue of $T$.
