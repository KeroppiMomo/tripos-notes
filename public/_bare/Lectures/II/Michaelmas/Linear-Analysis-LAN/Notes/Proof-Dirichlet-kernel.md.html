$\sum_{n=-N}^{N} e^{int} = \sin(N+\frac{1}{2})t / \sin \frac{1}{2}t$ follows from the geometric sum formula: $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n=-N}^{N} e^{int} ={}& \frac{e^{-iNt} (e^{i(2N+1)t} - 1)}{e^{it} - 1} \\ ={}& \frac{e^{i(N+\frac{1}{2})t} - e^{-i(N+\frac{1}{2})t}}{e^{\frac{1}{2}it} - e^{-\frac{1}{2}it}} \\ ={}& \frac{\sin (N+\frac{1}{2})t}{\sin \frac{1}{2}t}. \end{align*} $$

Note $D_N(f)$ is even. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} T_N(f) = \frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} f(s) D_N(s) \d s. \end{align*} $$ Clearly $S_N$ is linear.

For boundedness, note $$ \begin{align*} \|T_N(f)\| \leq \frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} |f(s)||D_N(s)| \d s \leq \|f\|_\infty \cdot \frac{1}{2π} \int_{-π}^{π} |D_N(s)| \d s \end{align*} $$ and the integral is finite.

To show $\|T_N(f)\| \geq \frac{1}{2π} \int_{-π}^{π} |D_N(s)| \d s$, take $f$ to be a continuous approximation of $\sign D_N$.

To show $\int_{-π}^{π} |D_N(s)| \d s$, note on $[\frac{2k}{2N+1}π, \frac{2(k+1)}{2N+1}π]$, $|\sin(\frac{2N+1}{2} θ)| \geq \sqrt{2}/2$ half the time, and $|\sin(θ)/2| \leq |θ/2| \leq C \frac{k}{N}$. So $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2π} \int_{-π}^{π} |D_N(θ)| \d θ \geq C\cdot \log N \to \infty. \end{align*} $$
