Suppose $σ(T) = \emptyset$. Then the resolvent function $R_T: \C \to \mathcal{B}(X)$ is holomorphic and defined on the entire $\C$. Moreover for $λ>\|T\|$, $$ \begin{align*} \|R_T(λ)\| ={}& \left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{T^n}{λ^{n+1}}\right\| \\ \leq{}& \frac{1}{λ} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left\|\frac{T}{λ}\right\|^n \\ ={}& \frac{1}{λ(1 - \|T/λ\|)} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{λ - \|T\|} \to 0 \end{align*} $$ as $|λ| \to \infty$. This also shows $R_T(λ)$ is bounded.

General case, using Hahn-Banach

Take any $ϕ\in \mathcal{B}(X)^\ast$, and let $f = ϕ \circ R_T: \C \to \C$. $f$ is entire: by continuity of $ϕ$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{f(μ) - f(λ)}{μ - λ} = ϕ\left(\frac{R_T(μ) - R_T(λ)}{μ - λ}\right) \to ϕ(R_T'(λ)) \qquad \text{as }μ \to λ, \end{align*} $$ and $f(λ) \to 0$ as $|λ|\to \infty$: $$ \begin{align*} |f(λ)| = |ϕ(R_T(λ))| \leq \|ϕ\| \|R_T(λ)\| \to 0 \qquad \text{as }|λ| \to \infty \end{align*} $$ so in particular $f(λ)$ is bounded as $f$ is continuous.

Hence by Liouville's theorem(!), $f = 0$. Thus for each $λ\in \C$, $ϕ(R_T(λ)) = 0$ for all $ϕ\in \mathcal{B}(X)^\ast$. By Hahn-Banach, $R_T(λ) = 0$, which is not invertible. Contradiction.

If $X=H$ is a Hilbert space

Take any $x,y\in H$ and define $$ \begin{align*} f: \C &\to \C \\ λ &\mapsto \langle R_T(λ)x, y\rangle. \end{align*} $$

$f$ is entire: by continuity of $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{f(μ) - f(λ)}{μ - λ} = \left\langle \frac{R_T(μ) - R_T(λ)}{μ - λ}x, y\right\rangle \to \langle R_T'(λ)x, y\rangle \qquad \text{as }μ\to λ, \end{align*} $$ and $f(λ)\to 0$ as $|λ|\to \infty$: by Cauchy-Schwarz $$ \begin{align*} |f(λ)| \leq \|R_T(λ) x\| \|y\| \leq \|R_T(λ)\| \|x\| \|y\| \to 0 \qquad \text{as }|λ|\to \infty \end{align*} $$ so in particular $f(λ)$ is bounded as $f$ is continuous.

Hence by Liouville's theorem, $f = 0$. Thus for each $λ\in \C$, $\langle R_T(λ) x , y\rangle =0$ for all $x,y\in H$ so $R_T(λ) = 0$, which is not invertible. Contradiction.
