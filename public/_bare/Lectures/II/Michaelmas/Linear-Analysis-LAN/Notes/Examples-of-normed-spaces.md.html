Sequence spaces

See $l_p$ spaces.

  • $c := \sb{x\in l_\infty}{x_n \text{ converges}}$, a complete subspace of $l_\infty$.

  • $c_0 := \sb{x\in l_\infty}{x_n\to 0}$, a subspace of $c$ isomorphic to $c$.

  • $c_{00} := \sb{x\in l_\infty}{x_0 \text{ is eventually all zero}}$, a proper dense subspace of $c_0$.

$l_p$ (for $1\leq p<\infty$) and $c_0$ are complete and separable. $l_\infty$ is not complete nor separable.

There is an isometric embedding $$ \begin{align*} l_\infty &\to \mathcal{B}(l_2) \\ a_n &\mapsto \left((x_n) \mapsto (a_n x_n)\right). \end{align*} $$

Function spaces

See $C(K)$ spaces.

By Stone-Weierstrass, $\set{\text{polynomials}}$ and $\set{\text{even polynomials}}$ are dense subspace of $C[0,1]$.

$C(K)$ is separable if $K$ is a compact metric space.

$C_c^\infty(\R)$ is the space of compactly supported infinitely differentiable $\R\to \R$ functions. This has two inequivalent norms, $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ and $\|\dd{x}\cdot \|_\infty$.
