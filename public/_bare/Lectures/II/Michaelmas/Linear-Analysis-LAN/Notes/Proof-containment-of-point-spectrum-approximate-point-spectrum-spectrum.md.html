Eigenvalue is approximate eigenvalue

If $λ$ is an eigenvalue of $T$ with an eigenvector $x\neq 0$, then setting $x_n = x/\|x\|$, we have $\|x_n \|= 1$ and $(λ \id - T)x_n = 0\to 0$ as $n\to \infty$. So $λ$ is an approximate eigenvalue.

$λ$ is in the resolvent set $\implies$ $λ$ is not an approximate eigenvalue

Suppose $λ \id - T$ is invertible, and let $(x_n)$ be a sequence in $X$ such that $\|x_n\| = 1$.

$λ\id - T$ is bounded below: $$ \begin{align*} &1 = \|x_n\| = \|(λ\id - T)\inv (λ\id - T)x_n\| \leq \|(λ\id - T)\inv\| \|(λ\id - T)x_n\| \\ \implies & \|(λ\id - T)x_n\| \geq \frac{1}{\|(λ\id - T)\inv\|} \end{align*} $$ for all $n$. Thus $(λ\id - T)x_n \not\to 0$.
