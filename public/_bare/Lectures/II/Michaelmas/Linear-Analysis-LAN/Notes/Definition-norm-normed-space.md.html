Let $X$ be a vector space over $\mathbb{F} = \R$ or $\C$.

A norm on $X$ is a function $\|\cdot \|\to \R$ satisfying

  1. positivity: $\|x\| \geq 0$ for all $x\in X$, and $\|x\| = 0$ iff $x = 0$;

  2. homogeneity: $\|λx\| = |λ| \, \|x\|$ for all $λ\in \mathbb{F}$, $x\in X$; and

  3. triangle inequality: $\|x + y\| \leq \|x\| + \|y\|$ for all $x,y\in X$.

A normed space is a pair $(X, \|\cdot \|)$ where $X$ is a vector space and $\|\cdot \|$ is a norm on $X$.

The norm induces a metric $d(x,y) = \|x - y\|$ and hence a norm topology on $X$.

$x\mapsto \|x\|$ is a 1-Lipschitz function, thus continuous.
