Definition (invertible bounded linear map) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a normed space.

$T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$ is said to be invertible if equivalently

  1. $T$ is invertible in the unital algebra $\mathcal{B}(X)$;

  2. there exists $S\in \mathcal{B}(X)$ such that $TS = ST = \id_X$;

  3. $T$ is a linear bijection and $T\inv$ is bounded, i.e. $T$ is an isomorphism of normed spaces.

Write $\mathcal{G}(X)$ for the group of invertible bounded linear operators $X\to X$.

If $X$ is a Banach space, then the inversion theorem says $T$ is invertible iff $T$ is a linear bijection.

Interaction with compact and finite-rank operators:

  • If $\dim X < \infty$, then $\mathcal{G}(X) \subseteq \mathcal{F}(X) = \mathcal{K}(X)$.

  • If $\dim X = \infty$, then $\mathcal{G}(X) \cap \mathcal{K}(X) = \emptyset$. (Proof)

Theorem (invertible operators as open subset and topological group) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a Banach space.

  1. Let $T\in \mathcal{G}(X)$ and $S\in \mathcal{B}(X)$. If $\|S\|<1/\|T\inv\|$, then $S - T\in \mathcal{G}(X)$.

    Therefore $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is an open subset of $\mathcal{B}(X)$.

    In particular, if $\|T\|<1$, then $$ \begin{align*} (\id - T)\inv = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} T^n, \qquad \|(\id - T)\inv\| \leq \frac{1}{1 - \|T\|}. \end{align*} $$

  2. $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is a topological group, i.e. composition and inverse are continuous.

  3. If $(T_n)_{n\in \N}$ is a sequence in $\mathcal{G}(X)$ such that $T_n \to T\in \mathcal{B}(X)\setminus \mathcal{G}(X)$, then $\|T_n\inv\|\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$.

Proof

Definition (spectrum, resolvent set, resolvent function) open_in_new

Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

The spectrum of $T$ is $$ \begin{align*} σ(T) = \sb{λ\in \C}{λ \id - T\not\in \mathcal{G}(X)}. \end{align*} $$

The resolvent set is $ρ(T) = \C \setminus σ(T)$. The resolvent function is $$ \begin{align*} R_T: ρ(T) &\to \mathcal{B}(X) \\ λ &\mapsto (λ \id - T)\inv. \end{align*} $$

  • $σ(T)$ is a closed subset of $\sb{z\in \C}{|z|\leq \|T\|}$, hence compact. (Proof)

  • If $|λ| > \|T\|$, then we saw $$ \begin{align*} R_T(λ) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{T^n}{λ^{n+1}}. \end{align*} $$

  • $R_T$ is a holomorphic function, in particular $\lim_{μ\to λ} \frac{R_T(μ) - R_T(λ)}{μ - λ} = -R_T(λ)^2$. (Proof)

Theorem (spectrum is non-empty) open_in_new

Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

Then $σ(T) \neq \emptyset$.

Proof

Definition (point spectrum, approximate point spectrum) open_in_new

Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

The point spectrum of $T$ is $$ \begin{align*} σ_\text{p}(T) = \set{\text{eigenvaues of $T$}} = \sb{λ\in \C}{λ\id - T \text{ is not injective}}. \end{align*} $$

$λ\in \C$ is an approximate eigenvalue of $T$ if there exists a sequence $(x_n)_{n\in \N}$ in $X$ (an approximate eigenvector) such that $\|x_n\| = 1$ and $(λ\id - T) x_n\to 0$ as $n\to \infty$. That is, $λ\id - T$ is not bounded below, i.e. there does not exist $C>0$ such that $\|(λ\id - T)x\| \geq C\|x\|$ for all $x\in X$.

The approximate point spectrum of $T$ is $$ \begin{align*} σ_\text{ap}(T) = \set{\text{approximate eigenvalues of $T$}}. \end{align*} $$

$σ_\text{p}(T) \subseteq σ_\text{ap}(T) \subseteq{}$$σ(T)$. (Proof)

If $\dim X < \infty$, then $σ_\text{p}(T) = σ_\text{ap}(T) = σ(T)$.

$\partial$$σ(T) \subseteq σ_\text{ap}(T)$. In particular $σ_{\text{ap}}(T)\neq \emptyset$. (Proof)

Theorem (spectral mapping theorem) open_in_new

Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

For any complex polynomial $p(z) = a_0 + a_1 z + \ldots + a_d z^d$, the spectrum of $p(T) \equiv a_0 \id + a_1 T + \ldots + a_d T^d$ is the image of the spectrum of $T$ under $p$: $$ \begin{align*} σ(p(T)) = p(σ(T)). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (spectral radius formula) open_in_new

Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

Define the spectral radius of $T$ as $r(T) = \sup{} \{|λ|\ |\ λ\in{}$$σ(T)$$\}$. Then $$ \begin{align*} r(T) = \lim_{n\to \infty} \|T^n\|^{1/n}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (non-zero approximate eigenvalue of compact operator is eigenvalue) open_in_new

Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

Suppose $T$ is compact. If $λ$ is a non-zero approximate eigenvalue of $T$, then $λ$ is an eigenvalue of $T$, i.e. $σ_\text{ap}(T) \subseteq σ_\text{p}(T) \cup \set{0}$.

Proof

In fact $σ(λ) \subseteq σ_\text{p}(λ) \cup \set{0}$. (Proof from Wikipedia)

Definition (invariant subspace) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a vector space, $Y$ be a subspace and $T: X \to X$ be a linear map.

$Y$ is an invariant subspace of $T$ if $T(Y) \subseteq Y$.

  • $Y = \set{0}$ and $X$ are the trivial invariant subspaces.

  • If $λ$ is an eigenvalue of $T$, then the eigenspace $E_T(λ) = \ker(λ\id - T)$ is an invariant subspace of $T$. If $X$ is normed, then $E_T(λ)$ is closed.

Proposition (spectrum of adjoint) open_in_new

Let $H\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Hilbert space.

For any $T\in \mathcal{B}(X)$, the spectrum of its adjoint is $$ \begin{align*} σ(T^\ast) = \sb{\overline{λ}}{λ\in σ(T)}. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (spectrum of Hermitian operators is real) open_in_new

Theorem (spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators) open_in_new

Let $H\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Hilbert space.

Suppose $T\in \mathcal{B}(H)$ is a compact Hermitian operator.

  1. If $λ\in{}$$σ(T)$ and $λ\neq 0$, then $λ$ is an eigenvalue of $T$, and the eigenspace $E_T(λ) = \ker(λ\id - T)$ is finite-dimensional.

  2. $σ(T) \setminus \set{0}$ is a sequence $λ_1, λ_2, \ldots$ which satisfies $|λ_1| \geq |λ_2| \geq \ldots$ and either is finite or converges to 0.

    In particular, $σ(T)$ is countable, and $σ(T) \cap \sb{z\in \C}{|z|\geq δ}$ is finite for all $δ>0$.

  3. There exists an orthonormal basis $e_1, e_2, \ldots$ for $(\ker T)^\perp$ and $μ_1, μ_2, \ldots\in \R$ satisfying $|μ_1| \geq |μ_2| \geq \ldots$ and either is finite or converges to 0 such that $$ \begin{align*} T\left(z +\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} x_n e_n\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} μ_n x_n e_n, \qquad z\in \ker T. \end{align*} $$

    Equivalently, writing $P_n$ for the orthogonal projection onto $E_T(λ_n)$, $$ \begin{align*} T = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} λ_n P_n. \end{align*} $$
