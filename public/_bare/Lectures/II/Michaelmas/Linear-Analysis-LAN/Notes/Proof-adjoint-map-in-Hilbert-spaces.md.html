Uniqueness

Suppose $S_1, S_2: H\to H$ are functions such that for all $x,y\in H$, $$ \begin{align*} \langle x, S_1 y\rangle = \langle Tx, y\rangle = \langle x, S_2y\rangle \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \langle x, S_1 y - S_2 y\rangle = 0. \end{align*} $$ Setting $x = S_1 y - S_2 y$, we see $S_1 y - S_2 y = 0$ for all $y\in H$. Hence $S_1 = S_2$.

Existence

Fix $y\in H$.

The linear function $$ \begin{align*} f: H &\to \mathbb{F} \\ x &\mapsto \langle Tx, y\rangle \end{align*} $$ is bounded, since $|\langle Tx, y\rangle| \leq \|Tx\| \|y\| \leq \|T\|\|y\|\|x\|$ by Cauchy-Schwarz. Therefore $f\in H^\ast$ with $\|f\| \leq \|T\|\|y\|$.

Since $H$ is a Hilbert space, Riesz representation theorem says there exists $T^\ast y\in H$ such that $\langle Tx, y\rangle = f(x) = \langle x, T^\ast y\rangle$ and $\|T^\ast y\| = \|f\|$. This defines a function $T^\ast: H\to H$.

$T^\ast$ is bounded linear

Apply bilinearity of $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle$: for all $x,y,z\in H$, $λ\in \mathbb{F}$, $$ \begin{align*} \langle x, T^\ast(λy + z)\rangle ={}& \langle Tx, λy+z\rangle \\ ={}& \overline{λ} \langle Tx, y \rangle + \langle Tx, z\rangle \\ ={}& \overline{λ} \langle x, T^\ast y\rangle + \langle x, T^\ast z\rangle \\ ={}& \langle x, λ T^\ast y + T^\ast z\rangle. \end{align*} $$ This holds for all $x\in H$, so by uniqueness, $T^\ast(λy + z) = λ T^\ast y + T^\ast z$.

$T^\ast$ is bounded, since $\|T^\ast y\| = \|f\| \leq \|T\|\|y\|$ so $\|T^\ast\| \leq \|T\|$.
