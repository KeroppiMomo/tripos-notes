Riemann-Lebesgue lemma

Let $f\in C(\mathbb{T})$, and we need to show $\hat f_n \to 0$ as $|n|\to \infty$.

Fix $ε>0$. By the Fejér kernel or the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, find a trigonometric function $g(t) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} a_n e^{int}$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \|f - g\|_{\infty} < ε. \end{align*} $$

Note a simple calculation shows $$ \begin{align*} \hat g_n = \begin{cases} a_n, &\text{if $-N<n<N$}, \\ 0, &\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Moreover observing that $|\hat h_n| \leq \frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} |h(t) e^{-int}| \d t \leq \|h\|_\infty$, we have $$ \begin{align*} |\hat f_n - \hat g_n| \leq \|f - g\|_\infty < ε \end{align*} $$ so $|\hat f_n| < ε$ for all $|n|>N$.

Bounded linear map

Linearity is clear.

We just observed $|\hat f_n| \leq \|f\|_\infty$, so $\|\mathcal{F}\| \leq 1$. Setting $f = 1$ shows $\|\mathcal{F}\| = 1$.

Injectivity

Suppose $f\in C(\mathbb{T})$ has $\hat f_n = 0$ for all $n\in \Z$. Fix $ε>0$ and let $g(t) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} a_n e^{int}$ be a trigonometric polynomial with $\|f - g\|_\infty < ε$.

Then on one hand $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2π} \int_{-π}^{π} f\bar g \d t = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} \bar a_n\cdot \underbrace{\frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} f(t) e^{-int} \d t}_{\hat f_n} = 0 \end{align*} $$ and on the other hand $$ \begin{align*} \left|\frac{1}{2π} \int_{-π}^{π} f\bar g \d t - \frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} |f|^2 \d t\right| \leq{}& \frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} |f||\bar g - \bar f| \d t \leq \frac{ε \|f\|_\infty}{2π}. \end{align*} $$ Since $ε>0$ is arbitrary, we get $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2π}\int_{-π}^{π} |f|^2 \d t= 0 \end{align*} $$ and since $f$ is continuous, we have $f = 0$.

Failure of surjectivity

Suppose $\mathcal{F}$ is surjective, so a continuous linear bijection. Since $C(\mathbb{T})$ and $c_0(\Z)$ are Banach spaces, by the inversion theorem, $\mathcal{F}\inv$ is continuous.

However, consider $a^{(k)}\in c_0(\Z)$ given by $$ \begin{align*} a_n^{(k)} := \begin{cases} 1, &|n|\leq k, \\ 0, &\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ which has norm $1$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F}\inv(a^{(k)}) = \sum_{n=-k}^{k} e^{int} \end{align*} $$ which attains $2k+1$ at $t=0$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \|\mathcal{F}\inv\| \geq 2k+1 \end{align*} $$ for all $k$, contradicting boundedness.
