Let $X$ be a finite-dimensional vector space. Choose a basis $e_1, \ldots, e_n$ for $X$. Define the norm $\|\cdot \|_1$ by $$ \begin{align*} \left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n} x_ke_k\right\|_1 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} |x_k|. \end{align*} $$ It suffices to show that any other norm $\|\cdot \|'$ is equivalent to $\|\cdot \|_1$.

$\id: (X, \|\cdot\|_1) \to (X, \|\cdot\|')$ is continuous

By triangle inequality and homogeneity, for $x = \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k e_k$, $$ \begin{align*} \|x\|' \leq \sum_{k=1}^{n} |x_k| \|e_k\|' \leq M\cdot \|x\|_1 \end{align*} $$ where $M = \max\{\|e_1\|', \|e_2\|', \ldots, \|e_n\|'\}$. Therefore $\id: (X, \|\cdot\|_1) \to (X, \|\cdot\|')$ is bounded.

$S := \sb{x\in X}{\|x\|_1 = 1}$ is compact in $(X, \|\cdot\|_1)$

Let $(x^{(r)})_{r\in \N}$ be a sequence in $S$.

For each $k$, $(x_k^{(r)})$ is a sequence in $\R$. By repeated application of Bolzano-Weierstrass, extract a subsequence $r_1<r_2< \ldots$ such that $(x_k^{(r_l)})_{l\in \N}$ is convergent for each $k$. Let $x_k = \lim_{l\to \infty} x_k^{(r_l)}$.

Then $x\in S$ since $$ \begin{align*} \|x\|_1 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} |x_k| = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \lim_{l\to \infty} |x_k^{(r_l)}| = \lim_{l\to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^{n} |x_k^{(r_l)}| = \lim_{l\to \infty} \|x^{(r_l)}\|_1 = 1 \end{align*} $$ and $x^{(r_l)}\to x$ in $(X, \|\cdot\|_1)$ since $$ \begin{align*} \lim_{l\to \infty} \|x^{(r_l)} - x\|_1 = \lim_{l\to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^{n} |x^{(r_l)}_k - x_k| = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \lim_{l\to \infty} |x_k^{(r_l)} - x_k| = \sum_{k=1}^{n} 0 = 0. \end{align*} $$ We could exchange limits as all sums are finite.

Therefore $S$ is sequentially compact, thus compact.

$\id: (X, \|\cdot\|') \to (X, \|\cdot\|_1)$ is continuous

$x\mapsto \|x\|'$ for $x\in S$ is continuous as a composition of inclusion $S \hookrightarrow (X, \|\cdot\|_1)$ and $\id: (X, \|\cdot\|_1) \to (X, \|\cdot\|')$, which are both continuous.

As $S$ is compact, $c_0 = \inf_{x\in S} \|x\|'$ is attained, at say $x_0\in S$. Since $x_0\neq 0$, $c_0 = \|x_0\|' > 0$. Therefore for any $x\in S$, $$ \begin{align*} \|x\|' \geq c = c\|x\|_1 \end{align*} $$ and by homogeneity, this holds for all $x\in X$.
