Existence

Since $d(x,Y) \equiv \inf\sb{\|x-y\|}{y\in Y}$, let $(y_n)_{n\in \N}$ be a sequence in $Y$ such that $\|x - y_n\| \to d(x,Y)$.

$(y_n)_{n\in \N}$ is Cauchy: by the parallelogram law, $$ \begin{align*} \|y_n - y_m\|^2 ={}& \|(x-y_m) - (x-y_n)\|^2 \\ ={}& 2\|x-y_m\|^2 + 2\|x - y_n\|^2 - \|2x - y_m - y_n\|^2 \\ ={}& 2\|x-y_m\| + 2\|x - y_n\| - 4\,\biggl\|x - \underbrace{\frac{y_m + y_n}{2}}_{\in Y}\biggr\| \\ \leq{}& 2\|x - y_m\| + 2\|x - y_n\| - 4 \, d(x,Y) \\ \to {}& 2\,d(x,Y) + 2\, d(x,Y) - 4\,d(x,Y) = 0 \end{align*} $$ so $\|y_n - y_m\| \to 0$ as $n,m\to \infty$.

As $Y$ is closed in a complete space $H$, $Y$ is complete, so $y_n$ converges in $Y$ to say $y\in Y$.

Now by continuity of $\|\cdot\|$, $$ \begin{align*} \|x - y\| = \lim_{n\to \infty} \|x - y_n\| = d(x,Y). \end{align*} $$

Uniqueness

Suppose $z\in Y$ also has $\|x - z\| = d(x,Y)$. By the parallelogram law, $$ \begin{align*} \|y-z\|^2 ={}& 2\|x-z\|^2 + 2\|x-y\|^2 - \|2x - y - z\|^2 \\ ={}& 2\|x-z\|^2 + 2\|x-y\|^2 - 4\biggl\|x - \underbrace{\frac{y + z}{2}}_{\in Y}\biggr\|^2 \\ \leq{}& 2\,d(x,Y) + 2\,d(x,Y) - 4\,d(x,Y) = 0 \end{align*} $$ so $y = z$.
