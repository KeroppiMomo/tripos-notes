Suppose $T$ is compact and let $λ\neq 0$.

$\ker (λ\id - T)$ is finite-dimensional

Let $N_λ := \ker(λ\id - T)$, which is closed hence a Banach space. Then $T|_{N_λ}: N_λ \to N_λ$ and is still compact. But this is given by $x\mapsto λx$, so $\overline{TB_{N_λ}} = λB_{N_λ}$ is compact. Therefore $N_λ$ is finite-dimensional.

$\im(λ \id - T)$ is closed

Since $X$ is Banach and $\ker(λ\id - T)$ is finite-dimensional, there is a closed subspace $M$ such that $X = \ker(λ\id - T) \oplus M$. Then $(λ\id - T)|_M: M\to \im(λ\id - T)$ is a continuous linear bijection.

If $\im(λ\id - T)$ is not closed, then $(λ \id - T)|_M$ cannot be an isomorphism, meaning there is a sequence $(x_n)_{n\in \N}$ in $M$ with $\|x_n\| = 1$ such that $(λ\id - T)(x_n) \to 0$. As with the previous proof, As $T$ is compact, extract a subsequence $(x_{n_k})_k$ such that $Tx_{n_k} \to x$ for some $x\in X$. This implies $$ \begin{align*} λx_{n_k} = \underbrace{(λ\id - T)x_{n_k}}_{0} + \underbrace{Tx_{n_k}}_{x} \to x \end{align*} $$ so $x\in M$. However from $(λ\id - T)(x_{n_k})\to 0$, we see $x\in \ker (λ\id - T)$, so $x\in M \cap \ker(λ \id - T) = \set{0}$. This contradicts with $\|x_{n_k}\| = 1$.

If $λ\id - T$ is injective, then $λ\id - T$ is surjective

The result follows form this claim, because a continuous linear bijection is an isomorphism, so if $λ$ is not an eigenvalue, then $λ$ is not in the spectrum.

Define $$ \begin{align*} Y_n := \im (λ\id - T)^n. \end{align*} $$ Suppose $λ \id - T$ is not surjective, so $Y_1$ is a proper closed subspace of $X$ (so a Banach space). Then by induction, $Y_n = \im ((λ\id - T)|_{Y_{n-1}})$ is closed (because $T|_{Y_{n-1}}$ is still compact) and proper in $Y_{n-1}$ (because $λ\id - T$ is injective: $Y_{n-2}\hookrightarrow Y_{n-1}$ and $Y_{n-1} \hookrightarrow Y_n$). Therefore we form a strictly descending chain of closed subspaces $$ \begin{align*} X \supsetneq Y_1 \supsetneq Y_2 \supsetneq \ldots. \end{align*} $$

Now apply Riesz's lemma: let $y_n\in Y_n$ be such that $d(y_n, Y_{n+1}) \geq |λ|/2$. We claim that $\|Ty_n - Ty_m\| \geq 1/2$ for all $n\neq m$, contradicting with the compactness of $T$.

Indeed, suppose $m<n$. We have $$ \begin{align*} \|Ty_n - Ty_m\| ={}& \|\underbrace{(λ\id - T)y_m}_{\in Y_{m+1}} - \underbrace{(λ\id - T)y_n}_{\in Y_{n+1}} + \underbrace{λy_n}_{\in Y_n} - \underbrace{λy_m}_{\in Y_m}\| \geq |λ|/2. \end{align*} $$ becauase by construction, $d(y_m, Y_{m+1}) \geq 1/2$, and the first three terms are in $Y_{m+1}$.
