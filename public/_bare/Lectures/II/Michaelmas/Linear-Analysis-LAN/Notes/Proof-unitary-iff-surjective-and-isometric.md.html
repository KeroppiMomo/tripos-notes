Unitary $\implies$ surjective

By definition, a unitary operator is invertible.

Unitary $\implies$ isometric

If $T$ is unitary, then $$ \begin{align*} \|Tx\|^2 = \langle Tx, Tx\rangle = \langle x, T^\ast T x\rangle = \langle x, x\rangle = \|x\|^2 \end{align*} $$ for all $x\in H$.

Isometric and surjective $\implies$ unitary

Suppose $\|Tx\| = \|x\|$ for all $x\in H$.

By the polarisation identities, $$ \begin{align*} \langle x,y\rangle = \langle Tx, Ty \rangle = \langle x, T^\ast T y\rangle \end{align*} $$ for all $x,y\in H$. Thus $T^\ast T = \id_H$.

As $T$ is isometric, it is injective. If we assume $T$ is surjective, $T$ is linear bijective. By the inversion theorem, $T$ is invertible.
