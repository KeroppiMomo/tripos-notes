Let $X\neq \set{0}$ be a complex Banach space and $T\in{}$$\mathcal{B}(X)$.

The point spectrum of $T$ is $$ \begin{align*} σ_\text{p}(T) = \set{\text{eigenvaues of $T$}} = \sb{λ\in \C}{λ\id - T \text{ is not injective}}. \end{align*} $$

$λ\in \C$ is an approximate eigenvalue of $T$ if there exists a sequence $(x_n)_{n\in \N}$ in $X$ (an approximate eigenvector) such that $\|x_n\| = 1$ and $(λ\id - T) x_n\to 0$ as $n\to \infty$. That is, $λ\id - T$ is not bounded below, i.e. there does not exist $C>0$ such that $\|(λ\id - T)x\| \geq C\|x\|$ for all $x\in X$.

The approximate point spectrum of $T$ is $$ \begin{align*} σ_\text{ap}(T) = \set{\text{approximate eigenvalues of $T$}}. \end{align*} $$

$σ_\text{p}(T) \subseteq σ_\text{ap}(T) \subseteq{}$$σ(T)$. (Proof)

If $\dim X < \infty$, then $σ_\text{p}(T) = σ_\text{ap}(T) = σ(T)$.

$\partial$$σ(T) \subseteq σ_\text{ap}(T)$. In particular $σ_{\text{ap}}(T)\neq \emptyset$. (Proof)
