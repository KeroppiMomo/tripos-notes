Let $X,Y,Z$ be normed spaces.

The (external) direct sum $Y\oplus Z$ of $Y$ and $Z$ is the algebraic direct sum of the vector spaces $Y$ and $Z$ equipped with the norm $$ \begin{align*} \|(y,z)\| = \|y\| + \|z\|. \end{align*} $$

Suppose $Y$ and $Z$ are subspaces of $X$. We say $X$ is the (topological) direct sum of $Y$ and $Z$, written $X = Y \oplus Z$, if $X = Y + Z$, $Y \cap Z = \set{0}$, and $$ \begin{align*} T: Y \oplus Z &\to X \\ (y, z) &\mapsto y + z \end{align*} $$ is an isomorphism of normed spaces, i.e. there exists $C>0$ such that $\|y\| + \|z\| \leq C\|y + z\|$.

  • The topology on $Y \oplus Z$ is the product of the topologies on $Y$ and $Z$.
