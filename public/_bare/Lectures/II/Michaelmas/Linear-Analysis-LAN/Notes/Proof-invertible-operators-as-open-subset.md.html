$I - T$ is invertible if $\|T\|<1$

Since $\|T^n\| \leq \|T\|^n$ and $\|T\|<1$, $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} T^n$ converges absolutely, hence (as $\mathcal{B}(X)$ is complete) converges. Let $S = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} T^n$.

Now $$ \begin{align*} ST = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T^n = TS \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} S(\id - T) = \id = (\id - T)S \end{align*} $$ and thus $(\id - T)\inv = S$.

Moreover $$ \begin{align*} \|S\| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \|T^n\| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \|T\|^n = \frac{1}{1-\|T\|}. \end{align*} $$

$S - T$ is invertible if $\|T\|<\frac{1}{\|S\|\inv}$

Suppose $S\in \mathcal{G}(X)$ and $T\in \mathcal{B}(X)$ are such that $\|T\| < 1/\|S\inv\|$. Now $$ \begin{align*} \|S\inv T\| \leq \|S\inv\|\|T\| < 1 \end{align*} $$ so the above shows that $\id - S\inv T$ is invertible. Hence $S - T = S(\id - S\inv T)$ is invertible, with inverse $$ \begin{align*} (\id - S\inv T)\inv S\inv = \Bigl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (S\inv T)^n\Bigr) S\inv. \end{align*} $$

Hence for each $S\in \mathcal{G}(X)$, the ball of radius $1/\|S\inv\|$ is contained within $\mathcal{G}(X)$, and thus $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is open.

$(S,T) \mapsto ST$ is continuous

$$ \begin{align*} \|S'T' - ST\|\leq \|(S' - S)T' + S(T' - T)\| \leq \|S' - S\| \|T\| + \|S\| \|T - T\|. \end{align*} $$

So for $ε>0$, taking $\|S'-S\| < \frac{ε}{2\|T\|}$ and $\|T' - T\| < \frac{ε}{2\|S\|}$ makes $\|S'T' - ST\| < ε$.

$T \mapsto T\inv$ is continuous

Fix $T\in \mathcal{G}(X)$. For $S\in \mathcal{G}(X)$, $$ \begin{align*} \|T\inv - S\inv\| = \|T\inv(S - T)S\inv\| = \|T\inv\|\|S\inv\|\|T - S\|. \tag{1} \end{align*} $$ Also $$ \begin{align*} \|S\inv\| \leq \|T\inv\| + \|T\inv - S\inv\| \leq \|T\inv\| + \|T\inv\|\|S\inv\|\|T - S\|. \end{align*} $$ So taking $\|T - S\| \leq 1/2\|T\inv\|$, $$ \begin{align*} \|S\inv\|\leq \|T\inv\| + \frac{1}{2}\|S\inv\| \implies \|S\inv\| \leq 2\|T\inv\|. \end{align*} $$

Substituting into $(1)$, $$ \begin{align*} \|T\inv - S\inv\| \leq 2\|T\inv\|^2 \|T - S\| \to 0 \qquad \text{as }S\to T. \end{align*} $$

$T_n \to T\not\in \mathcal{G}(X)$ $\implies$ $\|T_n\inv\|\to \infty$

By the contrapositive of part 1, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{\|T_n\inv\|} \leq \|T_n - T\| \to 0 \end{align*} $$ so $\|T_n\inv\| \to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$.
