Definition (curve on manifold, regular, closed, simple) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a smooth manifold.

A (smooth) curve is a smooth function $α: I\to X$ where $I\subseteq \R$ is an interval (so $(a,b), [a,b], (a,b], [a,b)$ where $a,b$ can be $\pm \infty$).

We say $α$ is regular if $α$ is an immersion, i.e. $α'(t) \neq 0$ for all $t\in I$.

We say $α$ is closed if $I = [a,b]$ is a closed interval and $α^{(k)}(a) = α^{(k)}(b)$ for all $k=0,1,2,\ldots$.

We say $α$ is simple if whenever $α(t) = α(t')$, $t$ and $t'$ are at the endpoints of $I$.

Definition (arc length of a curve) open_in_new

Let $\v x(t)$ be a curve. For some starting point $t_0$, the arc length $s(t)$ is the (signed) distance from $t_0$ to $t$, $$ s(t) = \int_{t_0}^t |\dv x(t')|\,dt'. $$

The arc length between two points on a curve is independent of the choice of parametrisation. (Proof)

If $\v x$ is regular, then $s'(t) = |\dot{\v x}(t)| > 0$, so $s$ is invertible by the inverse function theorem. Using $s$ to parametrise the curve i.e. $\v x(s)$, the tangent vector has unit norm: $$ \bigg|\dvd xs\bigg| = \frac{|d\v x/dt|}{ds/dt} = 1. $$

Definition (curvature of a curve) open_in_new

Let $\v x(s)$ be a parametrisation by the arc length. The curvature is $$ \kappa(s) = \bigg|\dvd[2]xs\bigg| = \bigg|\dvd ts\bigg| $$ where $\v t = d\v x/ds$ is the unit tangent vector.

Definition (principal normal) open_in_new

Let $\v x(s)$ be a curve, and suppose $κ(s)$${}\neq 0$ at some $s$. The principal normal at a point $\v x(s)$ is $$ \v n = \frac1\kappa\dvd[2]xs = \frac1\kappa\dvd ts. $$ $1/\kappa$ ensures this is a unit vector.

Definition (osculating plane) open_in_new

The plane spanned by $\v t$ and $\v n$ is called the osculating plane.

Definition (binormal) open_in_new

For curves in $\R^3$, define the binormal as $$ \v b = \v t\times\v n. $$

Definition (torsion) open_in_new

For a curve in $\R^3$, the torsion $\tau(s)$ is defined as $$ \dvd bs = -\tau\v n. $$

Derivation that change in b is parallel to n

Proposition (Frenet formulae) open_in_new

Let $α(s)$ be a curve parametrised by arc length. Its tangent $t$, normal $n$ and binormal $b$ satisfy the ordinary differential equation (called the Frenet formulae) $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{t'(s) \\ n'(s) \\ b'(s)} = \pmat{& k(s) & \\ -k(s) & & -τ(s) \\ &τ(s)&}\pmat{t(s) \\ n(s) \\ b(s)} \end{align*} $$ where $k$ is the curvature and $τ$ is the torsion.

The right-handed orthonormal basis $(t, n, b)$ is called the Frenet frame associated to $α$.

Theorem (fundamental theorem of curve in R3) open_in_new

Let $I\subseteq \R$ be an interval and $k, τ: I\to \R$ be smooth functions. Suppose $k>0$.

Given any $s_0\in I$, any $α_0\in \R^3$ and any right-handed orthonormal basis $\set{t_0, n_0, b_0}$ of $\R^3$, there exists a curve $α: I\to \R^3$ such that its curvature is $k$, its torsion is $τ$, $α(s_0) = α_0$, and $\set{t_0, n_0, b_0}$ is the Frenet frame at $s_0$.

Moreover, if $\tilde α: I\to \R^3$ is another curve with curvature $k$ and torsion $τ$, then there exists $R\in{}$$\op{SO}(3)$ and $v\in \R^3$ such that $\tilde α = R \circ α + v$.

Definition (curvature of plane curve) open_in_new

Let $α: I\to \R^2$ be a planar curve, parametrised by arc length. Let $n: I\to \R^2$ be a unit normal vector (i.e. orthogonal to $\dot α$) that varies smoothly.

The (signed) curvature $k_\text{signed}$ is defined that $$ \begin{align*} \ddot α(s) = k_\text{signed}(s) \,n(s). \end{align*} $$

$|k_\text{signed}|$ coincides with the curvature as a curve in $\R^3$.

Theorem (Jordan curve theorem) open_in_new

If $α: I\to \R^2$ is a simple closed curve in $\R^2$, then there exists $U \subset \R^2$ such that $U \cup α(I)$ is a compact manifold with boundary, homeomorphic to the unit disc $D^2$.

Theorem (isoperimetric inequality) open_in_new

Let $α:I\to \R^2$ be a simple closed curve in $\R^2$ and $A$ be the region it bounds. Then $$ \begin{align*} \op{length}(α)^2 \leq 4\pi \op{area}(A) \end{align*} $$ with equality iff $α$ is a circle.
