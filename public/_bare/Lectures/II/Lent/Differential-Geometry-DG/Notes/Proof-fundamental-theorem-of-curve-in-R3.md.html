By ODE theory, the linear system $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{t'(s) \\ n'(s) \\ b'(s)} ={}& \underbrace{\pmat{& k(s) & \\ -k(s) & & -τ(s) \\ &τ(s)&}}_{Ω(s)}\pmat{t(s) \\ n(s) \\ b(s)} \\ \pmat{t(s_0) \\ n(s_0) \\ b(s_0)} ={}& \pmat{t_0 \\ n_0 \\ b_0} \end{align*} \tag{*}$$ has a unique solution on $I$.

We claim $(t(s), n(s), b(s))$ is an orthonormal basis for all $s$. Form the matrix $F = \pmat{t^T \\ n^T \\ b^T}$, so $G := F F^T = \pmat{\langle t, t\rangle & \langle t, n\rangle & \langle t, b\rangle \\ \langle n,t\rangle & \langle n,n\rangle & \langle n, b\rangle \\ \langle b, t\rangle & \langle b, n\rangle & \langle b,b\rangle}$. Since $F' = ΩF$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} G' = F'F^T + F(F')^T = ΩFF^T + FF^T Ω^T = ΩFF^T - FF^TΩ = ΩG - GΩ \\ G(s_0) = I \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ as $Ω^T = -Ω$ and $(t_0, n_0, b_0)$ is an orthonormal basis.

Note $G(s) = I$ constant is a solution, so by uniqueness, $(t, n, b)$ is an orthonormal basis for all $s$.

Then take $$ \begin{align*} α(s) := α_0 + \int_{s_0}^{s} t(\tilde s) \d \tilde s. \end{align*} $$

Let $\tilde α$ be another curve with curvature $k$ and torsion $τ$. Take $R\in \op{SO}(3)$ such that $R\pmat{t(s_0) \\ n(s_0) \\ b(s_0)} = \pmat{\tilde t(s_0) \\ \tilde n(s_0) \\ \tilde b(s_0)}$ and $v = \tilde α(s_0) - α(s_0)$. Then $R\pmat{t \\ n \\ b}$ and $\pmat{\tilde t \\ \tilde n \\ \tilde b}$ solve the same system $(*)$, so equal by uniqueness. $\tilde α$ and $R \circ α + v$ are therefore equal.
