Let $S$ be a surface in $\R^3$ and $α: I\to S$ be a curve.

Given any $t_0\in I$ and $v_0\in T_{α(t_0)}S$, there exists a unique vector field $V$ along $α$ which is parallel along $α$ (i.e. $\mathrm{D}V/\d t = 0$) and has $V(t_0) = v_0$.

$V$ is known as the parallel transport of $v_0$ along $α$.

Proof

The map $T_{α(0)}S\to T_{α(1)}S$ sending $v_0$ to the parallel transport of $v_0$ along $α$ from $0$ to $1$ is an isometry with respect to $\I_{α(0)}$ and $\I_{α(1)}$. (Derivation)
