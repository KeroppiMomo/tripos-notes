Take a geodesic polar coordinates, such that one edge of $T$ corresponds to $θ = 0$ and one corresponds to $θ = α_1>0$. Let the third edge be parametrised by $γ(t) = ϕ(r(t), θ(t))$, $t\in [a,b]$.

We claim $θ$ is a strictly increasing function, so $γ$ is a graph of $r = h(θ)$ for some smooth function $h: [0, α_1]\to [0,\infty)$. Indeed if not, there exists a point $γ(t')$ such that $\dot θ(t') = 0$. But then $θ = θ(t')$ is the unique geodesic passing through $(r(t'), θ(t'))$ with tangent $ϕ_r$, and $γ$ is not that.

Then using the first fundamental form of geodesic polars and $K = -\sqrt{G}_{rr}/\sqrt{G}$, $$ \begin{align*} \int_{T} K \d A ={}& \int_{ϕ\inv(T)} K \sqrt{G} \d A \\ ={}& \lim_{ε\to 0} \int_{0}^{α_1} \int_{ε}^{h(r)} -\sqrt{G}_{rr} \d r \d θ \\ ={}& \lim_{ε\to 0} \int_{0}^{α_1} -\sqrt{G}_r(h(r), θ) + \sqrt{G}_r(ε, θ) \d θ \\ ={}& \int_{0}^{α_1} -\sqrt{G}_r(h(r), θ) + 1 \d θ \\ ={}& \int_{0}^{α_1} -\sqrt{G}_r(h(r), θ) \d θ + α_1 \end{align*} $$ so it remains to show $\int_{0}^{α_1} -\sqrt{G}_r(h(r)) \d θ = α_2 + α_3 - π$.

Let $ψ$ be a smooth determination of angle from $ϕ_r$ to $\dot γ$. Using our previous calculation Then $$ \begin{align*} \dot ψ = \underbrace{\left[\frac{\mathrm{D}\dot γ}{\d t}\right]}_{0} - \left[\frac{\mathrm{D}ϕ_r}{\d t}\right] = -\frac{G_r \dot θ}{2\sqrt{G}} = -\sqrt{G}_r \dot θ \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} ψ(b) - ψ(a) ={}& \int_{a}^{b} -\sqrt{G}_r(r(t), θ(t)) \dot θ \d t = \int_{0}^{α_1} -\sqrt{G}_r(h(θ), θ) \d θ. \end{align*} $$

$ψ(b) = α_2$ and $ψ(a) = π - α_3$ so we are done.
