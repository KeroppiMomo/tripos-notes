Definition (smooth surface in R3) open_in_new

A smooth surface in $\R^3$ is a subspace $Σ\subseteq \R^3$ that is a 2-dimensional manifold, i.e. such that for all $p\in Σ$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U\subseteq Σ$ of $p$ which is diffeomorphic to an open set in $\R^2$.

  • Unpacking the definition: for all $p\in Σ$, there exists an open ball $B\subseteq \R^3$ containing $p$, an open set $V\subseteq \R^2$ and a smooth map $f: B \to V$, such that by writing $U = B \cap Σ$ (which is open in $Σ$), $f|_U: U \to V$ is a homeomorphism and its inverse $V\hookrightarrow \R^3$ is smooth.

Definition (first fundamental form) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $p\in Σ$.

The first fundamental form $\I_p$ of $Σ$ at $p$ is the restriction of the Euclidean inner product $\langle ,\rangle$ in $\R^3$ on the tangent space $T_pΣ$, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \I_p: T_pΣ \times T_pΣ &\to \R \\ (v,w) &\mapsto \langle v,w\rangle. \end{align*} $$

Given an allowable parametrisation $σ: V\to U$ on $Σ$ near $p$, $\set{σ_u, σ_v}$ is a basis of $T_pΣ = \im \d σ_{σ\inv(p)}$. In this basis, $\I_p$ is represented by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} := \pmat{\langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle & \langle σ_u, σ_v\rangle \\ \langle σ_v, σ_u\rangle & \langle σ_v, σ_v\rangle} = (\d σ)^T (\d σ), \end{align*} $$ which is a function in $(u,v)\in V$. Formally we also write $$ \begin{align*} \I_p = E \d u^2 + 2F \d u \d v + G \d v^2. \end{align*} $$

A parametrisation is orthogonal if $F = 0$.

A parametrisation is isothermal if $F = 0$ and $E = G = λ^2$ for some function $λ$.

Lemma (length of curve on surface) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation on a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, and $γ(t) = σ(u(t), v(t)): [a,b] \to U$ be a smooth curve. Then $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}(γ) &= \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{Eu'(t)^2 + 2F u'(t) v'(t) + G v'(t)^2} \d t \\ &= \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{\I((u'(t), v'(t)), (u'(t), v'(t)))} \d t \end{align*} $$ where $\I(x,y) = x^T \pmat{E & F\\ F & G} y$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

Definition (isometry of surfaces in II Differential Geometry) open_in_new

Suppose $ψ: S\to \tilde S$ is a diffeomorphism of surfaces in $\R^3$. We say $ψ$ is an isometry if $$ \begin{align*} \I_p(v,w) = \tilde \I_{ψ(p)}(\d ψ_p(v), \d ψ_p(w)) \end{align*} $$ holds for all $p\in S$ and $v,w\in T_pS$, where $\I$ and $\tilde \I$ are the first fundamental forms of $S$ and $\tilde S$.

Isometry preserves length of curves.

Lemma (first fundamental form in different parametrisations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, with two allowable parametrisations $\tilde σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ and $σ: \tilde V\to U\subseteq Σ$ near $p\in Σ$.

Let $ϕ = \tilde σ\inv\circ σ: V\to\tilde V$ be the transition map. The first fundamental forms in the two parametrisations are related by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} = (Dϕ)^T \pmat{\tilde E & \tilde F \\ \tilde F & \tilde G} (Dϕ). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (area on surface) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation for an open set $U$ in a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

Define the area of $U$ as $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{area}(U) = \int_{V} \sqrt{EG - F^2} \d u \d v = \int_{V} \sqrt{\det \I} \d u \d v \end{align*} $$ where $\I = \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}$ is the first fundamental form.

If $U = U_1 \sqcup \ldots \sqcup U_n \sqcup Y$ such that $U_1, \ldots, U_n$ are images of a parametrisation and $Y$ has measure 0, then $\op{area}(U) := \sum_{i=1}^{n} \op{area}(U_i)$.

Motivation: viewing $σ_u$ and $σ_v$ as an infinitesimal parallelogram on $Σ$, we integrate over the area $\sqrt{\langle σ_u\times σ_v, σ_u\times σ_v\rangle} = \sqrt{\langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle\langle σ_v, σ_v\rangle - \langle σ_u, σ_v\rangle^2}$.

$\mathrm{area}(U)$ does not depend on the parametrisation. (Proof)

Definition (normal direction, Gauss map) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $p\in Σ$ be a point.

The normal direction to $Σ$ at $p$ is the Euclidean orthogonal complement $(T_p Σ)^\perp\subseteq \R^3$ of the tangent plane.

A unit vector in $(T_pΣ)^\perp$ is a unit normal vector.

The Gauss map $n: U\to S^2$ defined on a subset $U\subseteq Σ$ is a continuous map that sends a point to the unit normal vector.

Definition (two-sided surface) open_in_new

A smooth surface in $\R^3$ is two-sided if it admits a global continuous choice of unit normal vector, i.e. a globally well-defined Gauss map.

Lemma (shape operator is self-adjoint with respect to first fundamental form) open_in_new

For a smooth oriented surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, the shape operator $\mathbb{S}_p$ at a point $p\in Σ$ is a self-adjoint linear map with respect to the inner product $\I_p$ $= \langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle_{\R^3}$.

Proof

Definition (second fundamental form) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation on a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$. Define $$ \begin{align*} e \equiv L :={}& \langle n, σ_{uu}\rangle = -\langle n_u, σ_u\rangle, \\ f \equiv M :={}& \langle n, σ_{uv}\rangle = -\langle n_u, σ_v\rangle = -\langle n_v, σ_u\rangle, \\ g \equiv N :={}& \langle n, σ_{vv}\rangle = -\langle n_v, σ_v\rangle, \\ n ={}& \frac{σ_u \times σ_v}{\|σ_u \times σ_v\|} \end{align*} $$ which are functions of $(u,v)\in V$. (Differentiate $\langle n, σ_u\rangle=0=\langle n, σ_v\rangle$ to see those equalities.)

The second fundamental form $\II$ is represented by

  • the formal expression $$ \begin{align*} L \d u^2 + 2M \d u \d v + N \d v^2, \end{align*} $$

  • the symmetric bilinear form $$ \begin{align*} \II((u_1, v_1), (u_2, v_2)) = L u_1 u_2 + M(u_1 v_2 + v_1 u_2) + N v_1 v_2, \end{align*} $$

  • or its matrix representation $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{L & M \\ M & N} = -(Dn)^T (Dσ). \end{align*} $$

Lemma (second fundamental form in terms of shape operator) open_in_new

If $Σ$ is a smooth oriented surface in $\R^3$, then the second fundamental form at $p\in Σ$ is the symmetric bilinear form on $T_p Σ$ given by $$ \begin{align*} \II_p(v,w) = \langle \mathbb{S}_pv, w\rangle \equiv \I_p(\mathbb{S}_pv,w) \end{align*} $$ where $\mathbb{S}$ is the shape operator and $\I$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

  • Picking an allowable parametrisation $σ$ at $p$, the matrices of the bilinear forms in the basis $\set{σ_u, σ_v}$ then follow $$ \begin{align*} \II = \pmat{L & M \\ M & N} = \mathbb{S}^T \cdot \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} = \mathbb{S}^T \I. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (second fundamental form in different parametrisations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, with two allowable parametrisations $\tilde σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ and $σ: \tilde V\to U\subseteq Σ$ near $p\in Σ$.

Let $ϕ = \tilde σ\inv\circ σ: V\to\tilde V$ be the transition map. The second fundamental forms in the two parametrisations are related by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{L&M \\ M&N} = \sign(\det Dϕ) \cdot (Dϕ)^T \pmat{\tilde L & \tilde M \\ \tilde M & \tilde N} (Dϕ). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (curvature on surface in R3) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $p\in S$.

The principal curvatures of $S$ at $p$ are $$ \begin{align*} k_1 &:= \max_{\substack{v\in T_pS:\\ \I_p(v,v ) = 1}} \II_p(v,v), \\ k_2 &:= \min_{\substack{v\in T_pS:\\ \I_p(v,v ) = 1}} \II_p(v,v), \end{align*} $$ where $\I$ and $\II$ are the first and second fundamental forms. Equivalently, $k_1, k_2$ are the eigenvalues of the shape operator $\mathbb{S}_p$ (since $\mathbb{S}_p$ is self-adjoint so spectral theorem holds).

If $k_1\neq k_2$, then the eigenvectors of $\mathbb{S}_p$ are called the principal directions.

The mean curvature at $p$ is $$ \begin{align*} H(p) := \frac{1}{2} \tr \mathbb{S}_p = \frac{1}{2}(k_1 + k_2) \end{align*} $$ and the Gauss/Gaussian curvature at $p$ is $$ \begin{align*} K(p) := \det \mathbb{S}_p = k_1 k_2. \end{align*} $$

In a local parametrisation, the matrix relation $\II_p = \mathbb{S}_p^T \I_p$ implies $$ \begin{align*} K &= \frac{\det \II_p}{\det \I_p} = \frac{eg - f^2}{EG - F^2}, \\ H &= \frac{eG + 2fF + gE}{2(EG - F^2)}. \end{align*} $$

Definition (Christoffel symbols for surfaces in R3) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a surface in $\R^3$ and $ϕ$ be a local parametrisation.

The Christoffel symbols $Γ_{ij}^k$ are smooth functions in $(u,v)$ such that $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_{uu} ={}& Γ_{11}^1 ϕ_u + Γ_{11}^2 ϕ_v + e N, \\ ϕ_{uv} ={}& Γ_{12}^1 ϕ_u + Γ_{12}^2 ϕ_v + f N, \\ ϕ_{vv} ={}& Γ_{22}^1 ϕ_u + Γ_{22}^2 ϕ_v + g N \end{align*} $$ where $e,f,g$ are coefficients in the second fundamental form.

In terms of coefficients $E,F,G$ of the first fundamental form, $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{Γ_{11}^1 \\ Γ_{11}^2} ={}& \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}\inv \pmat{\frac{1}{2} E_u \\ F_u - \frac{1}{2} E_v}, \\ \pmat{Γ_{12}^1 \\ Γ_{12}^2} ={}& \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}\inv \pmat{\frac{1}{2} E_v \\ \frac{1}{2}G_u}, \\ \pmat{Γ_{22}^1 \\ Γ_{22}^2} ={}& \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}\inv \pmat{F_u - \frac{1}{2} G_u \\ \frac{1}{2}G_v}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Theorem (theorema egregium) open_in_new

The Gauss curvature is an isometry invariant, i.e. if $Σ_1$ and $Σ_2$ are two smooth surfaces in $\R^3$ and $f: Σ_1 \to Σ_2$ is an isometry, then $κ_{Σ_1}(p) = κ_{Σ_2}(f(p))$.

In a local parametrisation, the Gauss curvature can be expressed as $$ \begin{align*} K = \frac{1}{E}((Γ_{11}^2)_v - (Γ_{12}^2)_u + Γ_{11}^1 Γ_{12}^2 + Γ_{11}^2 Γ_{22}^2 - Γ_{12}^1 Γ_{11}^2 - Γ_{12}^2 Γ_{12}^2) \end{align*} $$ where $Γ_{ij}^k$ are the Christoffel symbols.

Proof using geodesic parallel coordinates

Proof using Christoffel symbols

In a orthogonal parametrisation (i.e. $F = 0$), this simplifies to $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{EG}}\left(\pdd{u} \frac{G_u}{\sqrt{EG}} + \pdd{v}\frac{E_v}{\sqrt{EG}}\right). \end{align*} $$

For an isomthermal parametrisation with $F = 0$, $E = G = λ^2$, $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{1}{λ^2} ∇^2(\log λ). \end{align*} $$

With $E = 1$, $F = 0$, we get $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{\sqrt{G}_{uu}}{\sqrt{G}}. \end{align*} $$

Definition (energy of curve) open_in_new

Let $γ: [a,b]\to \R^3$ be a smooth curve. Its energy is $$ \begin{align*} E(γ) = \int_{a}^{b} \|γ'(t)\|^2 \d t. \end{align*} $$

Definition (vector field along curve, covariant derivative, parallel) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $α: I\to S$ be a curve.

A vector field along $α$ is a smooth function $V: I\to \R^3$ such that $V(t) \in T_{α(t)}S$.

The covariant derivative of $V$ is a vector field $\mathrm{D}V/\d t$ along $α$ given by the orthogonal projection of $\d V/\d t$ onto $T_{α(t)}S$: $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} := \dfd{V}{t} - \left\langle \dfd{V}{t}, N\right\rangle N. \end{align*} $$

We say $V$ is parallel along $α$ if $\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} \equiv 0$.

If $V$ is a unit vector field (i.e. $\langle V, V\rangle = 1$), then $\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}, V\rangle = 0$ (see below). The algebraic value of the covariant derivative $[\mathrm{D}V/\d t]: I \to \R$ is the smooth function satisfying $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} = \left[\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}\right] (N\times V). \end{align*} $$

Leibniz's rule: for two vector fields $V,W$ along $α$, $$ \begin{align*} \dd{t}\langle V,W\rangle = \left\langle \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}, W\right\rangle + \left\langle V, \frac{\mathrm{D}W}{\d t}\right\rangle. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation) Therefore $\langle \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}, V\rangle = 0$ if $\langle V,V\rangle = 1$.

In a local parametrisation $ϕ$, writing $α(t) = ϕ(u(t), v(t))$ and $V(t) = a(t) ϕ_u(u(t), v(t)) + b(t) ϕ_v(u(t), v(t))$, we can express $\mathrm{D}V/\d t$ in the basis $\set{ϕ_u, ϕ_v}$ using the Christoffel symbols: $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} ={}\quad & (\dot a + Γ_{11}^1 a \dot u + Γ_{12}^1 a \dot v + Γ_{12}^1 b \dot u + Γ_{22}^1 b \dot v) ϕ_u \\ {}+{} &(\dot b + Γ_{11}^2a\dot u + Γ_{12}^2a \dot v + Γ_{12}^2b\dot u + Γ_{22}^2 b \dot v) ϕ_v. \end{align*} $$

If $f: S\to \tilde S$ is an isometry between surfaces in $\R^3$, then $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}(f \circ V)}{\d t} = \d f_{α(t)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}\right). \end{align*} $$

If $ϕ$ is an orthogonal parametrisation (i.e. $F = 0$), then with $N = (ϕ_u \times ϕ_v)/\|ϕ_u \times ϕ_v\|$, $$ \begin{align*} \left[\frac{\mathrm{D}(ϕ_u/\sqrt{E})}{\d t}\right] = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{EG}}(G_u\dot v - E_v \dot u). \end{align*} $$ Proof

Proposition (parallel transport) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a surface in $\R^3$ and $α: I\to S$ be a curve.

Given any $t_0\in I$ and $v_0\in T_{α(t_0)}S$, there exists a unique vector field $V$ along $α$ which is parallel along $α$ (i.e. $\mathrm{D}V/\d t = 0$) and has $V(t_0) = v_0$.

$V$ is known as the parallel transport of $v_0$ along $α$.

Proof

The map $T_{α(0)}S\to T_{α(1)}S$ sending $v_0$ to the parallel transport of $v_0$ along $α$ from $0$ to $1$ is an isometry with respect to $\I_{α(0)}$ and $\I_{α(1)}$. (Derivation)

Definition (geodesic) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

A geodesic $γ:[a,b]\to Σ$ is a critical point for the energy functional in the space of smooth curves from $γ(a)$ to $γ(b)$.

Formulation: consider any one-parameter variation of $γ$ with fixed end-points, i.e. smooth map $Γ: (-ε, ε)\times [a,b]\to Σ$ such that writing $γ_s(t) = Γ(s,t)$, we have $γ_0 = γ$, and $γ_s(a)$ and $γ_s(b)$ are independent of $s$. Then $γ$ is a geodesic if $$ \begin{align*} \dd{s} E(γ_s)\Big|_{s=0} = 0. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (geodesic equations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation.

A smooth curve $γ: [a,b]\to U$ is a geodesic iff it satisfies the geodesic equations $$ \begin{align*} \dd{t}(E\dot u + F\dot v) ={}& \frac{1}{2}(E_u \dot u^2 + 2F_u \dot u \dot v + G_u \dot v^2), \\ \dd{t}(F\dot u + G\dot v) ={}& \frac{1}{2}(E_v \dot u^2 + 2F_v \dot u \dot v + G_v \dot v^2). \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • These are the Euler-Lagrange equations.

Proposition (geodesic iff acceleration normal to surface) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

A smooth curve $γ: [a,b]\to Σ$ is a geodesic iff $\ddot γ(t)$ is normal to the surface for all $t$, i.e. $\mathrm{D}\dot γ/\d t$${}= 0$.

Consequently, geodesics are always parametrised with constant speed.

Let $S$ be a surface in $\R^3$ and let $α: I\to S$ be a curve parametrised by arc length.

The geodesic curvature of $α$ is the algebraic value of the covariant derivative of $\dot α$: $$ \begin{align*} k_g(t) := \left[\frac{\mathrm{D\dot α}}{\d t}\right](t) \end{align*} $$

$k_g \equiv 0$ iff $α$ is a geodesic.

Proposition (minimal surface) open_in_new

For a local parametrisation $ϕ: U\to V$, given a smooth function $h: U\to \R$, the normal variation of $V$ determined by $h$ is the family $$ \begin{align*} ϕ^t(u,v) := ϕ(u,v) + t\, h(u,v)\, N(u,v), \qquad t\in (-ε, ε) \end{align*} $$ where $N = \frac{ϕ_u \times ϕ_v}{\|ϕ_u \times ϕ_v\|}$. Proposition (Weierstrass representation of minimal surfaces) open_in_new

Let $ϕ: U\to V$ be a local parametrisation of a surface in $\R^3$, and write $ϕ(u,v) = (x(u,v), y(u,v), z(u,v))$. Suppose $U\subseteq \R^2 \simeq \C$ is a domain in the complex plane.

Define the complex functions $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1 = x_u - i x_v, \qquad ϕ_2 = y_u - i y_v, \qquad ϕ_3 = z_u - iz_v. \end{align*} $$

  1. We have $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1^2 + ϕ_2^2 + ϕ_3^2 ={}& (E - G) - 2iF, \\ \end{align*} $$ so $ϕ$ is isothermal iff $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1^2 + ϕ_2^2 + ϕ_3^2 = 0. \end{align*} $$

    If $ϕ$ is isothermal, $ϕ$ is minimal iff $ϕ_1$, $ϕ_2$, $ϕ_3$ are all holomorphic,

    In this case $$ \begin{align*} x(u,v) ={}& x(u_0, v_0) + \Re \int_{(u_0, v_0)}^{(u,v)} ϕ_1 \d ζ, \\ y(u,v) ={}& y(u_0, v_0) + \Re \int_{(u_0, v_0)}^{(u,v)} ϕ_2 \d ζ, \\ z(u,v) ={}& z(u_0, v_0) + \Re \int_{(u_0, v_0)}^{(u,v)} ϕ_3 \d ζ \end{align*} $$ where the path of integration in $U$ is arbitrary.

  2. For any $f$ holomorphic and $g$ meromorphic on $U$, given that $fg^2$ is holomorphic on $U$ (i.e. if $z\in U$ is a pole of $g$ of order $k$, then $z$ is a zero of $f$ of order $\geq 2k$), then $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1 = \frac{1}{2}f(1-g^2), \qquad ϕ_2 = \frac{i}{2}f(1 + g^2), \qquad ϕ_3 = fg \end{align*} $$ are holomorphic functions on $U$ satisfying $ϕ_1^2 + ϕ_2^2 + ϕ_3^2 = 0$.

    Conversely, if $ϕ_1, ϕ_2, ϕ_3$ are holomorphic functions on $U$ satisfying $ϕ_1^2 + ϕ_2^2 + ϕ_3^2 = 0$, then either $ϕ_1 = iϕ_2$ and $ϕ_3 = 0$, or $(ϕ_1, ϕ_2, ϕ_3)$ are of the above form, called the Weierstrass representation.

Proof

In the Weierstrass representation, the first fundamental form is given by $$ \begin{align*} λ^2 = E = G = \left(\frac{1}{2}|f| (1 + |g|^2)\right)^2, \qquad F = 0. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Letting $π: S^2 \to \C_\infty$ be the stereographic projection (i.e. $π\inv(z) = \frac{(2\Re z, 2 \Im z, |z|^2 - 1)}{|z|^2 + 1}$), we have $$ \begin{align*} g = π \circ N \end{align*} $$ so $N: U \to S^2$ is an analytic map of Riemann surfaces. (Derivation)

The Gaussian curvature is $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{16|g'|^2}{|f|^2 (1 + |g|^2)^4}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Define $U := \{v\in T_pS \mid \exists$geodesic $γ_v: [0,1]\to S$ such that $γ_v(0) = p$, $\dot γ_v(0) = v\} \subseteq T_pS$. The exponential map of $S$ at $p$ is $$ \begin{align*} \exp_p: U &\to S \\ v &\mapsto γ_v(1). \end{align*} $$

$U$ is open, contains $0\in T_pS$ and starlike. (Proof)

$\d$ $\exp_p|_0 = \id_{T_pS}$. (Derivation) Therefore the inverse function theorem implies $\exp_{p}|_{\tilde U}: \tilde U \to V$ is a diffeomorphism for a neighbourhood $\tilde U\subseteq U$ of $0$. We call $V$ a normal neighbourhood of $p$.

A geodesic normal coordinates is a local parametrisation $σ(u,v) := {\exp_p} \circ ξ|_{ξ\inv(\tilde U)}(u,v)$ for some linear isometry $ξ: \R^2 \to T_pS$.

A geodesic polar coordinates is $ϕ(r,θ) := {\exp_p} \circ η|_{η\inv(\tilde U)}(r,θ)$ for some polar coordinates $η: (0,\infty) \times (0,2π) \to T_pS$, (which is a local parametrisation of $V$ minus one radial geodesic).

Images of circles under $ϕ$ are called geodesic circles, and images of lines through origin are called radial geodesics.

Geodesic normal coordinates $σ$ has first fundamental form at $(0,0)$ $$ \begin{align*} E(0,0) = 1, \qquad F(0, 0) = 0, \qquad G(0,0) = 1. \end{align*} $$

Geodesic polar coordinates $ϕ$ has first fundamental form $$ \begin{align*} E \equiv 1, \qquad F\equiv 0, \qquad \lim_{r\to 0} \sqrt{G} = 0, \qquad \lim_{r\to 0} \sqrt{G}_r = 1. \end{align*} $$ The fact $F\equiv 0$ is called Gauss's lemma. (Proof) We showed $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{\sqrt{G}_{rr}}{\sqrt{G}}. \end{align*} $$

Definition (subdivision) open_in_new

A subdivision of a compact topological surface $Σ$ comprises

  1. a finite set $V \subseteq Σ$ of vertices;

  2. a finite set $\set{e_i: \text{continuous }[0, 1] \to Σ}_{i\in E}$ of edges,

such that

  • each $e_i$ is injective on $(0,1)$;

  • $e_i\inv(V) = \set{0,1}$;

  • $e_i(0,1)$ and $e_j(0,1)$ are disjoint for $i\neq j$; and

  • each connected component of $Σ \setminus (\bigcup_{i} e_i[0,1] \cup V)$ is homeomorphic to an open disc, called an open face. Call a face the closure of such a component.

Definition (triangulation) open_in_new

A subdivision is a triangulation if each closed face contains exactly three edges, and two closed faces are either disjoint or meet at an edge or a vertex.

Definition (Euler characteristic) open_in_new

The Euler characteristic of subdivision of a compact topological surface $Σ$ is defined as the integer $$ \begin{align*} χ(Σ) = \# \text{ vertices} - \#\text{ edges} + \# \text{ faces}. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (invariant of Euler characteristic) open_in_new

  1. Every compact topological surface $Σ$ admits a subdivision (and in fact triangulation).

  2. The Euler characteristic $χ(Σ)$ of $Σ$ does not depend on the choice of subdivision, i.e. it defines a topological invariant of the surface $Σ$.

Theorem (Gauss, geodesic triangle) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a surface in $\R^3$. Suppose $T\subset S$ is a geodesic triangle (i.e. a set bound by three geodesics and homeomorphic to a closed disc), contained in a geodesic polar coordinates centred at a vertex of $T$.

Then letting $α_1, α_2, α_3$ be the three internal angles of $T$, $$ \begin{align*} \int_T K \d A = α_1 + α_2 + α_3 - π. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (global Gauss-Bonnet without boundary) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a compact abstract smooth surface with an abstract Riemannian metric $g$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{Σ} κ_Σ \d A = 2π\, χ(Σ) \end{align*} $$ where $κ_Σ$ is the Gauss curvature and $χ(Σ)$ is the Euler characteristic of the surface.

Proof

Theorem (Bonnet, geodesic curvature of disc bounding curve) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a surface in $\R^3$.

Suppose $α: [a,b]\to U$ is a simple closed curve parametrised by arc length, lying in a local parametrisation $ϕ: V\to U\subseteq S$. Assume $ϕ$ is orthogonal (i.e. $F = 0$), $ϕ\inv\circ α$ bounds a disc $D$, and $ϕ\inv\circ α$ is positively oriented. Then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{a}^{b} k_g \d s + \int_{ϕ(D)} K \d A = 2π \end{align*} $$ where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature and $k_g$ is the geodesic curvature of $α$.

Proof

Theorem (global Gauss-Bonnet with boundary) open_in_new

Let $S$ be a surface with boundary in $\R^3$. Suppose $S$ consists of $n$ piecewise simple closed curves $α_1, \ldots, α_n$, mutually non-intersecting, parametrised by arc length, and positively oriented. Let $θ_1, \ldots, θ_p$ be the turning angles at the vertices of these curves.

Then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{S} K \d A + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \int_{α_i} k_g \d s + \sum_{i=1}^{p} θ_i = 2π\,χ(S) \end{align*} $$ where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature, $k_g$ is the geodesic curvature, and $χ(S)$ is the Euler characteristic.
