Showing the condition for isothermal coordinates is just computation.

We showed that if $ϕ$ is isothermal, then $ϕ$ is minimal iff $ϕ$ is harmonic. This is equivalent to $ϕ_1, ϕ_2, ϕ_3$ all satisfying the Riemann-Cauchy equations, i.e. they are holomorphic.

Now check $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1 = \frac{1}{2}f(1-g^2), \qquad ϕ_2 = \frac{i}{2}f(1+g^2), \qquad ϕ_3 = fg \end{align*} $$ are holomorphic and satisfy $ϕ_1^2 + ϕ_2^2 + ϕ_3^2 = 0$.

Conversely, given holomorphic $ϕ_1, ϕ_2, ϕ_3$, take $$ \begin{align*} f = ϕ_1 - iϕ_2, \qquad g = \frac{ϕ_3}{ϕ_1 - iϕ_2}. \end{align*} $$ $f$ is holomorphic, and unless $ϕ_1 = iϕ_2$ (so $ϕ_3 = 0$), $g$ is meromorphic.

Staring from $ϕ_1^2 + ϕ_2^2 + ϕ_3^2 = 0$, since $(ϕ_1 + iϕ_2)(ϕ_1 - iϕ_2) + ϕ_3^2 = 0$, we get $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1 + iϕ_2 = -\frac{ϕ_3^2}{ϕ_1 - iϕ_2} = -fg^2 \end{align*} $$ which is holomorphic.
