Definition (smooth functions, diffeomorphism) open_in_new

Let $f: V\to V'$ be continuous.

  • For an open set $V\subseteq \R^n$ and $V'= \R^m$, $f$ is smooth if it is infinitely differentiable (i.e. all partial derivatives of all orders exist).

  • For a general subset $V\subseteq \R^n$ and $V' = \R^m$, $f$ is smooth at $p\in V$ if there exists an open neighbourhood $B\subseteq \R^n$ of $p$ and a smooth map $F: B \to \R^m$ such that $F|_{B\cap V} = f|_{B \cap V}$.

  • For $V = Σ$ an abstract smooth surface and $V' = \R^m$, $f$ is smooth at $p\in Σ$ if for a chart $(U, ϕ)$ at $p$ in our atlas, the map $f\circ ϕ\inv: ϕ(U) \to \R^m$ is smooth at $ϕ(p)$.

  • For $V=Σ_1$ and $V'=Σ_2$ abstract smooth surface, $f$ is smooth at $p$ if for charts $(U, ϕ)$ at $p$ and $(U', ϕ')$ at $f(p)$, the composition $ϕ' \circ f \circ ϕ\inv$ is smooth at $ϕ(p)$.

$f$ is called a diffeomorphism if it is a homeomorphism, and $f$ and $f\inv$ are smooth.

  • Smoothness on abstract smooth surfaces does not depend on the choice of chart $(U,ϕ)$ at $p$ in our atlas, because if we use a different chart $(U', ϕ')$ at $p$, $$ \begin{align*} f\circ ϕ'\inv = (f\circ ϕ\inv) \circ (ϕ \circ ϕ'\inv) \end{align*} $$ where the transition map $ϕ \circ ϕ'\inv$ is a diffeomorphism. So $f\circ ϕ'\inv$ is smooth iff $f\circ ϕ\inv$ is smooth.

Definition (manifold, submanifold, codimension) open_in_new

Let $X\subseteq \R^N$ be a subspace.

$X$ is $k$-dimensional smooth/differential manifold if for all $x\in X$, there exists a diffeomorphism $ϕ: U\to V$, where $U\subseteq X$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$ and $V \subseteq \R^k$ is open.

Call $ϕ$ a coordinate system or chart, and $ϕ\inv$ a local parametrisation.

If $Y \subset X$ are both manifolds, we say $Y$ is a submanifold of $X$. The codimension is $$ \begin{align*} \op{codim}_X(Y) = \dim X - \dim Y. \end{align*} $$

By restricting $U$, we can WLOG take $V$ to be a ball. Then composing with a diffeomorphism, we can take $V = \R^k$.

A zero-dimensional manifold is a discrete subset of $\R^N$.

$X \times Y \subset \R^{N+M}$ is a manifold if $X\subset \R^N$ and $Y \subset \R^M$ are manifolds. (Sheet 1)

Definition (tangent space) open_in_new

Let $X \subset \R^N$ be a $k$-dimensional manifold, and $x\in X$. Choose a local parametrisation $ψ: V\to U\ni x$ around $x$.

The tangent space is the image $$ \begin{align*} T_x X := \d ψ_{ψ\inv(x)}(\R^k) \subset \R^{N} \end{align*} $$ a vector subspace of $\R^N$, where $\d$ is the differential operator.

This does not depend on parametrisation. (Proof)

$\dim T_x X = k$. (Proof) This shows the dimension of $X$ (at a point $x$) is well-defined.

Definition (differential map, immersion, submersion) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth function between manifolds, and $x\in X$.

Choose local parametrisations $ψ_1: V_1\to U_1$ of $X$ around $x$ and $ψ_2: V_2 \to U_2$ of $Y$ around $f(x)$. WLOG assume $f(\mathcal{V}_1) \subset \mathcal{V}_2$ by restricting.

The differential map of $f$ at $x$ is the linear map $$ \begin{align*} \d f_x = \underbrace{\d (ψ_2)_{ψ_2\inv(f(x))}}_{\R^{\tilde k} \to T_{f(x)} Y} \circ \underbrace{\d(ψ_2\inv \circ f \circ ψ_1)_{ψ_1\inv(x)}}_{\R^k \to \R^{\tilde k}} \circ \underbrace{(\d(ψ_1)_{ψ_1\inv(x)})\inv}_{T_x X \to \R^k}: T_x X \to T_y Y \end{align*} $$ between tangent spaces.

$f$ is an immersion at $x\in X$ if $\d f_x$ is an injection. $f$ is an submersion at $x\in X$ if $\d f_x$ is a surjection.

This does not depend on parametrisation.

Chain rule applies: if $f: X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$ are smooth maps between manifolds, then $\d(g \circ f)_x = \d g_{f(x)} \circ \d f_{x}$.

Theorem (inverse function theorem for manifolds) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map of manifolds and $x\in X$.

If $\d f_x$${}: T_x X \to T_y Y$ is an isomorphism, then there exists an open neighbourhood $U \subseteq X$ of $x$ such that $f|_U: U\to f(U)$ is a diffeomorphism.

Proof: pass to charts and apply the inverse function theorem in $\R^n$.

Definition (critical point, critical value, regular value) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map between manifolds.

$x\in X$ is a critical point of $f$ if $\d f_x$${}: T_x X\to T_{f(x)} Y$ is not surjective.

$y\in Y$ is a critical value of $f$ if there exists some critical point $x\in X$ such that $y = f(x)$.

$y\in Y$ is a regular value of $f$ if it is not a critical value.

If $y\not\in f(X)$, then $y$ is a regular value.

If $\dim Y > \dim X$, all points in $Y$ are critical values as $T_x X \to T_{f(x)} Y$ can never be surjective.

Theorem (preimage theorem) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map between manifolds.

If $y\in Y$ is a regular value, then $f\inv(y)$ (if non-empty) is a manifold of codimension $\dim Y$.

Moreover, $T_x f\inv(y) = \ker \d f_x$ for all $x\in f\inv(y)$.

Proof

If $\dim X = \dim Y$, then $f\inv(y)$ is a discrete subset of $X$. If $X$ is compact, then $f\inv(y)$ is finite.

Definition (measure zero in Rn) open_in_new

A subset $A\subset \R^n$ has measure zero if for all $\epsilon>0$, there exists a countable collection of $n$-dimensional cubes $I_j$ such that $$ \begin{align*} A\subset \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty I_j \quad \text{and}\quad \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} |I_j| < \epsilon \end{align*} $$ where $|I_j|$ denotes the volume of $I_j$.

Definition (measure zero on manifold) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a $k$-dimensional manifold and $S \subseteq X$ be a subset.

$S$ is said to be of measure zero if for all local parametrisation $ψ: V \to U$, the set $ψ\inv(S \cap U) \subseteq V\subseteq \R^k$ is of measure zero.

Theorem (Sard) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map between manifolds.

The set of critical values of $f$ is of measure zero as a subset of $Y$.

Therefore the set of regular values is dense in $Y$.

Definition (transversality) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map between manifolds, and let $Z \subseteq Y$ be a submanifold of $Y$.

Say $f$ is traversal to $Z$ (denoted $f \pitchfork Z$) if for all $x\in f\inv(Z)$, $$ \begin{align*} \im \d f_x + T_{f(x)} Z = T_{f(x)} Y \end{align*} $$ where $\d f_x$ is the differential map.

If $Z \cap f(X) = \emptyset$, then $f$ is transversal to $Z$.

$y\in Y$ is a regular value iff $f$ is transversal to the submanifold $\set{y}\subseteq Y$.

Definition (manifold with boundary) open_in_new

Define the half plane as $\mathbb{H}^k = \sb{(x_1, \ldots, x_k)\in \R^k}{x_k \geq 0}$ and its boundary $\partial \mathbb{H}^k = \sb{(x_1, \ldots, x_k)\in \R^k}{x_k = 0}$.

$X \subseteq \R^N$ is a $k$-dimensional manifold with boundary if for all $x\in X$, there exists a diffeomorphism $ϕ: U\to V$ where $U\subseteq X$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$ and $V \subseteq \mathbb{H}^k$ is an open set.

The boundary of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} \partial X ={}& \sb{x\in X}{\exists \text{chart } ϕ: U\to V\subseteq \mathbb{H}^k: \quad ϕ(x) \in \partial \mathbb{H}^k} \\ ={}& \sb{x\in X}{\forall \text{chart } ϕ: U\to V\subseteq \mathbb{H}^k: \quad ϕ(x) \in \partial \mathbb{H}^k}. \end{align*} $$

The interior of $X$ is $\op{int} X = X \setminus \partial X$.

As before we can WLOG take $V = \R^k$ or $\mathbb{H}^k$.

$\partial X$ is a $(k-1)$-dimensional manifold (without boundary) and $\op{int} X$ is a $k$-dimensional manifold.

Generally if $X$ is manifold with boundary and $Y$ is a manifold (without boundary), then $X \times Y$ is a manifold with boundary $\partial(X \times Y) = \partial X \times Y$.

This differs from the notion of boundary and interior in topological spaces.

Definition (smooth homotopy, smooth isotopy) open_in_new

Let $f,g: X\to Y$ be smooth maps between manifolds ($X$ cannot have boundary but $Y$ can).

A smooth homotopy $H: X\times [0,1]\to Y$ is a smooth map between manifolds which is a homotopy from $f$ to $g$, i.e. $H(x, 0) = f(x)$ and $H(x,1) = g(x)$ for all $x\in X$. If such $H$ exists, we say $f$ and $g$ are smoothly homotopic.

If $f,g$ are diffeomorphisms, a smooth isotopy $H: X \times [0,1]\to Y$ is a smooth homotopy with $x\mapsto H(x,t)$ is a diffeomorphism for each $t\in [0,1]$. If such $H$ exists, we say $f$ and $g$ are smoothly isotopic.

"Smooth homotopic" and "Smooth isotopic" are equivalence relations.

Lemma (homotopy lemma, number of preimages) open_in_new

Let $f,g: X\to Y$ be smooth maps between manifolds ($X$ cannot have boundary but $Y$ can), and $y\in Y$. Suppose

  1. $f$ and $g$ are smoothly homotopic;

  2. $X$ is compact;

  3. $\dim X = \dim Y$; and

  4. $y$ is a regular value of $f$ and $g$.

Then $$ \begin{align*} \# f\inv(y) = \# g\inv(y). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (degree mod 2) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map between manifolds ($X$ cannot have boundary but $Y$ can), and $y, y'\in Y$. Suppose

  1. $\dim X = \dim Y$;

  2. $X$ is compact;

  3. $Y$ is connected; and

  4. $y$ and $y'$ are regular values of $f$.

Then $$ \begin{align*} \# f\inv(y) \equiv \# f\inv(z) \pmod 2. \end{align*} $$

This common value (mod 2) is called the degree mod 2 of $f$ (denoted $\deg_2 f$).

Proof

We showed $\deg_2 f = \deg_2 g$ if $f$ and $g$ are smoothly homotopic.

A manifold $X$ is contractible (i.e. a constant map $X\to X$ is smoothly homotopic to identity) iff $X = \set{*}$.

Definition (intersection number mod 2) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a smooth map between manifolds, and $Z\subseteq Y$ be a submanifold.

Assuming $X$ is compact, $f\pitchfork Z$ and $\dim X + \dim Y = \dim Z$ (so the preimage theorem implies $f\inv(Y)\subset X$ is 0-dimensional so finite), define the intersection number mod 2 $$ \begin{align*} I_2(f,Z) := \# f\inv(Z)\pmod 2. \end{align*} $$

In particular if $X\subset Y$ (taking $f$ to be inclusion), $$ \begin{align*} I_2(X,Z) := \#(X \cap Z) \pmod 2. \end{align*} $$

Fact: if $f$ and $g$ are smoothly homotopic and $f,g\pitchfork Z$, then $I_2(f,Z) = I_2(g,Z)$.

If $f$ is not transversal to $Z$, perturb $f$ via a smooth homotopy.

Definition (abstract manifold) open_in_new

A (smooth) $k$-dimensional manifold is a Hausdorff, second countable topological space $X$ together with a collection of maps $ϕ_α: U_α \to X$ satisfying the following:

  1. $U_α \subset \R^k$ open and $ϕ_α: U_α \to ϕ_α(U_α) =: V_α$ is a homeomorphism. (In particular $V_α$ is open.) Define $ψ_α := ϕ_α\inv: V_α \to U_α$, called a chart.

  2. $\bigcup_{α} V_α = X$.

  3. Letting $V_{αβ} := V_α \cap V_{β}$, the map $ϕ_β\inv \circ ϕ_α := ϕ_α\inv(V_{αβ}) \to ϕ_β\inv(V_{αβ})$ between open sets of $\R^k$ is smooth.

  4. The atlas is "maximal": suppose $\tilde ϕ: \tilde U\to X$ is such that $\set{ϕ_α}_α \cup \set{\tilde ϕ}$ still satisfies 1, 2, 3, then $\tilde ϕ = ϕ_α$ for some $α$.

A collection of $ϕ_α$ satisfying 1, 2, 3 is called a smooth atlas.

Theorem (Whitney, embedding of manifolds) open_in_new

For any $k$-dimensional manifold $X$, there exists a smooth embedding $ϕ: X\to \R^{2k}$ of manifolds (i.e. $ϕ: X\to ϕ(X)$ is a diffeomorphism).
