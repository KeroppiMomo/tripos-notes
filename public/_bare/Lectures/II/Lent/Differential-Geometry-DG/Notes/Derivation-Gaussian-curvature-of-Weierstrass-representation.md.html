Proof 1

Using $λ^2 = \left(\frac{1}{2}|f|(1+|g|^2)\right)^2$, we plug into the formula for isothermal coordinates to get $$ \begin{align*} K ={}& {-\frac{1}{λ^2} ∇(\log λ)} \\ ={}& -\frac{1}{|f|^2(1 + |g|^2)^2} ∇\bigl(\log |f| + \log (1 + |g|^2)\bigr). \end{align*} $$

Note $\log |f|$ is harmonic because $f$ is holomorphic ($\log |f| = \Re \log f$ is the real part of a holomorphic function). Computing the second term gives our formula.

Proof 2

We showed $$ \begin{align*} N = π\inv \circ g \circ ϕ\inv \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \d N = \d π\inv \circ \d g \circ \d ϕ\inv \end{align*} $$ Note every map here is conformal: $ϕ$ is isothermal and $π$ is conformal. Therefore we can choose any orthonormal basis to compute the determinant of their matrices, and their product equals $\det \d N$ up to a sign due to orientation.

$$ \begin{align*} \det [\d ϕ\inv] ={}& \det \pmat{E \\ &G}\inv = λ^{-4} = \left(\frac{1}{2} |f| (1 + |g|^2)\right)^{-2}, \\ \det [\d g] ={}& \det \pmat{\Re g' & -\Im g' \\ \Im g' & \Re g'} = |g'|^2, \\ \det [\d π\inv] ={}& \frac{4}{(1 + |g|^2)^2} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} K = \det \d N = \pm\det[\d ϕ\inv] \det [\d g] \det [\d π\inv] = \pm \frac{16 |g'|^2}{|f|^2 (1 + |g|^2)^4}. \end{align*} $$

As the mean curvature is 0, the principal curvatures opposite sign, so $K < 0$.
