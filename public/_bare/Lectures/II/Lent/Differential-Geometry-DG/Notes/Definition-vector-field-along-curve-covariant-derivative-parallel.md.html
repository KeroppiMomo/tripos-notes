Let $S$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $α: I\to S$ be a curve.

A vector field along $α$ is a smooth function $V: I\to \R^3$ such that $V(t) \in T_{α(t)}S$.

The covariant derivative of $V$ is a vector field $\mathrm{D}V/\d t$ along $α$ given by the orthogonal projection of $\d V/\d t$ onto $T_{α(t)}S$: $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} := \dfd{V}{t} - \left\langle \dfd{V}{t}, N\right\rangle N. \end{align*} $$

We say $V$ is parallel along $α$ if $\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} \equiv 0$.

If $V$ is a unit vector field (i.e. $\langle V, V\rangle = 1$), then $\langle\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}, V\rangle = 0$ (see below). The algebraic value of the covariant derivative $[\mathrm{D}V/\d t]: I \to \R$ is the smooth function satisfying $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} = \left[\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}\right] (N\times V). \end{align*} $$

Leibniz's rule: for two vector fields $V,W$ along $α$, $$ \begin{align*} \dd{t}\langle V,W\rangle = \left\langle \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}, W\right\rangle + \left\langle V, \frac{\mathrm{D}W}{\d t}\right\rangle. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation) Therefore $\langle \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}, V\rangle = 0$ if $\langle V,V\rangle = 1$.

In a local parametrisation $ϕ$, writing $α(t) = ϕ(u(t), v(t))$ and $V(t) = a(t) ϕ_u(u(t), v(t)) + b(t) ϕ_v(u(t), v(t))$, we can express $\mathrm{D}V/\d t$ in the basis $\set{ϕ_u, ϕ_v}$ using the Christoffel symbols: $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t} ={}\quad & (\dot a + Γ_{11}^1 a \dot u + Γ_{12}^1 a \dot v + Γ_{12}^1 b \dot u + Γ_{22}^1 b \dot v) ϕ_u \\ {}+{} &(\dot b + Γ_{11}^2a\dot u + Γ_{12}^2a \dot v + Γ_{12}^2b\dot u + Γ_{22}^2 b \dot v) ϕ_v. \end{align*} $$

If $f: S\to \tilde S$ is an isometry between surfaces in $\R^3$, then $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{D}(f \circ V)}{\d t} = \d f_{α(t)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{D}V}{\d t}\right). \end{align*} $$
