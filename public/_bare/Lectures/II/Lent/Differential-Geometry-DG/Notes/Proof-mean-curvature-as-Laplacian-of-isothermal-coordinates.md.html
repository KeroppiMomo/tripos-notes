Substituting $E = G = λ^2$ and $F = 0$ into the mean curvature formula gives $$ \begin{align*} H = \frac{e + g}{2 λ^2} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \langle ϕ_{uu} + ϕ_{v v}, N\rangle = e + g = 2λ^2H \end{align*} $$ and hence it suffices to show $ϕ_{uu} + ϕ_{v v}$ is along $N$.

We compute $$ \begin{align*} \langle ϕ_{v v}, ϕ_u\rangle ={}& \pdd{v}\underbrace{\langle ϕ_v, ϕ_u\rangle}_{F = 0} - \langle ϕ_{v}, ϕ_{uv}\rangle \\ ={}& -\frac{1}{2}\pdd{u}\underbrace{\langle ϕ_v, ϕ_v\rangle}_{G = E} \\ ={}& -\frac{1}{2} \pdd{u}\langle ϕ_{u},ϕ_u\rangle = - \langle ϕ_{uu}, ϕ_u\rangle \end{align*} $$ and similarly $$ \begin{align*} \langle ϕ_{vv}, ϕ_v\rangle = -\langle ϕ_{uu}, ϕ_v\rangle. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $ϕ_{uu} + ϕ_{v v}$ is orthogonal to $ϕ_u$ and $ϕ_v$ as required.
