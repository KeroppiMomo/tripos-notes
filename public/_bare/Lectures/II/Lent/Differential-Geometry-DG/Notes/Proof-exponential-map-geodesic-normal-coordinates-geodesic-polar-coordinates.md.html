Let $v(θ) = η(1, θ)$ be the vector at an angle $θ$. Then $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(r,θ) = \exp_p(r v(θ)) = γ_{v(θ)}(r). \end{align*} $$

$E = 1$

Note $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_r = \dot γ_{v(θ)}. \end{align*} $$ and $γ_{v(θ)}$ has unit speed.

$F = 0$

The trick is to compute $$ \begin{align*} F_r ={}& \langle ϕ_{rr}, ϕ_θ\rangle + \langle ϕ_r, ϕ_{rθ}\rangle \\ ={}& \langle \underbrace{\ddot γ_{v(θ)}}_{\perp T_{γ_{v(θ)}}S}, ϕ_θ\rangle + \frac{1}{2}\pdd{θ}\underbrace{\langle ϕ_r, ϕ_r\rangle}_{1} \\ ={}& 0 \end{align*} $$ since $γ_{v(θ)}$ is a geodesic. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_θ = \d\exp_p|_{rv(θ)} (rv'(θ)) = r \d \exp_p|_{r v(θ)}(v'(θ)). \end{align*} $$ As $r\to 0$, $\d \exp_p|_{rv(θ)} \to \d \exp_p|_0 = \id$, so $ϕ_θ \to 0$. Hence $F \to 0$, so $F \equiv 0$.

$G \to 0$ as $r\to 0$

We just showed $ϕ_θ\to 0$, so $G = \langle ϕ_θ, ϕ_θ\rangle = 0$.

$\sqrt{G}_r \to 1$ as $r\to 0$

We compute $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{G} = r \; \|\d \exp_p|_{rv(θ)}(v'(θ))\| \end{align*} $$ Take derivative to get $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{G}_r = \|\d \exp_p|_{rv(θ)}(v'(θ))\| + r \pdd{r}\|\d \exp_p|_{rv(θ)}(v'(θ))\|. \end{align*} $$ Things are smooth, so the second term tends to zero. The first term tends to $\|\d \exp_p|_0(v'(θ))\| = \|v'(θ)\| = \|v(θ + π/2)\| = 1$.
