Define the half plane as $\mathbb{H}^k = \sb{(x_1, \ldots, x_k)\in \R^k}{x_k \geq 0}$ and its boundary $\partial \mathbb{H}^k = \sb{(x_1, \ldots, x_k)\in \R^k}{x_k = 0}$.

$X \subseteq \R^N$ is a $k$-dimensional manifold with boundary if for all $x\in X$, there exists a diffeomorphism $ϕ: U\to V$ where $U\subseteq X$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$ and $V \subseteq \mathbb{H}^k$ is an open set.

The boundary of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} \partial X ={}& \sb{x\in X}{\exists \text{chart } ϕ: U\to V\subseteq \mathbb{H}^k: \quad ϕ(x) \in \partial \mathbb{H}^k} \\ ={}& \sb{x\in X}{\forall \text{chart } ϕ: U\to V\subseteq \mathbb{H}^k: \quad ϕ(x) \in \partial \mathbb{H}^k}. \end{align*} $$

The interior of $X$ is $\op{int} X = X \setminus \partial X$.

As before we can WLOG take $V = \R^k$ or $\mathbb{H}^k$.

$\partial X$ is a $(k-1)$-dimensional manifold (without boundary) and $\op{int} X$ is a $k$-dimensional manifold.

Generally if $X$ is manifold with boundary and $Y$ is a manifold (without boundary), then $X \times Y$ is a manifold with boundary $\partial(X \times Y) = \partial X \times Y$.

This differs from the notion of boundary and interior in topological spaces.
