Let $T\subseteq T^\ast$ be consistent. By Lindenbaum, let $T^\ast \supseteq T$ be negation complete and consistent.

Let $v: \mathbf{Fml}\to \mathbf{2}$ be the valuation $$ \begin{align*} v(ϕ) = \begin{cases} 1, &\text{if }T^\ast \vdash ϕ, \\ 0, &\text{if }T^\ast \vdash \neg ϕ. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Negation completeness guarantees $T^\ast\vdash ϕ$ or $T^\ast\vdash \neg ϕ$ happens, and consistency guarantees they can't both happen. Therefore $v$ is well-defined.

$v$ is a homomorphism: it suffices to check $v$ is a homomorphism of implication algebras.

  • $v(\bot) = 0$ as $T^\ast$ is consistent.

  • We need to show $v((ϕ \Rightarrow ψ)) = v(ϕ) \to v(ψ)$. Check the cases:

    • If $v(ψ) = 1$, i.e. there is a $T^\ast$-proof $D$ of $ψ$, then here is a $T^\ast$-proof of $(ϕ\Rightarrow ψ)$:

      • $D$

      • $(ψ \Rightarrow (ϕ \Rightarrow ψ))$ (axiom of type 1)

      • $(ϕ \Rightarrow ψ)$ (modus ponens)

      So $T^\ast \vdash (ϕ \Rightarrow ψ)$.

    • If $v(ϕ) = 0$, i.e. $T^\ast\vdash \neg ϕ$, note ex falso implies $\set{ϕ, \neg ϕ} \vdash ψ$. Applying the deduction theorem twice gives $\emptyset \vdash (\neg ϕ \Rightarrow (ϕ \Rightarrow ψ))$. Then a proof using modus ponens results in $T^\ast \vdash (ϕ \Rightarrow ψ)$.

    • If $v(ϕ) = 1 = v((ϕ \Rightarrow ψ))$, then a proof using modus ponens results in $T^\ast \vdash ψ$, so $v(ψ) = 1$. The contrapositive handles the case $v(ϕ) = 1$ and $v(ψ) = 0$.
