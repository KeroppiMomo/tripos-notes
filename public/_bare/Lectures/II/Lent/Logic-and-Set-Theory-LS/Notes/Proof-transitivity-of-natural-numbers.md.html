$\N$

Prove $y\in x\in \N$ implies $y\in \N$ by induction on $x$.

Vacuously true for $x=\emptyset$.

Suppose this is true for $x$, and consider $y\in s(x) = x\cup \set{x}$.

  • If $y\in x$, then induction hypothesis says $y\in \N$.

  • Otherwise $y=x$ so clearly $y\in \N$.

Any natural number

Prove $z\in y\in x\in \N$ implies $z\in x$ by induction on $x$.

Vacuously true for $x$.

Suppose this is true for $x$, and consider $z\in y\in s(x) = x \cup \set{x}$.

  • If $y\in x$, then induction hypothesis says $z\in x$, so $z\in x\cup \set{x} = s(x)$.

  • Otherwise $y=x$, so $z\in y = x$ and hence $z\in x\cup \set{x} = s(x)$.
