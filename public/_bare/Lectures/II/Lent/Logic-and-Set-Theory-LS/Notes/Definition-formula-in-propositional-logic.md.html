Given a countable set $P$ of propositional letters, the alphabet of propositional logic is $$ \begin{align*} Σ = P \cup \set{\wedge, \vee, \Rightarrow, \neg, \bot, \top, ), (}. \end{align*} $$

The set of formulas (denoted $\mathrm{Fml}$) is the smallest subset of $Σ^\ast$ containing

  • all elements of $P$,

  • $\bot$ and $\top$,

and such that if $ϕ, ψ\in \mathrm{Fml}$, then so are

  • $(ϕ \wedge ψ)$,

  • $(ϕ\vee ψ)$,

  • $(ϕ\Rightarrow ψ)$, and

  • $\neg ϕ$.

Then we obtain a reasoning algebra $\mathbf{Fml} = (\mathrm{Fml}, +, \cdot, \to, -, 1, 0)$ by iteratively defining $ϕ + ψ := (ϕ \vee ψ)$, $ϕ\cdot ψ := (ϕ\wedge ψ)$, $ϕ\to ψ := (ϕ \Rightarrow ψ)$, $-ϕ := \neg ϕ$, $1 = \top$, and $0 = \bot$.

We drop parentheses in informal settings.
