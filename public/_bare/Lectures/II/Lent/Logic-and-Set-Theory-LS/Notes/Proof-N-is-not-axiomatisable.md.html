Any axiomatisation $T'$ is a subset of its theory $T:=\op{Th}(\mathbf{N})$, so it suffices to produce a model of $T$ which is not isomorphic to $\mathbf{N}$.

Similar to our previous proof: introduce a new signature $a_+$ by adding uncountably many constant symbols $$ \begin{align*} \sb{c_k}{k\in K} \end{align*} $$ where $K$ is uncountable. Define $$ \begin{align*} ψ_{kl} = \neg \ c_k=c_l, \quad S = \sb{ψ_{kl}}{k,l\in K, k\neq l}. \end{align*} $$

We claim $T \cup S$ is satisfiable. Any finite subset $T_0 \subseteq T \cup S$ involves only finitely many $c_k$'s, say $c_{k_0}, \ldots, c_{k_{n-1}}$, so expanding $\mathbf{N}$ to an $a_+$-structure $\mathbf{N}_+$ by interpreting the constant symbols $c_{k_0}, \ldots, c_{k_{n-1}}$ as $0, \ldots, n-1\in \N$ gives a model $\mathbf{N}_+$ of $T_0$.

Then a model of $T \cup S$ is an uncountable model of $T$, which is not isomorphic to $\mathbf{N}$.
