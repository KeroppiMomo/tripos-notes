Let $D = ϕ_0 ϕ_1 \ldots ϕ_n$ be a $T$-proof, and $v$ be a model of $T$. We will prove $v(ϕ_i) = 1$ by induction on $i$.

Suppose $v(ϕ_j) = 1$ for all $j<i$. Consider the condition $ϕ_i$ that satisfies:

  • $A_T$: if $ϕ_i\in T$, then by definition of a model, $v(ϕ_i) = 1$.

  • $C$: if there exists $j,k<i$ such that $ϕ_j = (ϕ_k \Rightarrow ϕ_i)$, then $v(ϕ_j) = 1 = v(ϕ_k)$ by the induction hypothesis, so by the definition of a valuation, $v(ϕ_j) = v(ϕ_k) \to v(ϕ_i)$. Thus $v(ϕ_i) = 1$ by the definition of $\to$ in $\mathbf{2}$.

  • $B$:

    • If $ϕ_i$ is an axiom of type 4, we noted $t(ϕ)$ and $ϕ$ are logically equivalent, so $ϕ_i$ is valid.

    • Otherwise, it suffices to check the following Boolean identities $$ \begin{align*} 1 &= x \to (y\to x), \\ 1 &= (x\to (y\to z)) \to ((x\to y)\to (x\to z)) \\ 1 &= (((x\to 0)\to 0)\to x) \end{align*} $$ which you can do by computing truth tables.
