Work in $\mathsf{FST}$ + $\mathsf{Inf}$.

A set $X$ is finite if there exists $n\in{}$$\N$ such that $X$ is in bijection with $n$; otherwise $X$ is infinite.

$X$ is countable if there exists an injection $X \hookrightarrow \N$; otherwise $X$ is uncountable.

$X$ is countably infinite if there exists a bijection $X\to \N$.
