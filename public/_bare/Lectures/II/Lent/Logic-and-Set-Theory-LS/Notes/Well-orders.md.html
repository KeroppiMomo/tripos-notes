Theorem (order induction and well-foundedness) open_in_new

Let $\mathbf{X} = (X, <)$ be a strict partial order. Write $\op{pred}(x) := \sb{z\in X}{z<x}$ for $x\in X$.

$\mathbf{X}$ is well-founded iff it satisfies order induction, i.e. for all $A\subseteq X$, if there holds $$ \begin{align*} \forall x\in X (\op{pred}(x) \subseteq A \implies x\in A), \end{align*} $$ then $A = X$.

Proof

Theorem (recursion) open_in_new

Work in finite set theory.

Let $(X, <)$ be a well-founded strict partial order and $ϕ$ be an $\in$-formula with $\op{free}(ϕ) = \set{a,b,c}$. Suppose

  • $ϕ$ is functional: for all $a,b\in X$ and $c,c'$ sets, if $ϕ(a,b,c)$ and $ϕ(a,b,c')$ hold, then $c = c'$; and

  • $ϕ$ is total: for all $a,b\in X$, there exists $c$ such that $ϕ(a,b,c)$ holds.

Assume either

  • $\mathsf{Repl}$ holds; or

  • there exists some $r$ such that for all $a,b\in X$, if $ϕ(a,b,c)$ holds, then $c\in r$.

Then there exists a unique function $F$ with domain $X$ such that for all $x\in X$, $$ \begin{align*} F(x) = z \iff ϕ(x, F|_{\op{pred}(x)}, z). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Proposition (transitive closure) open_in_new

Work in $\mathsf{FST}$ + $\mathsf{Inf}$ + $\mathsf{Repl}$.

For all sets $x$, there exists a smallest transitive superset $t \supseteq x$, i.e. if $t'\supseteq x$ is a transitive set, then $t' \supseteq t$. We call $\op{tcl}(x) := t$ the transitive closure of $x$.

Definition (well-order, initial segment) open_in_new

A well-order $\mathbf{X} = (X, <)$ is a well-founded strict linear order.

An initial segment of $\mathbf{X}$ is a subset $A \subseteq X$ such that $\op{pred}(x) \subseteq A$ for all $x\in A$.

$A$ is a proper initial segment iff $A = \op{pred}(x)$ for some $x\in X$. Proof

If $A \subseteq X$ is a subset, then $(A, {<} \cap (A \times A))$ is a wellorder.

Definition (sum of well-orders) open_in_new

Let $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ be well-orders. Assume $X$ and $Y$ are disjoint.

Their sum is $\mathbf{X} \oplus \mathbf{Y} := (X \sqcup Y, <^+)$, where $$ \begin{align*} a <^+ b :\iff \begin{cases} a\in X, b\in X, a<b, & \text{or} \\ a\in Y, b\in Y, a<b, & \text{or} \\ a\in X, b\in Y. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ This is a well-order.

Writing $\mathbf{1} = (1, <)$, $s(X)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbf{X} \oplus \mathbf{1}$.

Definition (product of well-orders) open_in_new

Let $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ be well-orders.

Their product is $\mathbf{X} \otimes \mathbf{Y} := (X \times Y, <^\times)$, where $<^\times$ follows reverse lexicographical order: $$ \begin{align*} (x,y)<^\times (x', y') :\iff y < y' \vee (y = y' \wedge x< x'). \end{align*} $$ This is a well-order.

Proposition (uniqueness of isomorphisms between well-orders) open_in_new

Let $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ be well-orders, and let $A,B \subseteq Y$ be initial segments.

If $f: \mathbf{X} \to \mathbf{A}$ and $g: \mathbf{X} \to \mathbf{B}$ are isomorphisms of well-orders, then $A = B$ and $f = g$.

Proof

This shows a well-order is not isomorphic to any proper initial segment.

Theorem (fundamental theorem of well-orders) open_in_new

Let $A$ be a set of well-orders such that no two distinct elements are isomorphic.

Then $(A, \prec)$ is a well-order, where $\mathbf{X}\prec \mathbf{Y}$ iff there is an isomorphism from $\mathbf{X}$ to a proper initial segment of $\mathbf{Y}$.
