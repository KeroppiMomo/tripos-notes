Suppose not, so there is some $ϕ\in \mathrm{Fml}^a$ such that $T \not\vdash ϕ$ and $T \not\vdash \neg ϕ$. Then (by deduction theorem) $T \cup \set{ϕ}$ and $T \cup \set{\neg ϕ}$ are both consistent. They both have models, which cannot be finite because $T$ has no finite model. By the upward and downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorems, there are models $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ of $T\cup \set{ϕ}$ and $T \cup \set{\neg ϕ}$ respectively of cardinality $κ$. But then they are isomorphic with $\mathbf{X} \vDash ϕ$ and $\mathbf{Y}\vDash \neg ϕ$, contradicting the [[FILE NOT FOUND: Lemma (isomorphism lemma)]].
