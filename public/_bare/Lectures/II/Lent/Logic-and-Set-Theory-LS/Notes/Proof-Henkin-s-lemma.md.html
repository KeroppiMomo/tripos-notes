By implicativity, we only need to consider implicative formulae. Induct on $ϕ\in \mathrm{IFml}^a$.

  • The case $ϕ = \bot$ follows from consistency of $T$.

  • The case $ϕ = (t=t')$ or $ϕ = rt_0 \ldots t_{n-1}$ is by definition of $\mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H}$.

  • If $ϕ = (ψ \Rightarrow χ)$, $$ \begin{align*} \mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H} \vDash (ψ \Rightarrow χ) &\iff \mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H} \vDash \neg ψ \text{ or }\mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H} \vDash χ &&\text{induction hypothesis}\\ &\iff T \vdash \neg ψ \text{ or } T \vdash χ \\ &\iff T \vdash (ψ \Rightarrow χ) &&\text{propositional tautology}. \end{align*} $$

  • If $ϕ = \forall x ψ$, first notice by the substitution lemma, $$ \begin{align*} \mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H} \vDash \forall x ψ &\iff \mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H}\tfrac{[t]}{x} \vDash ψ \quad \text{for all $t\in \mathrm{Term}^a$} \\ &\iff \mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H} \vDash ψ\tfrac{t}{x}\quad \text{for all $t\in \mathrm{Term}^a$}. \end{align*} $$

    If this does not hold, i.e. there is some $t\in \mathrm{Term}^a$ such that $\mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H}\vDash ψ\frac{t}{x}$, then induction hypothesis says $T \vdash ψ\frac{t}{x}$. By universality, $T \not\vdash \forall x ψ$.

    Conversely if $T \not\vdash \forall x ψ$, by negation completeness, $T \vdash \neg(\forall xψ)$, or equivalently (by implicativity) $T \vdash \exists x \neg ψ$. Find a witness $t\in \mathrm{Term}^a$ such that $T \vdash (\exists x \neg ψ) \Rightarrow (\neg ψ \frac{t}{x})$. By modus ponens, $T \vdash \neg ψ \frac{t}{x}$. Induction hypothesis then says $\mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H}\vDash \neg ψ\frac{t}{x}$, so $\mathbf{H}, \mathfrak{I}^\mathbf{H}\not\vDash \forall xψ$.
