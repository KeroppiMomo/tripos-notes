If $f$ is a homomorphism $\mathbf{A}^+\to \mathbf{B}^+$, then the claim follows from $\mathbf{A}$ being a reduct of $\mathbf{A}^+$ and $\mathbf{B}$ being a reduct of $\mathbf{B}^+$

If $f$ is a homomorphism $\mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{B}$, then the claim follows from equational definability: e.g. $$ \begin{align*} f(x + y) ={}& f((x\to 0)\to y) &&\text{definition} \\ ={}& ((f(x) \to f(0))\to f(y)) &&\text{homomorphism} \\ ={}& ((f(x) \to 0) \to f(y)) &&\text{homomorphism}\\ ={}& f(x) + f(y) &&\text{definition}. \end{align*} $$
