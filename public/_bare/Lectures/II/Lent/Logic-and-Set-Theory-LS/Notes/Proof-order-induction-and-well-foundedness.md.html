By definition, $x$ is $<$-minimal in $X\setminus A$ iff $\op{pred}(x) \subseteq A$ and $x\not\in A$.

The following are equivalent:

  • $X$ does not satisfy order induction.

  • There exists $A \subsetneq X$ such that for all $x\in X$, if $\op{pred}(x) \subseteq A$, then $x\in A$.

  • There exists $A \subsetneq X$ such that $X\setminus A$ has no $<$-minimal element.

  • There exists $\emptyset\neq B \subseteq X$ such that $B$ has no $<$-minimal element.

  • $X$ is not well-founded.
