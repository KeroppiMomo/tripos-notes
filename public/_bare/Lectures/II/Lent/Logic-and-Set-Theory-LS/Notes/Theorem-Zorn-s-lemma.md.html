Let $\mathbf{P} = (P, \leq)$ be a partial order.

Say $p\in P$ is an (upper) bound of a subset $A\subseteq P$ if $a\leq p$ for all $a\in A$. If such $p$ exists, say $A$ is bounded.

A subset $A \subseteq P$ is a chain if $(A, \leq)$ is a linear order. $P$ is said to be chain-complete if all chains are bounded.

Zorn's lemma $\mathsf{ZL}$ is the statement "all non-empty chain-complete partial orders have a maximal element".

In $\mathsf{FST}$ + $\mathsf{Repl}$, $\mathsf{ZL}$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{AC}$.
