Proposition (simply periodic analytic function) open_in_new

Let $f: \C/\Z\to \C_\infty$ be a meromorphic function (or equivalently, let $f: \C\to C_\infty$ be a $\Z$-periodic meromorphic function).

If $f$ is analytic on $\set{A < \Im z < B}$, then there $f$ has a Fourier series representation $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n e^{2πiz}, \end{align*} $$ uniformly convergent on compact sets.

In particular, all analytic $\C/\Z\to \C_\infty$ meromorphic functions are given by a Fourier series $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n e^{2πi z}$.

Definition (Weierstrass elliptic function) open_in_new

Given a lattice $Λ = \Zω_1 + \Zω_2 \subseteq \C$ ($ω_1, ω_2\neq 0$ and $ω_1/ω_2\not\in \R$), define the Weierstrass $\wp$-function $\wp: \C\to \C_\infty$ by $$ \begin{align*} \wp(z) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \sum_{ω\in Λ\setminus \set{0}} \Bigl(\frac{1}{(z-ω)^2} - \frac{1}{ω^2}\Bigr). \end{align*} $$

The series converges locally absolutely uniformly on $\C\setminus Λ$. This defines an analytic function on $\C\setminus Λ$ having double poles at each $ω\in Λ$ with residue 0. (Proof)

$\wp$ is even and $Λ$-periodic, i.e. $\wp(z) = \wp(-z)$ and $\wp(z + ω) = \wp(z)$ for all $ω\in Λ$. It has derivative $$ \begin{align*} \wp'(z) = -2 \sum_{ω\in Λ} \frac{1}{(z-ω)^3}. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

The degree of $\wp: \C/Λ \to \C_\infty$ is 2, and the ramification points are $0$, $ω_1/2$, $ω_2/2$ and $ω_3/2 := (ω_1+ω_2)/2$, with ramification degree 2. The branch points $\infty = \wp(0)$, $e_i := \wp(ω_i/2)$ are distinct. (Proof)

Theorem (classification of elliptic functions) open_in_new

Let $Λ = \Zω_1 + \Z ω_2\subseteq \C$ be a lattice ($ω_1, ω_2\neq 0$, $ω_1/ω_2\not\in \R$).

Let $f$ be an elliptic function, i.e. a meromorphic function $f: \C/Λ \to \C_\infty$, or equivalently, a meromorphic function $f: \C\to \C_\infty$ with periodic $Λ$.

There exists rational functions $Q_1, Q_2$ such that $$ \begin{align*} f = Q_1 \circ \wp + \wp' \cdot (Q_2 \circ \wp) \end{align*} $$ where $\wp$ is the Weierstrass elliptic function. In more sophisticated language, the field of elliptic functions is $\C(\wp, \wp')$.

In particular, there is an algebraic relation $$ \begin{align*} (\wp(z)')^2 = 4(\wp(z) - e_1)(\wp(z) - e_2)(\wp(z) - e_3) = 4\wp^3 + B\wp + C. \end{align*} $$

Proof
