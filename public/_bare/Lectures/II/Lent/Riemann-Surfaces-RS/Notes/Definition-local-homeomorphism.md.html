Let $X$ and $\tilde X$ be topological spaces.

$p: \tilde X\to X$ is a local homeomorphism if for all $\tilde x\in \tilde X$, there exists an open neighbourhood $\tilde U \ni \tilde x$ such that $p|_{\tilde U}: \tilde U \to p(\tilde U)$ is a homeomorphism.

A (regular) covering space is an example. If $X, \tilde X$ are Hausdorff and path-connected, this is known as a "covering space" in this course.
