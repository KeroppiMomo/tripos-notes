Definition (analytic continuation, complete analytic function) open_in_new

An analytic function element is a pair $(f, D)$ where $D \subset \C$ is a domain and $f: D\to \C$ is analytic.

Say $(g,E)$ is a direct analytic continuation of $(f,D)$ (written $(f,D)\sim (g,E)$) if $D \cap E \neq \emptyset$ and $f|_{D\cap E} = g|_{D\cap E}$.

Say that $(g,E)$ is an analytic continuation of $(f,D)$ (written $(f, D) \approx (g, E)$) if there exists function elements $(f_i, D_i)$ for $0\leq i\leq n$ with $(f_0, D_0) = (f, D), (f_n, D_n) = (g,E)$ and $(f_i, D_i) \sim (f_{i+1}, D_{i+1})$ for all $0\leq i<n$. This is an equivalence relation.

If $γ: [0,1]\to \C$ is continuous, say $(g,E)$ is an analytic continuation of $(f,D)$ along $γ$ if

  • $γ(i/n) \in D_i$ for all $i=0,1,\ldots,n$, and

  • $γ([i/n, (i+1)/n]) \subseteq D_i \cup D_{i+1}$ for all $i=0,1,\ldots,n-1$.

A complete analytic function is an equivalence class under $\approx$.

Definition (Riemann surface) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space which is Hausdorff and connected.

An analytic chart on $X$ is a pair $(U, ϕ)$ where $U \subseteq X$ is non-empty and open and $ϕ: U \to \C$ is a homeomorphism onto an open set of $\C$.

An atlas on $X$ is a set $\mathcal{A}$ of charts such that

  1. $\bigcup_{(U,ϕ)\in \mathcal{A}} U = X$; and

  2. for any $(U_1, ϕ_1), (U_2, ϕ_2) \in \mathcal{A}$ with $U_1 \cap U_2 \neq \emptyset$, the transition function $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1 \circ ϕ_2\inv: ϕ_2(U_1 \cap U_2) \to ϕ_1(U_1 \cap U_2) \end{align*} $$ is analytic.

Two atlases $\mathcal{A}$, $\mathcal{A}'$ on $X$ are equivalent if $\mathcal{A}\cup \mathcal{A}'$ is also an atlas. This is an equivalence relation.

A conformal structure on $X$ is an equivalence class of atlases.

A Riemann surface is a Hausdorff, connected topological space with a conformal structure.

Radó's theorem: in fact all Riemann surfaces are second countable.

Definition (analytic map on Riemann surfaces) open_in_new

Let $X, Y$ be Riemann surfaces with atlases $\mathcal{A}_X$, $\mathcal{A}_Y$.

A continuous function $f: X\to Y$ is analytic if for all $x\in X$, there exists charts $(U, ϕ)\in \mathcal{A}_X$ and $(\tilde U, \tilde ϕ)\in \mathcal{A}_Y$ with $x\in U$, $f(x)\in \tilde U$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \tilde ϕ\circ f\circ ϕ\inv: ϕ(U \cap f\inv(\tilde U)) \to \tilde ϕ(f(U) \cap \tilde U) \end{align*} $$ is analytic.

A conformal equivalence is an analytic bijection $X\to Y$ with an analytic inverse.

As transition functions are analytic, this holds for all pairs of charts. (Analogous proof)

Definition (meromorphic on Riemann surface) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a Riemann surface.

A meromorphic function on $X$ is an analytic map $X\to \C_\infty$ which is not identically $\infty$.

Here $\C_\infty$ is the Riemann sphere $\C \cup \set{\infty}$, homeomorphic to $S^2$ via the stereographic projection and with the conformal structure $$ \begin{align*} \set{(\C, z \mapsto z), (\C^\times \cup \set{\infty}, (z \mapsto 1/z, \infty \mapsto 0))}. \end{align*} $$

This agrees with our previous definition of meromorphic functions on $U \subseteq \C$.

Theorem (inverse function theorem for analytic functions) open_in_new

Let $f: D\to \C$ be a holomorphic function on a domain $D\subseteq \C$.

If $f'(z_0) \neq 0$, then there exist open neighbourhoods $U\subseteq D$ of $z_0$ and $V$ of $f(z_0)$ such that $f|_U: U\to V$ is biholomorphic, i.e. $f|_U$ has an holomorphic inverse.

Proof

If $f$ is a holomorphic bijection, then $f\inv$ is holomorphic as well by applying this result to a small neighbourhood.

Theorem (local mapping on Riemann surfaces) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be an analytic map between Riemann surfaces, $x\in X$ and $y = f(x)\in Y$. Suppose $f$ is not constant.

There exists some $d\geq 1$ and charts $(U, ϕ)$ near $x$ and $(\tilde U, \tilde ϕ)$ near $y$ such that $f(U) \subseteq \tilde U$, $ϕ(x) = 0 = \tilde ϕ(y)$ and $$ \begin{align*} \tilde ϕ\circ f \circ ϕ\inv(z) = z^d. \end{align*} $$

In particular if $f$ is injective in some neighbourhood of $x$, then $d = 1$ and $f|_U: U \to f(U)$ is a conformal equivalence.

$d$ is called the ramification degree or local degree of $f$ at $x$. If $d_x > 1$, we say $x$ is a ramification point of $f$, and $f(x)$ a branch point of $f$.

Proof

See also ramification degree in algebraic geometry.

This shows the set of ramification point is discrete in $X$. If $X$ is compact, it is finite.

For $f: X\to \C_\infty$ meromorphic, the ramification degree at a zero is the order of vanishing, and the ramification degree at a pole is the order of the pole.

Corollary (open mapping theorem on Riemann surfaces) open_in_new

Any non-constant analytic map $f: X\to Y$ between Riemann surfaces is open, i.e. sends open sets to open sets.

Proof

If $X$ is compact, then $f(X)$ is both open and closed, so $f$ is surjective by the connectedness of $Y$. Hence if $Y$ is not compact, then there is no non-constant analytic map $f: X\to Y$.

Definition (local homeomorphism) open_in_new

Let $X$ and $\tilde X$ be topological spaces.

$p: \tilde X\to X$ is a local homeomorphism if for all $\tilde x\in \tilde X$, there exists an open neighbourhood $\tilde U \ni \tilde x$ such that $p|_{\tilde U}: \tilde U \to p(\tilde U)$ is a homeomorphism.

A (regular) covering space is an example. If $X, \tilde X$ are Hausdorff and path-connected, this is known as a "covering space" in this course.

Definition (harmonic function) open_in_new

Let $D\subseteq \C$ be a domain. A function $f: D\to \R$ is harmonic if $$ \begin{align*} 0 = Δ f \equiv f_{x x} + f_{y y}. \end{align*} $$

Let $X$ be a Riemann surface. A function $f: X\to \R$ is harmonic if for all charts $(U, ϕ)$ in some atlas, $f \circ ϕ\inv: ϕ(U)\to \R$ is harmonic.

If $D$ is a disc, then $f$ is harmonic iff it is the real part of some analytic function $D\to \C$. (Proof)

Identity principle: $\sb{x\in X}{f(x) = 0}$ either is $X$ or has empty interior.

Definition (Riemann surface of a complete analytic function) open_in_new

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be an complete analytic function.

Define the Riemann surface of $\mathcal{F}$ as the quotient space $R_\mathcal{F} := S/{\sim}$ where $$ \begin{align*} S := \bigsqcup_{(f,D)\in \mathcal{F}} D \times \set{f} \end{align*} $$ and $\sim$ is the equivalence relation on $S$ given by $$ \begin{align*} (z, f) \sim (w,g) :\iff \text{$z = w$ and $f=g$ on a neighbourhood of $z$}. \end{align*} $$

This gives a well-defined continuous map $$ \begin{align*} π: R_\mathcal{F} &\to \C \\ [(z,f)] &\mapsto z. \end{align*} $$

A section of $π$ over $D$ is a function $s: D\to R_\mathcal{F}$ such that $π \circ s = \id$.

For $(f,D)\in \mathcal{F}$, let $$ \begin{align*} [f,D] := q(D\times \set{f})\subseteq R_\mathcal{F} \end{align*} $$ where $q$ is the quotient map, and a section $$ \begin{align*} s_f := π|_{[f,D]}\inv: D \to [f,D] \hookrightarrow R_\mathcal{F}. \end{align*} $$

$\sb{[f,D]}{(f,D)\in \mathcal{F}}$ is an open cover. (Proof)

$π|_{[f,D]}: [f,D]\to D$ and $q|_{D \times \set{f}}: D \times \set{f} \to [f,D]$ are homeomorphisms. (Proof)

$R_\mathcal{F}$ is Hausdorff and path-connected. (Proof)

Therefore there is a unique conformal structure on $R_\mathcal{F}$ (with charts $π|_{[f,D]}$) so that $π$ is analytic. Then there is a unique analytic function $F: R_\mathcal{F}\to \C$ such that $F|_{[f,D]} = f \circ π|_{[f,D]}$, given by $$ \begin{align*} F: R_\mathcal{F} &\to \C \\ [(z,f)] &\mapsto f(z) \end{align*} $$ Note $f = F \circ s_f$.

There is a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F} &\xrightarrow{\sim} \set{\text{continous section $s: D\to R_\mathcal{F}$ of $π$ over a domain $D\subseteq \C$}} \\ (f,D) &\mapsto s_f. \end{align*} $$ Proof

Definition (germs of analytic functions) open_in_new

Define the space of germs $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{G} ={}& \biggl(\bigsqcup_{(f,D)} D \times \set{f}\biggr)/{\sim} \end{align*} $$ where the union ranges over all function elements $(f,D)$ and $\sim$ is defined as before, and the local homeomorphism $$ \begin{alignat*}{2} π_\mathcal{G} : &&\mathcal{G}&\to \C \\ &&[(z,f)] &\mapsto z. \end{alignat*} $$

A germ of analytic function is a set of equivalence classes of the form $π_\mathcal{G}\inv(z_0)$.

Observe $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{G} = \bigsqcup_{\mathcal{F}} R_\mathcal{F} \end{align*} $$ the disjoint union of the Riemann surfaces of all complete analytic functions., so $\mathcal{G}$ is Hausdorff and disconnected.

For $z_0\in \C$, $(f,D)$ and $(g,E)$ determine same germ iff $f$ and $g$ agree on a neighbourhood of $z_0$, so $$ \begin{align*} π_\mathcal{G}\inv(z_0) \simeq \set{\text{power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n$ with a non-zero radius of convergence}}. \end{align*} $$

As before there is a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{analytic function $D\to \C$}} &\xrightarrow{\sim} \set{\text{continuous section of $π_\mathcal{G}$ over a domain $D\subseteq \C$}} \\ f &\mapsto π_{\mathcal{G}}|_{[f,D]}\inv. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (valency theorem) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a non-constant analytic map between Riemann surfaces $X,Y$ which are compact.

There exists an integer $n\geq 1$ such that for all $y\in Y$, $$ \begin{align*} n = \sum_{x\in f\inv(y)} d_x \end{align*} $$ where $d_x$ is the ramification degree of $f$ at $x$.

$n$ is called the degree or valency of $f$, and we say $X$ is a branched cover of $Y$.

Proof

For meromorphic $f: X\to C_\infty$, considering preimages of $0$ and $\infty$ gives $$ \begin{align*} \#\text{zeros of $f$} = \#\text{poles of $f$} \end{align*} $$ counted with multiplicity.

This gives another proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra.

Definition (subdivision) open_in_new

A subdivision of a compact topological surface $Σ$ comprises

  1. a finite set $V \subseteq Σ$ of vertices;

  2. a finite set $\set{e_i: \text{continuous }[0, 1] \to Σ}_{i\in E}$ of edges,

such that

  • each $e_i$ is injective on $(0,1)$;

  • $e_i\inv(V) = \set{0,1}$;

  • $e_i(0,1)$ and $e_j(0,1)$ are disjoint for $i\neq j$; and

  • each connected component of $Σ \setminus (\bigcup_{i} e_i[0,1] \cup V)$ is homeomorphic to an open disc, called an open face. Call a face the closure of such a component.

Definition (triangulation) open_in_new

A subdivision is a triangulation if each closed face contains exactly three edges, and two closed faces are either disjoint or meet at an edge or a vertex.

Theorem (invariant of Euler characteristic) open_in_new

  1. Every compact topological surface $Σ$ admits a subdivision (and in fact triangulation).

  2. The Euler characteristic $χ(Σ)$ of $Σ$ does not depend on the choice of subdivision, i.e. it defines a topological invariant of the surface $Σ$.

Theorem (Riemann-Hurwitz formula in Riemann Surfaces) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a non-constant analytic map between Riemann surfaces $X$ and $Y$ that are compact.

Their Euler characteristics satisfy $$ \begin{align*} χ(X) = n\cdot χ(Y) - \sum_{x\in X} (d_x - 1) \end{align*} $$ where $n$ is the degree of $f$ and $d_x$ is the ramification degree of $f$ at $x$.

Proof

If $f$ exists, then the genera satisfy $g(X) \geq g(Y)$.

If $g(X) = g(Y) > 1$, then $n=1$ so $f$ is an analytic isomorphism.

Definition (Weierstrass elliptic function) open_in_new

Given a lattice $Λ = \Zω_1 + \Zω_2 \subseteq \C$ ($ω_1, ω_2\neq 0$ and $ω_1/ω_2\not\in \R$), define the Weierstrass $\wp$-function $\wp: \C\to \C_\infty$ by $$ \begin{align*} \wp(z) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \sum_{ω\in Λ\setminus \set{0}} \Bigl(\frac{1}{(z-ω)^2} - \frac{1}{ω^2}\Bigr). \end{align*} $$

The series converges locally absolutely uniformly on $\C\setminus Λ$. This defines an analytic function on $\C\setminus Λ$ having double poles at each $ω\in Λ$ with residue 0. (Proof)

$\wp$ is even and $Λ$-periodic, i.e. $\wp(z) = \wp(-z)$ and $\wp(z + ω) = \wp(z)$ for all $ω\in Λ$. It has derivative $$ \begin{align*} \wp(z) = -2 \sum_{ω\in Λ} \frac{1}{(z-ω)^3}. \end{align*} $$ (Proof)

The degree of $\wp: \C/Λ \to \C_\infty$ is 2, and the ramification points are $0$, $ω_1/2$, $ω_2/2$ and $ω_3/2 := (ω_1+ω_2)/2$, with ramification degree 2. The branch points $\infty = \wp(0)$, $e_i := \wp(ω_i/2)$ are distinct. (Proof)

Lemma (homotopy lifting lemma, monodromy theorem) open_in_new

Let $p: \tilde X \to X$ be a covering space, $H: Y\times [0,1]\to X$ be a homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$.

For a lift $\tilde f_0$ of $f_0$, there exists a unique homotopy $\tilde H: Y \times [0,1] \to \tilde X$ such that

  • $\tilde H(y, 0) = \tilde f_0(y)$ for all $y\in Y$; and

  • $p\circ \tilde H = H$.

Alternatively, we can take $p: \tilde X\to X$ to be a local homeomorphism and $\tilde X$ to be Hausdorff, and assume any path from $H(y, 0)$ to $H(y,1)$ has a lift for all $y\in Y$.

Proof

This is known as the monodromy theorem in Riemann surfaces.

Taking $Y = \set{*}$, we see that given a path $γ: [0,1] \to X$ and a starting point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(γ(0))$, there exists a unique lift $\tilde γ: [0,1] \to \tilde X$ of $γ$ starting at $\tilde x_0$.

If $γ\simeq γ'$ as paths from $x_0$ and $x_1$, and $\tilde γ,\tilde γ'$ are their lifts starting at a common point $\tilde x_0 \in p\inv(x_0)$, then $\tilde γ\simeq \tilde γ'$ as paths. (Proof)

If $x_0\in X$ and $\tilde x_0\in p\inv(x_0)$, then $p_\ast$${}: π_{1}(\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to π_1(X, x_0)$ is an injection. (Proof)

Definition (properly discontinuous action) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space and $G$ be a group acting on $X$ by homeomorphisms.

We say the action is properly discontinuous if for all compact $K\subseteq X$, the set $\sb{g\in G}{gK\cap K \neq \emptyset}$ is finite.

Note "properly discontinuous" can mean many different things.

Proposition (Hausdorff covering space action) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a topological space and $G$ is a group acting on $X$ by homeomorphisms.

Suppose

  1. $X$ is Hausdorff;

  2. $X$ is locally compact, i.e. any $x\in X$ has a compact neighbourhood;

  3. the action is free, i.e. $\op{Stab}$$_G(x) = \set{e}$ for all $x\in X$; and

  4. the action is properly discontinuous, i.e. for all compact $K\subseteq X$, the set $\sb{g\in G}{gK\cap K \neq \emptyset}$ is finite.

Then the quotient space $X\backslash G$ is Hausdorff and $q: X\to X\backslash G$ is a covering space.

Proof

If $X$ is a Riemann surface and $G$ acts by conformal equivalences, then 1 and 2 are automatically satisfied. Then $X\backslash G$ has a unique conformal structure such that $q$ is analytic.

Theorem (uniformisation theorem) open_in_new

Every simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to one of the following:

  1. the Riemann sphere $\C_\infty$;

  2. the complex plane $\C$;

  3. the unit disc $\mathbb{D}$.

These are not conformally equivalent: $\C_\infty$ is compact, and the other two are not. There cannot be a conformal equivalence $\C \to \mathcal{D}$ by Liouville's theorem.

Any Riemann surface homeomorphic to $\C_\infty$ is conformally equivalent to $\C_\infty$ by compactness.

Corollary (classification of Riemann surfaces) open_in_new

Every Riemann surface $X$ is conformally equivalent to $$ \begin{align*} G \backslash \tilde X \end{align*} $$ where $\tilde X$ is one of $\C_\infty$, $\C$ or $\mathbb{D}$, and $G$ is properly discontinuous group of conformal automorphisms of $\tilde X$. We say $X$ is uniformised by $\tilde X$.

  1. If $\tilde X\isom \C_\infty$, then $X \isom \C_\infty$.

  2. If $\tilde X\isom \C$, then $X \isom \C$, $\C/\Z$ or $\C/Λ$ where $Λ$ is a lattice.

  3. If $\tilde X\isom \mathbb{D}$, then $X\isom G\backslash \mathbb{H}$ where $G$ is a properly discontinuous subgroup of $\op{PSL}_2(\R) = \op{SL}_2(\R)/\set{\pm 1}$, acting on the upper half plane $\mathbb{H}$ by Möbius transformations.

Moreover, $X$ is uniformised by at most one of $\C_\infty, \C, \mathbb{D}$.

Proof

Therefore all compact Riemann surfaces of genus $>1$ is uniformised by $\mathbb{D}$.
