Technique: use the fact that all analytic functions from compact Riemann surfaces to $\C$ are constant.

Any function $f: \C/Λ\to \C_\infty$ can be decomposed into an even and odd part: $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \underbrace{\frac{f(z) + f(-z)}{2}}_{\text{even}} + \underbrace{\frac{f(z) - f(-z)}{2}}_{\text{odd}}. \end{align*} $$ If $f$ is odd and elliptic, then since $\wp'$ is odd also, $f/\wp'$ is even. Therefore it suffices to show for any even elliptic $f$, there is a rational function $Q$ such that $f = Q \circ \wp$.

Since $f$ is even, its zeros are of the form $\pm a_1, \pm a_2, \ldots, \pm a_n$ and poles are of the form $\pm b_1, \pm b_2, \ldots, \pm b_n$. First assume all $\pm a_i$ and $\pm b_i$ are unramified for both $f$ and $\wp$. Then $$ \begin{align*} g(z) := \frac{(\wp(z) - \wp(a_1)) \ldots (\wp(z) - \wp(a_n))}{(\wp(z) - \wp(b_1))\ldots (\wp(z) - \wp(b_n))} \end{align*} $$ also has simple zeros $\pm a_1, \ldots, \pm a_n$ and simple poles $\pm b_1, \ldots, \pm b_n$. Therefore $f/g: \C/Λ\to \C$ is an analytic function, so constant. Hence $f$ is a rational function in $\wp$.

Now we work around the assumption. Note $f, \wp: \C/Λ \to \C_\infty$ have finitely many ramification points. Choose distinct $c,d\in \C_\infty$ and define $$ \begin{align*} \tilde f(z) := \frac{f(z) - c}{f(z) - d}, \end{align*} $$ Note $\tilde f\inv(0) = f\inv(c)$ and $\tilde f\inv(\infty) = f\inv(d)$. Except a finite number of pairs $(c,d)$, the zeros and poles of $\tilde f$ are unramified for $\tilde f$ and $\wp$. Therefore $\tilde f$ is a rational function in $\wp$, and hence so is $f$.

$f = (\wp')^2$

Recall $\wp'$ has a triple pole at $0$ and a simple root at $ω_1/2, ω_2/2, ω_3/2$. Since $\wp'(ω_i/2) = 0$, $\wp(z) - \wp(ω_i/2)$ has a double pole at $0$ and a double zero at $ω_i/2$. Thus $$ \begin{align*} g(z) := (\wp(z) - \wp(ω_1/2))(\wp(z) - \wp(ω_2/2))(\wp(z) - \wp(ω_3/2)). \end{align*} $$ has a pole at $0$ of order 6, and a double root at each $ω_i/2$. Hence $(\wp')^2 / g: \C/Λ\to \C$ is analytic, thus constant.

To determine the leading two coefficients of $\wp$ in $(\wp')^2$, we compute the Laurent series. Because $\wp(z) - z^{-2}$ and $\wp'(z) + 2z^{-3}$ are analytic at $0$ and vanish at $0$, we get $$ \begin{gather*} \wp(z) = z^{-2} + O(z), \quad \wp(z)^2 = z^{-4} + O(z^{-1}), \quad \wp(z)^3 = z^{-6} + O(z^{-3}), \\ \wp'(z) = -2z^{-3} + O(z), \quad \wp'(z)^2 = 4z^{-6} + O(z^{-2}). \end{gather*} $$ Comparing $z^{-6}$ and $z^{-4}$ terms, we have $$ \begin{align*} (\wp')^2 = 4\wp^3 + 0\wp^2 + B\wp + C. \end{align*} $$
