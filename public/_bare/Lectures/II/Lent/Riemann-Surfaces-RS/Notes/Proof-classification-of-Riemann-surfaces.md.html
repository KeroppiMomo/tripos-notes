Every Riemann surface $X$ is path-connected, locally path-connected and semi-locally simply connected, so it has a universal cover $p: \tilde X\to X$ where $G := π_1(X,x_0)$ acts freely and properly discontinuously on $X$, and $p$ induces a homeomorphism $G\backslash \tilde X\xrightarrow{\sim} X$.

Give $\tilde X$ a conformal structure so that $p$ is analytic. Note $G$ acts as identity in suitable local charts, so by conformal equivalences. Therefore the induced homeomorphism $G \backslash \tilde X \xrightarrow{\sim} X$ is analytic, hence a conformal equivalence. The uniformisation theorem implies $\tilde X$ is conformally equivalent to $\C_\infty$, $\C$ or $\mathbb{D}$.

As $G$ acts faithfully, $G$ embeds as a subgroup of the conformal automorphisms of $\tilde X$.

$\tilde X = \C_\infty$

By sheet 1 Q8, conformal automorphisms of $\C_\infty$ are the Möbius maps. But all Möbius maps have fixed points, so the only subgroup which acts freely on $\C_\infty$ is the trivial group. Hence $X = \C_\infty$.

$\tilde X = \C$

By sheet 1 Q8, conformal automorphisms of $\C$ are of the form $$ \begin{align*} z \mapsto az + b, \qquad a\in \C^\times, b\in \C. \end{align*} $$ If $a\neq 1$, then it has a fixed point. Therefore $G$ is a subgroup of translations. Because the action is properly discontinuous, $G$ is discrete.

By sheet 2 Q7, $G = \set{0}$, $\Zω$ or a lattice $Λ$, so $X \isom \C$, $\C/\Z$ or $\C/Λ$.

$\tilde X = \mathbb{D}$

By IB Complex Analysis sheet 2 Q7, we characterised that all conformal automorphisms of $\mathbb{D}$ are Möbius maps. Under a Möbius map $\mathbb{D}\xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{H}$, all conformal automorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$ are described by $\op{PSL}_2(\R)$.

Mutually exclusive

Suppose $X$ is uniformised by both $\tilde X_1$ and $\tilde X_2$, so there are analytic maps $p_i: \tilde X_i \to X$ for $i=1,2$. By the lifting theorem, $p_1$ lifts to an analytic map $f: \tilde X_1 \to \tilde X_2$.

  • If $\tilde X_1 = \C_\infty$ and $\tilde X_2 = \C$ or $\mathbb{D}$, since $\C_\infty$ is compact, $f$ is constant, so $p_1$ is constant, which is impossible.

  • If $\tilde X_1 = \C$ and $\tilde X_2 = \mathbb{D}$, then by Liouville's theorem, $f$ is constant, which is again impossible.
