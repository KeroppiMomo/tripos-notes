Covering space

Fix $x_0\in X$ and we need to find a evenly covered neighbourhood of $x_0$ under $q: X \to G\backslash X$.

By local compactness, let $x_0\in U\subseteq K$ where $U$ is open and $K$ is compact. Hypothesis says $\sb{g\in G}{gK\cap K\neq \emptyset}$ is finite.

For each $g\in G\setminus \set{e}$, as the action is free, $gx_0 \neq x_0$. Since $X$ is Hausdorff, take $V_g, W_g$ be disjoint open neighbourhoods of $x_0$ and $gx_0$ respectively. Define $$ \begin{align*} U' := U \cap \bigcap_{\substack{g\in G\setminus \set{e}: \\ gK \cap K \neq \emptyset}} V_g \cap g\inv W_g, \end{align*} $$ which is a finite intersection of open sets. Therefore $U'$ is an open neighbourhood of $x_0$.

$U'$ has the property that $gU' \cap U' = \emptyset$ unless $g = e$: indeed for $g\in G$,

  • if $gK\cap K = \emptyset$, then $gU' \cap U' = \emptyset$ as a subset; and

  • if $gK \cap K \neq \emptyset$, then $U' \subseteq V_g$ and $gU' \subseteq W_g$, so $gU' \cap U' = \emptyset$ by construction.

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} q\inv(q(U')) = \bigsqcup_{g\in G} gU' \end{align*} $$ is a disjoint union. $gU'$ is open as $g$ acts by homeomorphisms, so this is an open set, showing $q(U') \subseteq G\backslash X$ is open.

Furthermore, $q$ is an open map: for any open neighbourhood $V \subseteq X$ of $x_0$, we can WLOG assume $U\subseteq V$ above, and get an open neighbourhood $q(x_0)\subseteq q(U') \subseteq q(V)$.

Hence $q|_{gU'}: gU' \to q(U')$ is continuous, open and surjective. It is injective also: if $q(x) = q(y)$ for $x,y\in U'$, then there is $g\in G$ such that $gx = y$, so $gU'\cap U'\neq \emptyset$. Thus $g = e$ and $x = y$. Thus $q|_{gU'}$ is a homeomorphism.

Hausdorff

Practically the same:

Fix $x_1, x_2\in X$ such that $q(x_1) \neq q(x_2)$. By local compactness, find open sets $U_i$ and compact sets $K_i$ such that $x_i \in U_i \subseteq K_i$, for $i=1,2$.

$K_1 \cup K_2$ is a compact set of $X$. By hypothesis, $\sb{g\in G}{g(K_1 \cup K_2)\cap (K_1 \cup K_2) \neq \emptyset}$ is finite, so its subset $\sb{g\in G}{gK_1 \cap K_2 \neq \emptyset}$ is finite also.

For each $g\in G$, because $gx_1 \neq x_2$ and $X$ is Hausdorff, let $V_g$, $W_g$ be disjoint open neighbourhoods of $gx_1$ and $x_2$ respectively. Define $$ \begin{align*} U_1' &:= U_1 \cap \bigcap_{\substack{g\in G\setminus \set{e}: \\ gK_1 \cap K_2 \neq \emptyset}} g\inv V_g, \\ U_2' &:= U_2 \cap \bigcap_{\substack{g\in G\setminus \set{e}: \\ gK_1 \cap K_2 \neq \emptyset}} W_g, \end{align*} $$ which are open neighbourhoods of $x_1$ and $x_2$.

We claim that $gU_1' \cap U_2' = \emptyset$ for all $g\in G$. Indeed

  • if $gK_1 \cap K_2 = \emptyset$, then its subset $gU_1' \cap U_2' = \emptyset$ also; and

  • if $gK_1 \cap K_2 \neq \emptyset$, then $gU_1' \subseteq V_g$ and $U_2' \subseteq W_g$, so $gU_1' \cap U_2' = \emptyset$ by construction.

As before, because $q\inv(q(U_i')) = \bigcup_{g\in G} gU_i'$ is open, $q(U_i')\subseteq G\backslash X$ is an open neighbourhood of $q(x_i)$. Moreover our claim shows $q(U_1') \cap q(U_2') = \emptyset$.
