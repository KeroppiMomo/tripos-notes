Let $X$ be a topological space which is Hausdorff and connected.

An analytic chart on $X$ is a pair $(U, ϕ)$ where $U \subseteq X$ is non-empty and open and $ϕ: U \to \C$ is a homeomorphism onto an open set of $\C$.

An atlas on $X$ is a set $\mathcal{A}$ of charts such that

  1. $\bigcup_{(U,ϕ)\in \mathcal{A}} U = X$; and

  2. for any $(U_1, ϕ_1), (U_2, ϕ_2) \in \mathcal{A}$ with $U_1 \cap U_2 \neq \emptyset$, the transition function $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_1 \circ ϕ_2\inv: ϕ_2(U_1 \cap U_2) \to ϕ_1(U_1 \cap U_2) \end{align*} $$ is analytic.

Two atlases $\mathcal{A}$, $\mathcal{A}'$ on $X$ are equivalent if $\mathcal{A}\cup \mathcal{A}'$ is also an atlas. This is an equivalence relation.

A conformal structure on $X$ is an equivalence class of atlases.

A Riemann surface is a Hausdorff, connected topological space with a conformal structure.

Radó's theorem: in fact all Riemann surfaces are second countable.
