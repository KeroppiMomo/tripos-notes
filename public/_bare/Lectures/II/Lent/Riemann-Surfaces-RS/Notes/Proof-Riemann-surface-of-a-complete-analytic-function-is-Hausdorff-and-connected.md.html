Hausdorff

Let $x = [(z, f)]\in [f,D]$ and $y = [(w,g)]\in [g,E]$ be distinct points on $R_\mathcal{F}$.

If $z \neq w\in \C$, find disjoint open neighbourhoods $D'$ of $z$ and $E'$ of $w$ in $\C$. Then $π\inv(D')$ and $π\inv(E')$ are disjoint open neighbourhoods of $x$ and $y$.

Otherwise if $z=w$ and $f$ and $g$ do not agree on a neighbourhood of $z$, find some open connected neighbourhood $V\subseteq D \cap E$ of $z$ such that $f|_V\neq g|_V$. We claim $[f|_V,V]$ and $[g|_V, V]$ are disjoint open neighbourhoods of $x$ and $y$. If they are not disjoint, say there exists $ζ\in V$ such that $f$ and $g$ agree on a neighbourhood of $ζ$, then by the identity principle, $f|_V = g|_V$, which is a contradiction.

Path-connected

Let $x = [(z,f)]\in [f,D]$ and $y=[(w,g)]\in [g,E]$. First suppose $(f,D) \sim (g,E)$. As $D \cap E \neq \emptyset$ and $D$ and $E$ are path-connected, let $γ_1$ be a path from $z$ to $ζ$ in $D$ and $γ_2$ be a path from $ζ$ to $w$ in $E$, for some $ζ\in D\cap E$. Then $δ_1 := π|_{[f,D]}\inv\circ γ_1$ is a path from $x$ to $π|_{[f,D]}\inv(ζ)$ and $δ_2 := π|_{[g,E]}\inv \circ γ_2$ is a path from $π|_{[g,E]}\inv(ζ)$ to $w$. Since $$ \begin{align*} [f,D] \supseteq [f, V] = [g,V] \subseteq [g,E] \end{align*} $$ where $V$ is the connected component of $D \cap E$ containing $ζ$, we can join $δ_1$ and $δ_2$ to form a path from $x$ to $y$.

For $(f,D) \approx (g,E)$, keep joining paths.
