Existence of lift $\implies$ existence of analytic continuation

WLOG assume $γ$ is non-constant. Let $\tilde γ$ be a lift. Then $\tilde γ(I)$ can be covered by a finite set of opens $\tilde D_i = [D_i, f_i]\in R_\mathcal{F}$ for $(f_i, D_i)\in \mathcal{F}$, $1\leq i\leq n$. WLOG $D_i$ are discs, and there exists $0 = t_0 < \ldots < t_n = 1$ such that $\tilde γ([t_{i-1}, t_i]) \subset \tilde D_i$ for $1\leq i\leq n$. In particular, $γ(t_i)\in D_i \cap D_{i+1}$, and WLOG $(f_0, D_0) =(f,D)$ by shrinking $D'$.

Then $f_i(z) = f_{i+1}(z)$ for all $z\in γ(I) \cap D_i \cap D_{i+1}$, which is not a discrete set as $γ$ is non-constant, so by the identity theorem, $f_i = f_{i+1}$ on $D_i \cap D_{i+1}$ (a connected set). So $(f_i, D_i) \sim (f_{i+1}, D_{i+1})$, which implies $(f,D) \approx_γ (f_n,D_n) =: (g,E)$.

Existence of analytic continuation $\implies$ existence of lift

Suppose $(f,D) = (f_0, D_0) \sim \ldots \sim (f_n, D_n) = (g,E)$ is an analytic continuation along $γ$, with $0 = t_0 < \ldots < t_n = 1$, and $γ([t_{i-1}, t_i]) \subset D_i$. Then we get a lift $\tilde γ$ of $γ$ with $\tilde γ_i(0) = (γ(0), f) \in [D,f]$ by $\tilde γ(t) = (γ(t), f_i)\in [D_i, f_i]$ if $t_{i-1} \leq t \leq t_i$.
