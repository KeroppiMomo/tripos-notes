The condition $\|g\|_\infty \leq \|f\|_\infty$ can be dropped: if $\|f\|_\infty = 0$, just take $g = 0$. Otherwise define $$ \begin{align*} ϕ: \C &\to \C \\ z &\mapsto \begin{cases} z, &|z| \leq \|f\|_\infty, \\ \frac{\|f\|_\infty}{|z|} z, &|z| \geq \|f\|_\infty, \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ which is continuous with $\|ϕ\|_\infty = \|f\|_\infty$. Given some continuous $g: E\to \C$ such that $g|_K \equiv 0$ and $μ(\sb{x\in E}{g(x) \neq f(x)}) < ε$, let $$ \begin{align*} \tilde g = ϕ \circ g \end{align*} $$ which is still continuous with $\tilde g|_K \equiv 0$. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} &μ(\sb{x\in E}{\tilde g(x) \neq f(x)}) \\ ={}& μ(\sb{x\in E}{g(x) \neq f(x)}) + μ(\sb{x\in E}{\tilde g(x) \neq g(x) = f(x)}) \\ <{}& ε + μ(\sb{x\in E}{|f(x)| > \|f\|_\infty}) \\ ={}& ε. \end{align*} $$
