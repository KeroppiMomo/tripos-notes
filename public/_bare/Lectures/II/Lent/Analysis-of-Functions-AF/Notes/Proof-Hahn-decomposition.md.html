For any $A \in \mathcal{B}$, there exists positive $D\subseteq A$ such that $μ(D) \geq μ(A)$

We will construct sequences $(A_n)$ and $(B_n)$ as follows: let $A_0 := A$. Given $A_{i-1}$, take $B_{i} \subseteq A_{i-1}$ measurable such that $μ(B_i) \leq 0$ and there does not exist measurable $B'\subseteq A_{i-1}$ and $k\in \Z_{>0}$ such that $μ(B') \leq -1/k < μ(B_i)$. Let $A_i := A_{i-1} \setminus B_i$.

Now take $$ \begin{align*} D := \bigcap_{i\geq 0} A_i. \end{align*} $$

  • $μ(D) \geq μ(A)$: since $$ \begin{align*} A = D \sqcup \bigsqcup_{i\geq 1} B_i, \end{align*} $$ $σ$-additivity gives $$ \begin{align*} μ(A) = μ(D) + \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \underbrace{μ(B_i)}_{\leq 0} \leq μ(D). \end{align*} $$

  • $D$ is positive: take $B\subseteq D$ measurable. Fix $k\in \Z_{>0}$. Since $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} μ(B_i)$ converges to $μ(A) - μ(D) > -\infty$, we have $μ(B_i) \to 0$ as $i\to \infty$, so there exists $j$ such that $μ(B_j) > -1/k$. By construction as $B \subseteq D \subseteq A_{j-1}$, we have $μ(B) > -1/k$ as well. Thus $μ(B) \geq 0$

Constructing $P$

Let $s = \sup \sb{μ(B)}{B\in \mathcal{B} \text{ positive}}$. Then there exists a sequence $P_1, P_2, \ldots$ of positive sets such that $s = \lim_{n\to \infty} μ(P_n)$. Define $$ \begin{align*} P := \bigcup_{n\geq 1} P_n. \end{align*} $$ Then $P$ is a positive set. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} μ(P) = μ(P_n) + μ(\underbrace{P \setminus P_n}_{\subseteq P}) \geq μ(P_n) \end{align*} $$ so $μ(P) = s$.

$N := E \setminus P$ is negative

Let $A\subseteq N := E\setminus P$ be measurable. By the above claim, there exists a positive set $D\subseteq A$ with $μ(D) \geq μ(A)$. Since $D$ and $P$ are disjoint, $$ \begin{align*} μ(D \sqcup P) = μ(D) + μ(P) = μ(D) + s \geq s. \end{align*} $$ But $D \sqcup P$ is positive, so we must have $μ(D) = 0$ and hence $μ(A) \leq 0$.
