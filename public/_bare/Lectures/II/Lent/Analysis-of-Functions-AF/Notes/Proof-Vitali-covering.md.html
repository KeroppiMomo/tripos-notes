Since $\mathcal{U}$ has bounded diameter, let $V_1 = B_{r_1}(x_1)\in \mathcal{U}$ be such that $r_1 > r/2$ for any $B_r(x) \in \mathcal{U}$. Then having constructed $V_1, \ldots, V_n$, take $V_{n+1} = B_{r_{n+1}}(x_{n+1})\in \mathcal{U}$ be such that $V_{n+1}$ is disjoint from all of $V_1, \ldots, V_n$ (or stop if none exists) and $r_{n+1} > r/2$ for all other such $B_r(x)\in \mathcal{U}$.

Suppose $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} |V_i| < \infty$. Then $|V_i|\to 0$ so $r_i\to 0$ as $i\to \infty$. For any $V = B_r(x) \in \mathcal{U}$, there exists $n$ such that $r_{n+1} < r/2$. By construction of $V_n$, $V$ is not disjoint from $V_1, \ldots, V_{n}$. Let $m$ be minimal such that $V \cap V_m \neq \emptyset$. By construction of $V_m$, we have $r_m > r/2$. Geometry then tells us $B_{5r_m}(x_m) \supseteq B_r(x)$.
