Definition (complex measure, signed measure) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{B})$ be a measurable space. A function $μ: E \to X$ is said to be

  • a complex measure if $X=\C$...

  • a signed measure if $X=\R$...

and $μ(\emptyset) = 0$ and $μ$ is $σ$-additive.

Signed measure cannot take value in $\infty$, so a (positive) measure need not be a signed measure.

Definition (absolutely continuous, singular, concentrates) open_in_new

Let $μ, ν$ be positive measures on $(E, \mathcal{B})$.

  • Say $μ$ concentrates on a set $A \subseteq E$ if $μ(E \setminus A) = 0$.

  • Say $ν$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $μ$ (denoted $ν \ll μ$) if whenever $μ(A) = 0$ we have $ν(A) = 0$ for all $A\in \mathcal{B}$.

  • Say $ν$ is singular with respect to $μ$ (denoted $ν \perp μ$) if there exists $A, B\in \mathcal{B}$ such that $E = A \sqcup B$, $μ(A) = 0$ and $ν(B) = 0$.

Definition (positive, negative set) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ be a signed measure space.

$A \in \mathcal{B}$ is a positive set (with respect to $μ$) if $μ(B)\geq 0$ for all measurable $B \subseteq A$.

$A$ is a negative set if $μ(B) \leq 0$ for all measurable $B \subseteq A$.

A countable union of positive sets is positive; ditto for negative sets. (Proof)

Theorem (Hahn decomposition) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ be a signed measure space.

There exists $P,N\in \mathcal{B}$ such that $E = P \sqcup N$, $P$ is a positive set and $N$ is a negative set.

Proof

The decomposition is not unique, as you can move a measure-zero set between $P$ and $N$.

Theorem (Jordan decomposition of measures) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ be a signed measure space.

There exists unique positive measures $μ^+$ and $μ^-$ such that $μ = μ^+ - μ^-$ and $μ^+$${}\perp{}$$μ^-$, given by $$ \begin{align*} μ^+(A) = μ(A \cap P), \quad μ^-(A) = -μ(A \cap N) \end{align*} $$ where $E = P \sqcup N$ is any Hahn decomposition.

Proof

We can define integrals as $$ \begin{align*} \int f \d μ = \int f \d μ^+ - \int f \d μ^-. \end{align*} $$

The total variation measure is $|μ| = μ^+ - μ^-$. The total variation norm is $\|μ\| = |μ|(E)$. The space of all signed measures on $(E, \mathcal{B})$ then forms a Banach space.

Theorem (measure as integral outside negligible set) open_in_new

Let $μ,ν$ be positive measures on $(E, \mathcal{B})$. Assume $μ, ν$ are finite.

There exists $A \in \mathcal{B}$ and $f\in L^1(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ such that $$ \begin{align*} μ(E \setminus A) = 0 \quad \text{and}\quad ν(B) = \int_{B} f \d μ \quad \text{for all $B\subseteq A$ measurable}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (Lebesgue-Radon-Nikodym) open_in_new

Let $μ,ν$ be positive measures on $(E, \mathcal{B})$. Assume $μ, ν$ are finite.

  1. If $ν$${}\ll{}$$μ$, then there exists a $μ$-integrable function $f: E\to [0,\infty)$ such that $$ \begin{align*} ν(A) = \int_{A} f \d μ \qquad \text{for all }A \in \mathcal{B}. \end{align*} $$

    Moreover $f$ is unique up to a $μ$-measure-zero set.

    $f$ is called the Radon-Nikodym derivative, denoted $f = \dfd{ν}{μ}$.

  2. There exists unique measures $ν_a$ and $ν_s$ on $(E, \mathcal{B})$ such that $$ \begin{align*} ν = ν_a + ν_s, \qquad ν_a \ll μ, \quad ν_s \perp μ. \end{align*} $$

Proof

This can be extended to when $μ, ν$ are signed $σ$-finite.

Definition (weak Lp) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ be a measure space.

A measurable function $f: E \to \R$ or $\C$ is said to be in weak $L^p(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ if there exists some constant $C>0$ such that $$ \begin{align*} μ(\sb{x\in E}{|f(x)| > t}) \leq \frac{C}{t^p}. \end{align*} $$

If $f$ is in $L^p$, then $f$ is in weak $L^p$ by the Markov inequality.

Definition (Hardy-Littlewood maximal function) open_in_new

For $f\in L^1(\R^d)$, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} Mf(x) = \sup_{r>0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |f(y)| \d y. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (maximal inequality) open_in_new

For any $f\in L^1(\R^d)$, its Hardy-Littlewood maximal function satisfies $$ \begin{align*} |\sb{x\in \R^d}{Mf(x) > t}| < \frac{C_d}{t} \|f\|_1 \end{align*} $$ where $C_d = 5^d$ is some constant.

Proof

Therefore $Mf$ is weak $L^1$.

Theorem (Lebesgue differentiation) open_in_new

Let $f\in L^1(\R^d)$.

Almost every $x\in \R^d$ is a Lebesgue point of $f$, i.e. satisfies $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |f(y) - f(x)| \d y \to 0 \qquad \text{as $r\to 0$}. \end{align*} $$

This implies $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} f(y) \d y \to f(x) \qquad \text{as $r\to 0$}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (Lusin) open_in_new

Let $E$ be a compact metric space and $μ$ be a finite Borel measure.

For any measurable function $f: E\to \C$ and $ε>0$, there exists a continuous function $g: E\to \C$ such that $\|g\|_\infty \leq \|f\|_\infty$ and $$ \begin{align*} μ(\sb{x\in E}{f(x)\neq g(x)}) < ε. \end{align*} $$

Moreover if $f|_K \equiv 0$ on some closed set $K\subseteq E$, then $g$ can be chosen such that $g|_K \equiv 0$.

Proof

Theorem (Egorov) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$ be a finite measure space and $f, f_1, f_2, \ldots: E\to \C$ be measurable functions.

If $f_n(x)\to f(x)$ at almost every $x$, then for any $ε>0$, there exists a measurable set $A\in \mathcal{B}$ such that $f_n|_{A}$ converges uniformly to $f|_A$ and $μ(E \setminus A) < ε$.

Proof

Definition (Lp spaces) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{A}, μ)$ be a measure space and $f: E\to \C$ be a measurable function.

For $1\leq p<\infty$, the $L^p$ norm of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} \|f\|_p := \int |f(x)|^p \d μ(x). \end{align*} $$ For $p=\infty$, the $L^\infty$ norm of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} \|f\|_\infty := \inf \sb{t>0}{|f(x)|\leq t \text{ for $μ$-almost every $x$}} \end{align*} $$ with the convention $\inf \emptyset = \infty$.

Write $L^p(E) = L^p(E, \mathcal{A}, μ)$ for the space of measurable functions $f: E\to \C$ with $\|f\|_p<\infty$, and identifying functions which agree $μ$-almost everywhere.

Theorem (Minkowski integral inequality) open_in_new

Let $(E, \mathcal{A}, μ)$, $(F, \mathcal{B}, ν)$ be $σ$-finite measure spaces and $G: E \times F\to \C$ be a $\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}$-measurable function. Fix $p\in [1,\infty]$.

If $\int \|G(\cdot ,y)\|_p\d ν(y)<\infty$, then $$ \begin{align*} g(x) := \int G(x,y) \d ν(y) \end{align*} $$ exists for $μ$-almost every $x$, and $$ \begin{align*} \|g\|_p \leq \int \|G(\cdot ,y)\|_p\d ν(y). \end{align*} $$

Proof
