For $t\geq 0$, let $E = P_t\sqcup N_t$ be the Hahn decomposition with respect to the signed measure $ν - tμ$ (so $P_t$ positive, $N_t$ negative). Notice

  • If $A \subseteq P_t$, then $ν(A) \geq t\, μ(A)$;

  • If $A \subseteq N_t$, then $ν(A) \leq t \, μ(A)$.

Define $$ \begin{align*} f_n(x) := \sup \sb{t\in 2^{-n} \Z_{\geq 0}}{x\in P_t} \end{align*} $$ and $$ \begin{align*} f(x) = \limup_{n\to \infty} f_n(x). \end{align*} $$

$A_n := f_n\inv[0,\infty)$ satisfies $μ(E\setminus A_n) = 0$

Fix $t\in 2^{-n} \Z_{\geq 0}$. Notice $P_s$ is positive with respect to $ν - tμ$ if $s\geq t$, so because $$ \begin{align*} E \setminus A_n = f_n\inv(\infty) \subseteq \bigcup_{\substack{s\in 2^{-n} \Z_{\geq 0}: \\s\geq t}} P_s \end{align*} $$ and RHS is positive with respect to $ν - tμ$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \infty > ν(E \setminus A_n) \geq t\, μ(E \setminus A_n) \end{align*} $$ which holds for all $t$. Hence finiteness of $ν$ implies $μ(E \setminus A_n) = 0$.

$\left|ν(B) - \int_{B} f_n \d μ\right| \leq 2^{-n} μ(B)$ for any measurable $B \subseteq A_n$

Split $$ \begin{align*} B = \bigsqcup_{t\in 2^{-n} \Z_{\geq 0}} B_t, \qquad B_t = B \cap f_n\inv(t) \end{align*} $$ so it suffices to prove the claim for $B_t$ since then $$ \begin{align*} \left|ν(B) - \int_{B} f_n \d μ\right| &= \left|\sum_{t\in 2^{-n} \Z_{\geq 0}} ν(B_t) - \int_{B_t} f_n \d μ\right| \\ &\leq \sum_{t\in 2^{-n}\Z_{\geq 0}} \left|ν(B_t) - \int_{B_t} f_n \d μ\right| \\ &\leq \sum_{t\in 2^{-n} \Z_{\geq 0}} 2^{-n}μ(B_t) \\ &= 2^{-n} μ(B). \end{align*} $$

Now $$ \begin{align*} B_t \subseteq f_n\inv(t) \subseteq P_t \setminus P_{t + 2^{-n}} = P_t \cap N_{t + 2^{-n}}, \end{align*} $$ so from what we noted, $$ \begin{align*} t\, μ(B_t) \leq ν(B_t) \leq (t + 2^{-n}) μ(B_t). \end{align*} $$ Observing $\int_{B_t} f_n \d μ = \int_{B_t} t \d μ = t\, μ(B_t)$, we have $$ \begin{align*} 0 \leq ν(B_t) - \int_{B} f_n \d μ \leq 2^{-n} μ(B_t) \end{align*} $$ as required.

Conclusion

By the monotone convergence theorem, $$ \begin{align*} \int_{B} f_n \d μ \to \int_{B} f \d μ, \end{align*} $$ so for $B \subseteq \bigcap_{n} A_n =: A$, taking the limit as $n\to \infty$ gives $$ \begin{align*} \int_{B} f \d μ = ν(B). \end{align*} $$

Moreover $μ(E \setminus A) = μ(\bigcup_{n} (E \setminus A_n)) \leq \sum_{n} μ(E \setminus A_n) = 0$.

Finally $\int_{E} f \d μ = ν(E) < \infty$ and $f\geq 0$, so $f\in L^1(E, \mathcal{B}, μ)$.
