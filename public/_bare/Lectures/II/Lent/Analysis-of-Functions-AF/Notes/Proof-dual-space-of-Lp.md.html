Well-defined

Linearity of $L_g$ follows from linearity of integral. Hölder's inequality shows boundedness and $$ \begin{align*} \|L_g\| \leq \|g\|_q. \end{align*} $$

Isometry

We want to show $\|L_g\| \geq \|g\|_q$.

$1<p\leq \infty$

Let $g\in L^q(E)$ with $1<q<\infty$. Define $$ \begin{align*} f(x) := \begin{cases} |g(x)|^q / g(x), &g(x)\neq 0, \\ 0, &g(x) = 0. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ If $p<\infty$, then using $p(q-1) = q$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \|f\|_p^p = \|g^{q-1}\|_p^p = \|g\|_q^q = \|g\|_q^{p(q-1)} \implies \|f\|_p = \|g\|_q^{q-1} <\infty \end{align*} $$ and $$ \begin{align*} L_g(f) = \int |f(x)| |g(x)| \d μ = \int |g(x)|^q \d μ = \|g\|^q_q = \|f\|_p \|g\|_q. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $\|L_g\| \geq \|g\|_q$.

If $p = \infty$ and $q=1$, then $\|f\|_\infty \leq 1$ and $L_g(f) = \int |g(x)| \d μ = \|g\|_1$, so $\|L_g\| \geq \|g\|_1$.

$p=1$, $q=\infty$

It suffices to show if $g\in L^\infty(E)$, then $$ \begin{align*} A = \sb{x\in E}{|g(x)| > \|L_g\|} \end{align*} $$ has measure 0.

Let $F\in \mathcal{B}$ be any measurable set with $μ(F)<\infty$ and define $$ \begin{align*} B_k = \sb{x\in F}{|g(x)| \geq \|L_g\| + 1/k}. \end{align*} $$

Define $$ \begin{align*} f_k(x) = \frac{\mathbb{1}_{B_k}(x)}{g(x)}, \end{align*} $$ which is $L^1$ as $\|f_k\|_1 \leq (\|L_g\| + 1/k)\inv \cdot μ(B_k) < \infty$.

Then $$ \begin{align*} μ(B_k) = L_g(f_k) \leq \|L_g\|\|f_k\|_1 \leq \underbrace{\frac{\|L_g\|}{\|L_g\| + 1/k}}_{<1} μ(B_k) \end{align*} $$ so $μ(B_k) = 0$.

Then $\sb{x\in F}{|g(x)| > \|L_g\|} = \bigcup_{k} B_k$ has measure zero.

As $μ$ is $σ$-finite, take $F = F_1, F_2, \ldots$ with $\bigcup_{i} F_i = E$, then argue $A = \bigcup_{i} \sb{x\in F_i}{|g(x)| > \|L_g\|}$ has measure zero.

Surjectivity when $1\leq p<\infty$

We'll focus on when $μ$ is finite. Let $L \in L^p(E)^\ast$ be a bounded linear functional.

$L(\mathbb{1}_A) = \int \mathbb{1}_A g \d μ$ for some $g\in L^{1}(E)$

Define, for $A\in \mathcal{B}$, $$ \begin{align*} ν(A) = L(\mathbb{1}_A) \end{align*} $$ which is a complex measure: finite additivity is clear from linearity of $L$. Let $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ be countably many disjoint measurable sets. Letting $B_n = \bigsqcup_{m\geq n} A_m$, we have $$ \begin{align*} ν\Bigl(\bigsqcup_{n\geq 1} A_n\Bigr) = ν\Bigl(\bigsqcup_{m=1}^{n} A_m \sqcup B_{n+1}\Bigr) = \sum_{m=1}^{n} ν(A_m) + ν(B_{n+1}) \end{align*} $$ so it suffices to show $μ(B_{n}) \to 0$ as $n\to \infty$ for any decreasing sequence $(B_n)$ with $\bigcap_{n\geq 1} B_n = \emptyset$.

This follows from $$ \begin{align*} ν(B_n) = L(\mathbb{1}_{B_n}) \leq \|L\| \|\mathbb{1}_{B_n}\|_q = \|L\| \cdot μ(B_n)^{1/q} \to 0. \end{align*} $$

Moreover $ν$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $μ$, since if $μ(A) = 0$, then $\mathbb{1}_A$ vanishes $μ$-almost everywhere so equals 0 in $L_q$ and hence $ν(A) = L(\mathbb{1}_A) = L(0) = 0$.

Thus by Radon-Nikodym, there exists some $g\in L^1(E)$ such that $$ \begin{align*} L(\mathbb{1}_A) = ν(A) = \int_{A} g \d μ. \end{align*} $$

$L(f) = \int fg \d μ$ for all $f\in L^\infty(E)$

By linearity, $L(f) = \int fg \d μ$ for any simple function $f$.

Let $f\in L^\infty(E)$. Find a sequence $f_n: E\to \C$ of simple functions with $|f_n(x)| \leq |f(x)|$ and $f_n(x) \to f(x)$ for $μ$-almost every $x$ (e.g. by approximating $|f|$ and multiplying by the argument).

As we assumed $μ$ is finite, $f\in L^p(E)$. By [[FILE NOT FOUND: dominated convergence]], $f_n \to f$ in $L^p$, so $L(f_n) \to L(f)$ by the continuity of $L$.

By [[FILE NOT FOUND: dominated convergence]] again, $\int f_n g \d μ \to \int fg \d μ$, so $$ \begin{align*} L(f) = \lim_{n\to \infty} L(f_n) = \lim_{n\to \infty} \int f_ng \d μ = \int fg \d μ. \end{align*} $$

$g\in L^q(E)$

If $p=1$, our previous argument shows $\|g\|_\infty \leq \|L\|$ as $f_k$ is $L^\infty$.

Suppose $p>1$. For $R>0$, define $$ \begin{align*} g_R(x) = \begin{cases} g(x), &|g(x)|<R, \\ \frac{g(x)}{|g(x)|}R, &|g(x)|\geq R \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ which has $\|g_{R}\|_\infty \leq R$. Let $$ \begin{align*} f_R(x) = \begin{cases} |g_R(x)|^{q} / g_R(x), &g_R(x)\neq 0, \\ 0, &g_R(x) = 0, \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ which has $\|f_R\|_\infty \leq R^{q-1}$ and shown previously $\|g_R\|_q^{q-1} = \|f_R\| < \infty$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \|L\| \cdot \|f_R\|_p \geq |L(f_R)| = \left|\int f_R g \d μ \right| \leq \int |f_R| |g| \d μ \leq \int |f_R| |g_R| \d μ = \|g_R\|_q^{q} \end{align*} $$ and hence $$ \begin{align*} \|g_R\|_q \leq \|L\|. \end{align*} $$ As $|g_R|\to |g|$ as $R\to \infty$, by monotone convergence, $\|g_R\|_q \to \|g\|_q$, so $\|g\|_q \leq \|L\|$.

Conclusion

Now that $g\in L^q(E)$, we can talk about $L_g$. As $L(f) = L_g(f)$ on $f\in L^\infty(E)$, which is dense in $L^p(E)$, we have $L = L_f$ by continuity.
