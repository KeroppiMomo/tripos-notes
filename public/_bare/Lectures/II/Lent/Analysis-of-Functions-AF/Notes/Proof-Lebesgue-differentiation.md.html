Lebesgue point everywhere for $f$ continuous

Suppose $f$ is continuous and fix $x\in \R^d$ and $ε>0$. By continuity there exists $δ>0$ such that $|f(y) - f(x)| < ε$ whenever $y\in B_δ(x)$. Then for $r<δ$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} \underbrace{|f(y) - f(x)|}_{<ε} \d y \leq ε. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \lim_{r\to 0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |f(y)-f(x)| \d y = 0. \end{align*} $$

General case

Fix $f\in L^1(\R^d)$.

For any compactly supported continuous $g\in C_c(\R^d)$, writing $f = g + (f-g)$, $$ \begin{align*} |f(y) - f(x)| \leq |g(y) - g(x)| + |(g-f)(y) - (g-f)(x)| \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{multline*} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |f(y) - f(x)| \d y \\ \leq \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |g(y) - g(x)| \d y + \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |(g-f)(y) - (g-f)(x)| \d y. \end{multline*} $$ Taking lim sup on both sides and noting our result for continuous functions, $$ \begin{align*} &\limsup_{r\to 0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |f(y) - f(x)| \d y \\ \leq{} &\limsup_{r\to 0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} \underbrace{|(g-f)(y) - (g-f)(x)|}_{\leq |g-f|(y) + |g-f|(x)} \d y \\ \leq{} &\limsup_{r\to 0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |g-f|(y) \d y + |g-f|(x) \\ \leq{} &M(g-f)(x) + |g-f|(x) \end{align*} $$ where $M(g-f)$ is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

Letting $$ \begin{align*} A(ε) := \sb{x\in \R^d}{\limsup_{r\to 0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|} \int_{B_r(x)} |f(y) - f(x)| \d y > ε}, \end{align*} $$ the inequality above shows $$ \begin{align*} A(ε) \subseteq \sb{x\in \R^d}{M(g-f)(x) > ε/2} \cup \sb{x\in \R^d}{|g-f|(x) > ε/2}. \end{align*} $$

Taking the Lebesgue measure on both sides, and noting the maximal inequality and the Markov inequality, $$ \begin{align*} |A(ε)| \leq \frac{C_d}{ε/2} \|g-f\|_1 + \frac{1}{ε/2} \|g-f\|_1 = \frac{2(C_d + 1)}{ε}\|g-f\|_1. \end{align*} $$

This holds for all continuous $g$. As [[FILE NOT FOUND: the space of compactly supported continuous function $C_c(\R^d)$ is dense in $L^1(\R^d)$]], we can take $g$ such that $\|g - f\|_1\to 0$. This shows $|A(ε)| = 0$. Hence $$ \begin{align*} \R^d \setminus \sb{x\in \R^d}{\lim_{r\to 0} \frac{1}{|B_r(x)|}\int_{B_r(x)} |f(y) - f(x)| \d y = 0} = \bigcup_{n\in \Z_{>0}} A(1/n) \end{align*} $$ has Lebesgue measure 0.
