Let $μ,ν$ be positive measures on $(E, \mathcal{B})$. Assume $μ, ν$ are finite.

  1. If $ν$${}\ll{}$$μ$, then there exists a $μ$-integrable function $f: E\to [0,\infty)$ such that $$ \begin{align*} ν(A) = \int_{A} f \d μ \qquad \text{for all }A \in \mathcal{B}. \end{align*} $$

    Moreover $f$ is unique up to a $μ$-measure-zero set.

    $f$ is called the Radon-Nikodym derivative, denoted $f = \dfd{ν}{μ}$.

  2. There exists unique measures $ν_a$ and $ν_s$ on $(E, \mathcal{B})$ such that $$ \begin{align*} ν = ν_a + ν_s, \qquad ν_a \ll μ, \quad ν_s \perp μ. \end{align*} $$

Proof

This can be extended to when $μ, ν$ are signed $σ$-finite.
