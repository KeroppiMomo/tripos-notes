We claim there is a line $L\in (\mathbb{P}^2)^\times\simeq \mathbb{P}^2$ which is not tangent to $V$ at any point. Indeed consider the map $$ \begin{align*} V &\to (\mathbb{P}^2)^\times \simeq \mathbb{P}^2 \\ P &\mapsto \left(\pdfd{f}{x}(P): \pdfd{f}{y}(P): \pdfd{f}{z}(P)\right). \end{align*} $$ Because $V$ is smooth, we never have $0 = \pdfd{f}{x}(P) = \pdfd{f}{y}(P) = \pdfd{f}{z}(P)$, so this is a morphism. Its image is therefore a closed subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^2$ of dimension at most 1. Therefore there is some line not in its image, i.e. not tangent to $V$.

By a linear change of coordinates (which preserves hyperplanes), WLOG assume $L = \set{x=0}$. For a point $P\in V\cap L$ in the affine patch $\set{z\neq 0}$, since $\set{x=0}$ is not tangent to $L$, $\pdfd{f}{y}\neq 0$, so $x - x(P) = x$ is a local coordinates of $V$ at $P$. Therefore $ν_P(x) = 1$. Similar for the affine patch $\set{y\neq 0}$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \op{div}(L) = \sum_{P\in V\cap L} P. \end{align*} $$

Because $f(0, y, z)$ has degree $d$ (and is non-zero as $V\not\subseteq L$), there are $d$ solutions to $(y:z)\in \mathbb{P}^1$, so $\deg(\op{div}(L)) = d$.
