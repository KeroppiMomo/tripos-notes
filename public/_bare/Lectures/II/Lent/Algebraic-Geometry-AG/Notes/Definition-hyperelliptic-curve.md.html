A curve $V$ of genus $g>1$ is hyperelliptic if there exists a morphism $ϕ: V\to \mathbb{P}^1$ of degree 2.

If a curve $V$ has genus $g>1$, the following are equivalent:

  1. $V$ is hyperelliptic;

  2. there exists an effective divisor $D\geq 0$ with $\deg D = 2$ and $l(D) = 2$; (Proof)

  3. $V$ is not canonically embedded, i.e. the morphism associated to a canonical divisor is not an embedding, i.e. $K_V$ is not very ample. (Proof)

Every curve of genus 2 is hyperelliptic. (Proof)

There exist genus $g$ hyperelliptic curves for every $g>1$.
