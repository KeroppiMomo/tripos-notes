Suppose $κ/K$ is a finitely generated field extension by $x_1, \ldots, x_n$. Then there exists $κ_0 = K(x_1, \ldots, x_1)$ pure transcendental such that $κ_0 \subset κ$ is finite. And you can take $x_1, \ldots, x_m$ to be the maximal set of algebraically independent elements.

Let $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ and $y_1, \ldots, y_m$ be transcendence bases for a finitely generated field extension $K/k$, then $n=m$.

Therefore transcendence degree is well-defined.

Proof. Suppose not, WLOG $m>n$. $y_1$ is algebraic over $k(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$, so has some minimal polynomial $f_1$ by some proposition. This $f_1$ involves some $x_i$ so we replace $x_i$ with $y_1$ and consider the algebraically independent set $x_1, \ldots, x_{i-1}, x_{i+1}, \ldots, x_n, y_1$. Since $x_1$ is algebraic over $k(x_1, \ldots, x_{i-1}, x_{i+1}, \ldots, x_n, y_1)$, $y_2$ is algebraic over $k(x_i, \ldots, x_{i-1}, x_{i+1}, \ldots, x_n, y)$. Find minimum polynomial $f_2$ and proceed in this way, noting some new $x_i$ must be involved in $f_i$ for $i\leq n$ (because $y_1, \ldots, y_m$ are algebraically independent). So we conclude that $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ are algebraic over $k(y_1, \ldots, y_n)$. As each $y_{n+1}, \ldots, y_m$ is algebraic over $k(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$, they are also algebraic over $k(y_1, \ldots, y_n)$, and so $m=n$.
