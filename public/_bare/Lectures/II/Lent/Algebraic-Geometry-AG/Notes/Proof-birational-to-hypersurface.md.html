Let $k(X)$ have pure transcendental $K_0 = k(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ with $k(X)/K_0$ finite. By the primitive element theorem, there exists $y\in k(X)$ such that $k(X) = K_0(y)$. By the previous proposition, $y$ has some irreducible polynomial $f$ with $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n, y] \simeq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n, t]/(f)$. Taking function fields on both sides, we have $k(X)\simeq k(Z(f))$. By the geometric-algebraic correspondence, $X$ is birational to $Z(f)$.
