Definition (projective space) open_in_new

Let $k = \bar k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

The projective $n$-space over $k$ is $$ \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}^n := \mathbb{P}_k^n := \mathbb{P}^n(k) &:= \set{\text{lines through 0 in }k^{n+1}} \\ &= \set{(a_0, \ldots, a_n)\in k^{n+1} \setminus \set{0}}/{\sim} \end{align*} $$ where $(a_0, \ldots, a_n)\sim (b_0, \ldots, b_n)$ if there exists $λ\in k^\times$ such that $a_i = λb_i$ for all $0\leq i\leq n$.

We write $(a_0: a_1: \ldots: a_n)$ (or $[a_0: a_1: \ldots : a_n]$) for the class of $(a_0, \ldots, a_n)$.

We call $x_0, \ldots, x_n$ the homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb{P}^{n}$. (But they are no longer well-defined functions on $\mathbb{P}^n$.)

$\set{x_0 = 0} = \set{(0 : a_1 : \ldots : a_n)\in \mathbb{P}^n}$ can be identified with $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, and $\set{x_0 \neq 0} = \set{(1: a_1: \ldots: a_n)\in \mathbb{P}^n}$ can be identified with $\mathbb{A}^n$.

Considering $U_j := \set{x_j\neq 0}\simeq \mathbb{A}^{n}$ for various $j$, we have $\mathbb{P}^n = U_0 \cup U_1 \cup \ldots \cup U_n$ called the standard affine patches.

Definition (homogeneous polynomial) open_in_new

A polynomial $F\in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ is said to be homogeneous of degree $d$ if it is any sum of monomials of the form $c x_0^{r_0} \ldots x_n^{r_n}$ where $r_0 + \ldots + r_n = d$.

Given any polynomial $f\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ of degree $d$, the homogenisation of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} f^\ast = x_0^d \,f\Bigl(\frac{x_1}{x_0}, \ldots, \frac{x_n}{x_0}\Bigr) \in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n], \end{align*} $$ which is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$.

Given a homogeneous polynomial $F\in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$, its dehomogenisation is $$ \begin{align*} F_\ast = F(1, x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]. \end{align*} $$

Any polynomial $f\in k[x_1,\ldots, x_n]$ can be written as $f = \sum_{d\geq 0} f_d$ where $f_d$ is homogeneous of degree $d$.

$f = (f^\ast)_\ast$ and $F = x_0^r \, (F_\ast)^\ast$ where $r$ is the largest power such that $x_0^r \mid F$.

$(f\cdot g)^\ast = f^\ast\cdot g^\ast$ and $(F\cdot G)_\ast = F_\ast \cdot G_\ast$.

Euler's identity: if $F\in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ is homogeneous of degree $d$, then $d\cdot F = \sum_{i=0}^{n} x_i \pdfd{F}{x_i}$.

Definition (homogeneous ideal) open_in_new

An ideal $J\lhd k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ is homogeneous if $J$ is generated by homogeneous polynomials.

Given an ideal $I\lhd k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, the homogenisation of $I$, denoted $I^\ast$, is the ideal in $k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ generated by the homogenisation of $f$ for all $f\in I$.

$J\lhd k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ is homogeneous iff for all $f\in J$, we have $f_d\in J$ where $f = \sum_{d\geq 0} f_d$ is the decomposition into degree $d$-homogeneous polynomials. (Proof)

Definition (projective zero locus, projective algebraic set) open_in_new

Let $S\subseteq k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ be a set of homogeneous polynomials.

The zero locus in $\mathbb{P}^n$ is $$ \begin{align*} Z(S) := \sb{(a_0 : a_1 : \ldots : a_n)}{F(a_0, \ldots, a_n) = 0 \quad \forall F\in S}. \end{align*} $$ (We write $Z_a$ and $Z_p$ to distinguish the affine and projective cases when there is confusion.)

An algebraic set in $\mathbb{P}^n$ or a projective variety is any set of the form $Z(S)$.

Whether $F(a_0, \ldots, a_n) = 0$ does not depend on the representative $(a_0 : \ldots : a_n)\in \mathbb{P}^n$ as $F$ is homogeneous, since $F(λ a_0, \ldots, λ a_n) = λ^{\deg F} F(a_0, \ldots, a_n)$.

This shares the same basic properties as zero locus in $\mathbb{A}^n$.

Under $ϕ_0: \mathbb{A}^n \to U_0 = \set{x_0\neq 0}\subseteq \mathbb{P}^n: (x_1, \ldots, x_n) \mapsto (1:x_1:\ldots:x_n)$ a standard patch, $U_0 \cap Z(S) \equiv ϕ_0\inv(Z(S)) = Z(S$$_\ast$$)$ is an affine algebraic set. Together, $U_i \cap Z(S) = \set{x_i\neq 0} \cap Z(S)$ provide a cover of $Z(S)$ by affine algebraic sets.

  • the topology such that the closed sets are precisely the projective algebraic sets.

Projective algebraic sets and ideals open_in_new

Here we will show that $Z$ and $I$ give an inclusion-reversing bijective correspondence $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{projective algebraic sets in $\mathbb{P}^n$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{radical homogeneous ideals of $k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$}} \setminus \set{(x_0, \ldots, x_n)} \\ \set{\text{irreducible projective varieties in $\mathbb{P}^n$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{prime homogeneous ideals of $k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$}} \setminus \set{(x_0, \ldots, x_n)}\\ \set{\text{points in $\mathbb{P}^n$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{maximal homogeneous ideals of $k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$}} \setminus \set{(x_0, \ldots, x_n)}\\ X &\mapsto I(X) \\ Z(J) &\mapsfrom J. \end{align*} $$

These follow from the same proofs as with affine algebraic sets.

  • $S \subseteq I(Z(S))$ for any subset $S \subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$.

  • $X = Z(I(X))$ for any projective algebraic set $X$.

  • $X = Y$ iff $I(X) = I(Y)$ for any affine algebraic $X,Y$, showing injectivity of $I$.

Definition (function field of projective algebraic set) open_in_new

Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be an irreducible projective variety.

The function field of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} k(X) = \sb{\frac{f}{g}}{f, g\in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n] \text{ homogeneous}, \; \deg f = \deg g, \; g\not\in I(X)} \Big/ \sim \end{align*} $$ where $f/g \sim f'/g'$ iff $fg' - f'g\in{}$$I(X)$. Elements are called rational functions on $X$.

$k(X)$ is a field because $I(X)$ is a prime ideal.

Definition (local ring of projective variety) open_in_new

Let $X$ be an irreducible projective variety, and let $P\in X$.

The local ring of $X$ at $P$ is the subring of the function field $$ \begin{align*} O_{X,P} := \sb{f/g\in k(X)}{g\not\in \mathfrak{m}_P} = \sb{f/g\in k(X)}{g(P) \neq 0}. \end{align*} $$ If $ϕ\in k(X)$ is in $O_{X,x}$, we say $ϕ$ is regular at $x$.

Definition (rational map) open_in_new

Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be an irreducible quasiprojective variety.

For any polynomials $F_0, F_1, \ldots, F_m \in k[x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ all homogeneous and having the same degree, if not all $F_i$ is in $I(X)$, then $$ \begin{align*} F: X \setminus Z(F_0, \ldots, F_m) &\to \mathbb{P}^m \\ \v x &\mapsto (F_0(\v x) : \ldots : F_m(\v x)) \end{align*} $$ is a well-defined function on an open dense subset of $X$.

Such two $F, G$ are said to determine the same rational map if they agree on an open dense subset. A rational map is an equivalence class of maps of this form, denoted $ϕ: X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^m$.

$ϕ$ is regular at a point $P\in X$ if there is a representative $(F_0 : \ldots : F_m)$ of $ϕ$ such that $F_0(P), \ldots, F_m(P)$ are not all zero. The domain of $ϕ$ is the set of regular points.

If $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^m$ is a quasiprojective variety, we write $F: X \dashrightarrow Y$ is a rational map if $F$ takes values in $Y$ on its domain.

$(F_0 : \ldots : F_m)$ and $(G_0 : \ldots : G_m)$ determine the same rational map iff $F_i G_j - G_j F_i \in I(X)$ for all $i,j$. > In the case $X = \mathbb{P}^n$, this happens iff $F_i = h\cdot G_i$ for some $h\in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$.

A rational function $f/g\in k[X]$ on $X$ is the same as a rational map $(g : f): X\dashrightarrow \mathbb{A}^1 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^1$.

Definition (morphism between quasiprojective varieties) open_in_new

Let $X, Y$ be quasiprojective varieties, with $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ irreducible and $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^m$.

A rational map $ϕ: X\dashrightarrow Y$ is a morphism $ϕ: X\to Y$ if $ϕ$ is regular at all points of $X$, i.e. the domain of $ϕ$ is $X$.

$ϕ: X\to Y$ is an isomorphism if it has a two-sided inverse which is also a morphism.

Suppose $X,Y$ are irreducible projective varieties and $X_0, Y_0$ are some affine patches. If a morphism $f: X\to Y$ sends $X_0$ to $Y_0$, then the restriction $f: X_0\to Y_0$ is also a morphism. (Proof)

Projective projection open_in_new

Applying a change of coordinates, assume $P = (0:\ldots:0:1)\in \mathbb{P}^n$. The projection from $P$ is the rational map $$ \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}^n &\dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^{n-1} \\ (x_0 : \ldots : x_{n}) &\mapsto (x_0 : \ldots : x_{n-1}), \end{align*} $$ which is defined everywhere except $P$.

If $Q \neq P$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$ and $Q = (q_0 : \ldots : q_n)$, then the line through $P$ and $Q$ is $$ \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}^1 &\to \mathbb{P}^n\\ (t:u) &\mapsto (t q_0 : t q_1 : \ldots : t q_{n-1} : tq_n + u), \end{align*} $$ which is collapsed to a point. We can identify $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ with $\set{x_n = 0}$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$.

This is a well-defined morphism on any subvariety $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ with $P \not\in X$. If $Z(F)$ is a hypersurface, then if $R = (r_0 : \ldots : r_{n-1})\in \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ then the preimage of $R$ in $Z(F)$ is given by the locus where $F(r_0 : \ldots : r_{n-1} : x_n) = 0$, which is a union of at most $\deg F$ many points in $Z(F)$ (using $P \not\in Z(F)$).

However it can be possible that its restriction to a subvariety containing $P$ is a morphism: consider $π: C := Z(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2) \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^1, (x_0 : x_1 : x_2) \mapsto (x_0 : x_1)$, a projection from $P = (0,0,1)$. The other representation is $(x_1 : x_2)$.

Veronese embedding open_in_new

Let $n,d \geq 1$ and $N = \pmat{n+d\\n} - 1$. Consider the morphism $$ \begin{align*} ν_d: \mathbb{P}^n &\to \mathbb{P}^N \\ (x_0 : \ldots : x_n) &\mapsto (x_0^d : x_0^{d-1} x_1 : x_0^{d-1} x_2 : \ldots : x_n^d) \in \mathbb{P}^N \end{align*} $$ It is injective, called the $d$-uple or $d$-th Veronese embedding. In fact $ν_d(\mathbb{P}^n)$ is a projective variety in $\mathbb{P}^N$, and so $ν_d : \mathbb{P}^n \to ν_d(\mathbb{P}^n)$ is an isomorphism.

We can think of the zero locus of a degree-$d$ polynomial as the intersection of a linear subspace and $ν_d(\mathbb{P}^n)$.

Proposition (Segre embedding) open_in_new

Let $m,n\geq 1$, $N = nm + n + m = (n+1)(m+1)-1$. Write $x_0, \ldots, x_n$ for the coordinates in $\mathbb{P}^n$, $y_0, \ldots, y_m$ for the coordinates in $\mathbb{P}^m$, and $z_{00}, \ldots, z_{0m}, \ldots, z_{n0}, \ldots, z_{nm}$ for the coordinates in $\mathbb{P}^N$.

The Segre embedding $$ \begin{align*} σ_{n,m}: \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m &\to \mathbb{P}^N \\ ((x_0 : \ldots : x_n) , (y_0 : \ldots : y_m)) &\mapsto (x_0 y_0 : \ldots : x_0 y_m : \ldots : x_n y_0 : \ldots : x_n y_m) \end{align*} $$ is a bijection $σ_{n,m}: \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m \to Σ_{n,m}$ where $$ \begin{align*} Σ_{n,m} = Z(z_{ij}z_{pq} - z_{iq} z_{pj} \mid 0\leq i,p\leq n, \; 0\leq j,q\leq m,\; i\neq p, \; j\neq q) \end{align*} $$ is an irreducible projective variety.

Definition (tangent space of varieties) open_in_new

Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an affine algebraic set and $P\in X$ be a point. The affine tangent space of $X$ at $P$ is the vector space $$ \begin{align*} T_{X,P} ={}& \sb{(v_1, \ldots, v_n)\in k^n}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfd{f}{x_i}(P)\cdot v_i = 0 \quad \text{for all $f\in I(V)$}} \\ ={}& \sb{\v v\in k^n}{\d f_P(\v v) = 0 \quad \text{for all $f\in I(V)$}}. \end{align*} $$ ($I(V)$)

Let $X\subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be an irreducible quasiprojective variety, $P\in X$. The tangent space to $X$ at $P$ is $T_{X_i, P}$ for any standard affine open $X_i = X \cap \set{x_i \neq 0}$ containing $P$.

If $X\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ is affine and $I(X) = (f_1, \ldots, f_r)$, then by rank-nullity $$ \begin{align*} \dim T_{X,P} = n - \op{rank}(J_P) \end{align*} $$ where $J_P = (\pdfd{f_i}{x_j}(P))_{i,j}$ is the Jacobian matrix.

Definition (dimension, smooth points on variety) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a quasiprojective variety.

  • If $X$ is irreducible, its dimension is the minimum tangent space dimension $$ \begin{align*} \dim X := \min \sb{\dim T_{X,P}}{P\in X}. \end{align*} $$

    A point $P\in X$ is smooth or non-singular if $\dim T_{X,P} = \dim X$. Otherwise we say $P$ is singular. Write $X^{\text{sm}}$ for the set of smooth points of $X$.

  • If $X$ is reducible with irreducible components $X_1, \ldots, X_m$, the dimension of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} \dim X := \max_i \dim X_i. \end{align*} $$

    A point $P\in X$ is smooth if $P$ lies on $X_i$ for a unique $i$ and $P$ is a smooth point of $X_i$, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} X^\text{sm} := \bigcup_{i=1}^{m} \Bigl(X_i^\text{sm} \setminus \bigcup_{\substack{j=1: \\ j\neq i}}^{m} X_i\Bigr). \end{align*} $$

We say $X$ is smooth if it has no singular points.

$Z(f) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2$ with $f$ non-constant is 1-dimensional.

Definition (algebraic element, minimal polynomial, transcendental element, algebraic extension) open_in_new

Suppose $L/K$ is a field extension, and $α\in L$.

Let $ϕ: K[X] \to L$ be the evaluation homomorphism $ϕ(\sum_{i} c_iX^i) = \sum_{i} c_i α^i$. (Recall $K[X]$ is a PID.)

  • Say $α$ is algebraic over $K$ if $\ker ϕ\neq (0)$.

    In this case, the minimal polynomial of $α$ over $K$ is the unique monic polynomial $f$ such that $\ker ϕ = (f)$.

    Say $α$ is transcendental over $K$ if $\ker ϕ = (0)$.

  • Say $L/K$ is algebraic if every $α\in L$ is algebraic over $K$.

If $α$ is algebraic and $f$ is its minimal polynomial over $K$, then the first isomorphism theorem implies $$ \begin{align*} K(α) = K[α] \isom K[X]/(f). \end{align*} $$ so $K[α]$ is a field.

Moreover the basis $\set{1 + (f), X + (f), \ldots, X^{\deg f - 1} + (f)}$ of $K[X]/(f)$ shows that $$ \begin{align*} [K(α) : K] = \deg f. \end{align*} $$

If $α$ is transcendental, then $K[α] \isom K[X]$ and $K(α) \isom K(X)$.

Let $L/K$ be a field extension.

A finite set $x_1, \ldots, x_n\in L$ is algebraically independent over $K$ if there is no non-zero polynomial $f\in k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ such that $f(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = 0$. Equivalently, if the $k$-algebra homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} ϕ: k[X_1, \ldots, X_n] &\to L \\ X_i &\mapsto x_i \end{align*} $$ is injective.

Say $L/K$ is pure transcendental if an algebraically independent set $x_1, \ldots, x_n\in L$ exists such that $L = K(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$, or equivalently $$ \begin{align*} Φ: k(X_1, \ldots, X_n) &\to L \\ X_i &\mapsto x_i \end{align*} $$ is a field isomorphism.

Proposition (minimal polynomial with algebraically independent set) open_in_new

Let $K = k(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ and $L/K$ be a field extension.

If $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ are algebraically independent over $k$ and $x_{n+1}\in L$ is algebraic over $K$, then the kernel $I$ of the $k$-algebra homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} k[t_1, \ldots, t_n, t_{n+1}] &\to L \\ t_i &\mapsto x_i \end{align*} $$ is a principal ideal generated by an irreducible polynomial $f\in k[t_1, \ldots, t_n, t_{n+1}]$, called the minimal polynomial.

Moreover if $f$ contains the variable $t_i$ ($i = 1,\ldots, n$), then $x_1, \ldots, x_{i-1}, x_{i+1}, \ldots, x_n, x_{n+1}$ are algebraically independent.

Definition (transcendence basis, degree) open_in_new

Let $L/K$ be a field extension.

A finite set $x_1, \ldots, x_n\in L$ is a transcendence basis over $K$ if it is a maximal algebraically independent set, i.e. they are algebraically independent and $L/K(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ is an algebraic extension.

In this case say the transcendence degree of $L/K$ is $\op{trdeg}_K(L) = n$.

If $L/K$ is finitely generated, then transcendence degree is well-defined, i.e. a transcendence basis exists, and any two transcendence basis has the same length. (Proof) > Moreover $L/K(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ is algebraic and still finitely generated, so finite. Thus by the primitive element theorem (assuming $\op{char}(K) = 0$), there is $y\in L$ such that $L = K(x_1, \ldots, x_n, y)$.
