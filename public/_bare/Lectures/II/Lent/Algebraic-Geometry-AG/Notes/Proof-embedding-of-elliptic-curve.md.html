Very ample

$3P_0$ is very ample as $\deg 3P_0 \geq 3$, meaning $ϕ_{3P_0}$ is an isomorphism onto its image.

Isomorphic to cubic

Recall $l(D) = \deg(D)$ for any divisor $D$ with $\deg D>0$.

  • $L(P_0) = k$ has a basis $\set{1}$.

  • $L(2P_0)$ has a basis $\set{1, x}$ for some $x\in l(2P_0)\setminus l(P_0)$, meaning $x$ must have a double pole at $P_0$.

  • $L(3P_0)$ has a basis $\set{1, x, y}$ for some $y\in l(3P_0) \setminus l(2P_0)$, meaning $y$ must have a triple pole at $P_0$.

  • $L(4P_0)$ has a basis $\set{1, x, y, x^2}$ as $x^2$ has an order-4 pole at $P_0$.

  • $L(5P_0)$ has a basis $\set{1, x, y, x^2, xy}$ as $xy$ has an order-5 pole at $P_0$.

Now $L(6P_0)$ contains $\set{1, x, y, x^2, xy, x^3, y^2}$ with $x^3$ and $y^2$ both having an order-6 pole at $P_0$, but $L(6P_0)$ has dimension 6. Therefore there must have a linear relation $$ \begin{align*} a_1 + a_2x + a_3y + a_4 x^2 + a_5 xy + a_6x^3 + a_7 y^2 = 0 \end{align*} $$ where $a_6, a_7 \neq 0$. By a change of basis, we can set $a_3 = 0$ (by absorbing into the $xy$ term) and $a_5 = 0$ (by completing the square with the $y^2$ term). Factoring and homogenising, we get $$ \begin{align*} F(x,y,z) := y^2 z - \prod_{i=1}^{3} (x - λ_i z) = 0 \end{align*} $$ satisfies $F(x,y,1)$ for $λ_1, λ_2, λ_3\in k$ after some scaling.

The embedding $ϕ_{3P_0}$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_{3P_0} = (x : y : 1) : E &\to \mathbb{P}^2 \\ \end{align*} $$ so its image lies in $Z(F(x_1,x_2,x_3))\subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$.

$\dim ϕ_{3P_0}(E) = 1 = \dim Z(F)$, so as $ϕ_{3P_0}(E)$ is a closed irreducible subvariety, $ϕ_{3P_0}(E)$ is an irreducible component of $Z(F)$. If $Z(F)$ is reducible, then $ϕ_{3P_0}(E)$ is either a quadric surface or a hyperplane. This is impossible as a quadric surface or a hypersurface is birational (so isomorphic) to $\mathbb{P}^1$, which has genus $0\neq 1$. Hence $ϕ_{3P_0}: E \to Z(F)$ is an isomorphism.

Group law

We have $$ \begin{align*} P + Q + R = P_0 &\iff [P-P_0] + [Q-P_0] + [R-P_0] = 0 \\ &\iff P + Q + R - 3P_0 = \op{div}(f) \qquad \text{for some $f\in k(E)$}. \end{align*} $$ Here we must have $f\in L(3P_0)$, so $$ \begin{align*} f = L(1, x, y) = \frac{ϕ_{3P_0}^\ast L(x_1, x_2, x_3)}{ϕ_{3P_0}^\ast x_3} \end{align*} $$ for some linear form $L$. Therefore $\op{div}(ϕ_{3P_0}^\ast L(x_1, x_2, x_3)) = P+Q+R$, meaning $ϕ_{3P_0}(P)$, $ϕ_{3P_0}(Q)$ and $ϕ_{3P_0}(R)$ lie on the line $L$.
