Let $V$ be a curve and $D = \sum_{P} n_P$ be a divisor.

The space of rational functions with poles bounded by $D$ is $$ \begin{align*} L(D) := \sb{f\in k(V)}{ν_P(f) + n_P \geq 0} = \set{0} \cup \sb{f\in k(V)^\times}{\op{div}(f) + D \geq 0}. \end{align*} $$

Its dimension is denoted $l(D) := \dim_k L(D)$.

$L(D)$ is a $k$-vector space, because of the ultrametric inequality $ν_P(f+g) \geq \min\set{ν_P(f), ν_P(g)}$. As sets, we can identify $$ \begin{align*} (L(D) \setminus \set{0})/k^\times &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{effective divisor linearly equivalent to $D$}} \\ f &\mapsto \op{div}(f) + D. \end{align*} $$

For $V = \mathbb{P}^1$ with affine coordinate $t$, $L(m\cdot \infty)$ has a basis $\set{1, t, \ldots, t^m}$, so $l(m\cdot \infty) = m+1$.

$L(0) = k$ (so $l(0) = 1$), because the only regular functions are constants.

$L(D) = \set{0}$ (so $l(D) = 0$) for $\deg D<0$, because an effective divisor is not linearly equivalent to a negative degree divisor.

For any $P\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} l(D) - 1 \leq l(D - P) \leq l(D). \end{align*} $$ (Proof) By induction, we obtain that if $\deg(D) \geq 0$, then $$ \begin{align*} l(D) \leq \deg(D) + 1. \end{align*} $$

If $D' = D - \op{div}(g)$ for some $g\in k(V)^\times$, then $L(D') \xrightarrow{\sim} L(D): f \mapsto fg$ is a $k$-vector space isomorphism. Hence $l(D') = l(D)$ if $D'$ and $D$ are linearly equivalent.
