A divisor on a curve $V$ is a finite formal sum $D = \sum_{P\in V} n_P P$ with integer coefficients. We also write $ν_P(D) = n_P$. The set of divisors is the divisor group $\op{Div}(V) = \bigoplus_{P\in V} \Z \cdot P$.

The degree of a divisor $D = \sum n_P P$ is $$ \begin{align*} \deg(D) := \sum n_P. \end{align*} $$ The divisors of degree 0 are denoted by $\op{Div}^0(V)$, a subgroup of $\op{Div}(V)$.

The support of a divisor $D = \sum n_P P$ is the set $$ \begin{align*} \op{supp}(D) := \sb{P\in V}{n_p\neq 0}. \end{align*} $$

A divisor $D$ is effective (denoted $D \geq 0$) if $ν_P(D) \geq 0$ for all $P\in V$.

Given a non-zero rational function $f\in k(V)^\ast$, the divisor of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} \op{div}(f) := \sum_{P\in V} ν_P(f)\cdot P \qquad \text{(i.e. $ν_P(\op{div}(f)) = ν_P(f)$)}, \end{align*} $$ where $ν_P$ is the valuation at $P$. Any such divisor is called a principal divisor. This is a finite sum and has degree 0.

The set of principal divisors is a subgroup of $\op{Div}^0(V)$. The quotient of $\op{Div}(V)$ is the divisor class group $\op{Cl}(V)$, and the quotient of $\op{Div}^0(V)$ is the degree 0 divisor class group $\op{Cl}^0(V)$. We say that $D, D'\in \op{Div}(V)$ are linearly equivalent if they are in the same divisor class, i.e. there exists $f\in k(V)^\times$ such that $D' - D = \op{div}(f)$.

Note $\mathrm{Div}(V) \to \Z: D\to \deg(D)$ is a group homomorphism, which descends to $\mathrm{Cl}(V)\to \Z$.

$\op{div}: k(V)^\times \to \op{Div}(V)$ is a group homomorphism, because so is $ν_P: k(V)^\times\to \Z$.

For $f,g\in k(V)^\times$, $\op{div}(f) = \op{div}(g)$ iff $f = cg$ for some $c\in k^\times$. (Derivation)

$V$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ iff $\op{Cl}^0(V)$ is trivial, in which case $\op{Cl}(V)\isom \Z$. (Proof)
