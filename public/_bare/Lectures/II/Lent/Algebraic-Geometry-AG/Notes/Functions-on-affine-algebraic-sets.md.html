Definition (regular function, coordinate ring) open_in_new

Let $X\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an affine algebraic set. The ring of regular functions of $X$ or the coordinate ring of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} A(X) = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n] / I(X) \end{align*} $$ where $I(X)$ is the ideal of $X$.

$k$ embeds in $A(X)$ and $A(X)$ is a $k$-algebra.

$X$ is irreducible $\iff$ $I(X)$ is a prime ideal $\iff$ $A(X)$ is an integral domain.

Definition (function field of affine algebraic set) open_in_new

Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an irreducible affine algebraic set. (So $A(X)$ is an integral domain.)

The function field of $X$, denoted $k(X)$, is the field of fractions of $A(X)$. The elements are called rational functions or meromorphic functions.

Definition (localisation of ring at prime ideal) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain with a field of fractions $K$ and a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$.

The localisation of $R$ at $\mathfrak{p}$ is $$ \begin{align*} R_\mathfrak{p} = \sb{f/g\in K}{g\not\in \mathfrak{p}} \end{align*} $$ a subring of $K$. There is a natural injective ring homomorphism $ϕ: R \to R_\mathfrak{p}: r \mapsto r/1$.

Proposition (ideals in localisation) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain, $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal and $R_\mathfrak{p}$ be its localisation. Consider the functions $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{ideals of $R$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{ideals of $R_\mathfrak{p}$}} \\ I &\mapsto ϕ(I) \cdot R_\mathfrak{p} = \sb{f/g}{f\in I, g\not\in \mathfrak{p}}, \\ \sb{f\in R}{f/1\in J} = ϕ\inv(J) &\mapsfrom J. \end{align*} $$

  1. If $I$ is an ideal of $R$ but $I \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, then $ϕ(I) R_\mathfrak{p} = R_\mathfrak{p}$.

  2. $ϕ(\mathfrak{p}) R_\mathfrak{p}$ is the unique maximal ideal of $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

  3. $ϕ(ϕ\inv(J)) R_\mathfrak{p} = J$ for any ideal $J$ of $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

  4. They are inverses when restricted to $\set{\text{prime ideals of }R\text{ contained by }\mathfrak{p}} \leftrightarrow \set{\text{prime ideals of }R_\mathfrak{p}}$, giving a bijective correspondence.

Proof

Proposition (localisation of Noetherian ring is Noetherian) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain, $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal and $R_\mathfrak{p}$ be its localisation.

If $R$ is Noetherian, then so is $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

Proof

Definition (local ring of affine variety) open_in_new

Let $X$ be an irreducible affine variety, and let $x\in X$.

The local ring of $X$ at $x$ is the localisation of $A(X)$ at $\mathfrak{m}_x$ $$ \begin{align*} O_{X,x} := \sb{f/g\in k(X)}{g\not\in \mathfrak{m}_x} = \sb{f/g\in k(X)}{g(x) \neq 0}. \end{align*} $$ If $ϕ\in k(X)$ is in $O_{X,x}$, we say $ϕ$ is regular at $x$.

For $U$ a Zariski open set of $X$, define $$ \begin{align*} O_X(U) := \bigcap_{x\in U} O_{X,x}. \end{align*} $$

If $X$ is an irreducible affine variety, then $O_X(X)$${}= A(X)$.
