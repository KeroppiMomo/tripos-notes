Check the functions are well-defined.

$I\not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ $\implies$ $ϕ(I)\cdot R_\mathfrak{p} = R_\mathfrak{p}$

Let $f\in I\setminus \mathfrak{p}$. Then $1/f\in R_\mathfrak{p}$, so $1 = f\cdot 1/f \in ϕ(I)\cdot R_\mathfrak{p}$. As $ϕ(I) \cdot R_\mathfrak{p}$ is an ideal, it equals $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

Unique maximal ideal

Suppose $J$ is an ideal of $R_\mathfrak{p}$ but $J \not \subseteq ϕ(\mathfrak{p}) R_\mathfrak{p}$. Then there exists some $f/g\in J$ such that $f,g\not\in \mathfrak{p}$. Then $g/f\in R_\mathfrak{p}$, so $1 = (f/g)(g/f) \in J$ and thus $J = R_\mathfrak{p}$.

$ϕ(ϕ\inv(J)) R_\mathfrak{p} = J$

We need to show $$ \begin{align*} J = \sb{f/g}{f\in ϕ\inv(J), g\not\in \mathfrak{p}}. \end{align*} $$ This is obvious from $f/g \in J \iff f/1\in J \iff f \in ϕ\inv(J)$.

Bijective correspondence between prime ideals

First check they are well-defined. We have showed inverse in one direction, so it remains to show $\mathfrak{q}' := ϕ\inv(ϕ(\mathfrak{q})\cdot R_\mathfrak{p}) = \mathfrak{q}$ for a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $R$.

Taking $1\in R_\mathfrak{p}$ shows $\mathfrak{q}' \supseteq ϕ\inv(ϕ(\mathfrak{q})) \supseteq \mathfrak{q}$.

Conversely for $f\in \mathfrak{q}'$, by definition $f = g/h$ for $g\in \mathfrak{q}$ and $h\not\in \mathfrak{p}$, so $fh = g\in \mathfrak{q}$. Since $\mathfrak{q}$ is a prime ideal and $h\not\in \mathfrak{p}\subseteq \mathfrak{q}$, we have $f\in \mathfrak{q}$, showing $\mathfrak{q}' \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$.
