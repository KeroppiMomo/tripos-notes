$\sqrt{J} \subseteq I(Z(J))$

If $f^m \in J$, then for all $p\in Z(J)$, since $f^m(p) = 0$, we have $f(p) = 0$. So $f\in I(Z(J))$.

$I(Z(J)) \subseteq \sqrt{J}$

Suppose $g\in I(Z(J))$ is non-zero, and write $J = (f_1, \ldots, f_r)$. Consider $$ \begin{align*} \tilde J := (f_1, \ldots, f_r, x_{n+1} g - 1)\lhd k[x_1, \ldots, x_n, x_{n+1}] \end{align*} $$ where $f_i\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is considered as an element of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n, x_{n+1}]$.

$Z(\tilde J) = \emptyset$

Suppose to the contrary there is some $\tilde p = (p_1, \ldots, p_n, p_{n+1})\in Z(\tilde J)$, and let $p := (p_1, \ldots, p_n)$ be its projection to the first $n$ components. Now since $0 = f_i(\tilde p) = f_i(p)$, we have $p\in Z(J)$, so $g(\tilde p) = g(p) = 0$. But then $x_{n+1} g - 1$ does not vanish at $\tilde p$, contradiction.

Relating $g$ to $f_1, \ldots, f_r$

By Hilbert's weak Nullstellensatz, $\tilde J = k[x_1, \ldots, x_{n+1}] \ni 1$, so there exists $h_1, \ldots, h_r, h_{r+1}\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_{n+1}]$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^{r} h_i f_i + h_{r+1}(x_{n+1} g -1) = 1. \end{align*} $$ This remains true over the field of fractions $k(x_1, \ldots, x_{n+1})$. Set $x_{n+1} = 1/g(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ to get $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^{r} h_i\Bigl(x_1, \ldots, x_n, \frac{1}{g(x_1, \ldots, x_n)}\Bigr) f_i(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = 1. \end{align*} $$ Finally clear the denominator to get $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^{r} \tilde h_i(x_1, \ldots, x_n) f_i(x_1 ,\ldots, x_0) = g(x_1,\ldots, x_n)^m \end{align*} $$ for some $m$ and $\tilde h_i(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = g(x_1, \ldots, x_n)^m h_i(x_1 ,\ldots, x_n, \frac{1}{g(x_1, \ldots, x_n)}) \in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$. Thus $g^m\in J$.
