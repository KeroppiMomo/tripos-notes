Lemma (Nakayama) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a ring, $J$ be an ideal of $R$, $M$ be an $R$-module, and $N$ be an $R$-submodule of $M$.

Suppose $M$ is finitely generated.

  1. If $JM = M$, then there exists $r\in J$ such that $(1+r)M = 0$.

  2. If $JM + N = M$, then there exists $r\in J$ such that $(1+r)M \subseteq N$.

Proof

Note if $J$ is the unique maximal ideal of $R$ (meaning $R$ is a local ring), then $1+r$ is a unit for any $r\in J$, so we obtain $M \subseteq N$.

Theorem (local parameter) open_in_new

Let $C$ be a (possibly singular) curve.

If $P\in C$ is a smooth point, then $\mathfrak{m}_P$ is a principal ideal of the local ring $O_{C,P}$, generated by any generator of $\mathfrak{m}_P/\mathfrak{m}_P^2$ in $O_{C,P}/\mathfrak{m}_P^2$.

Any generator of $\mathfrak{m}_P$ in $O_{C,P}$ is called a local parameter or local coordinate for $C$ at $P$.

Proof

Suppose $C = Z(f)\subseteq \mathbb{A}^2$ is a (possibly singular) plane curve and $P\in C$ is a smooth point. Then $x - x(P)\in \mathfrak{m}_P$ is a local parameter at $P$ iff $\pdfd{f}{y}(P) \neq 0$. (Proof)

Corollary (valuation of rational function) open_in_new

Let $C$ be a (possibly singular) curve and $P$ be a smooth point of $C$.

There is a surjective group homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} ν_P: k(C)^\times \to \Z \end{align*} $$ called the valuation or order of vanishing at $P$ (with the convention $ν_P(0) = \infty$), satisfying the following:

  1. for any $f\in k(C)^\times$ and any local parameter $π_P$, $$ \begin{align*} f = u \cdot π_P^{ν_P(f)} \end{align*} $$ where $u$ is a unit in the local ring $O_{C,P}$.

  2. $$ \begin{align*} O_{C,P} ={}& \sb{f\in k(C)^\times}{ν_P(f) \geq 0} \cup \set{0}, \\ \mathfrak{m}_P ={}& \sb{f\in k(C)^\times}{ν_P(f) \geq 1} \cup \set{0}. \end{align*} $$

  3. The ultrametric or non-archimedean triangle inequality holds: $$ \begin{align*} ν_P(f+g) \geq \min\set{ν_P(f), ν_P(g)}, \end{align*} $$ and if $ν_P(f) \neq ν_P(g)$, equality holds.

Proof

Therefore if $f\in k(C)$, then $f$ or $1/f$ is regular at $P$, because $ν_P(1/f) = -ν_P(f)$.

Proposition (degree of morphism) open_in_new

Let $ϕ: X\to Y$ be a non-constant morphism of (possibly affine, possibly singular) curves.

$ϕ$ is dominant, and hence induces a field embedding $ϕ^\ast: k(Y) \hookrightarrow k(X)$. Moreover $k(X)/ϕ^\ast k(Y)$ is a finite field extension.

The degree of $ϕ$ is $[k(X) : ϕ^\ast k(Y)]$, denoted $\deg(ϕ)$.

Proof

If $\deg(ϕ) = 1$, then $ϕ^\ast: k(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} k(Y)$ is an isomorphism, so $ϕ$ is birational. If $X,Y$ are (smooth, projective) curves, then $ϕ$ is an isomorphism.

Definition (divisor, principal divisor, linear equivalence) open_in_new

A divisor on a curve $V$ is a finite formal sum $D = \sum_{P\in V} n_P P$ with integer coefficients. We also write $ν_P(D) = n_P$. The set of divisors is the divisor group $\op{Div}(V) = \bigoplus_{P\in V} \Z \cdot P$.

The degree of a divisor $D = \sum n_P P$ is $$ \begin{align*} \deg(D) := \sum n_P. \end{align*} $$ The divisors of degree 0 are denoted by $\op{Div}^0(V)$, a subgroup of $\op{Div}(V)$.

The support of a divisor $D = \sum n_P P$ is the set $$ \begin{align*} \op{supp}(D) := \sb{P\in V}{n_p\neq 0}. \end{align*} $$

A divisor $D$ is effective (denoted $D \geq 0$) if $ν_P(D) \geq 0$ for all $P\in V$.

Given a non-zero rational function $f\in k(V)^\ast$, the divisor of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} \op{div}(f) := \sum_{P\in V} ν_P(f)\cdot P \qquad \text{(i.e. $ν_P(\op{div}(f)) = ν_P(f)$)}, \end{align*} $$ where $ν_P$ is the valuation at $P$. Any such divisor is called a principal divisor. This is a finite sum and has degree 0.

The set of principal divisors is a subgroup of $\op{Div}^0(V)$. The quotient of $\op{Div}(V)$ is the divisor class group $\op{Cl}(V)$, and the quotient of $\op{Div}^0(V)$ is the degree 0 divisor class group $\op{Cl}^0(V)$. We say that $D, D'\in \op{Div}(V)$ are linearly equivalent if they are in the same divisor class, i.e. there exists $f\in k(V)^\times$ such that $D' - D = \op{div}(f)$.

Note $\mathrm{Div}(V) \to \Z: D\to \deg(D)$ is a group homomorphism, which descends to $\mathrm{Cl}(V)\to \Z$.

$\op{div}: k(V)^\times \to \op{Div}(V)$ is a group homomorphism, because so is $ν_P: k(V)^\times\to \Z$.

For $f,g\in k(V)^\times$, $\op{div}(f) = \op{div}(g)$ iff $f = cg$ for some $c\in k^\times$. (Derivation)

$V$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ iff $\op{Cl}^0(V)$ is trivial, in which case $\op{Cl}(V)\isom \Z$. (Proof)

Definition (hyperplane section, degree of curve) open_in_new

Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be a curve.

For a hyperplane $H = Z(L)$ (where $L$ is a linear form) which does not contain $V$, the hyperplane section $\op{div}(H)$ of $V$ by $H$ is the effective divisor given by $$ \begin{align*} ν_P(\op{div}(H)) := ν_P(L/x_i) \end{align*} $$ the valuation of $L/x_i$, where $x_i$ is any homogeneous coordinate with $x_i(P)\neq 0$.

The degree of $V$ is the degree of a hyperplane section.

This is well-defined: if $x_i(P), x_j(P)\neq 0$, then $ν_P(L/x_j) = ν_P(L/x_i) + ν_P(x_i/x_j) = ν_P(L/x_i)$ since $x_i/x_j$ is regular and non-zero at $P$.

We can similarly define, for $f\in k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ homogeneous of degree $d$, its divisor $\op{div}(f)$ by $$ \begin{align*} ν_P(\op{div}(f)) = ν_P(f/x_i^d) \end{align*} $$ for any $x_i$ with $x_i(P)\neq 0$. > This satisfies $\op{div}(fg) = \op{div}(f) + \op{div}(g)$, so for any linear form $L$ which does not identically vanish on $V$, we get $\op{div}(f) = \op{div}(f/L^d) + d \op{div}(L)$ which is linearly equivalent to $d \op{div}(L)$.

Any two hyperplane sections are linearly equivalent, as $L/L'$ is a rational function. Therefore any two hyperplane sections have the same degree.

Hyperplane section is not preserved under isomorphism, hence neither is the degree of curves.

If $V = Z(f) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$ is a plane curve and $f$ is homogeneous of degree $d$, then the degree of $V$ is $d$. (Proof)

Definition (rational functions with poles bounded by divisor) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a curve and $D = \sum_{P} n_P$ be a divisor.

The space of rational functions with poles bounded by $D$ is $$ \begin{align*} L(D) := \sb{f\in k(V)}{ν_P(f) + n_P \geq 0} = \set{0} \cup \sb{f\in k(V)^\times}{\op{div}(f) + D \geq 0}. \end{align*} $$

Its dimension is denoted $l(D) := \dim_k L(D)$.

$L(D)$ is a $k$-vector space, because of the ultrametric inequality $ν_P(f+g) \geq \min\set{ν_P(f), ν_P(g)}$. As sets, we can identify $$ \begin{align*} (L(D) \setminus \set{0})/k^\times &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{effective divisor linearly equivalent to $D$}} \\ f &\mapsto \op{div}(f) + D. \end{align*} $$

For $V = \mathbb{P}^1$ with affine coordinate $t$, $L(m\cdot \infty)$ has a basis $\set{1, t, \ldots, t^m}$, so $l(m\cdot \infty) = m+1$.

$L(0) = k$ (so $l(0) = 1$), because the only regular functions are constants.

$L(D) = \set{0}$ (so $l(D) = 0$) for $\deg D<0$, because an effective divisor is not linearly equivalent to a negative degree divisor.

For any $P\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} l(D) - 1 \leq l(D - P) \leq l(D). \end{align*} $$ (Proof) By induction, we obtain that if $\deg(D) \geq 0$, then $$ \begin{align*} l(D) \leq \deg(D) + 1. \end{align*} $$

If $D' = D - \op{div}(g)$ for some $g\in k(V)^\times$, then $L(D') \xrightarrow{\sim} L(D): f \mapsto fg$ is a $k$-vector space isomorphism. Hence $l(D') = l(D)$ if $D'$ and $D$ are linearly equivalent.

Definition (differential) open_in_new

Let $K/k$ be a field extension.

The space of differentials $Ω_{K/k}$ is the quotient $K$-vector space $M/N$, where $$ \begin{align*} M = \langle δx\mid x\in K\rangle \end{align*} $$ is the $K$-vector space spanned by formal symbols $δx$, and $N$ is the subspace of rules of ordinary calculus: $$ \begin{align*} N = \langle δ(x+y) - δx - δy, δ(xy) - xδy - yδx, δa \mid x,y\in K, a\in k\rangle. \end{align*} $$ We define $\d x$ to be the coset $δx + N$, The exterior derivative is the $k$-linear map $\d : K \to Ω_{K/k}$.

Quotient rule and power rule follow formally.

For $f\in k(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ a rational function and $y = f(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ for some $x_1, \ldots, x_n\in K$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \d y = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \pdfd{f}{X_i}(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \d x_i. \end{align*} $$

For any local parameter $π_P$ at $P$, define the valuation of the differential $h \d π_P$ at $P$ as $ν_P(h \d π_P) :={}$$ν_P$$(h)$.

Definition (canonical divisor, genus of curve) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a curve and $P\in V$.

For a rational differential $ω\in Ω_{V}$, the divisor of $ω$ is $$ \begin{align*} \op{div}(ω) = \sum_{P\in V} ν_P(ω) P \qquad \text{(i.e. $ν_P(\op{div}(ω)) = ν_P(ω)$)} \end{align*} $$ where $ν_P(ω)$ is the valuation of $ω$ at $P$.

Such a divisor is called a canonical divisor (denoted $K_V$), and its divisor class is called the canonical divisor class.

The genus $g(V)$ of $V$ is $l(K_V)$ for any canonical divisor $K_V$.

$\op{div}(ω)$ and $\op{div}(ω')$ are linearly equivalent for any non-zero rational differentials $ω, ω'$, because $Ω_V$ is one-dimensional so $ω' = f ω$ for some $f\in k(V)^\times$.

Definition (pullback of morphism on differential) open_in_new

Let $ϕ: V\to W$ be a non-constant morphism between curves, so dominant and induces a field embedding $k(W)\hookrightarrow k(V)$. Fix any $t\in k(W) \setminus k$. (Since $t$ and $ϕ^\ast t\not\in k$, $\d t$ spans $Ω_W$ and $\d (ϕ^\ast t)$ spans $Ω_V$.)

Define the pullback of $ϕ$ as the injective $k$-linear map $$ \begin{align*} ϕ^\ast: Ω_V &\to Ω_W \\ f \d t &\mapsto (ϕ^\ast f) \d(ϕ^\ast t). \end{align*} $$

This does not depend on the choice of $t$. (Proof)

If $ω\in Ω_W$, then for $P\in V$ and $Q = ϕ(P)\in W$, $$ \begin{align*} ν_P(ϕ^\ast(ω)) = e_P ν_{Q}(ω) + e_P - 1. \end{align*} $$ where $e_P$ is the ramification degree. In particular $ν_P(ϕ^\ast (\d π_Q)) = e_P - 1$ for a local parameter $π_Q$ of $W$ at $Q$. (Proof)

Theorem (Riemann-Roch) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a curve.

For any divisor $D$ and any canonical divisor $K_V$, $$ \begin{align*} l(D) - l(K_V - D) = 1 - g + \deg(D). \end{align*} $$ ($g$, $l$, $\deg$)

Setting $D = K_V$ (and noting $l(0) = 1$) gives $$ \begin{align*} \deg(K_V) = 2g - 2. \end{align*} $$

If $\deg(D) > \deg(K_V) = 2g - 2$, then $l(K_V-D) = 0$ so $$ \begin{align*} l(D) = 1 - g + \deg(D). \end{align*} $$

Proof

If $g(V) < g(W)$, then there is no non-constant morphism $V\to W$.

If $g(V) = g(W) > 1$, then $\deg ϕ = 1$ so $ϕ$ is an isomorphism.

Definition (morphism associated to divisor, very ample) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a curve and $D$ be a divisor. Suppose $n+1 :={}$$l(D)$${}\geq 2$ (i.e. $L(D) \not \subseteq k$), and let $B = \set{f_0, f_1, \ldots, f_n}$ be a basis for $L(D)$.

The morphism associated to $D$ with respect to $B$ is $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_D := (f_0 : f_1 : \ldots : f_n) : V \to \mathbb{P}^n. \end{align*} $$

If $ϕ_D: V\to ϕ_D(V)$ is an isomorphism, we say $ϕ_D$ is an embedding and $D$ is very ample.

Changing $B$ corresponds to a linear change of coordinates in $\mathbb{P}^n$.

Theorem (condition for very ample divisor) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a curve and $D$ be a divisor.

$D$ is very ample iff $$ \begin{align*} l(D - P - Q) = l(D) - 2 \qquad \text{for all $P,Q\in V$}. \end{align*} $$

Therefore if $D\geq 2g+1$, then $D$ is very ample.

Definition (hyperelliptic curve) open_in_new

A curve $V$ of genus $g>1$ is hyperelliptic if there exists a morphism $ϕ: V\to \mathbb{P}^1$ of degree 2.

If a curve $V$ has genus $g>1$, the following are equivalent:

  1. $V$ is hyperelliptic;

  2. there exists an effective divisor $D\geq 0$ with $\deg D = 2$ and $l(D) = 2$; (Proof)

  3. $V$ is not canonically embedded, i.e. the morphism associated to a canonical divisor is not an embedding, i.e. $K_V$ is not very ample. (Proof)

Every curve of genus 2 is hyperelliptic. (Proof)

There exist genus $g$ hyperelliptic curves for every $g>1$.
