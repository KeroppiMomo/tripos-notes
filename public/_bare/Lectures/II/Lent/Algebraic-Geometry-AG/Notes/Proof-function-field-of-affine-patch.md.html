Check this is well-defined. $ϕ$ is clearly a homomorphism. By homogenization, $ϕ$ is a surjective.

Let's show $ϕ$ is injective. Suppose $f/g$ and $f'/g'$ have $$f(1, x_1, \ldots, x_n) g'(1, x_1, \ldots, x_n) - g(1, x_1, \ldots, x_n) f'(1, x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in I(X).$$ Then the homogenization of this is in $I^\ast = I(X_0^\ast)$ the projective closure, so it suffices to show $X = X_0^\ast$. By last time, we have $X_0^\ast \subseteq X$. Also $X \setminus X_0^\ast \subseteq X \setminus X_0 \subset X \cap \set{x_0 = 0}$. Since $X$ is irreducible and $X = X_0^\ast \cup (X \cap \set{X_0 = 0})$, either $X = X_0^\ast$ or $X = X \cap \set{x_0 = 0}$. The latter cannot happen by hypothesis.
