For any $f\in O_{V,P}$, because $f - f(P)\in \mathfrak{m}_P$, $f - f(P) = π_P \cdot g$ for some $g\in O_{V,P}$. Taking the differential gives $$ \begin{align*} \d f = g \d π_P + π_P \d g. \end{align*} $$ Because $Ω_{V,P}$ is generated by $\sb{\d f}{f\in O_{V,P}}$ as a $O_{V,P}$-module, we get $Ω_{V,P} \subseteq O_{V,P} \d π_P + π_P Ω_{V,P}$. The $\supseteq$ direction is clear from definition, so $$ \begin{align*} Ω_{V,P} = O_{V,P} \d π_P + π_P Ω_{V,P}. \end{align*} $$

To apply Nakayama's lemma, we need to check $Ω_{V,P}$ is finitely generated as a $O_{V,P}$-module. Consider an affine open set $V_0\subseteq V$ containing $P$, and let $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ be the affine coordinates. Any $f\in O_{V,P}$ can be expressed as $f = g/h$ where $g,h\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and $h(P)\neq 0$, so by the quotient rule $$ \begin{align*} \d f = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \underbrace{\frac{h \pdfd{g}{x_i} - g \pdfd{g}{x_i}}{h^2}}_{\in O_{V,P}} \d x_i. \end{align*} $$ Hence $Ω_{V,P} = O_{V,P} \d x_1 + \ldots + O_{V,P} \d x_n$.

Apply Nakayama's lemma with $I = O_{V,P}$, $J = \mathfrak{m}_P O_{V,P}$ (the unique maximum ideal of $I$), $M = Ω_{V,P}$ and $N = O_{V,P} \d π_P$ to obtain $$ \begin{align*} Ω_{V,P} = O_{V,P} \d π_P. \end{align*} $$
