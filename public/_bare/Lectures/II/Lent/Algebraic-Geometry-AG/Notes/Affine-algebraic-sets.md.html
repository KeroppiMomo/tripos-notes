Definition (affine space) open_in_new

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

The affine $n$-space is $\mathbb{A}^n \equiv \mathbb{A}_k^n = k^n$ whose elements are points $p = (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$.

Definition (affine zero locus, affine algebraic set) open_in_new

Let $S \subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ a subset of polynomials in $n$ variables.

The zero locus or vanishing set of $S$ in $\mathbb A^n$ is $$ \begin{align*} Z(S) = \sb{p\in \mathbb{A}^n}{f(p) = 0 \; \forall f\in S} \end{align*} $$ a subset of the affine $n$-space $\mathbb{A}^n$.

An affine algebraic set or affine variety is any set of the form $Z(S)$.

If $f\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is non-constant, $Z(f)$ is called a hypersurface. If $f$ is linear, $Z(f)$ is called a hyperplane.

If $I\lhd k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is the ideal generated by $S$, then $Z(S) = Z(I)$.

For any $S \subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, there exists $f_1, \ldots, f_r\in S$ such that $Z(S) = Z(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$. (Proof)

$\emptyset = Z(1)$ and $\mathbb{A}^n = Z(0)$ are affine algebraic sets.

$Z$ is inclusion-reversing: if $S\subseteq T \subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, then $Z(T) \subseteq Z(S)$.

$\bigcap_{i} Z(S_i) = Z(\bigcup_{i} S_i)$ for any collection $\set{S_i\subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]}_{i}$.

$Z(S) \cup Z(T) = Z(S\cdot T)$ for $S, T\subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, where $S\cdot T = \sb{f\cdot g}{f\in S, g\in T}$. (Proof)

Definition (affine Zariski topology, distinguished open set) open_in_new

The Zariski topology on $\mathbb{A}^n$ is the set of the complements of all affine algebraic sets.

For a non-constant $f\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, the distinguished open set of $f$ is $$ \begin{align*} D(f) = \mathbb{A}^n \setminus Z(f). \end{align*} $$

This is a topology due to these remarks.

$\sb{D(f)}{f\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n] \text{ non-constant}}$ is a base for the Zariski topology.

Definition (irreducible topological space) open_in_new

A topological space $X$ is reducible if there exists proper closed sets $X_1$, $X_2$ of $X$ such that $X = X_1 \cup X_2$. Otherwise it is irreducible.

Definition (ideal of subset of affine space) open_in_new

Let $X \subseteq{}$$\mathbb{A}^n$ be a subset.

The ideal of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} I(X) = \sb{f\in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]}{f(p)\; \forall p\in X}. \end{align*} $$

Indeed $I(X)$ is an ideal of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$.

$I$ is inclusion reversing: if $X \subseteq Y \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$, then $I(Y) \subseteq I(X)$.

$I(X \cup Y) = I(X) \cap I(Y)$ for any $X,Y\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$.

Affine algebraic sets and ideals open_in_new

Here we will show that $Z$ and $I$ give an inclusion-reversing bijective correspondence $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{affine algebraic sets in $\mathbb{A}^n$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{radical ideals of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$}} \\ \set{\text{irreducible affine varieties in $\mathbb{A}^n$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{prime ideals of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$}} \\ \set{\text{points in $\mathbb{A}^n$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{maximal ideals of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$}} \\ X &\mapsto I(X) \\ Z(J) &\mapsfrom J. \end{align*} $$

  • $X = Z(I(X))$ for any affine algebraic set $X$. (Proof)

  • $X = Y$ iff $I(X) = I(Y)$ for any affine algebraic $X,Y$, showing injectivity of $I$. (Proof)

Definition (regular function, coordinate ring) open_in_new

Let $X\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an affine algebraic set. The ring of regular functions of $X$ or the coordinate ring of $X$ is $$ \begin{align*} A(X) = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n] / I(X) \end{align*} $$ where $I(X)$ is the ideal of $X$.

$k$ embeds in $A(X)$ and $A(X)$ is a $k$-algebra.

$X$ is irreducible $\iff$ $I(X)$ is a prime ideal $\iff$ $A(X)$ is an integral domain.

Definition (function field of affine algebraic set) open_in_new

Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an irreducible affine algebraic set. (So $A(X)$ is an integral domain.)

The function field of $X$, denoted $k(X)$, is the field of fractions of $A(X)$. The elements are called rational functions or meromorphic functions.

Definition (localisation of ring at prime ideal) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain with a field of fractions $K$ and a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$.

The localisation of $R$ at $\mathfrak{p}$ is $$ \begin{align*} R_\mathfrak{p} = \sb{f/g\in K}{g\not\in \mathfrak{p}} \end{align*} $$ a subring of $K$. There is a natural injective ring homomorphism $ϕ: R \to R_\mathfrak{p}: r \mapsto r/1$.

Proposition (ideals in localisation) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain, $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal and $R_\mathfrak{p}$ be its localisation. Consider the functions $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{ideals of $R$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{ideals of $R_\mathfrak{p}$}} \\ I &\mapsto ϕ(I) \cdot R_\mathfrak{p} = \sb{f/g}{f\in I, g\not\in \mathfrak{p}}, \\ \sb{f\in R}{f/1\in J} = ϕ\inv(J) &\mapsfrom J. \end{align*} $$

  1. If $I$ is an ideal of $R$ but $I \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, then $ϕ(I) R_\mathfrak{p} = R_\mathfrak{p}$.

  2. $ϕ(\mathfrak{p}) R_\mathfrak{p}$ is the unique maximal ideal of $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

  3. $ϕ(ϕ\inv(J)) R_\mathfrak{p} = J$ for any ideal $J$ of $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

  4. They are inverses when restricted to $\set{\text{prime ideals of }R\text{ contained by }\mathfrak{p}} \leftrightarrow \set{\text{prime ideals of }R_\mathfrak{p}}$, giving a bijective correspondence.

Proof

If $R$ is Noetherian, then so is $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

Definition (local ring of affine variety) open_in_new

Let $X$ be an irreducible affine variety, and let $x\in X$.

The local ring of $X$ at $x$ is the localisation of $A(X)$ at $\mathfrak{m}_x$ $$ \begin{align*} O_{X,x} := \sb{f/g\in k(X)}{g\not\in \mathfrak{m}_x} = \sb{f/g\in k(X)}{g(x) \neq 0}. \end{align*} $$ If $ϕ\in k(X)$ is in $O_{X,x}$, we say $ϕ$ is regular at $x$.

For $U$ a Zariski open set of $X$, define $$ \begin{align*} O_X(U) := \bigcap_{x\in U} O_{X,x}. \end{align*} $$

Morphisms between affine algebraic sets open_in_new

Definition (morphism between affine algebraic sets) open_in_new

Let $X\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$, $Y\subseteq \mathbb{A}^m$ be affine algebraic sets.

A morphism is a function $f: X\to Y$ of the form $f = (f_1, \ldots, f_m)$ where $f_1, \ldots, f_m\in{}$$A(X)$.

Write $\mathrm{Mor}(X,Y)$ for the set of morphisms $X\to Y$.

An isomorphism is a morphism with a two-sided inverse which is also a morphism.

Composition of morphisms is a morphism.

A morphism is a continuous function with respect to the Zariski topology: if $Z = Z(g_1, \ldots, g_r) \subseteq Y$, then $$ \begin{align*} f\inv(Z) = Z(f^\ast g_1, \ldots, f^\ast g_r) \end{align*} $$ where $f^\ast$ is the pullback.

A bijective morphism is not necessarily an isomorphism. (Example)

Definition (pullback of morphism between affine algebraic sets) open_in_new

Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism between affine algebraic sets.

The pullback associated to $f$ is the $k$-algebra homomorphism $$ \begin{align*} f^\ast: A(Y) &\to A(X) \\ g &\mapsto g \circ f. \end{align*} $$

$(g \circ f)^\ast = f^\ast \circ g^\ast$ if $f: X\to Y$ and $g: Y\to Z$ are morphisms.

Theorem (morphisms of affine algebraic sets and algebra homomorphisms between coordinate rings) open_in_new

Let $X\subseteq \mathbb{A}^x$ and $Y\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be affine algebraic sets. The pullback gives bijections $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{morphisms $X\to Y$}} &\leftrightarrow \set{\text{$k$-algebra homomorphisms $A(Y) \to A(X)$}} \\ \set{\text{isomorphisms $X\to Y$}}&\leftrightarrow \set{\text{$k$-algebra isomorphisms $A(Y) \to A(X)$}} \\ f &\mapsto f^\ast \\ (λy_1, \ldots, λy_n) &\mapsfrom λ \end{align*} $$ where $y_1, \ldots, y_n$ are coordinates in $\mathbb{A}^n$.

Proof
