Recall any proper closed subvariety of a curve $X$ is finite.

By continuity, for each $Q\in Y$, the preimage $ϕ\inv(Q)$ is a closed subvariety of $X$. As $ϕ$ is non-constant, $ϕ\inv(Q)$ is proper, so $Q$ has finite preimage.

Any infinite set of $Y$ is dense. Because $X$ is infinite and each $Q\in Y$ has finite preimage, $ϕ(X)$ is infinite, hence dense. Therefore $ϕ$ is dominant and induces $ϕ^\ast: k(Y) \hookrightarrow k(X)$.

Recall also $k(Y)$ and $k(X)$ have transcendence degree 1 over $k$. Take any $t\in k(Y)\setminus k$, which is necessarily transcendental. Then by injectivity, $ϕ^\ast(t)\in k(X)\setminus k$, so is transcendental (as $k$ is algebraically closed). Therefore $k(X)$ is algebraic over $k(ϕ^\ast(t))$, so finite (as $k(X)$ is finitely generated). Hence $$ \begin{align*} k(X) \; / \; ϕ^\ast k(Y) \; / \; k(ϕ^\ast(t)) \end{align*} $$ shows $k(X)/ϕ^\ast k(Y)$ is finite.
