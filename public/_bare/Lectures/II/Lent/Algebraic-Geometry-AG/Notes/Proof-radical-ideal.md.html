Let $r\in \sqrt{I}$, so there is some $m>0$ such that $r^m \in I$. For any $s\in R$, we have $(s r)^m = s^m r^m\in I$ as $I$ is an ideal, so $sr\in \sqrt{I}$.

It remains to show if $r^m \in I$ and $s^n\in I$, then $(r+s)^p\in I$ for some $p$. By the binomial theorem, $$ \begin{align*} (r+s)^{m+n-1} = \sum_{i=0}^{m+n-1} \pmat{m+n-1 \\ i} r^i s^{m+n-1-i}. \end{align*} $$ Since either $i\geq m$ or $m+n-1-i\geq n$, each term is a multiple of either $r^m$ or $s^n$, so in $I$. Hence $(r + s)^{m+n-1} \in I$.
