Huygen's principle open_in_new

  • Every point on a primary wavefront behaves as a source of spherical secondary wavelets;

  • The primary wavefront at a later time is the envelop of these wavelets.

Reflection and refraction open_in_new

A plane wave travels from a medium with refractive index $n_1 = c/v_1$ to one with $n_2 = c/v_2$.

(Usually larger $\lambda$ means smaller $n$.)

Law of reflection open_in_new

Angle of incidence $\theta_i = \theta_r$ angle of reflection. (Derivation)

If $n_2 > n_1$, there is a $\pi$ phase change upon reflection. If $n_2<n_1$, there is no phase change.

Snell's law open_in_new

Angle of incidence $\theta_1$ and angle of refraction $\theta_2$ are related by $$ \begin{align*} n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Total internal refraction occurs when $\sin\theta_2 > 1$.

Image-object relationship of refraction open_in_new

Refraction at a spherical surface of radius $R$: for small angles, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{n_1}{u} + \frac{n_2}{v} = \frac{n_2-n_1}{R} \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Take $R = \infty$ for planar surface. (Derivation)

Lens-maker's equation and lens equation open_in_new

A thin lens has focal length $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}\right) \equiv \frac{1}{f}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

The focal length $f$ is defined as the image distnace $v$ when $u=\infty$.

Sign convention:

Magnification of thin lens open_in_new

By similar triangles, $$ \begin{align*} M = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{v}{u} \end{align*} $$

Power of a lens open_in_new

Power of a lens $P$ (unit dioptres) is defined to be $$ \begin{align*} P = \frac{1}{f} \end{align*} $$

Astronomical telescope open_in_new

Angular magnification $$ \begin{align*} M \equiv \frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_o} = \frac{f_o}{f_i}. \end{align*} $$

N-slit interference open_in_new

When the distance to screen $\gg$ slit separation $d$, we can assume outgoing light rays are parallel.

Angular position $\theta$ introduces a phase shift of $kd\sin\theta$.

  • For $N=2$ slits each with displacement $\psi=A_0\cos(\omega t)$, the intensity at screen is $$ \begin{align*} I \propto \cos^2\left(\frac{kd\sin\theta}{2}\right). \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

    • Maxima when $d\sin\theta = n\lambda \iff kd\sin\theta = 2n\pi$;

    • Minima when $d\sin\theta = (n+1/2)\lambda \iff kd\sin\theta = (2n+1)\pi$.

  • For a general $N$, add up the phasors.

    • Principal maxima when $d\sin\theta = n\lambda \iff kd\sin\theta = 2n\pi$.

    • Minima when $d\sin\theta = n\lambda/N \iff kd\sin\theta = 2n\pi/N$ where $n\in\Z$ is not a multiple of $N$.

    Non-examinable: $$ \begin{align*} A = \frac{\sin((Nkd\sin\theta)/2)}{\sin((kd\sin\theta)/2)}A_0. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Rayleigh criterion open_in_new

To resolve two lines in diffraction spectrum, the maximum of one line lies over the first minimum of the other.

For an $N$ slit grating to resolve $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2$ in the first-order spectrum, if angles are small, $$ \begin{align*} N = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Single slit diffraction open_in_new

For a single slit of finite width $a$, the amplitude at screen is $$ \begin{align*} A = \frac{\sin((ka\sin\theta)/2)}{(ka\sin\theta)/2} A_0. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Minima when $a\sin\theta = n\lambda$.
