When the distance to screen $\gg$ slit separation $d$, we can assume outgoing light rays are parallel.

Angular position $\theta$ introduces a phase shift of $kd\sin\theta$.

  • For $N=2$ slits each with displacement $\psi=A_0\cos(\omega t)$, the intensity at screen is $$ \begin{align*} I \propto \cos^2\left(\frac{kd\sin\theta}{2}\right). \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

    • Maxima when $d\sin\theta = n\lambda \iff kd\sin\theta = 2n\pi$;

    • Minima when $d\sin\theta = (n+1/2)\lambda \iff kd\sin\theta = (2n+1)\pi$.

  • For a general $N$, add up the phasors.

    • Principal maxima when $d\sin\theta = n\lambda \iff kd\sin\theta = 2n\pi$.

    • Minima when $d\sin\theta = n\lambda/N \iff kd\sin\theta = 2n\pi/N$ where $n\in\Z$ is not a multiple of $N$.

    Non-examinable: $$ \begin{align*} A = \frac{\sin((Nkd\sin\theta)/2)}{\sin((kd\sin\theta)/2)}A_0. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Rayleigh criterion open_in_new

To resolve two lines in diffraction spectrum, the maximum of one line lies over the first minimum of the other.

For an $N$ slit grating to resolve $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2$ in the first-order spectrum, if angles are small, $$ \begin{align*} N = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)
