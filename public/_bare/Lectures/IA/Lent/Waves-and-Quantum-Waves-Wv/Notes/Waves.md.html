The wave equation open_in_new

A wavefunction $\psi(x,t)$ should satisfy the wave equation $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd[2]{\psi}{x} = \frac{1}{c^2}\pdfd[2]{\psi}{t} \end{align*} $$ where $c$ is the wave speed.

  • Any solution is in the form $\psi(x,t) = f(x-ct) + g(x+ct)$. (Derivation)

  • This is linear in $\psi$, so principle of superposition applies i.e. $\psi_1+\psi_2$ is a solution.

  • It has complex solution $\psi = \exp(i(kx \pm \omega t))$, and taking the real part gives the physical quantity.

In higher dimensions, $$ \begin{align*} \nabla^2 \psi \equiv \pdfd[2]{\psi}{x} + \pdfd[2]{\psi}{y} + \pdfd[2]{\psi}{z} = \frac{1}{c^2}\pdfd[2]{\psi}{t}. \end{align*} $$

Wavenumber, wavevector and angular frequency open_in_new

The wavefunction $\cos(kx-\omega t)$ has wavelength $\lambda$ and frequency $f$, and so we define the wavenumber $k$ (unit $\mathrm{m\inv}$) and angular frequency $\omega$ (unit $\mathrm{rad\ s\inv}$) to be $$ \begin{align*} k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}, \quad \omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f \implies c = \frac{\omega}{k}. \end{align*} $$

A plane wave in higher dimensions has a wavevector $\v k$, whose magnitude is $k=2\pi/\lambda$ pointing in the direction of propagation. It has wavefunction $$ \begin{align*} \psi(\v r, t) = A \cos(\v k\cdot\v r - \omega t). \end{align*} $$

Waves on a string open_in_new

A stretched string (with $\psi$ much less than string length) satisfies the wave equation, with $$ \begin{align*} c = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\rho}} \end{align*} $$ where $T$ is the tension and $\rho$ is the mass per length. (Derivation)

For a wavefunction $\psi(x,t)=A\cos(kx - \omega t)$, the kinetic and potential energy densities are $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{K}{x} = \pdfd{U}{x} = \frac{1}{2}\rho \omega^2 A^2 \sin^2(kx - \omega t) = \frac{1}{2}Tk^2A^2\sin^2(kx-\omega t). \end{align*} $$ (Derivation) Total energy density is $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{E}{x} = \rho\omega^2 A^2\sin^2(kx-\omega t), \qquad \left\langle\pdfd{E}{x}\right\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\rho\omega^2 A^2. \end{align*} $$

The wave propagates along the string, so it delivers power to the newly oscillating length: $$ \begin{align*} \langle P\rangle = \left\langle\pdfd{E}{x}\right\rangle \dfd{x}{t} = \frac{1}{2}\rho\omega^2 A^2 c. \end{align*} $$

Standing waves open_in_new

A standing wave has wavefunction in separated form $$ \begin{align*} \psi(x,y,t) = X(x) Y(y) \cos\omega t. \end{align*} $$

Positions where $\psi=0$ are called nodes, and positions having maximum amplitude are called antinodes.

Upon reflection,

  • a fixed end ($\psi=0$) gives a phase change of $\pi$, but

  • a free end ($\partial\psi/\partial x = 0$) gives no phase change.

Solutions are quantised by an integer $n$. The $n=1$ solution is called fundamental, and higher values are overtones or harmonics.

Example (standing wave in pipes) open_in_new

The pressure difference from atmosphere satisfies $$ \begin{align*} \psi_p \propto \pdfd{\psi}{x}. \end{align*} $$

  • Open end has $\psi_p = 0 = \pdfd{\psi}{x}$;

  • Closed end has $\psi = 0$.
