A stretched string (with $\psi$ much less than string length) satisfies the wave equation, with $$ \begin{align*} c = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\rho}} \end{align*} $$ where $T$ is the tension and $\rho$ is the mass per length. (Derivation)

For a wavefunction $\psi(x,t)=A\cos(kx - \omega t)$, the kinetic and potential energy densities are $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{K}{x} = \pdfd{U}{x} = \frac{1}{2}\rho \omega^2 A^2 \sin^2(kx - \omega t) = \frac{1}{2}Tk^2A^2\sin^2(kx-\omega t). \end{align*} $$ (Derivation) Total energy density is $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{E}{x} = \rho\omega^2 A^2\sin^2(kx-\omega t), \qquad \left\langle\pdfd{E}{x}\right\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\rho\omega^2 A^2. \end{align*} $$

The wave propagates along the string, so it delivers power to the newly oscillating length: $$ \begin{align*} \langle P\rangle = \left\langle\pdfd{E}{x}\right\rangle \dfd{x}{t} = \frac{1}{2}\rho\omega^2 A^2 c. \end{align*} $$
