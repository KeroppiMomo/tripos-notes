For a small length $\Delta x$ on a string, the kinetic energy is $$ \begin{align*} \Delta K &= \frac{1}{2}(\rho\Delta x) \left(\pdfd{\psi}{t}\right)^2 \\ \implies \pdfd{K}{x} &= \frac{1}{2}\rho\left(-\omega A\sin(kx - \omega t)\right)^2 \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \rho\omega^2 A^2 \sin^2(kx-\omega t). \end{align*} $$

The potential energy is the work done by tension to extend the string. The extension is $$ \begin{align*} \Delta l - \Delta x &= \sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta \psi)^2} - \Delta x \\ &=\Delta x\left(\sqrt{1 + \left(\pdfd{\psi}{x}\right)^2} - 1\right) \\ &\approx \frac{1}{2}\Delta x \left(\pdfd{\psi}{x}\right)^2. \end{align*} $$ Therefore the potential energy is $$ \begin{align*} \Delta U &= T(\Delta l - \Delta x) = \frac{1}{2} T \Delta x \left(\pdfd{\psi}{x}\right)^2 \\ \implies \pdfd{U}{x} &= \frac{1}{2} Tk^2 A^2 \sin^2(kx-\omega t). \end{align*} $$

In fact $\partial K/\partial x = \partial U/\partial x$ since $\omega/k = c = \sqrt{T/\rho}$.
