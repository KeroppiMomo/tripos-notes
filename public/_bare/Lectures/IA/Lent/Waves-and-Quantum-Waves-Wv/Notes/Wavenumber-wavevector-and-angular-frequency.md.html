The wavefunction $\cos(kx-\omega t)$ has wavelength $\lambda$ and frequency $f$, and so we define the wavenumber $k$ (unit $\mathrm{m\inv}$) and angular frequency $\omega$ (unit $\mathrm{rad\ s\inv}$) to be $$ \begin{align*} k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}, \quad \omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f \implies c = \frac{\omega}{k}. \end{align*} $$

A plane wave in higher dimensions has a wavevector $\v k$, whose magnitude is $k=2\pi/\lambda$ pointing in the direction of propagation. It has wavefunction $$ \begin{align*} \psi(\v r, t) = A \cos(\v k\cdot\v r - \omega t). \end{align*} $$
