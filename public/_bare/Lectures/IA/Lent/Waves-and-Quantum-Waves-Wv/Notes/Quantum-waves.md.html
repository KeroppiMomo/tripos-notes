De Broglie wavelength and frequency open_in_new

Matter behaves as waves. De Broglie argues that the wavelength and frequency are $$ \begin{align*} E = hf = \hbar\omega, \\ p = \frac{h}{\lambda} = \hbar k. \end{align*} $$ where $h$ is Planck's constant and $\hbar = h/2\pi$ is reduced Planck's constant.

Wavefunction of matter wave open_in_new

Matter wave has a complex wavefunction $\hat\psi(x,t)$, where $|\hat\psi(x)|^2$ is the probability density of observing a particle at $x$.

Therefore we have to normalise $$ \begin{align*} \int_{\R} |\hat\psi|^2 dx = 1. \end{align*} $$

De Broglie wave has wavefunction $$ \begin{align*} \hat\psi\propto \exp i(kx-\omega t). \end{align*} $$

Schrodinger's equation open_in_new

The wavefunction $\hat\psi(x,t)$ satisfies the time-dependent Schrodinger's equation $$ \begin{align*} i\hbar \pdfd{\hat\psi}{t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdfd[2]{\hat\psi}{x} + V(x,t)\hat\psi \end{align*} $$ where $V(x,t)$ is the potential energy.

If $V$ and total energy are constant, we have the time-independent Schrodinger's equation $$ \begin{align*} E\hat\psi = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdfd[2]{\hat\psi}{x} + V(x)\hat\psi \iff \pdfd[2]{\hat\psi}{x} + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E-V(x))\hat\psi = 0. \end{align*} $$

In higher dimensions, $$ \begin{align*} \nabla^2\hat\psi + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\left(E-V(x)\right)\hat\psi = 0. \end{align*} $$

Multiply by $e^{-i\omega t}$ to get the time-dependent solution.

Boundary conditions:

  • $V=\infty\implies\hat\psi = 0$.

  • $\hat\psi$ is continuous, since otherwise $p\propto ik\hat\psi = \partial\hat\psi/\partial x = \pm\infty$.

  • $\partial\hat\psi/\partial x$ is continuous, since otherwise $E\propto \partial^2\hat\psi/\partial x^2 = \pm\infty$.

Solution to Schrodinger's equation for constant potentials open_in_new

If $V$ is constant in some region, then we have

  • for $E>V$, $$ \begin{align*} \hat\psi(x) = Ae^{ikx} + Be^{-ikx}, \quad k = \frac{\sqrt{2m(E-V)}}{\hbar}, \end{align*} $$

  • for $E<V$,$$ \begin{align*} \hat\psi(x) = Ce^{\kappa x} + De^{-\kappa x}, \quad \kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V-E)}}{\hbar}. \end{align*} $$

(Derivation)

Infinite potential barrier open_in_new

In 1D, for $V(x) = \infty$ outside $[0,a]$, we impose that $\hat\psi(0) = \hat\psi(L) = 0$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \hat\psi(x) = A\sin kx, \qquad A = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}, \quad \frac{\sqrt{2m(E-V)}}{\hbar} = k = \frac{n\pi}{a}. \end{align*} $$ Therefore the energy is quantised: $$ \begin{align*} E_n - V = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}n^2. \end{align*} $$

In 3D, for $V(x,y,z) = \infty$ outside the box $[0,a]\times[0,b]\times[0,c]$, we can separate the equation $$ \begin{align*} \nabla^2\hat\psi + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E-V(x,y,z)) = 0 \implies \begin{cases} \pdfd[2]{\hat\psi_x}{x} + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E_x - V_x(x)) &= 0 \\ \pdfd[2]{\hat\psi_y}{y} + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E_y - V_y(y)) &= 0 \\ \pdfd[2]{\hat\psi_z}{z} + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E_z - V_z(z)) &= 0 \\ \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \hat\psi(x,y,z) &= A\sin (k_x x) \sin (k_y y) \sin(k_z z), \\ A &= \sqrt{\frac{2}{a} \frac{2}{b} \frac{2}{c}}, \\ E &= \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{n_x^2}{a^2} + \frac{n_y^2}{b^2} + \frac{n_z^2}{c^2}\right). \end{align*} $$

(For finite potential barrier, we can assume the amplitude reflected from the further edge is small to simplify)

Hydrogen atom open_in_new

With the potential energy between an electron and a proton $$ \begin{align*} V(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{e^2}{r}, \end{align*} $$ solutions to the Schrodinger's equation have quantised energy levels $$ \begin{align*} E_n = -\frac{me^4}{8\epsilon_0^2h^2} \frac{1}{n^2} = -\frac{13.6 \mathrm{\ eV}}{n^2}. \end{align*} $$
