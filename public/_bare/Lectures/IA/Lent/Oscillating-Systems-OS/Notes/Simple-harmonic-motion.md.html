A motion is harmonic when its frequency is independent of the amplitude.

A system is in simple harmonic motion if it satisfies $$ \ddot x = -\omega^2 x. $$

It has solutions in the form $$ x(t) = a_0 \cos(\omega t+\phi) \equiv A\cos\omega t + B\sin\omega t. $$

Derivatives of position in SHM open_in_new

The velocity $\dot x$ is $\pi/2$ phase ahead of $x$: $$ \dot x(t) = -\omega a_0\sin(\omega t + \phi) = \omega a_0\cos\big(\omega t+\phi+\frac\pi2\big) $$ and the acceleration is $\pi$ phase ahead: $$ \ddot x(t) = -\omega^2 a_0\cos(\omega t + \phi) = \omega^2 a_0\cos(\omega t+\phi+\pi) $$

Energy in a spring system open_in_new

Consider a spring system $$ m\ddot x = -kx \implies x=a_0 \cos(\omega t+\phi), \quad \omega^2=\frac km. $$

Its potential energy is $$ U = \frac12 kx^2 = \frac12 ka_0^2\cos^2(\omega t+\phi) \implies \langle U\rangle = \frac14 ka_0^2. $$ Its kinetic energy is $$ K = \frac12 m\dot x^2 = \frac12 ma_0^2\omega^2\sin^2(\omega t+\phi) = \frac12 ka_0^2\sin^2(\omega t+\phi) \implies \langle K\rangle = \frac14 ka_0^2. $$ The total energy is conserved: $$ E = U+K = \frac12 ka_0^2. $$

SHM from energy conservation open_in_new

When the total energy of the system is in the form $$ E = \frac12 \alpha \dot x^2 + \frac12 \beta x^2 $$ is conserved, the system is in simple harmonic motion with angular frequency $$ \omega = \sqrt{\frac\beta\alpha}. $$ (Derivation)

Superposition of SHM open_in_new

For superposition of oscillations with equal frequency, can use phasors to figure out amplitude and phase.

Beats: suppose two SHM with similar frequency and equal amplitude interferes. $$ a_0\cos\omega_1 t + a_0\cos\omega_2 t = 2a_0\cos\Big(\frac{\omega_1+\omega_2}2 t\Big) \cos\Big(\frac{\omega_1-\omega_2}2 t\Big). $$ The amplitude oscillates with angular frequency $(\omega_1-\omega_2)/2$.

Complex representation of SHM open_in_new

For physical $x = a_0\cos(\omega t+\phi)$, write $$ z = a_0 e^{i(\omega t+\phi)} = A e^{i\omega t}, \quad A\in\C. $$

Physical values: $$\begin{align*} x &= \Re z \\ \dot x &= \Re\dot z = \Re i\omega z = -\omega\Im z \\ \ddot x &= \Re\ddot z = -\omega^2\Re z \\ U &= \frac12 k(\Re z)^2 \\ K &= \frac12 m(\Re \dot z)^2 = \frac12 k(\Im z)^2 \end{align*}$$
