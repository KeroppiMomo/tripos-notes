Magnetic field $B$ has unit tesla $\mathrm{T} = \mathrm{N\ s\ C\inv\ m\inv} = \mathrm{kg\ s\inv\ C\inv}$.

Electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law) $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E} = -\dfd{\phi_B}{t} \end{align*} $$ where $\phi_B = \int_{S} \v B\cdot d\v A$ is the magnetic flux, with unit weber $\mathrm{Wb} = \mathrm{T\ m^2}$.

An inductor has self-inductance $L$, which has unit henry $\mathrm{H} = \mathrm{T\ m^2\ A\inv} = \mathrm{V\ s^2\ C\inv}$, defined as $$ \begin{align*} \phi_B = LI \implies \mathcal{E} = -L\dot I. \end{align*} $$

Energy stored in inductor = work done to build up a current $$ \begin{align*} W = \int_{0}^{T} L\dfd{I}{t}\cdot I\, dt = \frac{1}{2}LI^2. \end{align*} $$

Simple circuit with inductor open_in_new

  • Connecting with a battery: $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E} = L\dot I \implies I(t) = I(0) + \frac{\mathcal{E}}{L}t. \end{align*} $$

  • RL circuit: characteristic time for current decay $\tau = L/R$, and so characteristic time for energy decay $L/(2R)$ $$ \begin{align*} RI + L\dot I = 0 \implies I(t) = I(0) e^{-(R/L)t} \end{align*} $$
