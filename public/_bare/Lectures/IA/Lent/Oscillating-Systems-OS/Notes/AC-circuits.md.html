We use complex voltage $V = V_0 e^{i\omega t}$ and complex current $I = I_0 e^{i\omega t}$.

Note that $P = \Re(V) \Re(I) \neq \Re(VI)$.

Impedence open_in_new

The complex impedence $Z$ of an electric element is defined using $$ \begin{align*} V = ZI. \end{align*} $$

  • Resistor: $Z_R = R$ because $V = IR$.

  • Capacitor: $Z_C = 1/i\omega C$ because $V = q/C = I/i\omega C$.

  • Inductor: $Z_L = i\omega L$ because $V = L\dot I = i\omega LI$.

Equivalence impedence:

  • In series $$ \begin{align*} Z = \sum_i Z_i \implies R = \sum_i R_i, \quad \frac{1}{C} = \sum_i \frac{1}{C_i}, \quad L = \sum_i L_i. \end{align*} $$

  • In parallel $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{Z} = \sum_i \frac{1}{Z_i} \implies \frac{1}{R} = \sum_i \frac{1}{R_i} , \quad C = \sum_i C_i, \quad \frac{1}{L} = \sum_i L_i. \end{align*} $$

RC filter open_in_new

Connect an AC source with voltage $V = V_0 e^{i\omega t}$ to a resistor $R$ and capacitor $C$, and measure the voltage across the capacitor.

Then using potential divider formula, $$ \begin{align*} V_C = V \frac{Z_C}{Z_C + Z_R} = \frac{1}{i\omega RC + 1}V \implies |V_C| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega RC)^2 + 1}} V_0. \end{align*} $$ So this is a low-pass filter, i.e. large $|V_C / V|$ for small $\omega$.

Driven LCR circuit open_in_new

$$ \begin{align*} \frac{q}{C} + R\dot q + L\ddot q = v_0\cos(\omega t) \end{align*} $$ For steady state response, $q$ oscillates at $\omega$, so we can use impendence $I = V/Z$ where $Z = 1/i\omega C + R + i\omega L$. $$ \begin{align*} |I| = \frac{v_0}{\sqrt{R^2 + (\omega L - 1/\omega C)^2}} \end{align*} $$ At resonance $\omega = \omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$, the current is at a maximum with $Z = R$.

Power dissipated in the circuit is only by the resistor, so $$ \begin{align*} \langle P\rangle = \frac{1}{2}|I|^2 R = \frac{v_0^2 R}{2\left(R^2 + (\omega L-1/\omega C)^2\right)} \end{align*} $$ which has maximum power $\langle P\rangle = v_0^2/2R$ at resonance.

Width of resonant power peak (difference of $\omega$ at half maximum power) $$ \begin{align*} \delta\omega = \frac{R}{L} \implies \frac{\delta\omega}{\omega_0} = R\sqrt{\frac{C}{L}} = \frac{1}{Q}. \end{align*} $$ This can be used as a selective amplifier/filter.
