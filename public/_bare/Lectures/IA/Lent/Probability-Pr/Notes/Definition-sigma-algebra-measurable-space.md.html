Let $E$ be a set.

A set $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal P(E)$ of subsets of $\Omega$ is a $\sigma$-algebra or tribe on $E$ if it satisfies

  • $E \in \mathcal A$;

  • stability by complementation: $A^\complement\in\mathcal F$ for all $A\in\mathcal F$; and

  • stability by countable unions: if $(A_n)_{n\in\N}$ is a countable sequence in $\mathcal A$, then $\bigcup_{n\in\N} A_n \in \mathcal A$.

Equivalently:

  • $\mathcal F$ is non-empty;

  • $\mathcal F$ is closed under countable set operations (complement, union, intersections). (Derivation of closure under countable intersections)

Elements of $\mathcal{A}$ are called measurable sets, and $(E,\mathcal{A})$ is called a measurable space.

  • Every set $E$ admits the full tribe $\mathcal{P}(E)$ and the trivial tribe $\set{\emptyset, E}$.

  • If $(\mathcal{A}_i)_{i\in I}$ is a collection of $σ$-algebras on $E$, then $\bigcap_{i\in I} \mathcal{A}_i$ is also a $σ$-algebra on $E$.
