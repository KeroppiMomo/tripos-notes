Relativistic momentum open_in_new

For an object which has mass $m$ and velocity $\v v$ measured in an inertial frame, its momentum in the frame is $$ \v p = \gamma_v m\v v $$ where $\gamma_v = 1/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$. (Derivation)

In a frame, total momentum of a system is conserved.

Relativistic energy open_in_new

An object with mass $m$ and speed $v$ measured in an inertial frame has total energy $$ E = \gamma_v mc^2 $$ where $\gamma_v = 1/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$. (Motivation)

  • Rest mass energy: when the object is stationary, its total energy is called rest mass energy $$ E_0 = mc^2. $$

  • Kinetic energy: for a moving object, its kinetic energy is defined to be $$ K = E - E_0 = (\gamma - 1)mc^2. $$

Total energy of a system is conserved, but total kinetic energy is not.

4-momentum open_in_new

The 4-momentum of a particle with mass $m$ and speed $v$ is $$ \v P \equiv m\v U = \Big(\frac Ec, \v p\Big) = \pmat{E/c \\ p_x \\ p_y \\ p_z}.$$ (Derivation)

4-momentum of a system is the sum of the 4-momenta of the particles: $$ \v P = \sum_i \v P_i $$ so 4-momentum conserves in an inertial frame.

Energy-momentum relation open_in_new

For one particle with rest mass $m$, its total energy $E$ and momentum $p$ follows $$ E^2 - (pc)^2 = (mc^2)^2. $$ (Derivation)

  • If $\Delta s^2 < 0$, then we call this interval space-like and the events cannot be causally related.
