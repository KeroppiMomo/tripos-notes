Moment of inertia open_in_new

About an axis $A$, define the moment of inertia $I_A$ of a rigid body with many particles be $$ I_A = \sum_i m_i r_i^2 $$ $$ I_A = \int_\text{body} r^2\,dm $$ where $m$ is the mass and $r$ is the perpendicular distance from $A$ of a particle.

Principle of superposition holds.

Rotational Newton's second law open_in_new

Suppose a rigid body is rotating about a fixed axis $A$. Then $$ \v G_{ext} = I \ddv\theta$$ where $\v G_{ext}$ is the total external moment about $A$, $I$ is the moment of inertia about $A$, and $\ddv\theta$ is the angular acceleration. (Derivation)

Parallel axis theorem open_in_new

Let $A_{CM}$ be an axis through the centre of mass of a rigid body, and $I_{CM}$ is the moment of inertia about $A_{CM}$. If another axis $A$ is parallel to $A_{CM}$ and is distance $a$ from it, then the moment of inertia $I$ about axis $A$ is $$ I = I_{CM} + Ma^2 $$ where $M$ is the total mass of the body. Derivation

Perpendicular axis theorem open_in_new

For a lamina lying on the $x$-$y$ plane, with the $z$ axis in the direction perpendicular to the lamina. The moment of inertia along the three axes are related by $$ I_z = I_x + I_y. $$ (Derivation)
