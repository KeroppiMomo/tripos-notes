Centre of mass open_in_new

The centre of mass of a system of masses is defined as $$ \v R = \frac{\sum_i m_i \v r_i}{\sum_i m_i} = \frac1M \sum_i m_i \v r_i $$ $$ \v R = \frac1M \int_\text{body} \vec r \,dm. $$

Principle of superposition holds.

Moment open_in_new

For a particular origin $O$, if a force $\v F$ is acted at a point $\v r$ from $O$, then its moment vector is given by $$ \v G = \v r \times \v F. $$

Couple moment open_in_new

Two forces $\v F$ and $-\v F$ act at two different positions separated by a displacement $\v a$ (points from where $-\v F$ is to where $\v F$ is). Then about any origin, the moment of the couple is $$ \v G = \v a \times \v F. $$ (Derivation)

Equilibrium of rigid body open_in_new

For a rigid body to be in equilibrium, two conditions have to be satisfied:

  1. net external force = 0;

  2. net external moment about any axis = 0.

Moment of inertia open_in_new

About an axis $A$, define the moment of inertia $I_A$ of a rigid body with many particles be $$ I_A = \sum_i m_i r_i^2 $$ $$ I_A = \int_\text{body} r^2\,dm $$ where $m$ is the mass and $r$ is the perpendicular distance from $A$ of a particle.

Principle of superposition holds.

Rotational Newton's second law open_in_new

Suppose a rigid body is rotating about a fixed axis $A$. Then $$ \v G_{ext} = I \ddv\theta$$ where $\v G_{ext}$ is the total external moment about $A$, $I$ is the moment of inertia about $A$, and $\ddv\theta$ is the angular acceleration. (Derivation)

Parallel axis theorem open_in_new

Let $A_{CM}$ be an axis through the centre of mass of a rigid body, and $I_{CM}$ is the moment of inertia about $A_{CM}$. If another axis $A$ is parallel to $A_{CM}$ and is distance $a$ from it, then the moment of inertia $I$ about axis $A$ is $$ I = I_{CM} + Ma^2 $$ where $M$ is the total mass of the body. Derivation

Perpendicular axis theorem open_in_new

For a lamina lying on the $x$-$y$ plane, with the $z$ axis in the direction perpendicular to the lamina. The moment of inertia along the three axes are related by $$ I_z = I_x + I_y. $$ (Derivation)

General motion of a rigid body open_in_new

Angular momentum open_in_new

Angular momentum about an origin is defined to be $$ \v L = \v r \times \v p = m\v r\times \v v. $$

Rotational Newton's second law using angular momentum open_in_new

Analogous to $\v F_{ext} = d\v p/dt$, the rotational equivalence is about any point, $$ \v G_{ext} = \frac{d\v L}{dt}. $$ (Derivation)

For a rotating rigid body, integrating from $\v G = I\dv\omega$ gives $$ \v L = I\v\omega $$ about any axis.

Angular impulse open_in_new

The angular impulse is the integration of moment across some time, which also equals the change in angular momentum. $$ \v J_p = \int \v G\,dt = \Delta\v L $$

Rotational kinetic energy open_in_new

For a rigid body rotating about some fixed axis (pure rotation), its rotational kinetic energy is $$ K_{rot} = \frac12 I\omega^2. $$ (Derivation)

We can decompose the general motion of a rigid body into the linear motion of its centre of mass and the rotational motion about an axis through the centre of mass. $$ K = K_{lin,CM} + K_{rot,CM} = \frac12 MV_{CM}^2 + \frac12 I_{CM}\omega^2. $$ (Derivation)

Gyroscope open_in_new

In a gyroscope, the torque $\v G$ is always perpendicular to the angular momentum $\v L$, so $$ \v G = \v \Omega \times \v L $$ where $\v \Omega$ is the rate of precession. (Derivation)
