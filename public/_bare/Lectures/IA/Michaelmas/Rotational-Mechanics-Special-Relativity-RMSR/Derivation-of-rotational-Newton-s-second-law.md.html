Suppose the rigid body is rotating at angular speed $\dot\theta$ about axis $A$. For particle $i$ with mass $m_i$ and distance $r_i$ from axis $A$ is rotating about $A$, it has linear speed $v_i = r_i \dot\theta$.

Now the rigid body has some angular acceleration $\ddot\theta$ about axis $A$. This means that by Newton's second law, the force $F_{i\perp}$ acting on particle $i$ perpendicular to both the radius and axis $A$ follows $$ F_{i\perp} = m_i\frac{dv_i}{dt} = m_i r_i \ddot\theta. $$

This moment of this force about $A$ has magnitude $$ G_i = r_i F_{i\perp} = m_ir_i^2 \ddot\theta, $$ and direction along $A$.

Forces in other directions do not contribute to the angular acceleration, so $$ \vec G_i = m_ir_i^2 \ddv\theta. $$

Summing over all particles, $$ \vec G_{ext} = \sum_i m_ir_i^2 \ddv \theta = I \ddv\theta. $$
