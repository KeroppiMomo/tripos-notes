$$\begin{align*} \frac{d\v L}{dt} &= \frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i} m_i\v r_i\times\v v_i \\ &= \sum_i m_i\Big(\frac{d\v r_i}{dt} \times \v v_i + \v r_i \times \frac{d\v v_i}{dt}\Big) \\ &= \sum_i m_i(\underbrace{\v v_i \times \v v_i}_0 + \v r_i \times \v a_i) \\ &= \sum_i \v r_i \times (m_i \v a_i) \\ &= \sum_i \v r_i \times F_i &&\text{Newton's second law} \\ &= \v G_{total} = \v G_{ext} + \v G_{int} \end{align*}$$

By experiment, we know that all forces between two particles are in line with their relative displacement. For two particles in the system with positions $\v r_1$ and $\v r_2$ from the origin, Newton's third law states that they act equal and opposite forces $\v F$ and $-\v F$ on each other, so the total moment is $$ \v G = \v r_1 \times \v F - \v r_2 \times \v F = (\v r_1 - \v r_2) \times \v F = \v 0, $$ where the last step is given by $\v F$ being in line with $(\v r_1 - \v r_2)$.

Therefore, $\v G_{int} = \v 0$, and $$ \frac{d\v L}{dt} = \v G_{ext}. $$
