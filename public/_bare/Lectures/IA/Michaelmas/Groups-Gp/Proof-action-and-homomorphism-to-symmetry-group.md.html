Homomorphism $\mapsto$ action

Given a homomorphism $\phi: G \to \mathrm{Sym}(X)$, $$ \begin{align*} *: G\times X &\to X \\ gx &\mapsto ϕ(g)(x) \end{align*} $$ defines an action because

  • for all $x\in X$, $e*x = \phi(e)(x) = \mathrm{Id}_X(x) = x$; and

  • for all $g,h\in G$ and $x\in X$, $$\begin{align*} (gh)*x &= \phi(gh)(x) \\ &= (\phi(g)\circ\phi(h))(x) \\ &= \phi(g)\big(\phi(h)(x)\big) \\ &= g*(h*x). \end{align*}$$

Action $\mapsto$ homomorphism

Given an action $*: G\times X\to X$, define a function $$\begin{align*} \phi : G &\to \mathrm{Sym}(X) \\ g &\mapsto (x\mapsto g*x). \end{align*}$$

Note that $$\begin{align*} \bigl(ϕ(g) \circ ϕ(g')\bigr)(x) ={}& ϕ(g)\bigl(ϕ(g') (x)\bigr) \\ ={}& g * (g' * x) \\ ={}& (gg') * x \\ ={}& ϕ(gg')(x). \end{align*}$$

  • Taking $g' = g\inv$, we see that $ϕ(g)$ is a bijection with inverse $ϕ(g\inv)$, so $ϕ(g) \in \Sym(X)$.

  • Moreover this shows that $ϕ$ is a homomorphism.
