Work open_in_new

A force $\vec F$ does work to a body $$ dW = \vec F \cdot d\vec r $$ $$ W = \int_{\vec r_1}^{\vec r_2} \vec F\cdot d\vec r $$ where $\vec r$ is the displacement of the body.

Power open_in_new

Power is the rate of change of work done, $$ P = \frac{dW}{dt}. $$ And using definition of $dW$, $$ P = \frac{\vec F\cdot d\vec r}{dt} = \vec F\cdot\vec v. $$

Potential energy open_in_new

The potential energy $U$ of a system is the work done by an external force $\vec F_{ext}$ on the system, which is the same as $-\vec F_{int}$, negative internal force inside the system. $$ U = \int_{\vec r_1}^{\vec r_2} \vec F_{ext}\cdot d\vec r = -\int_{\vec r_1}^{\vec r_2} \vec F_{int}\cdot d\vec r $$ $$ \frac{dU}{dx} = F_{ext} = -F_{int} $$ $$ \vec\nabla U = \vec F_{ext} = -\vec F_{int} $$

Kinetic energy open_in_new

Define kinetic energy of a mass $m$ with speed $v$ as $$ K = \frac12 mv^2. $$

Work-energy theorem states that work done $W$ on a body equals the change in kinetic energy $\Delta K$ of the body: $$ W = \Delta K $$ which is equivalent to the conservation of mechanical energy $$ U + K = \text{const.} $$ by $\Delta U = -W$.
