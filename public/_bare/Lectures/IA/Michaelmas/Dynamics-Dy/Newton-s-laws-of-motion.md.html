1. If no net force acts on a body, its velocity vector stays unchanged.

  2. For a body with inertial mass $m$, the net force $\vec F$ and acceleration $\vec a$ are related by $$ \vec F = m\vec a. $$ Or, the net force $\vec F$ and the momentum $\vec p$ is related by $$ \vec F = \frac{d\vec p}{dt}. $$

  3. If body A exerts a force $\vec F_{A\to B}$ on another body B, then the force $\vec F_{B\to A}$ B exerts on A is equal and opposite: $$ \vec F_{A\to B} = -\vec F_{B\to A}. $$

Combining the second and third law, we have that for a system of multiple particles, the rate of change of the total momentum is the external force: $$ \frac{d\vec P}{dt} = \vec F_{ext}. $$ (Derivation)
