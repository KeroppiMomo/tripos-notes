This only works if the errors are uncorrelated.

($A,B,Z$ are quantities with errors; $k,n$ are constants without errors.) $$\begin{align*} Z &= A^n &&\implies & \left(\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}\right) &= |n|\left(\frac{\Delta A}{A}\right) \\ Z &= A\pm k &&\implies & \Delta Z &= \Delta A \\ Z &= kA &&\implies & \frac{\Delta Z}{Z} &=\frac{\Delta A}{A} \\ Z &= A \pm B &&\implies & (\Delta Z)^2 &= (\Delta A)^2 + (\Delta B)^2 \\ Z &= AB, A/B &&\implies & \left(\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}\right)^2 &= \left(\frac{\Delta A}{A}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta B}{B}\right)^2 \end{align*}$$

In general, for unrelated measurements $X_i$ with $$ Z = f(X_1, X_2, \ldots), $$ the error of $Z$ is $$ (\Delta Z)^2 = \sum_i \Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial X_i}\Big)^2(\Delta X_i)^2. $$
