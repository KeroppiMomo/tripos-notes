Define the momentum of a body with mass $m$ and velocity $\vec v$ as $$ \vec p = m\vec v. $$

So Newton's second law is $$ \vec F = \frac{d\vec p}{dt}. $$

Conservation of momentum open_in_new

By Newton's third law, $d\vec P/dt = \vec F_{ext}$, so momentum is conserved when there is no net external force.

1D Collision open_in_new

  • Elastic: total kinetic energy is conserved;

  • perfectly inelastic: two objects stick together after collision.

Elastic collision open_in_new

When a mass $m_1$ moving at velocity $u$ collides head-on with a stationary mass $m_2$, the final velocities are $$ v_1 = \frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2} u, \quad v_2 = \frac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2} u. $$ (Derivation)

Impulse open_in_new

Impulse is the change of momentum: $$ \Delta \vec p = \int_i^f \vec F\, dt. $$

We can use impulse to find out the average force: $$ F_{avg} \equiv \frac{1}{\Delta t} \int_{t_0}^{t_0+\Delta t} \vec F\,dt = \frac{\Delta\vec p}{\Delta t}. $$
