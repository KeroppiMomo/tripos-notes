Motional emf open_in_new

Charges in a wire moving across a transverse $\v B$ field experiences Lorentz force $\v F = q\v v\times \v B$. The force moves it across a distance of $\v l$, so there is a motional emf $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E} = (\v v\times\v B)\cdot\v l. \end{align*} $$

Faraday's law open_in_new

Electromagnetic induction occurs without movement, given by Faraday's law $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E} = -\dfd{\Phi_B}{t} \iff \int_{\partial S} \v E\cdot\, d\v l = -\dd{t}\int_S \v B\cdot d\v A \iff \nabla\times\v E = -\dfd{\v B}{t}. \end{align*} $$ where $\v \Phi_B = \int_S \v B\cdot d\v A$ is the magnetic flux.

Lenz's law states that the induced current is in the direction such that the magnetic field due to the induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux.

In the case of a changing magnetic field, $\v E$ is no longer conservative ($\nabla\times(\nabla\Phi) = 0$).

Inductance open_in_new

We define the mutual inductance as the magnetic flux $\Phi_{B1}$ through one circuit caused by the current $I_2$ through a second circuit $$ \begin{align*} M_{12} = \frac{\Phi_{B1}}{I_2}. \end{align*} $$

In the case of magnetic flux caused by the current through the same circuit, we have the self-inductance $$ \begin{align*} L = \frac{\Phi_B}{I}, \end{align*} $$ which has unit henry $\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{T\ m^2/A}$.

Emf across the circuit is then by Faraday's law $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E} = -\dfd{\Phi_B}{t} = -L\dot I. \end{align*} $$

Inductance of solenoids open_in_new

For a solenoid of length $l$, cross-section area $A$, with $N$ turns, $$ \begin{align*} L = \frac{NAB}{I} = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l}. \end{align*} $$

Inductance of thin wires open_in_new

The mutual inductance between wire $i$ (along curve $C_i$ with current $I_i$) and wire $j$ (along curve $C_j$ with current $I_j$) is $$ \begin{align*} L_{ij} = \frac{μ_0}{4π} \int_{C_i} \int_{C_j} \frac{\d \v x_i\cdot \d \v x_j}{|\v x_i - \v x_j|} = L_{ji}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Magnetic field energy open_in_new

Similar to capacitor, we can view the work done to build up a current in an inductor as energy stored in the magnetic field: magnetic field energy density is $$ \begin{align*} U_B = \frac{1}{2\mu_0} B^2. \end{align*} $$

Total energy in electromagnetic field $$ \begin{align*} U_{tot} = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_0 E^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}B^2. \end{align*} $$
