Due to a current, $$ \begin{align*} \v B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}. \end{align*} $$

Ampere argued that no work can be extracted by moving a magnet around a wire, so all trajectories should give the same loop integral of $\v H$: $$ \begin{align*} \oint_C \v B\cdot d\v r = \mu_0 I = \mu_0\int_S \v J\cdot d\v A \iff \nabla\times\v B = \mu_0 \v J. \end{align*} $$

With a time-varying electric field, we have to consider charge conservation: we have the continuity equation $$ \begin{align*} \int_{\partial V} \v J\cdot d\v A &= -\dd{t}\int_{V} \rho\, dV &&\iff & \nabla\cdot \v J &= -\dot\rho = -\epsilon_0\nabla\cdot\dv E \end{align*} $$ or physically, if current cannot continue, charges build up as $J = \dot\sigma$. This leads to an increasing electric field $J = \epsilon_0 \dot E$ (displacement current density).

We should have $$ \begin{align*} \nabla\times\v B = \mu_0\left(\v J + \epsilon_0 \dfd{\v E}{t}\right). \end{align*} $$
