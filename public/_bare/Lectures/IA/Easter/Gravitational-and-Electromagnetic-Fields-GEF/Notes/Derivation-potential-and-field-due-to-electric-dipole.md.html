Due to one charge, $$ \begin{align*} \Phi_1(\v r) = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}. \end{align*} $$ Total potential, using $|\v r|\ll|\v d|$, $$ \begin{align*} \Phi(\v r) &= \Phi_1(\v r-\v d) -\Phi_1(\v r) \\ &\approx -\nabla\Phi_1\cdot\v d \\ &= \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\vhat r\cdot \v d \\ &= \frac{\v p\cdot \vhat r}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2} \\ &= \frac{p\cos\theta}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}. \end{align*} $$

Electric field, using spherical coordinates, $$ \begin{align*} \v E(\v r) &= -\nabla\Phi(\v r) \\ &= -\pdfd{\Phi}{r} \vhat r - \frac{1}{r}\pdfd{\Phi}{\theta}\vhat\theta \\ &= \frac{p\cos\theta}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r^3} \vhat r + \frac{p\sin\theta}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}\vhat\theta \end{align*} $$
