Electromagnetic induction occurs without movement, given by Faraday's law $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E} = -\dfd{\Phi_B}{t} \iff \int_{\partial S} \v E\cdot\, d\v l = -\dd{t}\int_S \v B\cdot d\v A \iff \nabla\times\v E = -\dfd{\v B}{t}. \end{align*} $$ where $\v \Phi_B = \int_S \v B\cdot d\v A$ is the magnetic flux.

Lenz's law states that the induced current is in the direction such that the magnetic field due to the induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux.

In the case of a changing magnetic field, $\v E$ is no longer conservative ($\nabla\times(\nabla\Phi) = 0$).
