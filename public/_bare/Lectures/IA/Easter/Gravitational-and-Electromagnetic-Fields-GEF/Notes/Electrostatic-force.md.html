For two point charges $q_1,q_2$ with separation $\v r$, the Coulomb force is $$ \begin{align*} \v F = \frac{q_1q_2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}\vhat r. \end{align*} $$

We define the electric field $\v E$ as $$ \begin{align*} \v E = \frac{\v F_{electric}}{q} \end{align*} $$ so due to a point charge, $$ \begin{align*} \v E = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}\vhat r. \end{align*} $$

The electric flux density $\v D$ is defined as $$ \begin{align*} \int_S \v D\cdot d\v A = Q_\text{enclosed}. \end{align*} $$

Using divergence theorem, we obtain Gauss' law for electric field $$ \begin{align*} \v D &= \epsilon_0\v E, &\int_S \v E\cdot d\v A &= \frac{Q_\text{enclosed}}{\epsilon_0}, &\nabla\cdot\v E = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}. \end{align*} $$
