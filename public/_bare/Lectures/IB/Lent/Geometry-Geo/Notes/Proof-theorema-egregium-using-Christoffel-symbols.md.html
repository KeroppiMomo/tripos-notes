We saw $Γ_{ij}^k$ can be expressed in terms of the first fundamental form.

Using $$ \begin{align*} \pdd{v} ϕ_{uu} = \pdd{u} ϕ_{uv}, \end{align*} $$ and the definitions of $Γ_{ij}^k$, we expand to get $$ \begin{align*} (Γ_{11}^1)_v ϕ_u + (Γ_{11}^2)_v ϕ_v + e_v N + Γ_{11}^1 ϕ_{uv} + Γ_{11}^2 ϕ_{vv} + e N_v\\ = (Γ_{12}^1)_u ϕ_u + (Γ_{12}^2)_u ϕ_v + f_u N + Γ_{12}^1 ϕ_{uu} + Γ_{12}^2 ϕ_{uv} + f N_u. \end{align*} $$ Let the matrix of $\mathbb{S}_p$ in the basis $\set{ϕ_u, ϕ_v}$ be $\pmat{a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22}}$. Looking at the $ϕ_v$ component, $$ \begin{align*} (Γ_{11}^2)_v + Γ_{11}^1 Γ_{12}^2 + Γ_{11}^2 Γ_{22}^2 - ea_{22} = (Γ_{12}^2)_u + Γ_{12}^1 Γ_{11}^2 + Γ_{12}^2 Γ_{12}^2 - fa_{21}. \end{align*} $$

Since $\II_p = \mathbb{S}_p^T \I_p$, we get $\pmat{a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22}} = \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}\inv \pmat{e & f \\ f & g}$ so $a_{21} = \frac{-eF + fE}{EG - F^2}$ and $a_{22} = \frac{-fF + gE}{EG - F^2}$. Plugging in, $$ \begin{align*} (Γ_{11}^2)_v - (Γ_{12}^2)_u + Γ_{11}^1 Γ_{12}^2 + Γ_{11}^{2} Γ_{22}^2 - Γ_{12}^1Γ_{11}^2 - Γ_{12}^2 Γ_{12}^2 ={}& ea_{22} - fa_{21} \\ ={}& \frac{e(-fF + gE) - f(-eF + fE)}{EG - F^2} \\ ={}& E \cdot \frac{eg - f^2}{EG - F^2} \\ ={}& EK. \end{align*} $$ Rearranging gives the result.
