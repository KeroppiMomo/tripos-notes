Definition (parallel, meridian on surface of revolution) open_in_new

On a surface of revolution,

  • a curve obtained by rotating a point on $η$ is called a parallel;

  • a curve obtained by a fixed angle rotation of the entire $η$ is called a meridian.

Illustration

Lemma (parallel and meridian as geodesics) open_in_new

Consider a surface of revolution. When parametrised at constant speed,

  1. meridians are geodesics;

  2. a parallel $u\equiv u_0$ is a geodesic iff $f'(u_0)=0$.

Proof

Lemma (Clairaut's relation) open_in_new

Let $γ$ be a geodesic on a surface of revolution. Let $θ(t)$ denote the angle at which $γ$ meets the parallel. Then along $γ$, $$ \begin{align*} f\cos θ = \text{const.} \end{align*} $$
