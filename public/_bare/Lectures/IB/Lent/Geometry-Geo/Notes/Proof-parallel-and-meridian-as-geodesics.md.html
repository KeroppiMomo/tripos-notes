Meridian

Let $Σ$ be the surface. For any meridian, the plane $Π$ containing the meridian and the $z$-axis is a plane of reflectional symmetry for $Σ$, so $Π \cap Σ$ is a geodesic on $Σ$.

$Π \cap Σ$ is the union of two opposite meridians, but being geodesics is a local property, so each meridian in $Π \cap Σ$ is a geodesic.

Parallel

WLOG assume $η$ is parametrised by arc length. Then the first fundamental form is $$ \begin{align*} \d u^2 + f^2 \d v^2. \end{align*} $$

A geodesic satisfies the geodesic equations $$ \begin{align*} \dd{t}(\dot u) ={}& f f_u \dot v^2, \\ \dd{t}(f^2 \dot v) ={}& 0 \end{align*} $$ and the constant speed condition $$ \begin{align*} \dot u^2 + f^2 \dot v^2 = \text{const.} \end{align*} $$

For $u\equiv u_0$, we have $\dot u = 0$. The constant speed equation becomes $$ \begin{align*} \dot v = \frac{\text{const.}}{f(u_0)^2} \end{align*} $$ which implies the second geodesic equation. Note $\dot v \neq 0$ so the first geodesic equation holds iff $f'(u_0)=0$.
