We apply Picard's theorem.

The geodesics equations are $$ \begin{align*} &\pmat{E & F \\ F & G} \pmat{\ddot u \\ \ddot v} = \pmat{A(u,v,\dot u,\dot v) \\ B(u,v,\dot u,\dot v)} \\ \iff &\pmat{\ddot u \\ \ddot v} = \pmat{C(u,v,\dot u,\dot v) \\ D(u,v,\dot u,\dot v)} = \pmat{E & F \\ F&G}\inv \pmat{A(u,v,\dot u,\dot v) \\ B(u,v,\dot u,\dot v)} \end{align*} $$ where $A,B,C,D$ are smooth as $\det \pmat{E&F\\F&G}\neq 0$.

Introducing $p = \dot u, q=\dot v$, we obtain a system of first order equations $$ \begin{align*} \dot u ={}& p, \\ \dot v ={}& q, \\ \dot p ={}& C(u,v,p,q), \\ \dot q ={}& D(u,v,p,q). \end{align*} $$ The Lipschitz property holds for RHS as everything is smooth so mean value theorem can apply.
