Second fundamental form vanishing means $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& L = -\langle n_u, σ_u\rangle, \\ 0 ={}& M = -\langle n_u, σ_v\rangle = -\langle n_v, σ_u\rangle, \\ 0 ={}& N = -\langle n_v, σ_v\rangle. \end{align*} $$ Moreover since $\langle n, n\rangle=1$, we have $\langle n_u, n\rangle=0 = \langle n_v, n\rangle$.

Note $\set{σ_u, σ_v, n}$ form an orthogonal basis of $\R^3$. $n_u$ and $n_v$ are orthogonal to all three basis vectors, so $n_u = 0 = n_v$.

From mean value theorem, since $V$ is connected, $n$ is constant over $V$. So $n\cdot σ(u,v)$ is constant.
