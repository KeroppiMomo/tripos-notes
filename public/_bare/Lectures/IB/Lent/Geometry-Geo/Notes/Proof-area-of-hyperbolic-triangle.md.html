Ideal triangle

For an ideal triangle (one with three ideal vertices), since $\mathrm{Möb}(\mathbb{H})$ (where $\mathbb{H} = D$ or $h$) acts triply transitively on points of $\partial H$ with the correct cyclic order, the area of an ideal triangle is independent of which one you pick.

Work in $h$.

The area is $$ \begin{align*} \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}^{\infty} \frac{1}{y^2} \d y \d x = \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \d x = π. \end{align*} $$

Two ideal vertices

Let $A(α)$ be the area of a triangle with angles $0,0,α$.

This shows $A(α)$ is decreasing and continuous in $α$.

This shows that $$ \begin{align*} A(α) + A(β) = π + A(α + β). \end{align*} $$ $F(α) \equiv π-A(α)$ is continuous and increasing, and satisfies $$ \begin{align*} F(α) + F(β) = F(α + β) \end{align*} $$ so $F(α) = λα$ for some $λ\in \R$. So $$ \begin{align*} A(α) = π - λα. \end{align*} $$

This shows $$ \begin{align*} A(α) + A(π-α) = π, \end{align*} $$ so $λ = 1$.

General case

There are two ways to sum up the area: $$ \begin{align*} ABC + A'B'C + A'B'C' ={}& AB'C' + A'BC' \\ ABC + (π - (π-γ)) + π ={}& (π-α) + (π-β) \\ ABC ={}& π - α - β - γ. \end{align*} $$
