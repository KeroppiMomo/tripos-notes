Let $P\subseteq \R^2$ be a planar polygon with all edges paired up. For simplicity assume paired sides have the same Euclidean length, and all sides are oriented.

For a pair of edges $\set{e, \hat e}$, there exists a unique isometry $f_{e\hat e}: e\to \hat e$ respecting the orientation. Define an equivalence relation $P$ by saying $x\sim f_{e\hat e}(x)$ whenever $x\in e$ an edge.

$P/{\sim}$ is a topological surface.

  • Clearly $P/{\sim}$ is Hausdorff.

  • $P$ is locally homeomorphic to a open disc by gluing together maps:

    For a point in the interior (red), choose a disc small enough to avoid all edges.

    For a point on an edge (pink) or at a vertex (green and orange), take sufficiently small sectors in $P$. We can rescale the angle and glue all of them to $B(0,δ)$.

    If the equivalence class of a vertex has only one element (blue), then it is at the same end of a pair of identified edges. Identifying the radii of the sector makes it homeomorphic to a cone, which is homeomorphic to a disc on $\R^2$.

    See this example for more details.

  • $P/{\sim}$ is second countable. In the interior of $P$, take $B(p, δ)$ for $p\in \Q^2 \cap P$ and $δ\in \Q_+$. At a vertex of $P$, take sectors of radius $\Q_+$.
