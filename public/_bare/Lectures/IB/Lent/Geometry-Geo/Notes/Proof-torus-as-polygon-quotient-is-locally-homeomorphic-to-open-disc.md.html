In the interior

Around a point $x\in (0,1)^2$, a sufficiently small open disc $B(x, δ)$ avoids all edges and so $B(0,1) \isom B(x,δ) /{\sim} \subseteq [0,1]^2 /{\sim}$.

On an edge

Consider a point $p = (0,y_0) \sim (1, y_0)\in [0,1]^2/{\sim}$ on the edge of $[0,1]^2$. Fix $δ>0$ small enough so that the closed radius-$δ$ hemidiscs $\overline{U}, \overline{V}$ do not meet the vertices. Define a map $$ \begin{align*} f: &&\overline{U} \cup \overline{V} &\to \overline{B(0,δ)}\subseteq \R^2 \\ f_{\overline{U}}: && (x,y)\in \overline{U} &\mapsto (x, y-y_0) \\ f_{\overline{V}}: && (x,y)\in \overline{V} &\mapsto (x-1, y-y_0). \end{align*} $$ Since $f_\overline{U}$ and $f_\overline{V}$ agree on points identified in $[0,1]^2/{\sim}$, they glue to a map $π \circ f$ on $π(\overline{U} \cup \overline{V}) \subseteq [0,1]^2/{\sim}$ (where $π: [0,1]^2 \to [0,1]^2/{\sim}$ is the quotient map).

Moreover, $f_{\overline{U}}$ and $f_{\overline{V}}$ are continuous, so they induce continuous maps $\pi\circ f_{\overline{U}}$ on $π(\overline{U})$ and $π\circ f_{\overline{V}}$ on $π(\overline{V})$. These then glue to a continuous map $π\circ f: π(\overline{U} \cup \overline{V}) \to \overline{B(0, δ)} \subseteq \R^2$ as $\overline{U}$ and $\overline{V}$ are closed sets.

$π\circ f$ is bijective from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, so it is a homeomorphism. Restrict to $π(U \cup V)$ to get a disc neighbourhood of $p$.

At a vertex

All vertices are identified. Choosing a sufficiently small $0<δ<1/2$, map the radius-$δ$ quarter discs at each vertex to $B(0,δ)$, and argue as before.
