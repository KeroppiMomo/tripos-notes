Allowable parametrisation

Fix any point $p\in Σ$ and any $0\neq v\in T_pΣ$. Take a geodesic $γ: (-ε, ε)\to Σ$ with $γ(0) = p$ and $γ'(0) = v$. For all $t\in (-ε, ε)$, let $γ_t$ be a geodesic such that $γ_t(0) = γ(t)$ and $γ_t'(0)$ is orthogonal to $γ(t)$ at $t$. Assume all geodesics are parametrised by arc length.

If we shrink $ε$, the geodesics $γ_t$ for all $t\in (-ε, ε)$ is defined on $(-δ, δ)$ for some uniform $δ>0$. Define $$ \begin{align*} σ: V = (-δ, δ) \times (-ε, ε) &\to Σ \\ (u, v) &\mapsto γ_v(u). \end{align*} $$

Since geodesics are smooth and $γ_t$ varies smoothly with $t$, $σ$ is smooth.

$γ$ and $γ_0$ meet orthogonally at $p$, so $σ_u(0) = γ_0'(0)$ and $σ_v(0) = γ'(0)$ are linearly independent. Shrink $ε$ and $δ$ to ensure $σ_u$ and $σ_v$ are linearly independent throughout $V$, meaning that $Dσ$ has rank 2.

In particular $Dσ|_0: \R^2 \to T_p Σ$ is an isomorphism, so by the inverse function theorem, $σ$ is a homeomorphism by shrinking $ε$ and $δ$ again. Thus $σ$ is an allowable parametrisation.

More detail on applying inverse function theorem: let $\tilde σ$ be an allowable parametrisation near $p=\tilde σ(0)$. $\R^2 \xrightarrow{Dσ|_0} T_pΣ \xrightarrow{D\tilde σ|_0\inv} \R^2$ is a bijection, so $\tilde σ\inv \circ σ$ (a map between open subsets of $\R^2$) has invertible derivative at $0$. By the inverse function theorem, $\tilde σ\inv \circ σ$ is locally a bijection (homeomorphism). Since $\tilde σ$ is a homeomorphism, so is $σ$ locally (a map from open subset of $\R^2$ to open subset of $Σ$).

First fundamental form

$E$ can be computed directly: $$ \begin{align*} \langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle = \langle γ_v'(u), γ_v'(u)\rangle = 1 \end{align*} $$ by our assumption of parametrisation by arc length.

For $F$, the second geodesic equation of $u \mapsto γ_{v_0}(u) = σ(u,v_0)$ is $$ \begin{align*} \dd{u}(F(u,v_0)) = 0 \end{align*} $$ (because $u = t$ and $v = v_0$) so $F$ does not depend on $u$. Then $$ \begin{align*} F(u,v) = F(0,v) = \langle γ'(v), γ_v'(0)\rangle = 0 \end{align*} $$ since $γ_v$ is orthogonal to $γ$.

At $u=0$, $v\mapsto σ(0,v) = γ(v)$ is parametrised by arc length, so $$ \begin{align*} G(0, v) = \langle σ_v(0,v), σ_v(0, v)\rangle = \langle γ'(v), γ'(v)\rangle = 1. \end{align*} $$ It is also a geodesic, and the first geodesic equation gives $$ \begin{align*} \dd{v}(F(0,v)) = \frac{1}{2}G_u (0,v) \implies G_u(0,v)=0. \end{align*} $$
