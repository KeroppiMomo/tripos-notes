Let $π: S^2 \to \mathbb{RP}^2$ be the quotient map $π(x) = [x] = \set{x, -x}$.

Take two distinct points $π(p), π(q) \in \mathbb{RP}^2$. Since $p,-p,q,-q\in S^2$ are distinct, open balls $B(p,δ), B(-p,δ), B(q,δ), B(-q,δ) \subseteq \R^3$ are pairwise disjoint for a sufficiently small $δ>0$ (say half the minimum distance between any two of $p,-p,q,-q$). Note that $B(p,δ) \cap S^2$ is open in $S^2$ and similarly for the other balls.

Let $U = B(p, δ) \cap S^2 \subseteq S^2$ and $V = π(U)\subseteq \mathbb{RP}^2$. Then $$ \begin{align*} π\inv(V) = U\cup (-U) = \bigl(B(p,δ) \cup B(-p,δ)\bigr) \cap S^2, \end{align*} $$ which is open in $S^2$, so $V$ is an open neighbourhood of $π(p)$. Similarly $π(q)$ has an open neighbourhood $W=π(B(q,δ) \cap S^2) \subseteq \mathbb{RP}^2$. Since $B(p,δ), B(-p,δ), B(q,δ), B(-q,δ)$ are disjoint, $V$ and $W$ are disjoint in $\mathbb{RP}^2$. Thus $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is Hausdorff.
