First consider $0\in D$ and $a\in D\cap \R_{\geq 0}$. Let $γ(t) = (u(t), v(t)): [0,1]\to D$ be a smooth curve from $0$ to $a$. Its length is $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}(γ) ={}& 2\int_{0}^{1} \frac{\sqrt{\dot u^2 + \dot v^2}}{1 - u^2 - v^2} \d t \\ \geq{}& 2 \int_{0}^{1} \frac{|\dot u|}{1 - u^2} \d t \tag{1}\\ \geq{} & 2 \int_{0}^{1} \frac{\dot u}{1 - u^2} \d t \tag{2}\\ ={}& 2 \int_{0}^{a} \frac{\d u}{1 - u^2} = 2 \tanh\inv(a). \end{align*} $$ (1) is an equality iff $v \equiv 0$, and (2) is an equality iff $u$ is monotone. So (sub-curve of) the diameter $γ(t) = (at, 0)$ is globally length-minimising and parametrised at constant speed, and thus is the unique geodesic. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} d_\text{hyp}(0, a) = 2 \tanh\inv(a). \end{align*} $$

$\mathrm{Möb}(D)$ acts on $D$ transitively: given any $p,q\in D$, the Möbius map $$ \begin{align*} μ: z \mapsto λ\frac{z - p}{1 - \bar p z} \end{align*} $$ maps $p$ to $0$, and $λ$ can be chosen so that $|λ|=1$ and $q$ is mapped onto $D\cap \R_{\geq 0}$. Since it acts by isometry, $$ \begin{align*} d_\text{hyp}(p, q) = d_\text{hyp}(μ(p), μ(q)) = 2 \tanh\inv \left|\frac{q - p}{1 - \bar p q}\right|. \end{align*} $$

Moreover Möbius maps send circles/lines to circles/lines and are conformal. Since $μ$ sends $\partial D$ to itself, the diameter is sent to a circular arc meeting $\partial D$ orthogonally.
