At every point, $Σ$ admits an allowable parametrisation with first fundamental form $\d u^2 + G(u,v) \d v^2$ where $G(0, v) = 1$, $G_u(0,v)=0$, and $κ = -\sqrt{G}_{uu}/\sqrt{G}$.

It suffices to check the first fundamental forms agree under some allowable parametrisations.

$κ\equiv 0$

$\sqrt{G}$ solves $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{G}_{uu} ={}& 0, \\ G(0, v) ={}& 1, \\ G_u(0, v) ={}& 0 \end{align*} $$ so $G \equiv 1$. Hence $Σ$ has first fundamental form everywhere $$ \begin{align*} \d u^2 + \d v^2 \end{align*} $$ which is the same as $\R^2$ with $σ(u,v) = (u,v)$.

$κ\equiv 1$

Now $\sqrt{G}$ solves $$ \begin{align*} \sqrt{G}_{uu} ={}& \sqrt{G}, \\ G(0, v) ={}& 1, \\ G_u(0, v) ={}& 0. \end{align*} $$ The solution is $G(u,v) = \cos^2 u$, so $Σ$ has first fundamental form $$ \begin{align*} \d u^2 + \cos^2 u \d v^2, \end{align*} $$ which is the same as $S^2$ parametrised on $σ(u,v) = (\cos u \cos v, \cos u \sin v, \sin u)$.
