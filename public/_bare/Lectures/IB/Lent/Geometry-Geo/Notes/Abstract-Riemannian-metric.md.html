Definition (Riemannian metric on open set in R2) open_in_new

Let $V\subseteq \R^2$ be open.

An (abstract) Riemannian metric on $V$ is a smooth map $$ \begin{align*} V &\to \set{\text{inner product on $\R^2$}} \\ p &\mapsto \I_p =\pmat{E(p) & F(p) \\ F(p) & G(p)} \end{align*} $$ (where $E,G>0$ and $EG-F^2>0$).

Definition (length under Riemannian metric) open_in_new

Let an open set $V\subseteq \R^2$ be equipped with a Riemannian metric $p\mapsto \I_p = \pmat{E(p) & F(p) \\ F(p) & G(p)}$.

The length of a smooth curve $γ(t) = (u(t), v(t)): [a,b]\to V$ is $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}(γ) = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{E\dot u^2 + 2F\dot u \dot v + G\dot v^2} \d t \equiv \int_{a}^{b} \|\dot γ(t)\|_{\I_{γ(t)}} \d t. \end{align*} $$

Definition (Riemannian metric on abstract smooth surface) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be an abstract smooth surface with atlas $\set{(U_i, ϕ_i)}_{i\in I}$ of homeomorphisms $ϕ_i: U_i \to V_i\subseteq \R^2$.

An (abstract) Riemannian metric on $Σ$ is a collection $\left\{\pmat{E_i & F_i \\ F_i & G_i}\right\}_{i\in I}$ of Riemannian metrics on $\set{V_i}_{i\in I}$ such that the transition maps $f = ϕ_j \circ ϕ_i\inv$ are isometries (preserves lengths of curves), i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E_i & F_i \\ F_i & G_i} = (Df)^T \pmat{E_j & F_j \\ F_j & G_j} (Df). \end{align*} $$

Definition (length metric of Riemannian metric) open_in_new

Given an abstract Riemannian metric $g$ on a connected abstract smooth surface $Σ$, define the length metric $$ \begin{align*} d_g : Σ \times Σ &\to \R_{\geq 0} \\ (p,q) &\mapsto \inf_{γ} \mathrm{length}(γ) \end{align*} $$ where $γ$ varies over piecewise smooth curves from $p$ to $q$.

  • $(Σ, d_g)$ is a metric space, with the same topology as the topology $Σ$ has as a topological surface. (Proof)

Theorem (constant curvature genus g surfaces) open_in_new

There exists an Riemannian metric on

  • the sphere $S^2$ (with genus 0) with curvature $1$;

  • the torus $T^2$ (with genus 1) with curvature $0$; and

  • the compact surface $Σ_g$ with genus $g\geq 2$ and curvature $-1$. (Construction 1, Construction 2)
