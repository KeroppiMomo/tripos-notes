Definition (subdivision) open_in_new

A subdivision of a compact topological surface $Σ$ comprises

  1. a finite set $V \subseteq Σ$ of vertices;

  2. a finite set $\set{e_i: \text{continuous }[0, 1] \to Σ}_{i\in E}$ of edges,

such that

  • each $e_i$ is injective on $(0,1)$;

  • $e_i\inv(V) = \set{0,1}$;

  • $e_i(0,1)$ and $e_j(0,1)$ are disjoint for $i\neq j$; and

  • each connected component of $Σ \setminus (\bigcup_{i} e_i[0,1] \cup V)$ is homeomorphic to an open disc, called an open face. Call a face the closure of such a component.

Definition (triangulation) open_in_new

A subdivision is a triangulation if each closed face contains exactly three edges, and two closed faces are either disjoint or meet at an edge or a vertex.

Definition (Euler characteristic) open_in_new

The Euler characteristic of subdivision of a compact topological surface $Σ$ is defined as the integer $$ \begin{align*} χ(Σ) = \# \text{ vertices} - \#\text{ edges} + \# \text{ faces}. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (invariant of Euler characteristic) open_in_new

  1. Every compact topological surface $Σ$ admits a subdivision (and in fact triangulation).

  2. The Euler characteristic $χ(Σ)$ of $Σ$ does not depend on the choice of subdivision, i.e. it defines a topological invariant of the surface $Σ$.

  • $χ(\R^2) = 1$: it admits a subdivision with no vertices, no edges, 1 face.

  • $χ(S^2) = 2$: it admits a subdivision with 1 vertex, no edges, 1 face.

  • $χ(T^2) = 0$: it admits a subdivision with 1 vertex, 2 edges, 1 face.

  • $χ(\mathbb{RP}^2) = 1$: it admits a subdivision with 2 vertices, 2 edges, 1 face.

Proposition (Euler characteristic of a connected sum) open_in_new

The Euler characteristic of a connect sum $Σ_1 \# Σ_2$ is $$ \begin{align*} χ(Σ_1 \# Σ_2) = χ(Σ_1) + χ(Σ_2) - 2. \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • A $g$-torus is the connect sum of $g$ tori ($g$ is the called the genus of the surface). Its Euler characteristic is $χ = -2(g-1)$.
