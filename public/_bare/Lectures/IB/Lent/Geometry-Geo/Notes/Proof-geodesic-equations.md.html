Consider any one-parameter variation of $γ$ $$ \begin{align*} γ_s(t) = σ(u(s,t), v(s,t)). \end{align*} $$

By definition of the first fundamental form, $$ \begin{align*} E(γ_s) = \int_{a}^{b} E\dot u^2 + 2F\dot u\dot v + G\dot v^2 \d t. \end{align*} $$ The integrand has derivative with respect to $s$ $$ \begin{multline} \pdfd{u}{s}\left(E_u \dot u^2 + 2F_u \dot u\dot v + G_u \dot v^2\right) + \pdfd{v}{s} \left(E_v \dot u^2 + 2F_v \dot u \dot v + G_v \dot v^2\right) \\ + \pdfd{\dot u}{s}(2E\dot u + 2F\dot v) + \pdfd{\dot v}{s}(2F\dot u + 2G\dot v) \end{multline} $$ Differentiate under integral sign and integrate by parts, $$ \begin{align*} \dd{s} E(γ_s) = \int_{a}^{b} A\pdfd{u}{s} + B\pdfd{v}{s} \d t \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} A ={}& E_u \dot u^2 + 2F_u \dot u \dot v + G_u \dot v^2 - 2\dd{t}(E\dot u + F\dot v), \\ B ={}& E_v \dot u^2 + 2F_v \dot u \dot v + G_v \dot v^2 - 2\dd{t}(F\dot u + G\dot v). \end{align*} $$ The boundary terms vanish as $u$ and $v$ are constant at endpoints.

$\dd{s}E(γ_s) |_0 = 0$ iff $A$ and $B$ evaluated at $s=0$ vanish.
