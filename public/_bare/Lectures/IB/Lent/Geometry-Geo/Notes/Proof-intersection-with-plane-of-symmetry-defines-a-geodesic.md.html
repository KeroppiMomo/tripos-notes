Fix a point $p\in C\subseteq Σ$, and WLOG suppose $p=0$ (so that the reflection is linear). Note $\R^3$ can be written as two orthogonal direct sums $$ \begin{align*} Π \oplus Π^\perp = \R^3 = T_p Σ \oplus \R n(p). \end{align*} $$ The reflection $\mathrm{Refl}_Π$ acts on $Π \oplus Π^\perp$ as $\id_{Π} \oplus (-\id_{Π^\perp})$.

Since $\mathrm{Refl}_Π$ preserves $Σ$ and fixes $p$, the image of any curve through $p$ on $Σ$ remains through $p$ and on $Σ$, so $\mathrm{Refl}_Π$ preserves $T_pΣ$. Moreover since $\mathrm{Refl}_Π$ is an isometry (which preserves orthogonality), it therefore preserves $(T_pΣ)^\perp = \R n(p)$.

$T_p Σ$ cannot be $Π$, so $\mathrm{Refl}_Π$ does not act as identity on $T_pΣ$. Thus the eigenline $\R n(p)$ must lie inside $Π$.

Parametrise $C$ locally near $p$ as $t\mapsto γ(t)\in C\subseteq Σ$. Since $γ(t) \in Π$, so are $\dot γ(t), \ddot γ(t) \in Π$. Taking $γ$ to have constant speed, we have $\langle \dot γ(t), \ddot γ(t)\rangle = 0$, so $\ddot γ(t) \in Π$ is orthogonal to $\dot γ(t) \in Π \cap T_pΣ$. Thus $\ddot γ(t) \in \R n(p)$. Now invoke this result.
