Definition (hyperbolic disc) open_in_new

Define the hyperbolic disc $(D, g_\text{hyp})$ as the unit disc $D = B(0,1)\subseteq \R^2 \isom \C$ equipped with the abstract Riemannian metric $$ \begin{align*} g_{\text{hyp}} = \frac{4(\d u^2 + \d v^2)}{(1 - u^2 - v^2)^2} \equiv \frac{4\left|\d z\right|^2}{(1 - |z|^2)^2}. \end{align*} $$ for $z = u + iv$.

  • Lengths of curves are then defined as $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}_\text{hyp}(γ) = 2\int_{a}^{b} \frac{|\dot γ(t)|}{1 - |γ(t)|^2} \d t. \end{align*} $$

  • $E \equiv G$ and $F \equiv 0$, so the metric is conformal, and the angles agree with Euclidean angles.

Lemma (Möbius group on unit disc) open_in_new

Möbius transformations that preserves the unit disc are precisely those of the form $$ \begin{align*} z\mapsto e^{iθ} \frac{z-a}{1 - \bar a z}, \qquad θ\in \R, \; |a|<1. \end{align*} $$ Let $\mathrm{Möb}(D)$ denote this group.

Proof

Lemma (Möbius maps acts by isometries on hyperbolic disc) open_in_new

Lemma (geodesics on hyperbolic disc) open_in_new

In $(D, g_{\text{hyp}})$:

  1. Every pair of points is joined by a unique geodesic up to reparametrisation.

  2. The geodesics are diameters of the disc and circular arcs orthogonal to $\partial D$.

  3. The hyperbolic distance from $p$ to $q$ is $$ \begin{align*} d_\text{hyp}(p, q) = 2 \tanh\inv\left|\frac{p-q}{1 - \bar p q}\right|. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (hyperbolic upper half plane) open_in_new

The hyperbolic upper half plane $(h, g_{\text{hyp}})$ is the surface $h=\sb{z\in \C}{\Im(z) > 0}$ with the Riemannian metric $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\d x^2 + \d y^2}{y^2} = \frac{\left|\d z\right|^2}{\Im(z)^2}. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (hyperbolic disc and upper half plane are isometric) open_in_new

The hyperbolic disc $(D, g_\text{hyp})$ and the hyperbolic upper half plane $(h, g_\text{hyp})$ are isometric via $$ \begin{align*} T: h &\to D & T\inv : D &\to h\\ w &\mapsto \frac{w - i}{w + i}, & z &\mapsto i \left(\frac{1 + z}{1 - z}\right). \end{align*} $$

We write $\mathbb{H}$ for the hyperbolic plane without specifying a model.

Proof

  2. The geodesics are vertical half-lines and semicircles orthogonal to $\R$.

  3. The hyperbolic distance from $p$ to $q$ is $$ \begin{align*} d_\text{hyp}(p, q) = 2 \tanh\inv\left|\frac{p-q}{\bar p-q}\right|. \end{align*} $$

Definition (circle inversion) open_in_new

Let $Γ\subseteq \hat \C = \C\cup \set{\infty}$ be a circle/line.

Points $z,z'\in \hat \C$ are inverses for $Γ$ if every circle/line through $z$ and orthogonal to $Γ$ passes through $z'$.

Let $J_Γ$ denote the inversion map, sending $z$ to its inverse with respect to $Γ$.

  • For every $Γ$ and $z$, there exists a unique point inverse to $z$ with respect to $Γ$. (Proof)

Lemma (composition of inverse is a Möbius map) open_in_new

Lemma (isometry group of hyperbolic disc) open_in_new

The isometry group $\mathrm{Isom}(D)$ of the hyperbolic disc is generated by inversions in hyperbolic lines (i.e. circles/lines orthogonal to $\partial D$).

The orientation-preserving isometries $\mathrm{Isom}^+(D)$ are $\mathrm{Möb}(D)$.

Proof

Definition (elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic isometry on hyperbolic plane) open_in_new

Suppose $α\in \mathrm{Isom}^+(\mathbb{H}) \setminus \set{\id}$ be an orientation-preserving isometry of the hyperbolic plane. We say $α$ is

  1. elliptic if it fixes $p\in \mathbb{H}$;

  2. parabolic if it fixes a unique point on $\partial\mathbb{H}$;

  3. hyperbolic if it fixes two points on $\partial\mathbb{H}$.

Every $α\in \mathrm{Isom}^+(\mathbb{H}) \setminus \set{\id}$ falls into one of these classifications.

Definition (parallel, ultraparallel, intersecting hyperbolic lines) open_in_new

Two hyperbolic lines are said to be

  1. parallel if they meet at infinity (i.e. at $\partial H$ but not in $\mathbb{H}$);

  2. ultraparallel if they don't meet in $\mathbb{H}\cup \partial\mathbb{H}$;

  3. intersecting otherwise, i.e. if they meet at one point in $\mathbb{H}$.

Definition (hyperbolic triangle) open_in_new

A hyperbolic triangle is the region bound by three hyperbolic lines, no two of which are ultraparallel.

We say vertices lying at infinity ideal vertices.

Proposition (hyperbolic cosine formula) open_in_new

A hyperbolic triangle with side lengths $A,B,C$ and opposite angles are $α,β,γ$ satisfies $$ \begin{align*} \cosh C = \cosh A \cosh B - \sinh A \sinh B \cos γ. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Proposition (area of hyperbolic triangle) open_in_new

Let $T$ be a hyperbolic triangle with internal angles $α, β, γ$. (At an ideal vertex, the internal angle is zero.) Then $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{area}_\text{hyp}(T) = π - α - β - γ. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Corollary (area of hyperbolic n-gon) open_in_new

The area of a hyperbolic $n$-gon (region bound by $n$ hyperbolic geodesics) has an area $$ \begin{align*} (n-2)π - \sum_{i=1}^{n} α_i \end{align*} $$ where $α_i$ is the internal angles.

Proof: split the $n$-gon into $(n-2)$ hyperbolic triangles.

Constructing constant negative curvature surface with regular polygons open_in_new

Lemma (hyperbolic regular 4g-gon with angle summing up to 2pi) open_in_new

For $g\geq 2$, there is a hyperbolic regular $4g$-gon with internal angles $2π/4g = π/2g$.

Proof

A $4g$-gon with side labels $a_1 b_1 a_1\inv b_1\inv \ldots a_g b_g a_g\inv b_g\inv$ glues to give $Σ_g$.

In hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}$, say a flag comprises

  1. an oriented hyperbolic line;

  2. a point on that line;

  3. a choice of side to the line.

Given two flags, there exists a hyperbolic isometry taking one to another: use a Möbius map to match the point and the oriented line, and compose with an inverse if necessary to match the distinguished side.

Take a hyperbolic regular $n$-gon $G$ with internal angles $2π/4g$. Impose the edge identification: for any two edges $e,e'$ paired, there exists a hyperbolic isometry taking $e$ to $e'$ respecting the orientation of the line, and taking the inside of the polygon relative to $e$ to the outside relative to $e'$. (Illustration)

Now we give an atlas for $Σ_g = G/$side identification:

  1. If $p\in G$ lies in $\mathrm{int}(G)$, pick a small disc $B\subseteq \mathrm{int}(G)$. Then $q(B) \hookrightarrow D$ is an injection (where $q: G \to Σ_g$ is the quotient map).

  2. If $p\in G$ lies on an edge $e$ (excluding vertex), it has an identified $\tilde p$ on the paired edge $\tilde e$, and there exists an isometry $γ$ sending $e$ to $\tilde e$. Let $U = B(p,δ)\cap G$ and $\tilde U = B(p,δ)\setminus G$. Then the isometry $γ: U \hookrightarrow D$ and the inclusion $i: \tilde U \hookrightarrow D$ glue to give an injection $U\cup \tilde U\hookrightarrow D$, which descends to a map on the neighbourhood of $q(p) \in Σ_g$ into $D$.

  3. All vertices are identified. For a vertex $v\in G$, apply a isometry putting $v$ at $0$, and then rotate. The angles of all $4g$ sectors add up to $2π$, so they cover a neighbourhood of $0\in D$. Build a chart at $q(v)$ from this.

Then all transition maps are hyperbolic isometries, which are smooth and preserves the metric. Therefore $Σ_g$ inherits a metric everywhere locally isometric to $(D,g_\text{hyp})$.

Lemma (right-angled hyperbolic hexagon) open_in_new

For each $l_α, l_β, l_γ>0$, there exists a right-angled hyperbolic hexagon with side lengths $l_α, *, l_β, *, l_γ, *$ in cyclic order ($*$ are unspecified lengths). Moreover it is unique up to isometry.

Proof

Definition (pair of pants) open_in_new

A pair of pants is a topological space homeomorphic to the complement of three open discs in $S^2$, where the discs have disjoint closures.

Illustration

We can glue together two isometric right-angled hyperbolic hexagons with side lengths $l_α, *, l_β, *, l_γ, *$ to form a hyperbolic pair of pants. The lengths of the boundary circles are then $2l_α, 2l_β, 2l_γ$, which we can freely choose. The boundary circles are geodesics on the pair of pants, in the sense that they are hyperbolic geodesics under a chart.

With two hyperbolic pants with a boundary circle of common length, we can glue them together via a choice of isometry between the boundary circles. (Illustration)

By gluing hyperbolic pants together, we can obtain hyperbolic surfaces $Σ_g$ of genus $g\geq 2$. The boundary being glued together are geodesics.

Pant decomposition is not unique, giving a space of hyperbolic metrics on $Σ_g$. This gives rise to the moduli space $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{hyperbolic metric on $Σ_g$}} / (\text{diffeomorphisms of this abstract smooth surface}). \end{align*} $$
