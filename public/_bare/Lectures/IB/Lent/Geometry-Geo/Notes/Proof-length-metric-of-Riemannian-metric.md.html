$d_g$ is well-defined

Since $Σ$ is connected and locally path-connected, it is path-connected, so for any two points $p,q\in Σ$, there exists a continuous curve $γ$ from $p$ to $q$.

Around every point $p\in Σ$, there exists a homeomorphism $ϕ: U\to V$ from an open neighbourhood $U\subseteq Σ$ of $p$ to an open set $V\subseteq \R^2$. Pick a small ball $\tilde V = B(f(p), ε) \subseteq V$ around $f(p)$ (which is path-connected) and restrict $ϕ: ϕ\inv(\tilde V) \to \tilde V$ to see $p$ has a path-connected open neighbourhood $ϕ\inv(\tilde V)$. > Now for any $p\in Σ$, the set $\sb{q\in Σ}{\exists \text{continuous curve from }p\text{ to }q}$ is both open and closed (because $q$ is path-connected to $p$ iff any point on a sufficiently small neighbourhood of $q$ is path-connected to $p$), so by connectedness, this set must be the entire $Σ$.

We can cover the image of $γ$ (which is compact) by finitely many charts. Since any two points on an open and connected set $V\subseteq \R^2$ can be joined by a smooth path, replace the piece of $γ$ in the chart by a smooth curve. This gives a piecewise smooth curve from $p$ to $q$.

$d_g$ is a metric

Symmetry and triangle inequality are clear. It remains to show $d_g(p,q)=0\implies p=q$.

Let $p,q\in Σ$ be distinct, so there exists a chart $(U,ϕ)$ for $Σ$ at $p$ where $ϕ: V\to U$, $ϕ(p)=0$ and $q\not\in U$. Take $ε>0$ sufficiently small so that $\overline{B(0,ε)} \subseteq V$. For any curve $γ$ from $p$ to $q$, by continuity, there exist $r\in γ\cap ϕ\inv(\partial B(0,ε))$, and let $τ$ be a sub-curve of $γ$ from $p$ to $r$. Since $\mathrm{length}(τ) \leq \mathrm{length}(γ)$, it suffices to show that $\mathrm{length}(τ)$ is bounded below by some positive quantity.

For any $z\in ϕ\inv(\overline{B(0,ε)})$, the Riemannian metric $$ \begin{align*} \I_z = \pmat{E(z) & F(z) \\ F(z) & G(z)} \end{align*} $$ is positive definite, i.e. $E,G>0$ and $EG - F^2 > 0$. By compactness of $ϕ\inv(\overline{B(0,ε)})$, there exists $0 < δ_1 < δ_2$ such that $$ \begin{align*} δ_1 \|x\|^2 \leq x^T \I_z x \leq δ_2\|x\|^2 \end{align*} $$ for all $x\in \R^2$. Thus $$ \begin{align*} &δ_1 \int_{a}^{b} \|\dot γ\|_{\text{Eucl}} \d t \leq \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{\dot γ^T \I_z \dot γ} \d t \leq δ_2 \int_{a}^{b} \|\dot γ\|_{\text{Eucl}} \d t \\ \implies &δ_1 \,\mathrm{length}_{d_{\text{Eucl}}}(τ) \leq \mathrm{length}_{d_g}(τ) \leq δ_2 \mathrm{length}_{d_\text{Eucl}}(τ). \tag{*} \end{align*} $$ In particular $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}_{d_g}(τ) \geq δ_1 ε \end{align*} $$ as required.

Equivalent topology

$(*)$ shows that the Euclidean metric and the Riemannian metric are equivalent. Therefore mapping through a parametrisation, the induced topologies on $Σ$ are equivalent as well.
