Arc length open_in_new

Definition (arc length of a curve) open_in_new

For some starting point $t_0$, the arc length $s(t)$ is the (signed) distance from $t_0$ to $t$, $$ s(t) = \int_{t_0}^t |\dv x(t')|\,dt'. $$

  • The arc length between two points on a curve is independent of the choice of parametrisation. (Proof)

  • Using $s$ to parametrise the curve i.e. $\v x(s)$, the tangent vector has unit norm: $$ \bigg|\dvd xs\bigg| = \frac{|d\v x/dt|}{ds/dt} = 1. $$

Definition (first fundamental form) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation on a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$. Define $$ \begin{align*} E = \langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle, \quad F = \langle σ_u, σ_v\rangle, \quad G = \langle σ_v, σ_v\rangle \end{align*} $$ which are functions of $(u,v)\in V$.

The first fundamental form $\I$ of $Σ$ in the parametrisation $σ$ can be represented by

  • the formal expression $$ \begin{align*} E \d u^2 + 2F \d u \d v + G \d v^2, \end{align*} $$

  • the inner product on $\R^2$ $$ \begin{align*} I((u_1, v_1), (u_2, v_2)) = E u_1 u_2 + F(u_1 v_2 + v_1 u_2) + Gv_1 v_2, \end{align*} $$

  • or its matrix representation $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} = (Dσ)^T (Dσ). \end{align*} $$


  • The first fundamental form at $p = σ(0)$ represents the Euclidean inner product on $T_p Σ$: for $v = Dσ|_0(v_0)$ and $w = Dσ|_0(w_0)$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \langle v,w\rangle = I(v_0, w_0). \end{align*} $$

Lemma (length of curve on surface) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation on a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, and $γ(t) = σ(u(t), v(t)): [a,b] \to U$ be a smooth curve. Then $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}(γ) = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{Eu'(t)^2 + 2F u'(t) v'(t) + G v'(t)^2} \d t = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{\I(u'(t), v'(t))} \d \end{align*} $$ where $\I(x,y) = x^T \pmat{E & F\\ F & G} y$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

Definition (isometric, locally isometric) open_in_new

Let $Σ, Σ'$ be smooth surface in $\R^3$.

We say $Σ$ and $Σ'$ are isometric if there is a diffeomorphism $f: Σ\to Σ'$ such that for every smooth curve $γ: [a,b]\to Σ$, $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}_Σ(γ) = \mathrm{length}_{Σ'}(f\circ γ). \end{align*} $$

We say $Σ$ and $Σ'$ are locally isometric near $p\in Σ$ and $p'\in Σ'$ if there are open neighbourhoods $U\subseteq Σ$ of $p$ and $U'\subseteq Σ'$ of $p'$ such that $U$ and $U'$ are isometric.

Lemma (locally isometric iff first fundamental form agrees) open_in_new

Let $Σ, Σ'$ be smooth surface in $\R^3$.

$Σ$ and $Σ'$ are locally isometric near $p\in Σ$ and $p'\in Σ'$ iff there are allowable parametrisations $σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ with $σ(0)=p$ and $σ': V\to U'\subseteq Σ$ with $σ'(0)=p'$ such that the first fundamental forms $\I_Σ = \I_{Σ'}$ as functions on $V$.

Proof

Lemma (first fundamental form in different parametrisations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, with two allowable parametrisations $\tilde σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ and $σ: \tilde V\to U\subseteq Σ$ near $p\in Σ$.

Let $ϕ = \tilde σ\inv\circ σ: V\to\tilde V$ be the transition map. The first fundamental forms in the two parametrisations are related by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} = (Dϕ)^T \pmat{\tilde E & \tilde F \\ \tilde F & \tilde G} (Dϕ). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Lemma (angle on tangent plane) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation at $σ(0) = p\in U\subseteq Σ$.

The angle $θ$ between $v = Dσ|_0(v_0)$ and $w=Dσ|_0(w_0)$ on the tangent plane is $$ \begin{align*} \cos θ = \frac{\I(v_0, w_0)}{\sqrt{\I(v_0, v_0)} \sqrt{\I(w_0, w_0)}} \end{align*} $$ where $\I(x,y) = x^T \pmat{E&F \\ F& G} y$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

Lemma (conformal iff first fundamental form is isotropic) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation at $σ(0) = p\in U$.

$E(0)=G(0)$ and $F(0)=0$ in the first fundamental form iff $σ$ is conformal at $0$, i.e. if smooth curves $γ$ and $\tilde γ$ on $V$ pass through $0$ at an angle $θ$, then $σ\circ γ$ and $σ\circ \tilde γ$ on $U$ pass through $p$ at angle $θ$ as well.

Proof

Definition (area on surface) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation for an open set $U$ in a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

Define the area of $U$ as $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{area}(U) = \int_{V} \sqrt{EG - F^2} \d u \d v = \int_{V} \sqrt{\det \I} \d u \d v \end{align*} $$ where $\I = \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}$ is the first fundamental form.

For any open sets $U\subseteq Σ$, define $\mathrm{area}(U)$ by covering it by images of allowable parametrisations.

  • Motivation: viewing $σ_u$ and $σ_v$ as an infinitesimal parallelogram on $Σ$, we integrate over the area $\sqrt{\langle σ_u\times σ_v, σ_u\times σ_v\rangle} = \sqrt{\langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle\langle σ_v, σ_v\rangle - \langle σ_u, σ_v\rangle^2}$.

  • $\mathrm{area}(U)$ does not depend on the parametrisation. (Proof)

Definition (second fundamental form) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation on a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$. Define $$ \begin{align*} L ={}& \langle n, σ_{uu}\rangle = -\langle n_u, σ_u\rangle, \\ M ={}& \langle n, σ_{uv}\rangle = -\langle n_u, σ_v\rangle = -\langle n_v, σ_u\rangle, \\ N ={}& \langle n, σ_{vv}\rangle = -\langle n_v, σ_v\rangle, \\ n ={}& \frac{σ_u \times σ_v}{\|σ_u \times σ_v\|} \end{align*} $$ which are functions of $(u,v)\in V$. (Differentiate $\langle n, σ_u\rangle=0=\langle n, σ_v\rangle$ to see those equalities.)

The second fundamental form $\II$ is represented by

  • the formal expression $$ \begin{align*} L \d u^2 + 2M \d u \d v + N \d v^2, \end{align*} $$

  • the symmetric bilinear form $$ \begin{align*} \II((u_1, v_1), (u_2, v_2)) = L u_1 u_2 + M(u_1 v_2 + v_1 u_2) + N v_1 v_2, \end{align*} $$

  • or its matrix representation $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{L & M \\ M & N} = -(Dn)^T (Dσ). \end{align*} $$

Lemma (second fundamental form as orthogonal distance to tangent plane) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation at $p=σ(0)$.

The orthogonal distance from $σ(u,v)$ to the affine tangent plane $p + T_pΣ$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2}(Lu^2 + 2Muv + Nv^2)\big|_0 + O(u^3, v^3) = \frac{1}{2}\II((u,v), (u,v))\big|_0 + O(u^3, v^3) \end{align*} $$ where $\II(x,y)=x^T \pmat{L&M\\M&N} y$ is the second fundamental form.

Proof

Lemma (second fundamental form vanishes implies surface on affine plane) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation.

If $V$ is connected and the second fundamental form vanishes identically, then $U$ lies in an affine plane in $\R^3$.

Proof

Lemma (second fundamental form in different parametrisations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, with two allowable parametrisations $\tilde σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ and $σ: \tilde V\to U\subseteq Σ$ near $p\in Σ$.

Let $ϕ = \tilde σ\inv\circ σ: V\to\tilde V$ be the transition map. The second fundamental forms in the two parametrisations are related by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{L&M \\ M&N} = \sign(\det Dϕ) \cdot (Dϕ)^T \pmat{\tilde L & \tilde M \\ \tilde M & \tilde N} (Dϕ). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (shape operator) open_in_new

For a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, the shape operator at a point $p\in Σ$ is the negative derivative of the Gauss map $$ \begin{align*} \mathbb{S}_p = -Dn|_p\in \mathrm{End}(T_p Σ). \end{align*} $$

  • The Gauss map is $n: Σ\to S^2$, so $Dn_p: T_pΣ \to T_{n(p)}S^2 = T_pΣ$.

Lemma (second fundamental form in terms of shape operator) open_in_new

If $Σ$ is a smooth oriented surface in $\R^3$, then the second fundamental form at $p\in Σ$ is the symmetric bilinear form on $T_p Σ$ given by $$ \begin{align*} \II_p(v,w) = \langle \mathbb{S}_pv, w\rangle \equiv \I_p(\mathbb{S}_pv,w) \end{align*} $$ where $\mathbb{S}$ is the shape operator and $\I$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

  • Picking an allowable parametrisation $σ$ at $p$, the matrices of the bilinear forms in the basis $\set{σ_u, σ_v}$ then follow $$ \begin{align*} \II = \pmat{L & M \\ M & N} = \mathbb{S}^T \cdot \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} = \mathbb{S}^T \I. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (shape operator is self-adjoint with respect to first fundamental form) open_in_new

For a smooth oriented surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, the shape operator $\mathbb{S}_p$ at a point $p\in Σ$ is a self-adjoint linear map with respect to the inner product $\I_p$ $= \langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle_{\R^3}$.

Proof

Definition (Gauss curvature) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

The Gauss curvature of $Σ$ at $p\in Σ$ is the determinant of the shape operator $$ \begin{align*} κ(p) = \det(\mathbb{S}_p) = \det (Dn|_p: T_p Σ \to T_p Σ). \end{align*} $$

Lemma (Gauss curvature in terms of fundamental forms) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation at $σ(0) = p\in U\subseteq Σ$.

Then the Gauss curvature is given by the ratio of the determinants of the second and first fundamental forms $$ \begin{align*} κ(p) = \left.\frac{LN - M^2}{EG - F^2}\right|_{0} = \left.\frac{\det \II}{\det \I}\right|_0 \end{align*} $$

Proof: follows from the matrix description of $\II(v, w) = \I(\mathbb{S}v, w)$.

  • Note $κ$ is independent of our choice of parametrisation, which agrees with how the matrices $\I$ and $\II$ transform under change of parametrisation.

Definition (flat surface) open_in_new

We say a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$ is flat if $κ$ $\equiv 0$.

  • $κ\equiv 0 \iff \det Dn \equiv 0 \iff Dn$ has rank $<2$ $\iff n_u \times n_v \equiv 0$.

Definition (elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic point on smooth surface) open_in_new

For a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, we say $p\in Σ$ is

  • elliptic if $κ$$(p) > 0$;

  • parabolic if $κ(p) = 0$; or

  • hyperbolic if $κ(p) < 0$.

Lemma (local behaviour of surface given sign of Gauss curvature) open_in_new

Let $p\in Σ$ be on a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

  1. if $p$ is elliptic, then in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of $p$, $Σ$ lies on one side of its affine tangent plane $p+T_p Σ$.

  2. If $p$ is hyperbolic, then in any sufficiently small neighbourhood of $p$, $Σ$ meets both sides of its affine tangent plane.

Proof

  • For $p$ parabolic, $Σ$ is on one side for a cylinder, but intersects with the affine tangent plane for the monkey saddle $σ(u,v) = (u,v,u^3 - 3v^2 u)$.

Proposition (existence of elliptic point on compact smooth surface) open_in_new

Any smooth surface in $\R^3$ that is compact has an elliptic point.

Proof

Theorem (Gauss curvature as area distortion of Gauss map) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and let $p\in Σ$.

Pick any sequence of decreasing open neighbourhoods $U_i \subseteq Σ$ shrinking to $p$, i.e. for all $ε>0$, there exists $I\in \N$ such that $U_i \subseteq B(p, ε)\subseteq \R^3$ for all $i>I$.

If $κ(p) \neq 0$, then $$ \begin{align*} |κ(p)| = \lim_{i\to \infty} \frac{\mathrm{area}_{S^2}(n(U_i))}{\mathrm{area}_Σ(U_i)}. \end{align*} $$
