Definition (energy of curve) open_in_new

Let $γ: [a,b]\to \R^3$ be a smooth curve. Its energy is $$ \begin{align*} E(γ) = \int_{a}^{b} \|γ'(t)\|^2 \d t. \end{align*} $$

Definition (geodesic) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

A geodesic $γ:[a,b]\to Σ$ is a critical point for the energy functional in the space of smooth curves from $γ(a)$ to $γ(b)$.

Formulation: consider any one-parameter variation of $γ$ with fixed end-points, i.e. smooth map $Γ: (-ε, ε)\times [a,b]\to Σ$ such that writing $γ_s(t) = Γ(s,t)$, we have $γ_0 = γ$, and $γ_s(a)$ and $γ_s(b)$ are independent of $s$. Then $γ$ is a geodesic if $$ \begin{align*} \dd{s} E(γ_s)\Big|_{s=0} = 0. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (geodesic equations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation.

A smooth curve $γ: [a,b]\to U$ is a geodesic iff it satisfies the geodesic equations $$ \begin{align*} \dd{t}(E\dot u + F\dot v) ={}& \frac{1}{2}(E_u \dot u^2 + 2F_u \dot u \dot v + G_u \dot v^2), \\ \dd{t}(F\dot u + G\dot v) ={}& \frac{1}{2}(E_v \dot u^2 + 2F_v \dot u \dot v + G_v \dot v^2). \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • These are the Euler-Lagrange equations.

Proposition (geodesic iff acceleration normal to surface) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

A smooth curve $γ: [a,b]\to Σ$ is a geodesic iff $\ddot γ(t)$ is normal to the surface for all $t$, i.e. $\mathrm{D}\dot γ/\d t$${}= 0$.

Consequently, geodesics are always parametrised with constant speed.

Proof

Lemma (geodesic, length minimisation and energy minimisation) open_in_new

Let $γ$ be a smooth curve on a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

  1. If $γ$ has constant speed $\|\dot γ\|$ and locally minimises length among paths with the same endpoints, then $γ$ is a geodesic.

  2. If $γ$ globally minimises energy among paths with the same endpoints (and so is a geodesic), then $γ$ also globally minimises length and has constant speed.

Proof

Corollary (intersection with plane of symmetry defines a geodesic) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, and suppose there exists a plane $Π\subseteq \R^3$ such that $C = Π \cap Σ$ is a smooth curve and reflection in $Π$ preserves $Σ$. Then $C$ is a geodesic on $Σ$ when parametrised at constant speed.

Proof

Definition (parallel, meridian on surface of revolution) open_in_new

On a surface of revolution,

  • a curve obtained by rotating a point on $η$ is called a parallel;

  • a curve obtained by a fixed angle rotation of the entire $η$ is called a meridian.

Illustration

Lemma (parallel and meridian as geodesics) open_in_new

Consider a surface of revolution. When parametrised at constant speed,

  1. meridians are geodesics;

  2. a parallel $u\equiv u_0$ is a geodesic iff $f'(u_0)=0$.

Proof

Lemma (Clairaut's relation) open_in_new

Let $γ$ be a geodesic on a surface of revolution. Let $θ(t)$ denote the angle at which $γ$ meets the parallel. Then along $γ$, $$ \begin{align*} f\cos θ = \text{const.} \end{align*} $$

Proof

Lemma (existence of geodesics) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, $p\in Σ$ and $v \in$ $T_p Σ$.

There exists some $ε>0$ and a geodesic $γ: (-ε, ε)\to Σ$ such that $γ(0)=p$ and $γ'(0)=v$. Moreover $γ$ depends smoothly on the initial condition $(p,v)$.

$γ$ is unique in the following sense: if $\tilde γ: (-\tilde ε, \tilde ε)\to Σ$ is another such geodesic, then $\tilde γ = γ$ on $(-ε, ε) \cap (-\tilde ε, \tilde ε)$.

Therefore there exists a unique maximal geodesic $γ: (T_-, T_+)\to S$.

Proof

Corollary (geodesic parallel coordinates) open_in_new

Any smooth surface in $\R^3$ has allowable parametrisation near any point with the first fundamental form $$ \begin{align*} \d u^2 + G(u,v) \d v^2, \end{align*} $$ satisfying $$ \begin{align*} G(0, v) = 1, \qquad G_u(0, v) = 0. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Lemma (curvature when E=1, F=0) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ$ be an allowable parametrisation on $Σ$.

If the first fundamental form is $\d u^2 + G(u,v) \d v^2$, then the Gauss curvature is $$ \begin{align*} κ = -\frac{\sqrt{G}_{uu}}{\sqrt{G}}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (theorema egregium) open_in_new

The Gauss curvature is an isometry invariant, i.e. if $Σ_1$ and $Σ_2$ are two smooth surfaces in $\R^3$ and $f: Σ_1 \to Σ_2$ is an isometry, then $κ_{Σ_1}(p) = κ_{Σ_2}(f(p))$.

In a local parametrisation, the Gauss curvature can be expressed as $$ \begin{align*} K = \frac{1}{E}((Γ_{11}^2)_v - (Γ_{12}^2)_u + Γ_{11}^1 Γ_{12}^2 + Γ_{11}^2 Γ_{22}^2 - Γ_{12}^1 Γ_{11}^2 - Γ_{12}^2 Γ_{12}^2) \end{align*} $$ where $Γ_{ij}^k$ are the Christoffel symbols.

Proof using geodesic parallel coordinates

Proof using Christoffel symbols

In a orthogonal parametrisation (i.e. $F = 0$), this simplifies to $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{EG}}\left(\pdd{u} \frac{G_u}{\sqrt{EG}} + \pdd{v}\frac{E_v}{\sqrt{EG}}\right). \end{align*} $$

For an isomthermal parametrisation with $F = 0$, $E = G = λ^2$, $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{1}{λ^2} ∇^2(\log λ). \end{align*} $$

With $E = 1$, $F = 0$, we get $$ \begin{align*} K = -\frac{\sqrt{G}_{uu}}{\sqrt{G}}. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (surface of constant curvature) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

  1. If $κ$ $\equiv 0$, then $Σ$ is locally isometric to the plane $\R^2$ (first fundamental form $\d u^2 + \d v^2$).

  2. If $κ \equiv 1$, then $Σ$ is locally isometric to the sphere $S^2$ (first fundamental form $\d u^2 + \cos^2 u \d v^2$).
