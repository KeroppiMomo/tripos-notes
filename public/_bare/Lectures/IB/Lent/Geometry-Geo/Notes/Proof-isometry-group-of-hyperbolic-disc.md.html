In $D$, the Riemannian metric $$ \begin{align*} g_\text{hyp} = \frac{4\left|\d z\right|^2}{(1 - |z|^2)^2} \end{align*} $$ is invariant under $C: z\mapsto \bar z$. We know $\mathrm{Möb}(D)$ acts by isometries, and inversions are compositions of $C$ and Möbius maps, so they are isometries.

Conversely, let $α\in \mathrm{Isom}(D)$. Find $T\in \mathrm{Möb}(D)$ mapping $α(0) \mapsto 0$. Then $T\circ α \in \mathrm{Isom}(D)$ sends the geodesic $D \cup \R_{\geq 0}$ to another geodesic through $0$, which can only be a radius of $D$. So apply a rotation $R$ of the disc so that $R \circ T \circ α$ preserves $D \cup \R_{\geq 0}$. Moreover it fixes $D \cup \R_{\geq 0}$ pointwise, because the distance along this geodesic is monotonic.

$R\circ T\circ α$ preserves angles, so it preserves the unique geodesic orthogonal to $\R\cup D$ at 0, which is $i\R \cap D$. Therefore for any $p\in i\R\cap D$, $R\circ T\circ α$ sends $p$ to either $p$ or $\bar p$. Composing with the inversion $C$ if necessary, we obtain an isometry $S = R\circ T$ or $C\circ R\circ T$ such that $S\circ α$ fixes $\R\cap D$ and $i\R\cap D$ pointwise. This implies $S\circ α$ fixes every geodesic through $0$, and so $S\circ α$ is the identity. Hence $α = S\inv$.
