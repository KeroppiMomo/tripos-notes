Let $e = σ_u$, $f=σ_v/\sqrt{G}$ and $n=(σ_u \times σ_v)/\|σ_u \times σ_v\|$. The first fundamental form implies $$ \begin{align*} \langle e, e\rangle ={}& E = 1, \\ \langle f, f\rangle ={}& \frac{G}{G} = 1, \\ \langle e, f\rangle ={}& \frac{F}{\sqrt{G}} = 0, \end{align*} $$ so $e,f,n$ form an orthonormal basis at all points $(u,v)$.

Differentiating these equations with respect to $u$ and $v$ gives $$ \begin{align*} e_u ={}& αf + λ_1 n, \\ e_v ={}& βf + μ_1 n, \\ f_u ={}& -αe + λ_2 n, \\ f_v ={}& -βe + μ_2 n. \end{align*} $$ for scalars $α,β,λ_1,λ_2,μ_1,μ_2$ (functions of $u,v$).

Since $e_{v} = σ_{uv} = \pdd{u}(\sqrt{G} f)$, we get $$ \begin{align*} β f + μ_1n = \sqrt{G}_u f + \sqrt{G}(-αe + λ_2n) \end{align*} $$ and equating coefficients, $$ \begin{align*} α = 0, \qquad β = \sqrt{G}_u. \end{align*} $$

Moreover $$ \begin{align*} λ_1 μ_2 - λ_2 μ_1 ={}& e_u\cdot f_v - f_u \cdot e_v \\ ={}& \pdd{u}(e\cdot f_v) - \pdd{v}(e\cdot f_u) \\ ={}& -β_u + α \\ ={}& -\sqrt{G}_{uu}. \end{align*} $$

Since the shape operator $\mathbb{S}: T_p Σ \to T_p Σ$ satisfies $\mathbb{S}σ_u = -n_u$ and $\mathbb{S}σ_v = -n_v$ by chain rule, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} n_u \times n_v = (\det \mathbb{S})(σ_u \times σ_v) = κ\sqrt{G} \cdot n. \end{align*} $$ Using $n=e\times f$, LHS is $$ \begin{align*} n_u \times n_v = (-λ_1 e - λ_2 f) \times (-μ_1 e - μ_2 f) = (λ_1 μ_2 - λ_2μ_1) n. \end{align*} $$ Hence we have deduced $$ \begin{align*} κ\sqrt{G} = -\sqrt{G}_{uu}. \end{align*} $$
