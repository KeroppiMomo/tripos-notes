Let $π: S^2 \to \mathbb{RP}^2$ be the quotient map.

Fix $π(p) \in \mathbb{RP}^2$. Let $\overline{D}$ be a closed disc neighbourhood of $p\in S^2$ sufficiently small such that $\overline{D} \subseteq$ an open hemisphere. Then $$ \begin{align*} π|_{\overline{D}} : \overline{D} \to π(\overline{D}) \subseteq \mathbb{RP}^2 \end{align*} $$ is injective. By construction it is also surjective. Moreover, it is a restriction of a continuous function $π$ and is thus continuous.

$π|_{\overline{D}}$ is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, so by the topological inverse function theorem, $π|_{\overline{D}}$ is a homeomorphism. This implies $π|_{D}: D\to π(D)$ is also a homomorphism where $D = \mathrm{int}(\overline{D})$. Thus $π(D)$ is an open neighbourhood of $π(p)$ which is homeomorphic to an open disc.
