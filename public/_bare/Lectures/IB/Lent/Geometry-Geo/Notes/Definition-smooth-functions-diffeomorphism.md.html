Let $f: V\to V'$ be continuous.

  • For an open set $V\subseteq \R^n$ and $V'= \R^m$, $f$ is smooth if it is infinitely differentiable (i.e. all partial derivatives of all orders exist).

  • For a general subset $V\subseteq \R^n$ and $V' = \R^m$, $f$ is smooth at $p\in V$ if there exists an open neighbourhood $B\subseteq \R^n$ of $p$ and a smooth map $F: B \to \R^m$ such that $F|_{B\cap V} = f|_{B \cap V}$.

  • For $V = Σ$ an abstract smooth surface and $V' = \R^m$, $f$ is smooth at $p\in Σ$ if for a chart $(U, ϕ)$ at $p$ in our atlas, the map $f\circ ϕ\inv: ϕ(U) \to \R^m$ is smooth at $ϕ(p)$.

  • For $V=Σ_1$ and $V'=Σ_2$ abstract smooth surface, $f$ is smooth at $p$ if for charts $(U, ϕ)$ at $p$ and $(U', ϕ')$ at $f(p)$, the composition $ϕ' \circ f \circ ϕ\inv$ is smooth at $ϕ(p)$.

$f$ is called a diffeomorphism if it is a homeomorphism, and $f$ and $f\inv$ are smooth.

  • Smoothness on abstract smooth surfaces does not depend on the choice of chart $(U,ϕ)$ at $p$ in our atlas, because if we use a different chart $(U', ϕ')$ at $p$, $$ \begin{align*} f\circ ϕ'\inv = (f\circ ϕ\inv) \circ (ϕ \circ ϕ'\inv) \end{align*} $$ where the transition map $ϕ \circ ϕ'\inv$ is a diffeomorphism. So $f\circ ϕ'\inv$ is smooth iff $f\circ ϕ\inv$ is smooth.
