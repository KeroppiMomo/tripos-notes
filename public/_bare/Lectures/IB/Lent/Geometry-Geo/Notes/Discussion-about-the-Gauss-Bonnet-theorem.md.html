Definition (geodesic polygon) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be an abstract smooth surface with an abstract Riemannian metric $g$.

A geodesic polygon on $(Σ, g)$ is an embedding (i.e. the image under an injective homeomorphism) of a closed disc such that the boundary can be decomposed into finitely many geodesic segments.

Theorem (local Gauss-Bonnet) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be an abstract smooth surface with an abstract Riemannian metric $g$.

If $R$ is a geodesic $n$-gon with internal angle $α_1, α_2, \ldots, α_n$, then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{R} κ_Σ \d A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} α_i - (n-2)π, \end{align*} $$ where $κ_Σ$ is the Gauss curvature of the surface.

Idea of the proof:

  1. Apply Green's theorem to the right function.

  2. Since the sides are geodesics, the line integral concentrates at corners and contributes quantities related to the internal angles.

Theorem (global Gauss-Bonnet without boundary) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a compact abstract smooth surface with an abstract Riemannian metric $g$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{Σ} κ_Σ \d A = 2π\, χ(Σ) \end{align*} $$ where $κ_Σ$ is the Gauss curvature and $χ(Σ)$ is the Euler characteristic of the surface.

Proof

Proof sketch:

  1. Every Riemannian $Σ$ admits a decomposition into geodesic polygons.

  2. Then local Gauss-Bonnet implies global Gauss-Bonnet:

    Take a geodesic decomposition $\set{P_1, \ldots, P_F}$ of $Σ$, and suppose $P_i$ has $V_i$ internal angles $α_{i1}, \ldots, α_{iV_i}$. Then additivity of integration yields $$ \begin{align*} \int_{Σ} κ_Σ \d A ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{F} \int_{P_i} κ_Σ \d A \\ ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{F} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{V_i} α_{ij} - (V_i - 2)π \right) \\ ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{F} \sum_{j=1}^{V_i} α_{ij} - π\sum_{i=1}^{F} V_i + 2π \sum_{i=1}^{F} 1 \\ ={}& 2πV - 2πE + 2πF = 2π\,χ(Σ). \end{align*} $$

    The first term has a contribution $2π$ from every vertex. In the second term, each face contributes its number of edges to the sum, which double counts every edge.

Applications:

  1. Area of a spherical triangle ($κ\equiv 1$) is (sheet 2) $$ \begin{align*} \text{area}_{S^2}(T) = α_1 + α_2 + α_3 - π. \end{align*} $$

  2. Area of a hyperbolic triangle ($κ\equiv -1$) is $$ \begin{align*} \text{area}_{\mathbb{H}}(T) = π - α_1 - α_2 - α_3. \end{align*} $$

  3. Area of the compact hyperbolic surface $Σ_g$ with genus $g\geq 2$ is $$ \begin{align*} \text{area}(Σ_g) = -2π \,χ(Σ_g) = (4g-4)π. \end{align*} $$

    We can also see this from our constructions: using regular $4g$-gon $P$ with internal angles $2π/4g$, $\text{area}(Σ_g) = \text{area}_{\text{hyp}}(P) = (4g-2)π - \sum_{i=1}^{4g} 2π/4g = (4π - 4)π$. Using right-angled hexagons, a genus $g$ surface can be cut into $2g-2$ pairs of pants, each made of two right-angled hexagons, which has area $(6-2)π - \sum_{i=1}^{6} π/2 = π$.

  4. A closed geodesic (i.e. $γ(t+T) \equiv γ(t)$ for some $T>0$) on a flat surface $Σ$ cannot bound a disc in $Σ$. (Proof)
