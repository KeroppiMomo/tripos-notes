Definition (arc length of a curve) open_in_new

Let $\v x(t)$ be a curve. For some starting point $t_0$, the arc length $s(t)$ is the (signed) distance from $t_0$ to $t$, $$ s(t) = \int_{t_0}^t |\dv x(t')|\,dt'. $$

The arc length between two points on a curve is independent of the choice of parametrisation. (Proof)

If $\v x$ is regular, then $s'(t) = |\dot{\v x}(t)| > 0$, so $s$ is invertible by the inverse function theorem. Using $s$ to parametrise the curve i.e. $\v x(s)$, the tangent vector has unit norm: $$ \bigg|\dvd xs\bigg| = \frac{|d\v x/dt|}{ds/dt} = 1. $$

Definition (first fundamental form) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $p\in Σ$.

The first fundamental form $\I_p$ of $Σ$ at $p$ is the restriction of the Euclidean inner product $\langle ,\rangle$ in $\R^3$ on the tangent space $T_pΣ$, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \I_p: T_pΣ \times T_pΣ &\to \R \\ (v,w) &\mapsto \langle v,w\rangle. \end{align*} $$

Given an allowable parametrisation $σ: V\to U$ on $Σ$ near $p$, $\set{σ_u, σ_v}$ is a basis of $T_pΣ = \im \d σ_{σ\inv(p)}$. In this basis, $\I_p$ is represented by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} := \pmat{\langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle & \langle σ_u, σ_v\rangle \\ \langle σ_v, σ_u\rangle & \langle σ_v, σ_v\rangle} = (\d σ)^T (\d σ), \end{align*} $$ which is a function in $(u,v)\in V$. Formally we also write $$ \begin{align*} \I_p = E \d u^2 + 2F \d u \d v + G \d v^2. \end{align*} $$

A parametrisation is orthogonal if $F = 0$.

A parametrisation is isothermal if $F = 0$ and $E = G = λ^2$ for some function $λ$.

Lemma (length of curve on surface) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation on a smooth surface $Σ$ in $\R^3$, and $γ(t) = σ(u(t), v(t)): [a,b] \to U$ be a smooth curve. Then $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}(γ) &= \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{Eu'(t)^2 + 2F u'(t) v'(t) + G v'(t)^2} \d t \\ &= \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{\I((u'(t), v'(t)), (u'(t), v'(t)))} \d t \end{align*} $$ where $\I(x,y) = x^T \pmat{E & F\\ F & G} y$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

Definition (isometry, local isometry of surfaces in IB Geometry) open_in_new

Let $Σ, Σ'$ be smooth surfaces in $\R^3$.

We say $Σ$ and $Σ'$ are isometric if there is a diffeomorphism $f: Σ\to Σ'$ such that for every smooth curve $γ: [a,b]\to Σ$, $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}_Σ(γ) = \mathrm{length}_{Σ'}(f\circ γ). \end{align*} $$

We say $Σ$ and $Σ'$ are locally isometric near $p\in Σ$ and $p'\in Σ'$ if there are open neighbourhoods $U\subseteq Σ$ of $p$ and $U'\subseteq Σ'$ of $p'$ such that $U$ and $U'$ are isometric.

Lemma (locally isometric iff first fundamental form agrees) open_in_new

Let $Σ, Σ'$ be smooth surface in $\R^3$.

$Σ$ and $Σ'$ are locally isometric near $p\in Σ$ and $p'\in Σ'$ iff there are allowable parametrisations $σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ with $σ(0)=p$ and $σ': V\to U'\subseteq Σ$ with $σ'(0)=p'$ such that the first fundamental forms $\I_Σ = \I_{Σ'}$ as functions on $V$.

Proof

Lemma (first fundamental form in different parametrisations) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$, with two allowable parametrisations $\tilde σ: V\to U\subseteq Σ$ and $σ: \tilde V\to U\subseteq Σ$ near $p\in Σ$.

Let $ϕ = \tilde σ\inv\circ σ: V\to\tilde V$ be the transition map. The first fundamental forms in the two parametrisations are related by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{E & F \\ F & G} = (Dϕ)^T \pmat{\tilde E & \tilde F \\ \tilde F & \tilde G} (Dϕ). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Lemma (angle on tangent plane) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation at $σ(0) = p\in U\subseteq Σ$.

The angle $θ$ between $v = Dσ|_0(v_0)$ and $w=Dσ|_0(w_0)$ on the tangent plane is $$ \begin{align*} \cos θ = \frac{\I(v_0, w_0)}{\sqrt{\I(v_0, v_0)} \sqrt{\I(w_0, w_0)}} \end{align*} $$ where $\I(x,y) = x^T \pmat{E&F \\ F& G} y$ is the first fundamental form.

Proof

Lemma (conformal iff first fundamental form is isotropic) open_in_new

Let $Σ$ be a smooth surface in $\R^3$ and $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation at $σ(0) = p\in U$.

$E(0)=G(0)$ and $F(0)=0$ in the first fundamental form iff $σ$ is conformal at $0$, i.e. if smooth curves $γ$ and $\tilde γ$ on $V$ pass through $0$ at an angle $θ$, then $σ\circ γ$ and $σ\circ \tilde γ$ on $U$ pass through $p$ at angle $θ$ as well.

Proof

Definition (area on surface) open_in_new

Let $σ: V\to U$ be an allowable parametrisation for an open set $U$ in a smooth surface in $\R^3$.

Define the area of $U$ as $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{area}(U) = \int_{V} \sqrt{EG - F^2} \d u \d v = \int_{V} \sqrt{\det \I} \d u \d v \end{align*} $$ where $\I = \pmat{E & F \\ F & G}$ is the first fundamental form.

If $U = U_1 \sqcup \ldots \sqcup U_n \sqcup Y$ such that $U_1, \ldots, U_n$ are images of a parametrisation and $Y$ has measure 0, then $\op{area}(U) := \sum_{i=1}^{n} \op{area}(U_i)$.

Motivation: viewing $σ_u$ and $σ_v$ as an infinitesimal parallelogram on $Σ$, we integrate over the area $\sqrt{\langle σ_u\times σ_v, σ_u\times σ_v\rangle} = \sqrt{\langle σ_u, σ_u\rangle\langle σ_v, σ_v\rangle - \langle σ_u, σ_v\rangle^2}$.

$\mathrm{area}(U)$ does not depend on the parametrisation. (Proof)
