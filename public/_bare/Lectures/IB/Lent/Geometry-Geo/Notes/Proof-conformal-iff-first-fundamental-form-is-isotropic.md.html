For two curves $γ(t) = (u(t), v(t))$ and $\tilde γ(t) = (\tilde u(t), \tilde v(t))$ in $V$ with $γ(0)=0=\tilde γ(0)$, if $γ'(0)$ and $\tilde γ'(0)$ are non-zero, then they meet at an angle $θ$ where $$ \begin{align*} \cos θ = \frac{u'\tilde u' + v'\tilde v'}{\sqrt{u'^2 + v'^2} \sqrt{\tilde u'^2 + \tilde v'^2}}\Bigg|_0. \end{align*} $$ From before, $σ\circ γ$ and $σ\circ \tilde γ$ meet at an angle $θ$ iff $$ \begin{align*} \frac{u'\tilde u' + v'\tilde v'}{\sqrt{u'^2 + v'^2} \sqrt{\tilde u'^2 + \tilde v'^2}}\Bigg|_0 = \cos θ = \frac{Eu'\tilde u' + F(u'\tilde v' + v'\tilde u') + Gv'\tilde v'}{\sqrt{Eu'^2 + 2Fu'v' + Gv'^2} \sqrt{E\tilde u'^2 + 2F\tilde u' \tilde v' + G\tilde v'^2}}\Bigg|_0. \tag{*} \end{align*} $$

FFF isotropic $\implies$ conformal

When $E(0)=G(0)$ and $F(0)=0$, $(*)$ is satisfied.

Conformal $\implies$ FFF isotropic

Take $γ(t) = (t, 0)$ and $\tilde γ(t) = (0, t)$ to get $θ=π/2$ and so $F(0) = 0$.

Similarly take $γ(t) = (t,t)$ and $\tilde γ(t) = (t,-t)$ to see $E(0) = G(0)$.
