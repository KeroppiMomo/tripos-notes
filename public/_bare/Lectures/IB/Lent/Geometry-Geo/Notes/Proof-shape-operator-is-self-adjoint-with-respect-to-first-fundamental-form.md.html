We want to show $$ \begin{align*} \I_p(\mathbb{S}_p w_1, w_2) = \I_p(w_1, \mathbb{S}_p w_2) \qquad \forall w_1,w_2\in T_pS \end{align*} $$ so working in a parametrisation $σ$, it suffices to show the statement for $w_1, w_2\in \set{σ_u, σ_v}$. This follows from the symmetry of $\I_p$ if $w_1 = w_2$.

For $w_1 = σ_u, w_2 = σ_v$, we have $\mathbb{S}_p w_1 = -\d N_p σ_u = -N_u$ and $\mathbb{S}_p w_2 = -\d N_pσ_v = -N_v$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \I_p(\mathbb{S}_p w_1, w_2) ={}& \langle -N_u, ϕ_v\rangle \\ ={}& \pdd{u} \underbrace{\langle -N, ϕ_v\rangle}_{0} - \langle N, ϕ_{uv}\rangle \\ ={}& \pdd{v} \underbrace{\langle -N, ϕ_u\rangle}_{0} - \langle N, ϕ_{uv}\rangle \\ ={}& \langle -N_v, ϕ_u\rangle = \I_p(w_1, \mathbb{S}_p w_2). \end{align*} $$

Alternatively: Use the symmetry of the first and second fundamental forms: $$ \begin{align*} \I_p(\mathbb{S}_p v, w) ={}& \II_p(v,w) \\ ={}& \II_p(w, v) \\ ={}& \I_p(\mathbb{S}_p w, v) \\ ={}& \I_p(v, \mathbb{S}_p w). \end{align*} $$
