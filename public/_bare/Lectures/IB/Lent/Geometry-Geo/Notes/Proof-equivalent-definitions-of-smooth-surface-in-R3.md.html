Locally a graph $\implies$ smooth surface

At each point $p\in Σ$, suppose WLOG $Σ$ is locally a graph $\sb{(x,y,g(x,y))}{(x,y)\in U}$ on the $(x,y)$-plane. Then $(U, π_{xy})$ is a chart where $π_{xy}(x,y,z) = (x,y)$ is smooth with a smooth inverse $(x,y)\mapsto (x,y,g(x,y))$.

Locally a graph $\implies$ locally a level set

$Σ$ locally is $f\inv(0)$ where $f$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} f(x,y,z) = z - g(x,y). \end{align*} $$ Moreover $f$ has non-zero derivative as $\pdfd{f}{z} = 1 \neq 0$.

Locally a grpah $\implies$ locally image of allowable parametrisation

We have a parametrisation $$ \begin{align*} σ(x,y) = (x,y,g(x,y)) \end{align*} $$ which is allowable because $$ \begin{align*} Dσ = \pmat{1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ g_x & g_y} \end{align*} $$ has rank 2.

Locally a level set $\implies$ locally a graph

Assuming $Df|_{(x_0, y_0, z_0)} \neq 0$, permuting coordinates if necessary, WLOG we have $\partial f/\partial z|_{(x_0, y_0, z_0)} \neq 0$.

By the implicit function theorem, there exists an open neighbourhood $V \subseteq \R^2$ of $(x_0, y_0)$ and a smooth function $g: V\to \R$ such that $g(x_0, y_0) = z_0$, and moreover in an open neighbourhood of $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$, $$ \begin{align*} (x,y,z) \in Σ \iff f(x,y,z) = 0 \iff z = g(x,y). \end{align*} $$

Smooth surface in $\R^3$ $\implies$ locally image of allowable parametrisation

For a chart $(U, ϕ)$ at $p\in Σ$ with $ϕ: U\to V\subseteq \R^2$, the inverse $σ=ϕ\inv: V \to U$ is an allowable parametrisation, because $$ \begin{align*} \id_{V} = σ \circ ϕ \implies I_2 = Dσ|_{ϕ(u,v)}\circ Dϕ|_{(u,v)} \end{align*} $$ so $Dϕ$ has rank 2.

Locally image of allowable parametrisation $\implies$ locally a graph

Let $p\in Σ$, $V\subseteq \R^2$ be open and $σ: V\to Σ$ be an allowable parametrisation, with $σ(0) = p$.

Writing $σ(u,v) = (σ_1(u,v), σ_2(u,v), σ_3(u,v))$ for $(u,v)\in V$, its derivative is $$ \begin{align*} Dσ = \pmat{\pdfd{σ_1}{u} & \pdfd{σ_1}{v} \\ \pdfd{σ_2}{u} & \pdfd{σ_2}{v} \\ \pdfd{σ_3}{u} & \pdfd{σ_3}{v}}, \end{align*} $$ which has rank 2 at $(u,v)=0$. So WLOG suppose $Dσ_1|_0$ and $Dσ_2|_0$ are linearly independent.

Let $π_{xy}(x,y,z) = (x,y)$ and consider $ϕ = π_{xy} \circ σ: V \to \R^2$. The derivative $$ \begin{align*} Dϕ|_0 = Dπ_{xy}|_{p} \circ Dσ|_0 = \pmat{1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0} \pmat{\pdfd{σ_1}{u} & \pdfd{σ_1}{v} \\ \pdfd{σ_2}{u} & \pdfd{σ_2}{v} \\ \pdfd{σ_3}{u} & \pdfd{σ_3}{v}} = \pmat{\pdfd{σ_1}{u} & \pdfd{σ_1}{v} \\ \pdfd{σ_2}{u} & \pdfd{σ_2}{v}} \end{align*} $$ which is invertible. By the inverse function theorem, $ϕ$ is locally invertible, i.e. $Σ$ is locally a graph.
