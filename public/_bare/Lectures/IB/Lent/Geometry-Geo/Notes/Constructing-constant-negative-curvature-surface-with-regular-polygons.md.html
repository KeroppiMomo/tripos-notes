Lemma (hyperbolic regular 4g-gon with angle summing up to 2pi) open_in_new

For $g\geq 2$, there is a hyperbolic regular $4g$-gon with internal angles $2π/4g = π/2g$.

Proof

A $4g$-gon with side labels $a_1 b_1 a_1\inv b_1\inv \ldots a_g b_g a_g\inv b_g\inv$ glues to give $Σ_g$.

In hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}$, say a flag comprises

  1. an oriented hyperbolic line;

  2. a point on that line;

  3. a choice of side to the line.

Given two flags, there exists a hyperbolic isometry taking one to another: use a Möbius map to match the point and the oriented line, and compose with an inverse if necessary to match the distinguished side.

Take a hyperbolic regular $n$-gon $G$ with internal angles $2π/4g$. Impose the edge identification: for any two edges $e,e'$ paired, there exists a hyperbolic isometry taking $e$ to $e'$ respecting the orientation of the line, and taking the inside of the polygon relative to $e$ to the outside relative to $e'$. (Illustration)

Now we give an atlas for $Σ_g = G/$side identification:

  1. If $p\in G$ lies in $\mathrm{int}(G)$, pick a small disc $B\subseteq \mathrm{int}(G)$. Then $q(B) \hookrightarrow D$ is an injection (where $q: G \to Σ_g$ is the quotient map).

  2. If $p\in G$ lies on an edge $e$ (excluding vertex), it has an identified $\tilde p$ on the paired edge $\tilde e$, and there exists an isometry $γ$ sending $e$ to $\tilde e$. Let $U = B(p,δ)\cap G$ and $\tilde U = B(p,δ)\setminus G$. Then the isometry $γ: U \hookrightarrow D$ and the inclusion $i: \tilde U \hookrightarrow D$ glue to give an injection $U\cup \tilde U\hookrightarrow D$, which descends to a map on the neighbourhood of $q(p) \in Σ_g$ into $D$.

  3. All vertices are identified. For a vertex $v\in G$, apply a isometry putting $v$ at $0$, and then rotate. The angles of all $4g$ sectors add up to $2π$, so they cover a neighbourhood of $0\in D$. Build a chart at $q(v)$ from this.

Then all transition maps are hyperbolic isometries, which are smooth and preserves the metric. Therefore $Σ_g$ inherits a metric everywhere locally isometric to $(D,g_\text{hyp})$.
