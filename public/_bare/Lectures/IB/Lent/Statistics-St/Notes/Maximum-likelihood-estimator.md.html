Definition (maximum likelihood estimator) open_in_new

The likelihood of $θ$ at the observed data $X$ is $L_X: θ \mapsto p(X; θ)$, viewed as a function of $θ$.

The maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) of $θ$ is the value of $θ$ that maximises $L_X$, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \hat θ_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_{θ\in Θ} L_X(θ), \end{align*} $$ which is an estimator for $θ$ (a function of $X$).

Properties:

  • $\hat θ_{\text{MLE}}$ is a function of any sufficient statistic. (Derivation)

  • Under a bijection $h$, the MLE for $η = h(θ)$ is $\hat η_{\text{MLE}} = h(\hat θ_{\text{MLE}})$.

  • Under certain conditions, the MLE is asymptotically normal: $\sqrt{n}(\hat θ_{\text{MLE}} - θ) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, I\inv)$ where $I$ is the Fisher information matrix.

Likelihood principle

All information about $θ$ obtained from an experiment with data $X$ is contained in the likelihood function of $θ$ given $X$.

This is stronger than the sufficiency principle.
