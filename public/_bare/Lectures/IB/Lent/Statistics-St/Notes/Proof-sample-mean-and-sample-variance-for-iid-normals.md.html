This immediately follows from applying the more general result for normal linear models to $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{Y_1 \\ \vdots \\ Y_n} = \pmat{1 \\ \vdots \\ 1}μ + ε. \end{align*} $$

Define some $n\times n$ orthogonal matrix with the first row $1/\sqrt{n}$ $$ \begin{align*} U = \pmat{1/\sqrt{n} & \ldots & 1/\sqrt{n} \\ * & \ldots & * \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ * & \ldots & *} \in M_{n\times n}(\R) \end{align*} $$

The actual matrix is not important, but one such matrix is $$ \begin{align*} U = \pmat{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} & \ldots & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 & 0 & \ldots & 0 \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{3\times 2}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{3\times 2}} & -\frac{2}{\sqrt{3\times 2}} & 0 & \ldots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}} & \ldots & -\frac{(n-1)}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}}. \end{align*} $$

Let $X = (X_1, \ldots, X_n) \sim N(μ \v 1_n, σ^2I_n)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} Y := UX = \pmat{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i} X_i \\ *} = \pmat{\sqrt{n}\, \overline{X} \\ *}. \end{align*} $$ The mean and variance of $Y$ are $$ \begin{align*} \expect(Y) ={}& U\cdot μ 1_n = \pmat{\sqrt{n}\, μ \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\0}, \\ \var(Y) ={}& U^T \cdot σ^2I_n\cdot U = σ^2I_n \end{align*} $$ by properties of the orthogonal matrix $U$. Since linear transform of a multivariate normal is still a multivariate normal, $$ \begin{align*} Y \sim N\left(\pmat{\sqrt{n}\, μ \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0}, σ^2 I_n\right). \end{align*} $$

Note $Y_1 = \sqrt{n}\, \overline{X}$, and $Y^T Y = X^T U^T U X = X^T X$, so $$ \begin{align*} Y_2^2 + \ldots + Y_n^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 - n\,\overline{X}^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (X_i - \overline{X})^2 = S_{XX}. \end{align*} $$ Since $Y_1, Y_2, \ldots, Y_n$ have no correlation, they are independent. Moreover $Y_1 = \sqrt{n}\overline{X} \sim N(\sqrt{n}μ, σ^2)$ and $Y_2, \ldots, Y_n \sim N(0, σ^2)$ iid, and hence $\overline{X} \sim N(μ, σ^2/n)$ and $S_{XX}/σ^2 = (Y_2/σ)^2 + \ldots + (Y_n/σ)^2$ is a sum of $n-1$ standard normals so follows $χ_{n-1}^2$.
