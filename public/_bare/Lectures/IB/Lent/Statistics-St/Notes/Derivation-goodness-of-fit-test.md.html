Likelihood function $$ \begin{align*} L_N(p) \propto \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i^{N_i} \end{align*} $$ (where $\propto$ hides factors constant to $p$). Log-likelihood function $$ \begin{align*} l_N(p) = \log L_N(p) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} N_i \log p_i + \text{const.}. \end{align*} $$ Compute $$ \begin{align*} 2\log Λ = 2\left(\log \sup_{p_i\geq 0, \sum_{i} p_i=1} L_N(p) - \log \sup_{θ} L_N(p_0(θ))\right) = 2(l(\hat p^{(1)}_{\text{MLE}}) - l(p_0(\hat θ_{\text{MLE}}))). \end{align*} $$

To find the maximum likelihood estimator for $p$ under $H_1$, we maximise $l_N(p)$ subject to $p_i\geq 0$ and $\sum_{i} p_i=1$ using Lagrange multiplier: $$ \begin{align*} &0 = \pdd{p_i}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k} N_i \log p_i + λ\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k} p_i - 1\Bigr)\right) = \frac{N_i}{p_i} - λ \\ \implies & N_i = λp_i. \end{align*} $$ Applying the constraint $\sum_{i} p_i = 1$, we solve $λ = \sum_{i=1}^{k} N_i = n$ so $$ \begin{align*} p_i = \frac{N_i}{n}. \end{align*} $$

Thus substituting $$ \begin{align*} 2\log Λ = 2 \sum_{i=1}^{k} N_i \log \frac{N_i/n}{p_{0i}(\hat θ_\text{MLE})} = 2\sum_{i=1}^{k} N_i \log \frac{N_i}{np_{0i}(\hat θ_{\text{MLE}})}. \end{align*} $$

Apply Pearson's approximation, $$ \begin{align*} 2\log Λ \approx \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{(N_i - np_{0i}(\hat θ_{\text{MLE}}))^2}{N_i}. \end{align*} $$

$H_1$ has $k$ variables ($p_1, \ldots, p_k$) and $1$ constraint ($\sum_{i=1}^{k} p_i = 1$) so $k-1$ degrees of freedom. $H_0$ has no free variables so $0$ degrees of freedom. Thus by Wilks' theorem, $2\log Λ\xrightarrow{d} χ_{k-1}^2$.
