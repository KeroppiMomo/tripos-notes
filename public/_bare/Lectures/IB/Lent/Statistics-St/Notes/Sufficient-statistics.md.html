Sufficiency principle

If $T(X)$ is a sufficient statistic and in two experiments with data $x$ and $y$ we have $T(x) = T(y)$, then the "evidence" about $θ$ given the two experiments is the same.

Definition (sufficient statistic) open_in_new

A statistic $T = T(X)$ is said to be sufficient for $θ$ if the conditional distribution of $X$ given $T(X)$ does not depend on $θ$.

Theorem (Fisher-Neyman factorisation theorem) open_in_new

Suppose $X$ follows a distribution $p(x; θ)$ depending on an unknown parameter $θ$.

A statistic $T$ is sufficient for $θ$ iff $$ \begin{align*} p(x; θ) = g(T(X), θ)\, h(x) \end{align*} $$ for some functions $g$ and $h$.

Proof

Definition (minimal sufficient statistic) open_in_new

A sufficient statistic $T(X)$ is minimal sufficient if it is a function of every other statistic, i.e. if $S(X)$ is a sufficient statistic, then $S(x) = S(y) \implies T(x) = T(y)$ for all $x,y\in \mathcal{X}$.

  • Minimal sufficient statistics are unique up to a bijection.

Theorem (equal iff probability ratio independent of parameter implies minimal sufficient) open_in_new

Suppose $T$ is a statistic, satisfying that $T(x) = T(y)$ iff $p(x; θ)/p(y; θ)$ does not depend on $θ$. Then $T$ is minimal sufficient.

Proof

  • If the range of $X$ depends on $θ$, the condition means $p(x;θ) = c(x,y)p(y;θ)$ for some $c(x,y)$.

Theorem (Rao-Blackwell) open_in_new

Suppose $T$ is a sufficient statistic.

For any estimator $\tilde θ$ of $θ$, let $\hat θ = \expect_θ(\tilde θ\mid T)$ be another estimator. Then $$ \begin{align*} \op{MSE}(\hat θ) \leq \op{MSE}(\tilde θ) \end{align*} $$ with equality iff $\tilde θ$ is a function of $T$.

Proof

  • This reinforces the sufficiency principle: we should only consider estimators that are functions of sufficient statistics, since otherwise we can always make a better one.
