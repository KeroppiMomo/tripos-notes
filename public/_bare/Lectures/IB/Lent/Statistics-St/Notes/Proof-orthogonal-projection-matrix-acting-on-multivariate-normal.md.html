Distribution of $Pε$ and $(I-P)ε$

The random variable $$ \begin{align*} \tilde ε = \pmat{Pε \\ (I-P)ε} = \pmat{P \\ I-P} ε \end{align*} $$ is a multivariate normal with mean $$ \begin{align*} \expect(\tilde ε) = \pmat{P\\I-P} \expect(ε) = \v 0 \end{align*} $$ and variance $$ \begin{align*} \var(\tilde ε) ={}& \pmat{P \\ I-P} \var(ε) \pmat{P\\I-P}^T \\ ={}& σ^2 \pmat{P \\ I-P} \pmat{P^T & I-P^T} \\ ={}& σ^2 \pmat{P^2 & P(I-P) \\ (I-P)P & (I-P)^2} \\ ={}& σ^2 \pmat{P & 0 \\ 0 & I-P} \end{align*} $$ since $P^T = P = P^2$. Therefore $Pε \sim N(0, σ^2 P)$ and $(I-P)ε\sim N(0, σ^2(I-P))$ are independent.

Distribution of $\|Pε\|^2$

Write $P = UU^T$ where $U\in \R^{n\times \mathrm{rank}(P)}$ has orthonormal columns. Then $$ \begin{align*} \|Pε\|^2 = ε^T P^T P ε = ε^T U\underbrace{U^T U}_{I_{\mathrm{rank}(P)}}U^T ε = \|U^T ε\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rank}(P)} (U^T ε)_i^2. \end{align*} $$ Since $U^T ε \sim N(0, U^T σ^2I_nU) = N(0, σ^2 I_{\mathrm{rank}(P)})$, each $(U^Tε)_i$ are independent $N(0,σ^2)$. Therefore $\|Pε\|^2/σ^2$ is a sum of $\mathrm{rank}(P)$ independent $N(0,1)$ random variables, so $\|Pε\|^2/σ^2 \sim χ_{\mathrm{rank}(P)}^2$.
