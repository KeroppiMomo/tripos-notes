We try to find a linear relationship between $X\in \R^{n\times p}$ the design matrix (independent variables) and $Y\in \R^{n}$ the response (dependent variables). Each row is a single observation.

Definition (normal linear model) open_in_new

The (homoscedastic) normal linear model assumes $$ \begin{align*} Y\mid X \sim N(X β, σ^2 I_n) \end{align*} $$ where $β\in \R^p$ and $σ^2>0$ are unknown parameters.

This can alternatively be expressed as $$ \begin{align*} Y = X β + ε \end{align*} $$ where $ε\sim N(0_n, σ^2 I_n)$.

Definition (linear condition expectation model) open_in_new

The (homoscedastic) linear conditional expectation model assumes $$ \begin{align*} \expect(Y\mid X) = X β, \qquad \var(Y\mid X) = σ^2 I_n \end{align*} $$ where $β\in \R^p$ and $σ^2>0$ are unknown parameters.

Least square estimator open_in_new

The least square estimator for $β$ is $$ \begin{align*} \hat β = \arg\min_{β\in \R^p} \|Y - Xβ\|. \end{align*} $$ $\hat β$ solves the normal equation $$ \begin{align*} X^T(Y - X\hat β) = 0. \end{align*} $$

If $X$ has full rank, $X^T X$ is invertible, so $$ \begin{align*} \hat β = (X^T X)\inv X^T Y. \end{align*} $$

Let $μ = Xβ$. Then the least squares problem is equivalent to $$ \begin{align*} \hat μ = \arg\min_{μ\in \mathrm{Col}(X)} \|Y - μ\| \end{align*} $$ where $\mathrm{Col}(X)$ is the column space of $X$.

  • The fitted value $\hat μ$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} \hat μ = X\hat β = PY \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} P = X(X^T X)\inv X^T \end{align*} $$ is the orthogonal projection matrix into $\mathrm{Col}(X)$.

  • The vector of residues is given by $$ \begin{align*} R = Y - \hat μ = (I - P)Y. \end{align*} $$

  • The residual sum of squares is $$ \begin{align*} \text{RSS} = \|R\|^2. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (MLE in normal linear model) open_in_new

In the normal linear model, the maximum likelihood estimators for $β$ and $σ^2$ are $$ \begin{align*} \hat β_{\text{MLE}} ={}& \arg\min_{β\in \R^p} \|Y - Xβ\|, \\ \hat σ^2_{\text{MLE}} ={}& \frac{\text{RSS}}{n} =\frac{1}{n}\|Y - X\hat β_{\text{MLE}}\|^2 = \frac{1}{n}\min_{β\in \R^p} \|Y-Xβ\|^2. \end{align*} $$ The resulting maximum likelihood is $$ \begin{align*} L_{Y\mid X}(\hat β_{\text{MLE}}, \hat σ^2_{\text{MLE}}) = \left(\frac{2πe}{n}\mathrm{RSS}\right)^{-n/2}. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (Gauss-Markov) open_in_new

Assume $X$ has full rank.

In the linear conditional expectation model, the least square estimator $\hat β$ is the best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE), i.e. $\hat β$ is linear in $Y$, unbiased, and for any other linear unbiased estimator $\tilde β$, we have $\var \hat β$ $\preceq$ $\var \tilde β$ (which means $\var(a^T \hat β) \leq \var(a^T \tilde β)$).

Definition (orthogonal projection matrix) open_in_new

A square matrix $P \in \R^{n\times n}$ is an orthogonal projection matrix if it is symmetric ($P^T = P$) and idempotent ($P^2=P$).

  • See orthogonal projection.

  • $P$ is a orthogonal projection matrix iff $P=UU^T$ where the columns of $U\in \R^{n\times \mathrm{rank}(P)}$ form an orthonormal basis for the column space of $P$. (Proof)

  • Eigenvalues of $P$ are either 0 or 1.

  • $\mathrm{rank}(P) = \tr(P)$.

  • $I-P$ is also an orthogonal projection matrix.

  • $\|Y\|^2 = \|PY\|^2 + \|(I - P)Y\|^2$ for any $Y\in \R^n$.

Lemma (orthogonal projection matrix acting on multivariate normal) open_in_new

Suppose $ε\sim N(0, σ^2 I_n)$ and $P$ is an $n\times n$ orthogonal projection matrix. Then

  1. $Pε \sim N(0, σ^2 P)$ and $(I_n-P)ε\sim N(0,σ^2(I_n-P))$ are independent; and

  2. $\|Pε\|^2/σ^2 \sim χ^2_{\mathrm{rank}(P)}$.

Proof

Theorem (distribution of MLE in normal linear model) open_in_new

In a normal linear model, assume $X$ is fixed and has full rank. Then maximum likelihood estimators

  1. $\hat β_{\text{MLE}}$ and $\hat σ^2_{\text{MLE}}$ are independent;

  2. $\hat β_{\text{MLE}} \sim N(β, σ^2(X^TX)\inv)$; and

  3. $n\,\hat σ^2_{\text{MLE}}/σ^2 \sim χ_{n-p}^2$.

Proof

  • Since $\hat σ_{\text{MLE}}^2$ is biased ($\expect(\hat σ^2_{\text{MLE}}) = \frac{n-p}{n}σ^2$), it is more common to use the unbiased estimator $$ \begin{align*} \hat σ^2 = \frac{n}{n-p}\hat σ_{\text{MLE}}^2 = \frac{1}{n-p} \|Y - X\hat β_{\text{MLE}}\|^2. \end{align*} $$

F-test open_in_new

F-distribution open_in_new

The $F$-distribution with $d_1$ and $d_2$ degrees of freedom, denoted as $F_{d_1, d_2}$, is defined to be the distribution of $$ \begin{align*} R = \frac{S_1/d_1}{S_2/d_2} \end{align*} $$ where $S_1 \sim χ^2_{d_1}$ and $S_2\sim χ_{d_2}^2$ are independent.

  • If $T\sim$ $t_{d}$, then $T^2 \sim F(1, d)$.

t-test for one parameter in linear model open_in_new

The t-test for testing $H_0: β_j = β_{0j}$ against $H_1: β_j \neq β_{0j}$ (for some particular $j$) is to reject $H_0$ when $$ \begin{align*} \frac{|\hat β_{j,\text{MLE}} - β_{0j}|}{\sqrt{\hat σ^2{(X^T X)\inv}_{jj}}} > t_{n-p}(α/2) \end{align*} $$ where $\hat σ^2 = \frac{1}{n-p}\|Y - X\hat β_{\text{MLE}}\|^2$ is the unbiased estimator for $σ^2$. (Derivation)

This gives a $(1-α)$-confidence interval for $β_j$: $$ \begin{align*} \hat β_{j,\text{MLE}} \pm t_{n-p}(α/2) \sqrt{\hat σ^2 {(X^T X)\inv}_{jj}}. \end{align*} $$

We can rephrase this as nested models: $$ \begin{align*} Y - β_{0j} X_j = \pmat{X_1 & \ldots & X_j & \ldots & X_p} \pmat{β_1 \\ \vdots \\ β_{j} - β_{0j} \\ \vdots \\ β_{p}} + ε. \end{align*} $$ This is equivalent to the F-test for nested models. (Derivation: TODO)

F-test for all parameters in linear model open_in_new

The F-test for testing $H_0: β = β_0$ against $H_1: β\neq β_0$ is to reject $H_0$ when $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\|X(\hat β_{\text{MLE}} - β_0)\|^2/p}{\hat σ^2} > F_{p,n-p}(α) \end{align*} $$ where $\hat σ^2 = \frac{1}{n-p}\|Y - X\hat β_{\text{MLE}}\|^2$ is the unbiased estimator for $σ^2$.

This gives a $(1-α)$-confidence set for $β$ $$ \begin{align*} \sb{β\in \R^n}{\frac{\|X(\hat β_{\text{MLE}} - β)^2\|}{p\hat σ^2} \leq F_{p,n-p}(α)}, \end{align*} $$ which is an ellipsoid.

This is the F-test for nested models.

Analysis of variance open_in_new

Model $$ \begin{align*} Y_{ij} = μ_i + ε_{ij} \end{align*} $$ where $j=1,\ldots,n_i$, $i=1,\ldots,k$ and the noise follows $ε_{ij} \sim N(0,σ^2)$. Want to test $H_0: μ_1 = \ldots = μ_k$ against $H_1: μ_1,\ldots,μ_k\in \R$ unrestricted, excluding $H_0$.

This is a nested model: $$ \begin{align*} Y = \underbrace{\pmat{\v 1_{n_1} \\ \v 1_{n_2} & \v 1_{n_2} \\ \vdots & & \ddots \\ \v 1_{n_k} & && \v 1_{n_k}}}_{X^{\text{full}}}\,\underbrace{\pmat{μ \\ μ_2-μ \\ \vdots \\ μ_k - μ}}_{β} + ε \end{align*} $$ with $$ \begin{align*} \hat μ^{\text{saturated}} = Y, \qquad \hat μ^{\text{full}} = \pmat{\overline{Y_{1+}} \v 1_{n_1} \\ \vdots \\ \overline{Y_{k+}} \v 1_{n_k}}, \qquad \hat μ^{\text{sub}} = \overline{Y_{++}} \v 1_{n}. \end{align*} $$ where $\overline{Y_{i+}} = \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} Y_{ij}$ and $\overline{Y_{++}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} Y_{ij}$.
