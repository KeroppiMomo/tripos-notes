Consider the implicit Runge-Kutta method $$ \begin{align*} k_1 ={}& f(t_n, y_n + \tfrac{1}{4}h(k_1-k_2)), \\ k_2 ={}& f(t_n + \tfrac{2}{3}h, y_n + \tfrac{1}{12}h(3k_1 + 5k_2)) \\ y_{n+1} ={}& y_n + \tfrac{1}{4}h(k_1 + 3k_2). \end{align*} $$

Applying it to $y'=λy$, $$ \begin{align*} hk_1 ={}& hλ(y_n + \tfrac{1}{4}hk_1 - \tfrac{1}{4}hk_2), \\ hk_2 ={}& hλ(y_n + \tfrac{1}{4}hk_1 + \tfrac{5}{12}hk_2). \end{align*} $$ Solving this linear system, $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{hk_1 \\ hk_2} ={}& \pmat{1-\frac{1}{4}z & \frac{1}{4}z \\ -\frac{1}{4}z & 1-\frac{5}{12}z}\inv \pmat{zy_n \\ zy_n} \\ \implies y_{n+1} ={}& y_n + \pmat{\frac{1}{4} & \frac{3}{4}}\pmat{1-\frac{1}{4}z & \frac{1}{4}z \\ -\frac{1}{4}z & 1-\frac{5}{12}z}\inv \pmat{zy_n \\ zy_n} \\ ={}& \underbrace{\frac{1+\frac{1}{3}z}{1 - \frac{2}{3}z + \frac{1}{6}z^2}}_{r(z)} y_n. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{D} = \sb{z\in \C}{|r(z)|<1}. \end{align*} $$

Then it's complex analysis: $r(z)$ has singularities at $2\pm i\sqrt{2}$, which are away from $\C^-$. Therefore $r(z)$ is holomorphic on $\C^-$ and is bounded there. Hence by the maximum modulus principle, it attains its maximum modulus on $\partial\C^- = i\R$. Considering a point $it\in i\R$, $$ \begin{align*} |r(it)| = \left|\frac{1 + \frac{1}{3}it}{1-\frac{1}{6}t^2 - \frac{2}{3}it}\right| \end{align*} $$ and since $$ \begin{align*} |1-\tfrac{1}{6}t^2 - \tfrac{2}{3} it|^2 = 1 + \tfrac{1}{9}t^2 + \tfrac{1}{36}t^4 \geq 1+\tfrac{1}{9}t^2 = |1+\tfrac{1}{3}it|^2, \end{align*} $$ we obtain $|r(it)|\leq 1$.
