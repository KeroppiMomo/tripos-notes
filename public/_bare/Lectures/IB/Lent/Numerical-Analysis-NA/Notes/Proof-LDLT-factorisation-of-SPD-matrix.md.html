$A = LDL^T$ $\implies$ SPD

$A$ is symmetric because $A^T = (L^T)^T D^T L^T = LDL^T$.

$A$ is positive definite because for all $\v x\in \R^n$, let $\v y=L^T \v x$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \v x^T A \v x = \v y^T D \v y = \sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{ii} y_i^2 \geq 0 \end{align*} $$ with equality iff $\v y=0$ iff $\v x=0$.

Positive definite $\implies$ strictly regular

Suppose $A$ is positive definite, and let $A_k$ be its $k$-th principal minor. For any $\v x = (x_1, \ldots, x_k)\in \R^k$ non-zero, let $\v x_\ast = (x_1, \ldots, x_k, 0, \ldots, 0)\in \R^n$, which is non-zero. Then $$ \begin{align*} 0 < \v x_\ast^T A \v x_\ast = \v x^T A_k \v x \end{align*} $$ so $A_k \v x\neq 0$. Hence $A_k$ is non-singular.

SPD $\implies$ $A=LDL^T$

By the above $A$ has a $LDL^T$ factorisation. $D$ has positive entries because taking $\v x = (L^T)\inv \v e_k\neq \v 0$, $$ \begin{align*} 0 < \v x^T A \v x = \v e_k^T D \v e_k = d_{kk} \end{align*} $$ for each $k=1,2,\ldots,n$.
