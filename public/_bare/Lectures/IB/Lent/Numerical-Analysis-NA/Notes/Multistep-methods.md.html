Multistep method

For an initial value problem, an $s$-step method use $\v y_n, \v y_{n+1}, \ldots, \v y_{n+s-1}$ to find $\v y_{n+s}$ by $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m \v y_{n+m} = h\sum_{m=0}^{s} b_m \v f_{n+m} \end{align*} $$ where $\v f_{n+m} = \v f(t_{n+m}, \v y_{n+m})$.

  • We say it is homogeneous if it satisfies the normalisation condition $a_s = 1$.

  • It is explicit if $b_s = 0$, and implicit otherwise.

Define the polynomials $$ \begin{align*} ρ(w) = \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m w^m, \qquad σ(w) = \sum_{m=0}^{s} b_m w^m. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (order of multistep method) open_in_new

The following are equivalent conditions for a multistep method to have order $p\geq 1$:

  1. its coefficients satisfy $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m ={}& 0, \\ \sum_{m=0}^{s} m^k a_m ={}& k \sum_{m=0}^{s} m^{k-1} b_m, \qquad \forall k=1,2,\ldots,p; \end{align*} $$

  2. it is exact on $\mathcal{P}_p$: $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m Q(t_{n+m}) = h\sum_{m=0}^{s} b_m Q'(t_{n+m}) \qquad \forall Q\in \mathcal{P}_p; \end{align*} $$

  3. the associated polynomials satisfy $$ \begin{align*} ρ(e^{z}) - zσ(e^{z}) = O(z^{p+1}), \qquad \text{as } z\to 0 \end{align*} $$ or equivalently (with the substitution $w=e^z$ and noting $\log w = O(|w-1|)$) $$ \begin{align*} \frac{ρ(w)}{\log w} = σ(w) + O(|w-1|^{p}), \qquad \text{as }w\to 1. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (Dahlquist equivalence theorem) open_in_new

A multistep method is convergent iff

  • it is of order $p\geq 1$; and

  • the polynomial $ρ$ satisfies the root condition, i.e. all complex roots of $ρ$ lie in $|z|\leq 1$ and all roots with modulus 1 are simple.

Constructing multistep methods with maximum order open_in_new

To construct an $s$-step method, we can take some $ρ\in \mathcal{P}_s$ such that $ρ(1) = 0$ and $ρ$ satisfies the root condition. Then we can determine $σ$ by expanding $$ \begin{align*} \frac{ρ(w)}{\log w} = σ(w) + O(|w-1|^p). \end{align*} $$

  • For an implicit method, we allow $σ\in \mathcal{P}_s$, so we obtain an order of $p=s+1$.

  • For an explicit method, we allow $σ\in \mathcal{P}_{s-1}$, so we obtain an order of $p=s$.

Adams methods open_in_new

The choice $ρ(w)$ $= w^s - w^{s-1} = w^{s-1}(w-1)$ corresponds to Adams methods.

  • Adams-Bashforth methods are explicit, and has order $p=s$.

  • Adams-Moulton methods are implicit, and has order $p=s+1$.

Proposition (coefficients of Adams methods as integrals of polynomials) open_in_new

The coefficients $b_m$ in an $s$-step Adams method are given by $$ \begin{align*} b_m = \int_{s-1}^{s} l_m(t) \d t \end{align*} $$ where $l_m(t) = ω(t)/(t-m)ω'(m)$ are the Lagrange fundamental polynomials:

  • for an Adams-Moulton (explicit) method, the nodes are $0,1,\ldots,s$, i.e. $ω(t) = \prod_{m=0}^{s} (t-m)$;

  • for an Adams-Bashforth (implicit) method, the nodes are $0,1,\ldots,s-1$, i.e. $ω(t) = \prod_{m=0}^{s-1} (t-m)$.

Backward differentiation formula open_in_new

We can construct an implicit $s$-step, $s$-order method by taking $σ(w)$ $= b_s w^s$ and normalising $b_s = 1$.

Proposition (coefficients of the backward differentiation formula) open_in_new

In the $s$-step backward differentiation formula, the coefficients are given by $$ \begin{align*} ρ(w) ={}& \sum_{k=1}^{s} \frac{1}{k}w^{s-k} (w-1)^k \in \mathcal{P}_s, \\ \end{align*} $$ or equivalently $$ \begin{align*} a_s ={}& \sum_{k=1}^{s} \frac{1}{k}, \\ a_m ={}& \frac{(-1)^{s-m}}{s-m}\pmat{s \\ m}. \end{align*} $$

Higher order multistep methods open_in_new

(Non-examinable)

There are no $s$-step method of order $2s+1$ (derivation), but there exists unique implicit method of order $2s$ and explicit method of order $2s-1$.

However:

Theorem (first Dahlquist barrier) open_in_new

If an $s$-step method is convergent (i.e. the root condition is satisfied), then

  • $p\leq s$ for an explicit method (achieved by Adams-Bashforth);

  • $p\leq s+1$ for an implicit method when $s$ is odd (achieved by Adams-Moulton);

  • $p\leq s+2$ for an implicit method when $s$ is even (achieved by $ρ(w) = w^s - 1$).
