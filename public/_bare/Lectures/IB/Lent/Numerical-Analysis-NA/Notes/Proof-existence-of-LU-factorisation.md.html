LU factorisable $\implies$ strictly regular

Suppose $A = LU$. Consider their $k$-th principal minor $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{A_k & * \\ *&*} = A = LU = \pmat{L_k & * \\ *&*} \pmat{U_k & * \\ *&*}\implies A_k = L_k U_k. \end{align*} $$ $L$ and $U$ are triangular with non-zero diagonal entries so $L_k$ and $U_k$ are non-singular. Hence so is $A_k$.

Strictly regular $\implies$ LU factorisable

Proceed by induction on $n$. The case $n=1$ is clear.

For $n>1$, partition $A\in \R^{n\times n}$ as $$ \begin{align*} A = \pmat{A_{n-1} & \v w \\ \v v^T & a_{nn}}. \end{align*} $$ We want to get $A = LU$ where $$ \begin{align*} L = \pmat{L_{n-1} & \v 0 \\ \v x^T & 1}, \qquad U = \pmat{U_{n-1} & \v y \\ \v 0^T & u_{nn}}. \end{align*} $$ Multiply to see we want $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{A_{n-1} & \v w \\ \v v^T & a_{nn}} = \pmat{L_{n-1} U_{n-1} & L_{n-1} \v y \\ \v x^T U_{n-1} & \v x^T \v y + u_{nn}}. \end{align*} $$

By induction hypothesis, since $A_{n-1}\in \R^{n\times n}$ is strictly regular, we can factorise $A_{n-1} = L_{n-1} U_{n-1}$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \v w = L_{n-1} \v y, \quad \v v = U_{n-1}^T \v x, \quad u_{nn} = a_{nn} - \v x^T \v y \end{align*} $$ are solvable since $L_{n-1}$ and $U_{n-1}$ are non-singular.

Finally we see that $u_{nn}\neq 0$, as $0\neq \det A = \det L \det U = 1\cdot u_{nn}\det U_{n-1}$.
