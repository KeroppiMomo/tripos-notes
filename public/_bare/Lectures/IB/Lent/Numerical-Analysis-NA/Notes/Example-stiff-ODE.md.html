Consider the autonomous system $$ \begin{align*} \v y' = A\v y, \qquad \v y(0) = \v y_0. \end{align*} $$ where $A$ is diagonalisable into $A = WDW\inv$, $D = \mathrm{diag}(λ_1 ,\ldots, λ_n)$. The exact solution is $$ \begin{align*} \v y(t) = We^{tD} W\inv \v y_0 \end{align*} $$ If $\Re λ_i < 0$, then $\v y(t) \to 0$ as $t\to \infty$.

Now suppose we use the Euler method to numerically solve this. We have the recurrence relation $$ \begin{align*} \v y_{n+1} = \v y_n + hA\v y_n = (I+hA) \v y_n. \end{align*} $$ We can diagonalise as $I + hA = W(I + hD)W\inv$, so the numerical solution is $$ \begin{align*} \v y_{n} = W(I + hD)^n W\inv \v y_0. \end{align*} $$ To recover the correct asymptotic behaviour $\v y_n\to 0$, we require $$ \begin{align*} |1 + λ_i h| < 1 \qquad \forall i. \end{align*} $$

For example, if $A = \pmat{-100 & 0 \\ 0 & -1}$, then due to the very negative eigenvalue $-100$, we require $h<1/50$. But this has nothing to do with accuracy, since the component $e^{-100}$ tends to 0 very quickly.
