Consider an $s$-step multistep method with order $p$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m Q(m) = \sum_{m=0}^{s} b_m Q'(m) \end{align*} $$ for all $Q\in \mathcal{P}_p$.

We'll use Kermit interpolation: there exists a unique polynomial $p\in \mathcal{P}_{2s+1}$ such that $p(m) = a_m$ and $p'(m) = -b_m$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m p(m) - b_m p'(m) = \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m^2 + b_m^2 > 0 \end{align*} $$ so $p < 2s+1$.
