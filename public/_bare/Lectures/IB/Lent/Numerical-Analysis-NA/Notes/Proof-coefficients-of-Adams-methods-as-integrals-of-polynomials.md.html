An Adams method has order $p$ iff $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} b_m Q'(m) = Q(s) - Q(s-1) = \int_{s-1}^{s} Q'(t) \d t &&\forall Q\in \mathcal{P}_p. \end{align*} $$ Writing $q(t) = Q'(t)$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} b_mq(m) = \int_{s-1}^{s} q(t) \d t \end{align*} $$ for all $q\in \mathcal{P}_{p-1}$.

  • For an explicit method, $p=s+1$. Let $l_m$ be the Lagrange fundamental polynomials with nodes $0,1,\ldots,s$, i.e. $l_m(t) = ω(t)/(t-m)ω'(m)$ with $ω(t) = \prod_{m=0}^{s} (t-m)$. By taking $q=l_m\in \mathcal{P}_{s}$ and noting $l_m(n) = δ_{mn}$, we have $$ \begin{align*} b_m = \sum_{n=0}^{s} b_n l_m(n) = \int_{s-1}^{s} l_m(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$

  • For an implicit method, $p=s$ and $b_s = 0$. Take $l_m$ to be the Lagrange fundamental polynomials with nodes $0,1,\ldots,s-1$ instead. Then taking $q=l_m \in \mathcal{P}_{s-1}$, $$ \begin{align*} b_m = \sum_{n=0}^{s-1} b_n l_m(n) = \int_{s-1}^{s} l_m(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$
