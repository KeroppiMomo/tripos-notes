Suppose there exists a monic polynomial $q_n \in \mathcal{P}_n$ such that $\|q_n\|_\infty < γ$. Consider $r = γT_n - q_n$.

At the points $t_k = \cos πk/n$ for $k=0,1,\ldots,n$, we have $γT_n(t_k) = (-1)^k γ$. However $\|q_n\|_{\infty} < γ$, so the sign of $r(t_k)$ is the same as $γT_n(t_k)$. Therefore there are $n+1$ points of alternation in signs, so there are $n$ zeros by the intermediate value theorem.

However since both $γT_n$ and $q_n$ are monic, $r\in \mathcal{P}_{n-1}$, which means $r \equiv 0$ and so $q_n = γT_n$, contradicting with $\|q_n\|_\infty < γ$.
