Use Taylor's theorem with integral remainder $$ \begin{align*} f(x) = q_n(x) + R(x) \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} q_n(x) ={}& \sum_{i=0}^{n} \frac{1}{i!}(x-a)^i f^{(i)}(a) \in \mathcal{P}_n, \\ R(x) ={}& \frac{1}{n!} \int_{a}^{x} (x-t)^n f^{(n+1)}(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$

We can rewrite $$ \begin{align*} R(x) = \frac{1}{n!}\int_{a}^{b} (x-t)_+^nf^{(n+1)}(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$

Applying $λ$, and noting $λ$ vanishes on $\mathcal{P}_n$, $$ \begin{align*} λ(f) = λ(R) ={}& \frac{1}{n!}λ\left(\int_{a}^{b} (x-t)_+^n f^{(n+1)}(t) \d t\right) \\ ={}& \frac{1}{n!} \int_{a}^{b} \underbrace{λ(x\mapsto (x-t)_+^n)}_{K_λ(t)} f^{(n+1)}(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$
