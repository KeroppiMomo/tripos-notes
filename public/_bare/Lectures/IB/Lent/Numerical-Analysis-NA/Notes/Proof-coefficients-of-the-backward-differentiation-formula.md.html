Proof 1

To have order $s$, we require $$ \begin{align*} ρ(w) = \log w\cdot σ(w) + O(|w-1|^{s+1}) = w^s \log w + O(|w-1|^{s+1}). \end{align*} $$

Observe $$ \begin{align*} \log w = -\log \frac{1}{w} = -\log\left(1 - \frac{w-1}{w}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{w-1}{w}\right)^k. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} w^s \log w = \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{s} \frac{1}{k} w^{s-k} (w-1)^k}_{\in \mathcal{P}_s} + O(|w-1|^{s+1}), \end{align*} $$ and we identity the bracketed term with $ρ(w)$.

Proof 2

To have order $s$, we require $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{m=0}^{s} a_m Q(m) = Q'(s) \end{align*} $$ which is numerical differentiation. Take $$ \begin{align*} Q(t) = l_m(t) \end{align*} $$ the Lagrange fundamental polynomials of nodes $0,1,\ldots,s$ to get $$ \begin{align*} a_m = l_m'(s). \end{align*} $$

Now we just compute the derivative. Use the form $$ \begin{align*} l_m(t) ={}& \frac{\prod_{n=0}^{m-1} (t-n) \prod_{n=m+1}^{s} (t-n)}{\prod_{n=0}^{m-1} (m-n) \prod_{n=m+1}^{s} (m-n)} \\ ={}& \frac{(-1)^{s-m}}{m!\, (s-m)!}\prod_{n=0}^{m-1} (t-n) \prod_{n=m+1}^{s} (t-n) \end{align*} $$ to see, for $m\neq s$, $$ \begin{align*} l_m'(s) ={}& \frac{(-1)^{s-m}}{m!\,(s-m)!} \prod_{n=0}^{m-1} (s-n) \prod_{n=m+1}^{s-1} (s-n) \\ ={}& \frac{(-1)^{s-m}}{m!\,(s-m)!} \frac{s!}{s-m} \\ ={}& \frac{(-1)^{s-m}}{s-m} \pmat{s \\ m}, \end{align*} $$ and for $m=s$, $$ \begin{align*} l_s(t) ={}& \frac{1}{s!} \prod_{m=0}^{s-1} (t-m) \\ l_s'(s) ={}& \frac{1}{s!} \sum_{m=0}^{s-1} \frac{s!}{s-m} \\ ={}& \sum_{m=1}^{s} \frac{1}{m}. \end{align*} $$
