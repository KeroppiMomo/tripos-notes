It suffices to show all strictly diagonally dominant (SDD) matrices are non-singular, because all principal minors of a SDD matrix are also SDD.

Let $\v x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in \R^n\setminus \set{\v 0}$. Suppose $|x_i| = \max_{1\leq j\leq n} |x_j|>0$. Then $$ \begin{align*} |(A\v x)_i| ={}& \left|\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} x_j\right| \\ \geq{} & |a_{ii}| |x_i| - \sum_{j\neq i} |a_{ij}|\underbrace{|x_j|}_{\leq |x_i|} \\ \geq{}& \left(|a_{ii} - \sum_{j\neq i} |a_{ij}|\right) |x_i| > 0 \end{align*} $$ since $A$ is strictly diagonally dominant. Hence $A\v x\neq \v 0$.
