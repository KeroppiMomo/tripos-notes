Proof 1

The result follows from two claims:

$f(x) - p(x) = f[x_0, \ldots, x_n, x] \, ω(x)$

Let $\tilde p\in \mathcal{P}_{n+1}$ be the polynomial that interpolates $f$ on $x_0, \ldots, x_n, x$. From the proof of the Newton form, we have $$ \begin{align*} f(x) - p(x) = \tilde p(x) - p(x) = f[x_0, \ldots, x_n, x] \,\prod_{i=0}^{n} (x-x_i) = f[x_0, \ldots, x_n, x]\, ω(x). \end{align*} $$

$f[x_0, \ldots, x_k] = \frac{1}{k!} f^{(k)}(ξ)$ for some $ξ$ if $f$ is $C^k$

Let $p\in \mathcal{P}_k$ be the polynomial that interpolates $f$ on $x_0,\ldots,x_k$.

$f-p\in C^k[a,b]$ has (at least) $(k+1)$ roots $x_0, \ldots, x_k$, so by Rolle's theorem, $(f-p)' \in C^{k-1}[a,b]$ has $k$ roots, one on each interval $(x_i, x_{i+1})$. Repeatedly application of Rolle's theorem yields a root $ξ\in (a,b)$ of $(f-p)^{(k)}$.

The leading coefficient of $p\in \mathcal{P}_k$ is $f[x_0, \ldots, x_k]$, so $$ \begin{align*} f^{(k)}(ξ) = p^{(k)}(ξ) = k!\, f[x_0, \ldots, x_k]. \end{align*} $$

Proof 2

Fix $x$. We will directly show $f(x) - p(x) = \frac{1}{(n+1)!} f^{(n+1)}(ξ) \, ω(x)$ for some $ξ$.

If $x=x_i$ for some $i$, then both sides vanish so we are done.

Otherwise $ω(x)\neq 0$, and consider the function $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(t) = f(t) - p(t) - \frac{f(x) - p(x)}{ω(x)} ω(t). \end{align*} $$ This has the property that $ϕ$ vanishes at $x_0, \ldots, x_n$, as well as $x$. Moreover, $ϕ\in C^{(n+1)}[a,b]$.

$ϕ$ has $(n+2)$ roots, so applying Rolle's theorem repeatedly gives a root $ξ\in (a,b)$ of $ϕ^{(n+1)}$. Then $$ \begin{align*} 0 = ϕ^{(n+1)}(ξ) ={}& f^{(n+1)}(ξ) - p^{(n+1)}(ξ) - \frac{f(x) - p(x)}{ω(x)} ω^{(n+1)}(t) \\ ={}& f^{(n+1)}(ξ) - \frac{f(x) - p(x)}{ω(x)} (n+1)! \end{align*} $$ where $p^{(n+1)}(ξ) = 0$ because $p\in \mathcal{P}_n$ and $ω^{(n+1)}(t) = (n+1)!$ because $ω$ is a monic degree-$(n+1)$ polynomial. Rearranging yields the result.
