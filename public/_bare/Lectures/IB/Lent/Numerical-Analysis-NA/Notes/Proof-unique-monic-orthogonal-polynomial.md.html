Existence

We perform Gram-Schmidt orthogonalisation on $\set{1, x, x_2, \ldots, x^n}$: define $Q_0 \equiv 1$, and $$ \begin{align*} Q_n(x) := x^n - \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{\langle x^n, Q_k\rangle}{\|Q_k\|^2} Q_k \end{align*} $$ for each $n=1,2,\ldots$.

See that $Q_n$ is monic. Besides, $\langle Q_n, Q_k\rangle = 0$ for all $k<n$, since $$ \begin{align*} \langle Q_n, Q_k\rangle = \langle Q_n, Q_k\rangle - \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{\langle x^n,Q_i\rangle}{\|Q_i\|^2} \underbrace{\langle Q_i, Q_k\rangle}_{\text{$0$ if $i\neq k$}} = \langle Q_n, Q_k\rangle - \frac{\langle x^n, Q_k\rangle}{\|Q_k\|^2} \langle Q_k, Q_k\rangle = 0 \end{align*} $$ by induction.

Uniqueness

Suppose $Q_n$ and $\tilde Q_n$ are both monic and orthogonal to $\mathcal{P}_{n-1}$. Then $Q_n - \tilde Q_n \in \mathcal{P}_{n-1}$, so $\langle Q_n-\tilde Q_n, Q_n - \tilde Q_n \rangle = \langle Q_n, Q_n-\tilde Q_n\rangle - \langle \tilde Q_n ,Q_n - \tilde Q_n\rangle = 0$ and thus $Q_n = \tilde Q_n$.

Expansion

By Gram-Schmidt, $\mathcal{P}_n = \mathrm{span}(x^k)_{k=0}^n = \mathrm{span}(Q_k)_{k=0}^n$, so for all $p\in \mathcal{P}_n$, there exists $c_k$ such that $$ \begin{align*} p = \sum_{k=0}^{n} c_k Q_k. \end{align*} $$ Taking $\langle \cdot,Q_k\rangle$ on both sides yields $\langle p,Q_k\rangle = c_k \langle Q_k, Q_k\rangle$.
