Consider the ODE $y' = λy$.

Forward Euler

The scheme is $$ \begin{align*} y_{n+1} = y_n + hλ y_n \end{align*} $$ so $y_n \to 0$ iff $|1+λh|<1$. Therefore the linear stability domain is $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{D} = \sb{z\in \C}{|z+1| < 1}. \end{align*} $$ Thus forward Euler is not $A$-stable.

Trapezoidal rule

The scheme is $$ \begin{align*} y_{n+1} = y_n + \frac{1}{2}λh(y_n + y_{n+1}) \implies y_{n+1} = \frac{1+z/2}{1-z/2}y_n. \end{align*} $$

$\frac{|1+z/2|}{|1-z/2|} < 1$ iff $\Re z < 0$, so the linear stability domain is $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{D} = \sb{z\in \C}{\Re z < 0} = \C^- \end{align*} $$ and thus the method is $A$-stable.

Backward Euler

The scheme is $$ \begin{align*} y_{n+1} = y_n + λhy_{n+1} \implies y_{n+1} = \frac{y_n}{1-z}. \end{align*} $$ Thus $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{D} = \sb{z\in \C}{|z-1|>1} \end{align*} $$ and so this method is $A$-stable.
