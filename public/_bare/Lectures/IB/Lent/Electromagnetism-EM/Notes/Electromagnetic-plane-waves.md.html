Consider a plane wave where $\v E$ and $\v B$ depend only on $(x,t)$ but not $(y,z)$.

A more general solution has the form $$ \begin{align*} \v E = \Re(\v E_0 \exp i(\v k\cdot \v x - ω t)), \qquad \v B = \Re(\v B_0 \exp i(\v k\cdot \v x-ωt)) \end{align*} $$ where $\v E_0, \v B_0$ are constant complex vector amplitudes, and $\v k$ is the real wavevector. The Maxwell's equations reduce to $$ \begin{align*} ∇\cdot \v E &= 0 &&\implies &\v k\cdot \v E_0 &= 0, \\ ∇\cdot \v B &= 0 &&\implies &\v k\cdot \v B_0 &= 0, \\ ∇\times \v E &= -\pdfd{\v B}{t} &&\implies &\v k\times \v E_0 &= ω\v B_0, \\ ∇\times \v B &= μ_0 ε_0 \pdfd{\v E}{t} &&\implies &\v k\times \v B_0 &= -\frac{ω}{c^2}\v E_0. \end{align*} $$ These imply the dispersion relation $$ \begin{align*} ω^2 = c^2 k^2. \end{align*} $$

Linearly polarised plane wave open_in_new

If we restrict $\v E = E(x,t) \v e_y$ to only be in the $y$ direction, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} \v E(x,t) &= \bigl(f(x - ct) + g(x+ct)\bigr)\v e_y, \\ \v B(x,t) &= \frac{1}{c}\bigl(f(x-ct) - g(x+ct)\bigr)\v e_z, \end{align*} $$ which is a sum of wave travelling without change of form in the $+x$ direction and another travelling in the $-x$ direction. (Derivation)

A monochromatic wave has the form $$ \begin{align*} E = E_0 \cos(kx - ωt), \qquad B = \frac{E_0}{c}\cos(kx - ωt) \end{align*} $$ where $E_0$ is a constant amplitude, $k=\pm ω/c$ is the wavenumber, and $ω$ is the angular frequency.

This corresponds to our previous discussion with $\v E_0$ real. Then $\v B_0 = \frac{1}{ω}\v k\times \v E_0$ is also real, and $\v k, \v E_0, \v B_0$ form a right-handed orthogonal triad.

Elliptically polarised plane wave open_in_new

For $\v E_0 = \v a - i\v b$, $\v B_0 = \v a' - i\v b'$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \v E &= \v a \cos(\v k\cdot \v x - ωt) + \v b \sin(\v k\cdot \v x - ωt), \\ \v B &= \v a' \cos(\v k\cdot \v x - ωt) + \v b' \sin(\v k\cdot \v x - ωt), \\ \end{align*} $$ with $|\v a'| = |\v a|/c, |\v b'| = |\v b|/c$ and $\v k,\v a,\v a'$ and $\v k,\v b,\v b'$ being right-handed orthogonal triads. The fields trace out an ellipse and the wave is elliptically polarised.

Circular polarisation occurs when $|\v b| = |\v a|$ and $\v a\cdot \v b = 0$. It is right-handed if $\v b = \vhat k\times \v a$ and left-handed if $\v b = -\vhat k\times \v a$.

Energy for an electromagnetic plane wave open_in_new

For the plane wave solution here, the averaged (over time or space) energy density and flux density are $$ \begin{align*} \langle w\rangle &= \frac{1}{2}ε_0 |\v E_0|^2, \\ \langle \v S\rangle &= \langle w\rangle c\vhat k, \end{align*} $$ implying the wave transports energy at speed $c$ in the direction $\vhat k$. (Derivation)
