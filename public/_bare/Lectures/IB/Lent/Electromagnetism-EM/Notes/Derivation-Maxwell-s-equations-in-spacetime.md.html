Use the relation $$ \begin{align*} F^{0i} = F_{i0} = \frac{E_i}{c}, \quad F^{ij} = ε_{ijk} B_k, \quad J^0 = ρc, \quad J^i = J_i. \end{align*} $$

First equation

(M1) becomes $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \partial_i E_i \implies 0 =\partial_i F^{0i}. \end{align*} $$ (M4) becomes $$ \begin{align*} & ε_{ijk}\partial_j B_k = μ_0 J_i + μ_0ε_0 (c\partial_0)E_i \\ \implies& \partial_j F^{ij} = μ_0 J^i - \partial_0 F^{i0} \\ \implies& \partial_ν F^{iν} = μ_0J^i. \end{align*} $$ Thus we get $$ \begin{align*} \partial_ν F^{μν} = μ_0 J^μ. \end{align*} $$

Second equation

Since $F_{ij} = ε_{ijk} B_k$, we have $$ \begin{align*} B_i = \frac{1}{2} ε_{ijk} F_{jk}. \end{align*} $$ So (M2) becomes $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \partial_i B_i = \frac{1}{2}ε_{ijk} \partial_i F_{jk} \implies 0 = ε_{0ijk}\partial_i F_{jk}. \end{align*} $$

(M3) becomes $$ \begin{align*} &ε_{ijk} \partial_j E_k = -(c\partial_0) B_i \\ \implies& cε_{ijk}\partial_jF_{k0} + \frac{c}{2}ε_{ijk}\partial_0 F_{jk} = 0 \\ \implies& ε_{ijk}\partial_j F_{k0} + \frac{1}{2}ε_{ijk} \partial_0 F_{jk} = 0. \end{align*} $$ Multiply by $ε_{ilm}$ to get $$ \begin{align*} 0 &= \partial_l F_{m0} - \partial_m F_{l0} + \frac{1}{2} \partial_0 F_{lm} - \frac{1}{2}\partial_0 F_{ml} \\ &= \partial_l F_{m0} + \partial_m F_{0l} + \partial_0 F_{lm} \end{align*} $$ using antisymmetry.

These imply $$ \begin{align*} \partial_μ F_{νρ} + \partial_ν F_{ρμ} + \partial_ρ F_{μν} = 0 \iff ε^{μνρσ} \partial_νF_{ρσ} = 0. \end{align*} $$
