For a volume $V$ with boundary $S = \partial V$, Gauss's law states that $$ \begin{align*} \oint_S \v E\cdot \d \v S = \frac{Q}{ε_0} \end{align*} $$ where $Q = \int_{V} ρ \d V$ is the total charge enclosed. This is obtained from (M1) and divergence theorem.

Spherically symmetric charge distribution open_in_new

For a spherically symmetric charge distribution $ρ(r)$ contained within $r\leq R$ (in spherical polars), $$ \begin{align*} \v E(r) = \frac{Q}{4πε_0 r^2}, \qquad r>R. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

In the limit $R\to 0$, we have a point charge $Q$, corresponding to $$ \begin{align*} ρ(\v x) = Q\, δ(\v x). \end{align*} $$

The Coulomb force is the corresponding Lorentz force. $$ \begin{align*} \v F =q\v E = \frac{Qq}{4πε_0 r^2} \v e_r, \end{align*} $$ similar to gravity, but it can be both repulsive and attractive, and is much stronger.

Cylindrically symmetric charge distribution open_in_new

For a cylindrically symmetric charge distribution $ρ(r)$ contained within $r\leq R$ (in cylindrical polars), $$ \begin{align*} \v E(r) = \frac{λ}{2πε_0 r}, \qquad r>R, \end{align*} $$ where $λ$ is the charge per unit length. (Derivation)

In the limit $R\to 0$, we have a line charge $λ$, corresponding to $$ \begin{align*} ρ(\v x) = λ\,δ(x)\,δ(y). \end{align*} $$

Planar symmetric charge distribution open_in_new

Consider a planar charge distribution $ρ(z)$ (in Cartesian coordinates), contained within $-d<z<d$. Further assume reflectional symmetry $ρ(z) = ρ(-z)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \v E(z) = \sign(z) \frac{σ}{2ε_0} \v e_z, \end{align*} $$ where $σ$ is the charge per unit area. (Derivation)

In the limit $d\to 0$, we have a surface charge $σ$, corresponding to $$ \begin{align*} ρ = σ\, δ(z). \end{align*} $$
