The 4-potential is the 4-vector $$ \begin{align*} A^μ = \left(\frac{Φ}{c}, \v A\right). \end{align*} $$ Then the electromagnetic tensor is the antisymmetric derivative $$ \begin{align*} F_{μν} = \partial_μA_ν - \partial_ν A_μ. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

Now a gauge transformation $$ \begin{align*} \tilde A_μ = A_μ + \partial_μ χ \end{align*} $$ leaves $F_{μν}$ unchanged because $\partial_μ\partial_ν χ - \partial_ν \partial_μ χ = 0$.

The equivalent of the Coulomb gauge is the Lorentz gauge in which $$ \begin{align*} \partial_μ A^μ = 0. \end{align*} $$ This can be assume WLOG: if $\partial_μ A^μ \neq 0$, take $χ$ to be the solution of the forced wave equation $-\square χ = \partial_μ A^μ$.
