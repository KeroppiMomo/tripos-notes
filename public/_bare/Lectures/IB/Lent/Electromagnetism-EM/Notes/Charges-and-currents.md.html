Electric charge is an elementary property, with unit the coulomb $\mathrm{C}$.

On a macroscopic scale, we have a continuous charge density $ρ(\v x, t)$, calculated by integrating the total charge over a small volume but large enough to contain a large number of charges.

Total charge in a volume $V$ is $$ \begin{align*} Q = \int_{V} ρ \d V. \end{align*} $$

Electric current density $\v J(\v x,t)$ is the flux of charge per unit area.

The current flowing through a surface $S$ is $$ \begin{align*} I = \int_{S} \v J\cdot \d \v S. \end{align*} $$ Its unit is the ampere $\mathrm{A} = \mathrm{C}\ \mathrm{s}\inv$.

Discrete charge distributions open_in_new

For $N$ particles, each of charge $q_i$ at position $\v x_i(t)$, the charge distribution is $$ \begin{align*} ρ(\v x, t) ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{N} q_i\, δ(\v x - \v x_i(t)), \\ \v J(\v x, t) ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{N} q_i\, \dv x_i\, δ(\v x - \v x_i(t)). \end{align*} $$

Charge conservation open_in_new

Electric charges are conserved. The equation of charge conservation is $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{ρ}{t} + ∇\cdot \v J = 0. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)
