The spacetime derivative of a scalar field $f(X)$ is a covector field $$ \begin{align*} \partial_μ f = (\partial_0 f, \partial_i f) = \left(\frac{1}{c} \pdfd{f}{t}, ∇f\right). \end{align*} $$

The spacetime divergence of a 4-vector field $V(X) = (V^0, \v v)$ is a scalar field $$ \begin{align*} \partial_μ V^μ = \partial_0V^0 + \partial_i V^i = \frac{1}{c}\pdfd{V^0}{t} + ∇\cdot \v v. \end{align*} $$

The spacetime equivalent of the curl is the antisymmetric derivative of a covector field $Y(X)$ $$ \begin{align*} \partial_μ Y_ν - \partial_ν Y_μ. \end{align*} $$

The generalisation of the Laplacian $∇^2$ is the d'Alembertian, a scalar differential operator $$ \begin{align*} \square = η^{μν}\partial_μ \partial_ν = -\frac{1}{c^2} \pdd[2]{t} + ∇^2. \end{align*} $$ The wave equation for a scalar field is $\square f = 0$. It is only a scalar equation for waves that travel at $c$.
