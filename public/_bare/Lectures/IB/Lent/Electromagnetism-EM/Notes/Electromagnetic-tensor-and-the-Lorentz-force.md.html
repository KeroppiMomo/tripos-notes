The Lorentz force as a 4-vector is $$ \begin{align*} f_μ = qF_{μν}U^ν \end{align*} $$ where $F_{μν}$ is the electromagnetic tensor having components $$ \begin{align*} F_{00} &= 0, \\ F_{0i} &= -E_i/c, \\ F_{i0} &= E_i/c, \\ F_{ij} &= ε_{ijk} B_k. \end{align*} $$ In marix form, $$ \begin{align*} F_{μν} = \pmat{0 & -E_x/c & -E_y/c & -E_z/c \\ E_x/c & 0 & B_z & -B_y \\ E_y/c & -B_z & 0 & B_x \\ E_z/c & B_y & -B_x & 0}. \end{align*} $$

The spatial component says $$ \begin{align*} \dfd{\v p}{τ} = γ_\v uq(\v E + \v u \times \v B) \iff \dfd{\v p}{t}=q(\v E + \v u\times \v B) = \v F \end{align*} $$ where $\v F$ is the 3-force.

The time component says $$ \begin{align*} \dfd{E}{τ} = γ_\v u q\v E\cdot \v u \iff \dfd{E}{t} = q\v E\cdot \v u = \v F\cdot \v u. \end{align*} $$

Lorentz transformation of electromagnetic field open_in_new

If an inertial frame $S'$ moves at a velocity $\v v$ relative to $S$, then the components of $\v E$ and $\v B$ parallel and perpendicular to $\v v$ transform as $$ \begin{align*} \v E_\parallel' &= \v E_\parallel, & \v E_\perp' &= γ(\v E_\perp + \v v\times \v B_\perp), \\ \v B_\parallel' &= \v B_\parallel, & \v B_\perp' &= γ(\v B_\perp - \frac{1}{c^2}\v v\times \v E_\perp). \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

We obtain a (Lorentz invariant) scalar $$ \begin{align*} F_{μν} F^{μν} \propto |\v E|^2 - c^2 |\v B|^2 \end{align*} $$ and a pseudo-scalar (transforming with a minus sign if $\det Λ = -1$) $$ \begin{align*} ε_{μνρσ} F^{μν} F^{ρσ} \propto \v E \cdot \v B. \end{align*} $$
