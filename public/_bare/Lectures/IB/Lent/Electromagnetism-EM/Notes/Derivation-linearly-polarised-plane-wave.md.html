Seeking a solution of the form $\v E = E(x,t)\v e_y$, the wave equation $\pdfd[2]{\v E}{t} = c^2 \, ∇^2\v E$ reduces to $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd[2]{E}{t} = c^2 \pdfd[2]{E}{x} \end{align*} $$ which has general equation $$ \begin{align*} E(x,t) = f(x-ct) + g(x+ct). \end{align*} $$

$\v B$ can be found by $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{\v B}{t} &= -∇\times \v E \\ &= -\v e_z \pdfd{E_y}{x} \\ &= -(f'(x-ct) + g'(x+ct))\v e_z \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \v B(x,t) = \frac{1}{c}\bigl(f(x - ct) - g'(x+ct)\bigr) \v e_z \end{align*} $$ up to a constant (which doesn't do anything).

This also satisfies $$ \begin{align*} ∇\times \v B &= \v e_y \pdfd{B_z}{x} = \frac{1}{c}\bigl(f'(x-ct) - g'(x+ct)\bigr)\v e_y = μ_0ε_0 \pdfd{\v E}{t}. \end{align*} $$
