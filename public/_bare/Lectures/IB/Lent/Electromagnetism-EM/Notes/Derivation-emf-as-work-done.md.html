For a particle confined to $C(t)$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{q}\int_{C(t)} \v F\cdot \d \v x = \int_{C} (\v E + \dv x \times \v B) \cdot \d \v x. \end{align*} $$ The work done by magnetic force $(\dv x \times \v B)\cdot \d \v x = \v B\cdot (\d \v x \times \dv x)$ only has contribution from the component of $\dv x$ perpendicular to $\d \v x$ i.e. $C(t)$, which equals the perpendicular component of $\v v$.
