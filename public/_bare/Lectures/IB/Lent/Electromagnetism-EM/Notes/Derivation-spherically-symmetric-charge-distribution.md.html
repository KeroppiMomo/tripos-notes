$\v E$ also has spherical symmetry, so it takes the form $\v E = E(r)\, \v e_r$. This satisfies $∇\times \v E = 0$.

For $r>R$, apply Gauss's law to a sphere $S$ of radius $r$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \frac{Q}{ε_0} ={}&\oint_{S} \v E\cdot \d \v S \\ ={}& \oint_{S} E(r) \d S \\ ={}& 4πr^2 E(r). \end{align*} $$ Thus $$ \begin{align*} \v E = \frac{Q}{4πε_0 r^2} \v e_r. \end{align*} $$
