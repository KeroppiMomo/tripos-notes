If we restrict $\v E = E(x,t) \v e_y$ to only be in the $y$ direction, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} \v E(x,t) &= \bigl(f(x - ct) + g(x+ct)\bigr)\v e_y, \\ \v B(x,t) &= \frac{1}{c}\bigl(f(x-ct) - g(x+ct)\bigr)\v e_z, \end{align*} $$ which is a sum of wave travelling without change of form in the $+x$ direction and another travelling in the $-x$ direction. (Derivation)

A monochromatic wave has the form $$ \begin{align*} E = E_0 \cos(kx - ωt), \qquad B = \frac{E_0}{c}\cos(kx - ωt) \end{align*} $$ where $E_0$ is a constant amplitude, $k=\pm ω/c$ is the wavenumber, and $ω$ is the angular frequency.

This corresponds to our previous discussion with $\v E_0$ real. Then $\v B_0 = \frac{1}{ω}\v k\times \v E_0$ is also real, and $\v k, \v E_0, \v B_0$ form a right-handed orthogonal triad.
