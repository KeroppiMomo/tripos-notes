Taylor expand $$ \begin{align*} \v A(\v x) ={}& \frac{μ_0}{4π} \int_{V} \frac{\v J(\v x')}{|\v x-\v x'|} \d^3\v x' \\ ={}& \frac{μ_0}{4π} \int_{V} \v J(\v x')\left(\frac{1}{|\v x|} + \frac{\v x\cdot \v x'}{|\v x|^3} + \ldots\right) \d^3 \v x' \\ \approx{}& \frac{μ_0}{4π}\left(\frac{\int_{V} \v J(\v x') \d^3 \v x'}{|\v x|} + \frac{x_i \int_{V} x'_i J_j(\v x') \d^3 \v x'}{|\v x|^3}\right). \end{align*} $$

The first term vanishes, because $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \int_{\partial V} x_i J_j \d S_j &&\v J=0 \text{ on }\partial V \\ ={}& \int_{V} \partial_j(x_i J_j) \d^3 \v x &&\text{divergence theorem} \\ ={}& \int_{V} δ_{ij} J_j + x_i \underbrace{\partial_jJ_j}_{∇\cdot \v J = 0} \d^3 \v x \\ ={}&\int_{V} J_i \d^3\v x. \end{align*} $$

The integral in the second term is anti-symmetric, as $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \int_{\partial V} x_i x_j J_k \d S_k \\ ={}& \int_{V} \partial_k(x_i x_j J_k) \d^3 \v x \\ ={}& \int_{V} δ_{ik} x_j J_k + x_i δ_{jk} J_k + x_i x_j \underbrace{\partial_k J_k}_{0} \d^3 \v x \\ ={}& \int_{V} x_j J_i \d^3 \v x + \int_{V} x_i J_j \d^3 \v x. \end{align*} $$

Thus we can write $$ \begin{align*} \int_{V} x_i J_j \d^3 \v x = ε_{ijk} m_k \iff m_i = \frac{1}{2}ε_{ijk} \int_{V} x_j J_k \d^3 \v x. \end{align*} $$ Hence $$ \begin{align*} \v A(\v x) \approx \frac{μ_0}{4π} \frac{x_i ε_{ijk} m_k}{|\v x|^3} = \frac{μ_0}{4π} \frac{\v m\times \v x}{|\v x|^3}. \end{align*} $$

Finally $$ \begin{align*} \v B = ∇\times \v A &\approx \frac{μ_0}{4π}∇\times \left(\frac{\v m\times \v x}{|\v x|^3}\right) \\ &= \frac{μ_0}{4π}\left(\left(-\frac{3\v x}{|\v x|^5}\right) \times (\v m\times \v x) + \frac{(∇\cdot \v x) \v m-(\v m\cdot ∇)\v x}{|\v x|^3}\right) \\ &= \frac{μ_0}{4π} \left(\frac{-3|\v x|^2\v m + 3(\v m\cdot \v x)\v x + 3|\v x|^2\v m - |\v x|^2\v m}{|\v x|^5}\right) \\ &= \frac{μ_0}{4π} \frac{3(\v m\cdot \v x)\v x - |\v x|^2 \v m}{|\v x|^5}. \end{align*} $$
