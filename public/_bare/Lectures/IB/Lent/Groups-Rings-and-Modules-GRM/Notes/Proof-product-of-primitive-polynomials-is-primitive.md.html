Let $f=a_0 + a_1 X + \ldots + a_n X^n$ and $g=b_0 + b_1 X + \ldots + b_m X^m$ be primitive, with $a_n, b_m \neq 0$. Suppose for a contradiction that $fg$ is not primitive.

Since $R$ is a unique factorisation domain, find some irreducible $p\in R$ that divides the non-unit $c(fg)$. As $c(f)$ and $c(g)$ are units, $p\not\mid c(f)$ and $p\not\mid c(g)$. Therefore there exists a minimal $k=0,1,\ldots,n$ such that $p\not\mid a_k$ and a minimal $l = 0,1,\ldots,m$ such that $p\not\mid b_l$.

Now consider the coefficient of $X^{k+l}$ in $fg$, which is $$ \begin{align*} C = \underbrace{a_0 b_{k+l} + \ldots + a_{k-1} b_{l+1}}_{\text{divisible by $p$}} + a_k b_l + \underbrace{a_{k+1} b_{l-1} + \ldots + a_{k+l} b_0}_{\text{divisible by $p$}}. \end{align*} $$ The first $k$ terms involves $a_0, \ldots, a_{k-1}$, and the last $l$ terms involves $b_0, \ldots b_{l-1}$, which are all divisible by $p$. $p\mid c(fg)$ so $p\mid C$, and therefore $p\mid a_k b_l$. But by construction $p\not\mid a_k, b_l$ and $p$ is prime, so we have a contradiction.
