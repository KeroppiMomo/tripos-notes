Theorem (first isomorphism theorem for groups) open_in_new

If $\phi: G\to H$ is a homomorphism, then $\ker\phi$ $\lhd$ $G$ and $G$ $/$ $\ker\phi$ $\isom$ $\im\phi$.

Proof

Theorem (second isomorphism theorem for groups) open_in_new

Let $H\leq G$ and $K\lhd G$ be groups. Then

  • $HK = \sb{hk}{h\in H, k\in K}$ is a subgroup;

  • $H\cap K \lhd H$; and

  • $HK/K \isom H/H\cap K$.

Proof

  • $H/K$ makes no sense. This theorem says that the two ways of coming up with something similar, namely $HK/K$ (by enlarging $H$) and $H/H\cap K$ (by restricting $K$), are the same.

Theorem (correspondence theorem for groups) open_in_new

Let $N\lhd G$ be groups. The sets $\mathcal{G} = \sb{\text{subgroups }H\leq G}{N\lhd H}$ and $\mathcal{N} = \set{\text{subgroups } S\leq G/N}$ have the "same structure", in the following way:

Define the homomorphism $p: G\to G/N$ by $p(g) = gN$. There exists a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{G} &\xleftrightarrow{\quad} \mathcal{N} \\ H &\shortmid\!\xrightarrow{\quad} p(H) = H/N \\ \sb{g\in G}{gN \in S}= p\inv(S) &\xleftarrow{\quad}\!\shortmid S. \end{align*} $$ Moreover, $N\lhd H\lhd G$ iff $G/H \lhd G/N$.

Proof

Theorem (third isomorphism theorem for groups) open_in_new

Proof

Proposition (abelian simple groups) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a group. $G$ is non-trivial, simple and abelian iff $G \isom C_p$ for some prime $p$.

Theorem (composition series) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a group.

If $G$ is finite, then there exists subgroups $$ \begin{align*} G = H_1 \rhd H_2 \rhd \ldots \rhd H_n = \set{e} \end{align*} $$ such that $H_{i}/H_{i+1}$ is simple.

Proof

Theorem (action and permutation representation) open_in_new

An action of a group $G$ on a set $X$ is the same thing as a permutation representation (a homomorphism $G\to \Sym(X)$), i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{Homomorphisms }G\to \Sym(X)} &\xleftrightarrow{\quad} \set{\text{Actions } G\times X\to X} \\ ϕ &\shortmid\!\xrightarrow{\quad } \bigl((g,x) \mapsto ϕ(g)(x)\bigr) \\ \bigl(g\mapsto (x\mapsto g*x)\bigr) &\xleftarrow{\quad }\!\shortmid * \end{align*} $$ is a bijection.

Proof

Notation (kernel and image of permutation representation) open_in_new

For a group action $G\actson X$ given by a permutation representation $ϕ: G\to \Sym(X)$, define $G^X =$ $\im$$(ϕ)$ and $G_X =$ $\ker$$(ϕ)$.

Proposition (largest normal subgroup) open_in_new

Let $H\leq G$ be groups.

The largest normal subgroup of $G$ contained in $H$ is $$ \begin{align*} K = \bigcap_{g\in G} gHg\inv. \end{align*} $$ In particular, if $G$ is finite and $|G:H| = n$, then $G/K$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_n$. Thus $|G/K| \mid n!$ and $|G/K|\geq n$.

Proof

Corollary (non-abelian simple group having proper subgroup) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a group.

If $G$ is non-abelian, simple and has a proper subgroup of index $n>1$, then $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $A_n$. Moreover, $n\geq 5$.

Proof

Definition (automorphism) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a group. A permutation $G\to G$ that is also a homomorphism is called an automorphism.

The set of all automorphisms of $G$, denoted as $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$, is a subgroup of $\mathrm{Sym}(G)$ called the automorphism group of $G$.

Proof

Definition (normaliser) open_in_new

Let $H\leq G$ be groups.

The normaliser of $H$ in $G$ is the largest subgroup of $G$ containing $H$ in which $H$ is normal: $$ \begin{align*} N_G(H) = \sb{g\in G}{ghg\inv \in H \quad \forall h\in H}. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (classification of finite abelian groups) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite abelian group.

Then there exists positive integers $d_1, \ldots, d_n$ such that $$ \begin{align*} G\isom C_{d_1} \times \ldots \times C_{d_n}. \end{align*} $$

Moreover, we can choose $d_i$ such that $d_{i+1} \mid d_i$, in which case the expression is unique.

Proof: apply the structure theorem to $\Z$-modules (abelian groups).

Sylow theorems open_in_new

Theorem (Sylow) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite group of order $p^a m$, where $p$ is prime and $p\not\mid m$.

  1. The set of Sylow $p$-subgroups $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G) = \sb{P\leq G}{|P| = p^a}$ is non-empty.

  2. Any two Sylow $p$-subgroups $H, H' \in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ are conjugate, i.e. $H' = gHg\inv$ for some $g\in G$.

  3. The number $n_p = |\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)|$ of Sylow $p$-subgroups satisfies

    • $n_p = |G :$ $N_G(P)$$|$ for any $P\in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$, and so $n_p \mid |G|$;

    • $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod p$, and so $n_p \mid m$.

Proof

Corollary (unique Sylow subgroup is normal) open_in_new

Let $G$ be a finite group.

If $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ $= \set{P}$ has size 1, then $P\lhd G$.

Proof
