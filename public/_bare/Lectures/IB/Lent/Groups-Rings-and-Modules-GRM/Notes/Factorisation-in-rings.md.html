Definition (Euclidean domain) open_in_new

A ring $R$ is a Euclidean domain if it is an integral domain and there exists a Euclidean function $$ \begin{align*} ϕ: R\setminus \set{0} \to \Z_{\geq 0} \end{align*} $$ satisfying

  1. $ϕ(ab) \geq ϕ(b)$ for all $a,b\neq 0$; and

  2. for all $a,b\in R$ with $b\neq 0$, there exists $q,r\in R$ such that $a = qb + r$ and either $r=0$ or $ϕ(r)<ϕ(b)$.

A field is a Euclidean domain: take the Euclidean function to be $ϕ(r) = 1$.

Definition (principal ideal domain) open_in_new

A ring is called a principal ideal domain (PID) if it is an integral domain and every ideal is principal.

Proposition (Euclidean domain is PID) open_in_new

If $R$ is a Euclidean domain, then $R$ is a principal ideal domain.

Proof

Proposition (equivalence of maximal ideal, prime ideal, prime element, irreducible element in PID) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain, and $r\in R$. If $r\neq 0$, the following are equivalent:

  1. $r$ is irreducible;

  2. $r$ is prime;

  3. $(r)$ is a prime ideal;

  4. $(r)$ is a maximal ideal.

In particular, if $I\lhd R$ is non-zero, then $I$ is prime iff $I$ is maximal.

Proof

Definition (unique factorisation domain) open_in_new

A ring $R$ is called a unique factorisation domain (UFD) if

  1. $R$ is an integral domain;

  2. every non-zero non-unit in $R$ can be written as a product of irreducibles; and

  3. if $p_1 \ldots p_n = q_1 \ldots q_m$ where $p_i,q_i$ are all irreducibles, then $n=m$ and up to reordering, $q_i$ and $q_i$ are associates for all $i$.

Theorem (PID is UFD) open_in_new

If $R$ is a principal ideal domain, then $R$ is a unique factorisation domain.

Proof

Proposition (irreducible iff prime in UFD) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a unique factorisation domain, and $r\in R$ be an element.

Then $r$ is irreducible iff $r$ is prime.

Proof

  • "$\Leftarrow$" is true in all rings.

  • Comparing with the case in a PID, in fact a UFD is a PID iff every non-zero prime ideal is maximal.

Summary: $$ \begin{align*} \text{field} \implies \text{Euclidean} \implies \text{PID} \implies \text{UFD} \implies \text{integral} \end{align*} $$

Definition (greatest common divisor, least common multiple) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain, and $a_1, \ldots, a_n\in R$.

We say $d\in R$ is a greatest common divisor (GCD) of $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ if

  1. $d\mid a_i$ for all $i$; and

  2. if $d'\mid a_i$ for all $i$, then $d' \mid d$.

We say $m\in R$ is the least common multiple (LCM) of $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ if

  1. $a_i \mid m$ for all $i$; and

  2. if $a_i \mid m'$ for all $i$, then $m\mid m'$.

Theorem (existence and uniqueness of GCD and LCM in UFD) open_in_new

In a unique factorisation domain, the greatest common divisor and least common multiple exist and are unique up to associates.
