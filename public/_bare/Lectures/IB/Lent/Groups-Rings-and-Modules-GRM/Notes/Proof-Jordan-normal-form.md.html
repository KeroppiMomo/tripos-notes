Lemma (primes in the complex polynomial ring) open_in_new

The primes in $\C[X]$ are exactly $(X-λ)$ for any $λ\in \C$.

Proof

As in the proof for the rational canonical form, $V_α$ is finitely generated. This time we apply the primary decomposition theorem to get $$ \begin{align*} V_α \isom \C[X]/((X-λ_1)^{a_1}) \oplus \ldots \oplus \C[X]/((X-λ_r)^{a_r}), \end{align*} $$ where there is no $\C[X]$ summands because $V$ is a finite-dimensional vector space ($\C$-module) while $\C[X]$ is infinite-dimensional.

The vector space $\C[X]/((X-λ_i)^{a_i})$ has a basis $\set{1, X-λ_i, (X-λ_i)^2, \ldots, (X-λ_i)^{a_i-1}}$, in which the endomorphism $β(v) = (X-λ_i)\cdot v$ has matrix form $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{0 \\ 1 \\ & 1\\ && \ddots\\ &&& 1 & 0} \in M_{a_i \times a_i}(\C). \end{align*} $$ Since $α = β + λ_i\,\id$, the matrix of $α$ is then $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{λ_i \\ 1 & λ_i \\ & 1 & λ_i \\ &&\ddots & \ddots \\ &&& 1 & λ_i} = J_{a_i}(λ_i) \end{align*} $$ which is a Jordan block.

Therefore combining the bases for $V$, $$ \begin{align*} α = \pmat{J_{a_1}(λ_1) \\ &\ddots \\ &&J_{a_r}(λ_r)} \end{align*} $$ as required.
