Fix some non-zero $a\in R$. Consider the map of multiplication by $a$ $$ \begin{align*} μ_a: R&\to R \\ r &\mapsto a\cdot r. \end{align*} $$ This is a group homomorphism by the distributive property (but not a ring homomorphism), with a trivial kernel as $R$ is an integral domain. Therefore $μ_a$ is injective.

Since $R$ is finite, this implies $μ_a$ is also surjective. Therefore we can find $b\in R$ such that $a\cdot b = 1\in R$. Hence $a$ is a unit.
