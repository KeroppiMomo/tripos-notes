If $G$ is simple, then we are done by taking $H_2 = \set{e}$.

Otherwise, let $H_2$ be some proper normal subgroup of $G$ of maximal order. We claim that $G/H_2$ is simple. If not, then we can find a normal subgroup $S\lhd G/H_2$. By the correspondence theorem, $S=G/K$ for some $H_2\lhd K\lhd G$, contradicting with $H_2$ being a maximal normal subgroup.

Replace $G$ with $H_2$ and induct. Since $|H_2| < |G|$, the orders of the subgroups are strictly decreasing so the process terminates in at most $|G|$ steps.
