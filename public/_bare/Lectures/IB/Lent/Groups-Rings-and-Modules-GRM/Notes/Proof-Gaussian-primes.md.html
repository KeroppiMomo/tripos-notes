First check those are primes:

  • If a prime $p$ in $\Z$ satisfies $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$, then $p\not\equiv 1,2\pmod 4$, so $p$ is a prime in $\Z[i]$.

  • If $N(z) = p$ is prime, suppose $z = uv$ where $u,v\in \Z[i]$. Then $$ \begin{align*} p = N(z) = N(u) N(v) \qquad \text{(in $\Z_{\geq 0}$)}. \end{align*} $$ Since $p$ is prime, either $N(u) = 1$ or $N(v) = 1$, so either $u$ or $v$ is a unit in $\Z[i]$.

Let $z\in \Z[i]$ be prime. Since $N(z) = z\bar z > 1$, let $p\in \Z$ be a prime that divides $N(z)$.

  • If $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$, then $p$ is a prime in $\Z[i]$, so $$ \begin{align*} p \mid N(z) = z\bar z &\implies p\mid z \quad \text{or} \quad p\mid \bar z \\ &\implies p\mid z \quad \text{and}\quad p\mid\bar z. \end{align*} $$ But $z$ is prime so irreducible, so $z$ and $p$ are associates.

  • If $p\equiv 1,2\pmod 4$, then $p = a^2+b^2$ is a sum of two squares $a,b\in \Z$ and thus $p = w\bar w = N(w)$ where $w = a+ib\in \Z[i]$ is prime. Then since $$ \begin{align*} w\bar w = p \mid N(z) = z\bar z \end{align*} $$ and $w,\bar w,z,\bar z$ are all primes, $z=w$ or $z=\bar w$. Thus $N(z) = p$.
