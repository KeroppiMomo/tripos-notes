Let $H\leq G$ be the proper subgroup with index $n>1$.

Action of $G$ on $G/H$ by $$ \begin{align*} g*g_1H = g g_1 H \end{align*} $$ gives a homomorphism $ϕ : G\to \Sym(G/H) \isom S_n$. Since $G$ is simple, $\ker(ϕ)$ is either trivial or $G$. But as $H$ is proper, picking some $g\in G\setminus H$ gives $g*H \neq H$, so $g\not\in \ker(ϕ)$. Thus $\ker(ϕ) = {e}$. By the first isomorphism theorem, $G \isom G/\ker(ϕ) \isom \im(ϕ) \leq S_n$.

Observe that $A_n \lhd S_n$ because it has index $2$. By the second isomorphism theorem, $A_n \cap \im(ϕ) \lhd \im(ϕ)$, so by simplicity, $A_n \cap \im(ϕ)$ is either trivial or $\im(ϕ)$. For the former case, $$G \isom \im(ϕ) \isom \im(ϕ) / (A_n \cap \im(ϕ)) \isom (A_n \im(ϕ)) / A_n \leq S_n/A_n \isom C_2$$ which is impossible since $G$ is non-abelian. Therefore $A_n\cap \im(ϕ) = \im(ϕ)$, which means $G\isom \im(ϕ) \leq A_n$.

Finally, $S_1$ and $S_2$ are abelian, and $S_3$ and $S_4$ have no non-abelian simple subgroups.
