$\ker(ϕ) \lhd R$

Since $ϕ$ is a group homomorphism, $\ker(ϕ)$ is a subgroup.

If $a\in \ker(ϕ)$ and $b\in R$, then $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(a\cdot b) = ϕ(a) \cdot ϕ(b) = 0_S \cdot ϕ(b) = 0_S \end{align*} $$ so $a\cdot b\in \ker(ϕ)$. Hence $\ker(ϕ)$ is an ideal.

Ring isomorphism

The first isomorphism theorem for groups has shown that $f$ is well-defined, bijective and a group homomorphism. It remains to show multiplicativity: $$ \begin{align*} f\bigl((r_1 + \ker ϕ) \cdot (r_2 + \ker ϕ)\bigr) ={}& f(r_1 r_2 + \ker ϕ) \\ ={}& ϕ(r_1 r_2) \\ ={}& ϕ(r_1) ϕ(r_2) \\ ={}& f(r_1+\ker ϕ) f(r_2 + \ker ϕ). \end{align*} $$
