Since $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ is finite and abelian, by classification $\mathbb{F}_p^\times \isom C_{d_1} \times C_{d_2} \times \ldots C_{d_m}$ where $1<d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \ldots \mid d_m$. If $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ is not cyclic, then $m>1$ so in particular $C_{d_1} \times C_{d_1}$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$.

All elements in this subgroup has order dividing $d_1$, so they are all roots of the polynomial $X^{d_1} - 1\in \mathbb{F}_p[X]$. But the ring $\mathbb{F}_p$ of integers mod $p$ is an integral domain, so the polynomial can have at most $\deg(X^{d_1} - 1) = d_1$ roots, contradicting with the $d_1^2$ roots we exhibited.
