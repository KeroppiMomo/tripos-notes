Since $V$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{F}$-module, its basis finitely generates the $\mathbb{F}[X]$-module $V_α$ as well (since $\mathbb{F}\leq \mathbb{F}[X]$).

Apply the structure theorem to see (as $\mathbb{F}[X]$-modules) $$ \begin{align*} V_α \isom \mathbb{F}[X] \oplus \ldots \oplus \mathbb{F}[X] \oplus \mathbb{F}[X]/(f_1) \oplus \ldots \oplus \mathbb{F}[X]/(f_r) \end{align*} $$ where $f_i \neq 0$ and $f_i\mid f_{i+1}$. This isomorphism is also valid as $\mathbb{F}$-modules (vector spaces over $\mathbb{F}$), and we see that $V$ is finite-dimensional while $\mathbb{F}[X]$ is infinite-dimensional. Therefore there is no $\mathbb{F}[X]$ components: $$ \begin{align*} V_α \isom \mathbb{F}[X]/(f_1) \oplus \ldots \oplus \mathbb{F}[X]/(f_r). \end{align*} $$

Since $\mathbb{F}$ is a field, WLOG we can take $f_i$ to be monic by multiplying by a unit.

Viewing this as $\mathbb{F}$-modules and writing $f_i = a_{i0} + a_{i 1} X + \ldots + a_{i,(d_i-1)} X^{d_i-1} + X^{d_i}$, the vector space $\mathbb{F}[X]/(f_i)$ has a basis $\set{1, X, X^2, \ldots, X^{d_i-1}}$ (since it generates and is linearly independent). Since the action of $α$ under the isomorphism is multiplying by $X$, in this basis $α(1) = X, α(X) = X^2, \ldots, α(X^{d_i-1}) = X^{d_i} = -a_{i 0} - a_{i 1}X - \ldots a_{i,(d_i -1)} X^{d_i-1}$ so in matrix form, $$ \begin{align*} α|_{\mathbb{F}[X]/(f_i)} = \pmat{0 &&&& -a_{0 i} \\ 1 &&&& -a_{i 1} \\ &1 &&& -a_{i 2} \\ &&\ddots&&\vdots \\ &&&1 & -a_{i,d_i-1}} = c(f_i). \end{align*} $$

Thus combining all bases, $$ \begin{align*} α = \pmat{c(f_1) \\ & \ddots \\ && c(f_r)}. \end{align*} $$
