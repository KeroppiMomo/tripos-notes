Theorem (first isomorphism theorem for groups) open_in_new

If $\phi: G\to H$ is a homomorphism, then $\ker\phi$ $\lhd$ $G$ and $G$ $/$ $\ker\phi$ $\isom$ $\im\phi$.

Proof

Theorem (second isomorphism theorem for groups) open_in_new

Let $H\leq G$ and $K\lhd G$ be groups. Then

  • $HK = \sb{hk}{h\in H, k\in K}$ is a subgroup;

  • $H\cap K \lhd H$; and

  • $HK/K \isom H/H\cap K$.

Proof

  • $H/K$ makes no sense. This theorem says that the two ways of coming up with something similar, namely $HK/K$ (by enlarging $H$) and $H/H\cap K$ (by restricting $K$), are the same.

Theorem (correspondence theorem for groups) open_in_new

Let $N\lhd G$ be groups. The sets $\mathcal{G} = \sb{\text{subgroups }H\leq G}{N\lhd H}$ and $\mathcal{N} = \set{\text{subgroups } S\leq G/N}$ have the "same structure", in the following way:

Define the homomorphism $p: G\to G/N$ by $p(g) = gN$. There exists a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{G} &\xleftrightarrow{\quad} \mathcal{N} \\ H &\shortmid\!\xrightarrow{\quad} p(H) = H/N \\ \sb{g\in G}{gN \in S}= p\inv(S) &\xleftarrow{\quad}\!\shortmid S. \end{align*} $$ Moreover, $N\lhd H\lhd G$ iff $G/H \lhd G/N$.

Proof
