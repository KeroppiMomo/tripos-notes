Definition (integral domain) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a ring.

An element $a\in R$ is called a zero divisor if

  1. $a\neq 0$; and

  2. there exists $b\in R\setminus \set{0}$ such that $ab=0$.

$R$ is called an integral domain if

  1. $R\neq \set{0}$; and

  2. it has no zero divisors, i.e. for all $a,b\in R$, $$ \begin{align*} ab = 0\implies a=0 \quad \text{or} \quad b=0. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (finite integral domain is a field) open_in_new

If $R$ is an integral domain that is finite, then $R$ is a field.

Proof

Definition (field of fractions) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an integral domain.

A field of fractions for $R$ is a field $F$ such that

  1. $R\leq F$ is a subring; and

  2. every $λ\in F$ can be written as $ab\inv$, where $a,b\in R$ and $b\inv$ is the multiplicative inverse in $F$.

Theorem (existence of field of fractions) open_in_new

Every integral domain has a field of fractions.

Proof
