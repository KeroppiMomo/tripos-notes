$A_n$ is generated by 3-cycles

Any element $g\in A_n$ (when viewed in $S_n$) is a product of evenly many transpositions. We can combine the product of two transpositions: $$ \begin{align*} (ab)(ab) ={}& e, \\ (ab)(bc) ={}& (abc), \\ (ab)(cd) ={}& (ab)(bc)(bc)(cd) = (abc)(bcd). \end{align*} $$ as products of 3-cycles.

If $H\lhd A_n$ contains a 3-cycle, then it contains all 3-cycles

It suffices to show that any two 3-cycles in $A_n$ are conjugate in $A_n$.

Let $δ, δ'\in A_n$ be 3-cycles. We know any two 3-cycles are conjugate in $S_n$, so write $$ \begin{align*} δ' = σδσ\inv, \qquad σ\in S_n. \end{align*} $$ If $σ\in A_n$, then we are done.

Otherwise if $σ\in S_n \setminus A_n$, since $n\geq 5$, there exists a transposition $τ$ that is disjoint from the 3-cycle $δ$ (pick any two elements not moved by $δ$). Then $τ$ and $δ$ commute, so $$ \begin{align*} δ' = σττ\invδσ\inv = (στ) δ (στ)\inv \end{align*} $$ where $στ \in A_n$.
