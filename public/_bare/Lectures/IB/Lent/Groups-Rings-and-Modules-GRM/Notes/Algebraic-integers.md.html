Definition (algebraic integer) open_in_new

A complex number $α\in \C$ is called an algebraic integer if it is the root of a monic polynomial in $\Z[X]$.

We write $$ \begin{align*} \Z[α] = \bigcap_{\substack{R\leq \C \\ α\in R}} R, \end{align*} $$ or equivalently, $$ \begin{align*} \Z[α] ={}& \im(e_α), & e_α : \Z[X] &\to \C \\ && f &\mapsto f(α). \end{align*} $$

Proposition (minimal polynomial) open_in_new

Let $α\in \C$ be a algebraic integer.

The ideal $I = \ker($$e_α$$)\lhd \Z[X]$ is principal, generated by a monic and irreducible polynomial $f_α\in \Z[X]$ known as the minimal polynomial.

Proof

Proposition (rational algebraic integer is an integer) open_in_new

Let $α\in \C$ be an algebraic integer.

If $α\in \Q$, then $α\in \Z$.
