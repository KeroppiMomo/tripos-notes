$I$ is prime $\implies$ $R/I$ is an integral domain

Suppose $I\lhd R$ is prime, and let $a+I, b+I \in R/I$ satisfying $(a+I)(b+I) = 0+I$. This means $$ \begin{align*} ab+I = 0+I \implies ab \in I. \end{align*} $$ Since $I$ is prime, either $a\in I$ or $b\in I$, so either $a+I$ or $b+I$ is $0_{R/I}$.

$R/I$ is an integral domain $\implies$ $I$ is prime

Conversely suppose $R/I$ is an integral domain, and let $a,b\in R$ such that $ab\in I$. Then $0_{R/I} = ab + I = (a+I)\cdot (b+I)$, and since $R/I$ is an integral domain, $a+I$ or $b+I$ is $0_{R/I}$, i.e. $a\in I$ or $b\in I$.
