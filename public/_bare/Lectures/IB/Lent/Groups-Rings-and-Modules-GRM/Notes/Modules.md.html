Definition (module) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a ring.

An $R$-module is a quadruple $(M, +, 0_M, \cdot)$ where

  • $(M, +, 0_M)$ is an abelian group; and

  • $\cdot: R\times M \to M$

satisfying, for all $m_1, m_2, m\in M$ and $r_1, r_2, r\in R$,

  1. $(r_1 + r_2) \cdot m = r_1\cdot m + r_2 \cdot m$;

  2. $r\cdot (m_1 + m_2) = r\cdot m_1 + r\cdot m_2$;

  3. $r_1(r_2\cdot m) = (r_1 r_2)\cdot m$;

  4. $1_R \cdot m = m$.

Definition (submodule) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module.

An $R$-submodule $N$ of $M$, denoted as $N\leq M$, is a subgroup of $M$ such that $rn\in N$ for all $r\in R$ and $n\in N$.

Definition (quotient module) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module and $N\leq M$ be an $R$-submodule.

The quotient module is the quotient group $M/N$ equipped with $$ \begin{align*} R \times M/N &\to M/N \\ (r, m + N) &\mapsto rm + N. \end{align*} $$

Definition (module homomorphism, isomorphism) open_in_new

Let $M,N$ be $R$-modules.

A function $ϕ: M\to N$ is an $R$-module homomorphism if it is a group homomorphism such that $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(r\cdot m) = r\cdot ϕ(m) \qquad \forall r\in R, \,m\in M. \end{align*} $$

A bijective homomorphism is called an isomorphism.

Isomorphism theorems for modules open_in_new

Theorem (first isomorphism theorem for modules) open_in_new

Let $ϕ: M\to N$ be a homomorphism of $R$-modules. Then

  1. $\ker(ϕ) \leq M$ is a $R$-submodule;

  2. $\im(ϕ) \leq N$ is a $R$-submodule; and

  3. there is an $R$-module isomorphism $$ \begin{align*} f: \quad M/\ker(ϕ) &\to \im(ϕ) \\ m + \ker(ϕ) &\mapsto ϕ(m). \end{align*} $$

Proof: follows from the first isomorphism theorem for groups.

Theorem (second isomorphism theorem for modules) open_in_new

Let $L,K\leq M$ be $R$-submodules. Then

  1. $L+K$ is an $R$-submodule of $K$; and

  2. $(L+K)/K \isom L/(L\cap K)$.

Proof: follows from the second isomorphism theorem for groups.

Theorem (correspondence theorem for modules) open_in_new

Let $N\leq M$ be submodules. There is a bijection $$ \begin{align*} \set{\text{submodules of $M$ containing $N$}} &\xleftrightarrow{\quad} \set{\text{submodules of $M/N$}}. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (third isomorphism theorem for modules) open_in_new

Let $N\leq L\leq M$ be submodules. Then $$ \begin{align*} ^{\large (M/N)}/_{\large (L/N)} \isom M/L. \end{align*} $$

Proof: follows from the third isomorphism theorem for groups.

Annihilator and submodule generated by element open_in_new

Definition (annihilator) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module.

For an element $m\in M$, its annihilator is $$ \begin{align*} \op{Ann}(m) = \sb{r\in R}{r\cdot m = 0.} \end{align*} $$

For a subset $S\subseteq M$, its annihilator is $$ \begin{align*} \op{Ann}(S) = \sb{r\in R}{r\cdot s = 0 \ \forall s\in S} = \bigcap_{m\in M} \op{Ann}(m). \end{align*} $$

  • $\op{Ann}(S)\lhd R$ is an ideal.

Definition (submodule generated by element) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module.

For an element $m\in M$, the submodule generated by $m$ is $$ \begin{align*} Rm = \sb{r\cdot m}{r\in R} \leq M. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (quotient of annihilator is isomorphic to submodule generated) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module.

For any element $m\in M$, there is an $R$-module isomorphism $R/$$\mathrm{Ann}$$(m) \isom$ $Rm$.

Proof

Definition (finite generation) open_in_new

An $R$-module $M$ is said to be finitely generated if there exists $m_1, m_2, \ldots, m_k\in M$ such that $$ \begin{align*} M = Rm_1 + \ldots + Rm_k = \sb{r_1 m_1 + \ldots + r_k m_k}{r_i\in R}. \end{align*} $$

Lemma (finitely generated iff surjective homomorphism) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module.

$M$ is finitely generated iff there exists a surjective $R$-module homomorphism $R^k \twoheadrightarrow M$.

Proof

Corollary (quotient of finitely generated module is finitely generated) open_in_new

If $M$ is a finitely generated module and $N\leq M$ is a submodule, then $M/N$ is also finitely generated.

Proof

Definition (direct sum of modules) open_in_new

Let $M_1, \ldots, M_k$ be $R$-modules.

Their direct sum $M_1\oplus \ldots \oplus M_k$ is the set $M_1 \times \ldots \times M_k$ with operations $$ \begin{align*} (m_1, \ldots, m_k) + (m_1', \ldots, m_k') ={}& (m_1+m_1', \ldots, m_k + m_k') \\ r\cdot (m_1, \ldots, m_k) ={}& (rm_1, \ldots, rm_k) \end{align*} $$ forming an $R$-module.

Lemma (direct sum of quotient modules) open_in_new

Let $M_1, \ldots, M_k$ be modules, with submodules $N_i\leq M_i$ for each $i$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \bigoplus_{i=1}^k (M_i/N_i) = \left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^k M_i\right)/\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^k N_i\right). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (linear independence in modules) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module and $m_1, \ldots, m_k\in M$.

$\set{m_1, \ldots, m_k}$ is $R$-linearly independent if for any $r_1, \ldots, r_k\in R$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^{k} r_i m_i = 0 \implies r_1 = \ldots = r_k = 0. \end{align*} $$

Definition (free generation, basis) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module.

We say a subset $S\subseteq M$ freely generates $M$ if

  1. $S$ generates $M$, i.e. any $m\in M$ is a finite $R$-linear combination of $S$; and

  2. any function on $S$ to an $R$-module can be extended to an $R$-module homomorphism, i.e. for $R$-module $N$ and function $f: S\to N$, there exists an $R$-module homomorphism $ϕ_f: M\to N$ such that $ϕ_f(s) = f(s)$ for all $s\in S$.

A module $M$ is called free if it is freely generated by some $S\subseteq M$. We call $S$ a basis.

Proposition (equivalent definition of free generation) open_in_new

Let $M$ be an $R$-module and $S = \set{m_1, \ldots, m_k}\subseteq M$ be a finite subset. The following are equivalent:

  1. $S$ generates $M$ freely;

  2. $S$ generates $M$ and $S$ is linearly independent;

  3. every $m\in M$ can be uniquely expressed as $m=r_1 m_1 + \ldots + r_k m_k$ for $r_i\in R$.

Proof

Definition (module of relations) open_in_new

Let $M$ be a finitely generated modules, with generators given by a surjective homomorphism $θ: R^k \twoheadrightarrow M$.

$\ker θ$ is the module of relations for $M$ with respect to $θ$.

$M$ is said to be finitely presented if $\ker θ$ is finitely generated.

Proposition (invariance of dimensions) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a non-zero ring. If $R^n$ $\isom R^m$ as $R$-modules, then $n=m$.

Proof

Definition (elementary operations for matrices over rings) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a ring and $A\in M_{m\times n}(R)$. Write $\v c_i$ and $\v r_i$ to denote the $i$-th column and row.

There are three types of elementary column operations on $A$:

  1. Right multiply $T_{i,j}$, which swaps $\v c_i$ and $\v c_j$;

  2. Right multiply $E_{i,\lambda}$, which replaces $\v c_i$ by $\lambda \v c_i$ (where $λ\in R$ is a unit);

  3. Right multiply $C_{i,j,\lambda}$, which replaces $\v c_j$ by $\v c_j + \lambda\v c_i$ (where $i\neq j$).

There are three types of elementary row operations on $A$:

  1. Left multiply $T_{i,j}$, which swaps $\v r_i$ and $\v r_j$;

  2. Left multiply $E_{i,\lambda}$, which replaces $\v r_i$ by $\lambda \v r_i$ (where $λ\in R$ is a unit);

  3. Left multiply $C_{i,j,\lambda}$, which replaces $\v r_i$ by $\v r_i + \lambda\v r_j$ (where $i\neq j$).

Definition (equivalent matrices) open_in_new

Two $m\times n$ matrices $A, A'$ are said to be equivalent if there exists invertible $m\times m$ matrix $P$ and $n\times n$ matrix $Q$ such that $$ \begin{align*} A' = PAQ. \end{align*} $$

Equivalently, if $A'$ can be obtained from $B$ by a sequence of row and column operations.

Definition (Fitting ideals) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a ring and $A$ be a matrix over $R$.

The $k$-th Fitting ideal $\mathrm{Fit}_k(A)\lhd R$ is the ideal generated by the determinants of all $k\times k$ submatrices of $A$.

Proposition (equivalent matrices have same Fitting ideals) open_in_new

If $A,B$ are equivalent matrices, then their Fitting ideals $\mathrm{Fit}_k(A) = \mathrm{Fit}_k(B)$ agree for all $k$.

Proof

Theorem (Smith normal form) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a Euclidean domain.

Any $m\times n$ matrix over $R$ is equivalent to one of the form $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{d_1 \\ & \ddots \\ && d_r \\ &&&0} \end{align*} $$ where $d_i\neq 0$ and $d_i \mid d_{i+1}$.

The $d_i$ are called the invariant factors of $A$, and they are unique up to associates.

Proof

Lemma (generating set of submodule of tuples of PIDs) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain.

Any submodule of $R^m$ can be generated by $m$ or fewer elements.

Proof

Theorem (basis for submodule of tuples of Euclidean domain) open_in_new

Let $R$ be an Euclidean domain and $N\leq R^m$ be a submodule.

There exists $v_1, \ldots, v_m\in R^m$ such that

  1. $\set{v_1, \ldots, v_m}$ is a basis for $R^m$; and

  2. $\set{d_1 v_1, \ldots, d_r v_r}$ is a basis for $N$, for some $0\leq r\leq m$ and $d_i\in R$ satisfying $d_i \mid d_{i+1}$.

Proposition (Chinese remainder theorem for modules) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a Euclidean domain and $a,b\in R$. If $\mathrm{gcd}(a,b) = 1$, then as $R$-modules, $$ \begin{align*} R/(a) \oplus R/(b) \isom R/(ab). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (primary decomposition theorem) open_in_new

Let $R$ be a Euclidean domain.

For any finitely generated $R$-module $M$, $$ \begin{align*} M \isom R \oplus \ldots \oplus R \oplus R/(p_1^{n_1}) \oplus \ldots \oplus R/(p_k^{n_k}) \end{align*} $$ where $p_i$ are primes.

Proof

Definition (vector space as module over polynomial ring) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $\mathbb{F}$, and fix an endomorphism $α$, i.e. a $V\to V$ linear map.

Define $V_α$ as the $\mathbb{F}[X]$-module $V$ with operation $$ \begin{align*} \mathbb{F}[X] \times V &\to V \\ (f, v) &\mapsto (f(α))(v), \end{align*} $$ where $f(α)$ involves self-composition, scaling and sums.

Theorem (rational canonical form) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space over $\mathbb{F}$, and fix an endomorphism $α$, giving an $\mathbb{F}[X]$-module $V_α$.

If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $$ \begin{align*} V_α \isom \mathbb{F}[X]/(f_1) \oplus \ldots\oplus \mathbb{F}[X]/(f_r) \end{align*} $$ where $f_i\neq 0$ and $f_i\mid f_{i+1}$.

This gives a basis for $V$ where the matrix of $α$ has the block diagonal matrix $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{c(f_1) \\ & \ddots \\ && c(f_r)} \end{align*} $$ where $c(f_i)$ is the companion matrix of $f_i$: if $f = a_0 + a_1 X + \ldots + a_{d-1} X^{d-1} + X^d$, then $$ \begin{align*} c(f) = \pmat{0 &&&& -a_0 \\ 1 &&&& -a_1 \\ &1 &&& -a_2 \\ &&\ddots&&\vdots \\ &&&1 & -a_{d-1}}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (Jordan normal form) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space over $\mathbb{C}$, and fix an endomorphism $α$, giving an $\mathbb{C}[X]$-module $V_α$.

If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $$ \begin{align*} V_α \isom \C[X]/((X-λ_1)^{a_1}) \oplus \ldots \oplus \C[X]/((X - λ_r)^{a_r}) \end{align*} $$ where $λ_i\in \C$ may not be distinct.

This gives a basis for $V$ where the matrix of $α$ is in Jordan canonical form $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{J_{a_1}(λ_1) \\ &\ddots \\ &&J_{a_r}(λ_r)}. \end{align*} $$
