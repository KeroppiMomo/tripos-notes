A ring is a quintuple $(R, +, \cdot, 0_R, 1_R)$ where

  • $R$ is a set,

  • $0_R, 1_R \in R$,

  • $+: R\times R\to R$ is the addition, and

  • $\cdot : R\times R\to R$ is the multiplication

such that

  1. $(R, +, 0_R)$ is an abelian group;

  2. $(R, \cdot, 1_R)$ is a monoid, i.e.

    • $\cdot$ is associative: $a\cdot (b\cdot c) = (a\cdot b) \cdot c$;

    • $1_R$ is the multiplicative identity: $1_R \cdot a = a\cdot 1_R = a$;

  3. multiplication is distributive over addition:

    • $a\cdot (b+c) = a\cdot b + a\cdot c$;

    • $(a+b)\cdot c = a\cdot b + a\cdot c$.


  • In this course, all rings are commutative, i.e. $a\cdot b = b\cdot a$.

  • $r\cdot 0_R = 0_R$. (Derivation)

  • $1_R = 0_R$ iff $R$ is the zero ring $\set{0}$. (Derivation)
