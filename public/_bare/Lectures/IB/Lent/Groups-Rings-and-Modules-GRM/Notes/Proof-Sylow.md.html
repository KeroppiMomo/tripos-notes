Existence of Sylow $p$-subgroup

Let $G$ act on the set of subsets (not subgroups) of $G$ of size $p^a$ $$ \begin{align*} Ω = \sb{X\subseteq G}{|X| = p^a} \end{align*} $$ by $$ \begin{align*} g*X = \sb{gx}{x\in X}. \end{align*} $$ We will find an orbit of size $m$.

Firstly, from combinatorics we get $$ \begin{align*} |Ω| = \pmat{p^a m \\ p^a} = \prod_{i=0}^{p^a-1} \frac{p^a m - i}{p^a - i}. \end{align*} $$ Note that for $0\leq n\leq a$, we have $p^n \mid p^a m-i$ iff $p^n\mid p^a - i$ (since they differ by a multiple of $p^n\mid p^a$), so all factors of $p$ in the numerator cancel out with those in the denominator. Hence $p\not\mid |Ω|$.

We won't use this, but we can do better and say $|Ω| \equiv m \pmod p$. This is a special case of Lucas' theorem, and the following proof is adopted from here. > We will show that $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{pn \\ pm} \equiv \pmat{n \\ m} \pmod p, \qquad n\geq m. \end{align*} $$ Write $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{pn \\ pm} = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1} \prod_{i=0}^{p-1} \frac{p(n-k) - i}{p(m-k)-i} = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1} \Biggl(\frac{n-k}{m-k} \underbrace{\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} \frac{p(n-k)-i}{p(m-k)-i}}_{C_k}\Biggr). \end{align*} $$ The denominators in $C_k$ are not divisible by $p$, so by multiplicative inverses exist. Hence $$ \begin{align*} C_k \equiv \prod_{i=1}^{p-1} \frac{-i}{-i} \equiv 1 \pmod p. \end{align*} $$ Thus $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{pn \\ pm} \equiv \prod_{k=0}^{m-1} \frac{n-k}{m-k} = \pmat{n \\ m} \pmod p. \end{align*} $$

Since orbits partition $Ω$, there exists some orbit $G\cdot T$ where $T\in Ω$ such that $p\not\mid |G\cdot T|$. By orbit-stabiliser, $|G\cdot T| |\mathrm{Stab}_G(T)| = p^a m$. So $p^a \mid |\mathrm{Stab}_G(T)|$.

Furthermore, for each $t\in T$, the coset $\mathrm{Stab}_G(T) \, t\subseteq T$. Also every $t\in T$ lies in a coset $\mathrm{Stab}_G(T) \, t$. Thus the cosets partition $T$, resulting in $|\mathrm{Stab}_G(T)| \mid |T| = p^a$. Hence $|\mathrm{Stab}_G(T)| = p^a$ and $\mathrm{Stab}_G(T)$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$.

Sylow $p$-subgroups are conjugate

We will prove a stronger claim: for all $P\in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ and subgroup $Q\leq G$ of size $p^b$ where $b\leq a$, there exists $g\in G$ such that $gQg\inv \leq P$.

Let $Q$ act on $G/P$ by left multiplication: $$ \begin{align*} q * (gP) = qgP. \end{align*} $$ By orbit-stabiliser, the size of an orbit divides $|Q| = p^b$ and so is either 1 or a positive power of $p$. But $p\not\mid m = |G/P|$, so since orbits partition $G/P$, there exists a size 1 orbit $gP$. This has the property that for all $q\in Q$, we have $qgP = gP$ so $g\inv q g\in P$. Hence $g\inv Q g\leq P$.

$n_p = |G : N_G(P)|$

Let $G$ act on $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ by conjugation. By the above, there is exactly one orbit of size $n_p$. By orbit-stabiliser, $n_p = |G : \mathrm{Stab}_G(P)|$, where $P$ is any Sylow $p$-subgroup and $\mathrm{Stab}_G(P) = \sb{g\in G}{gPg\inv = P} = N_G(P)$.

Then apply Lagrange to see $n_p \mid |G|$.

$n_p \equiv 1 \pmod p$

Pick some $P\in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$. Let $P$ act on $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ by conjugation. Orbit-stabiliser says all orbits have size 1 or some positive power of $p$. As orbits partition $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$, it suffices to show that there is only one orbit of size 1, namely $\set{P}$.

Suppose $\set{Q}$ is a size 1 orbit, where $Q \in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$. This means $hQh\inv \in Q$ for all $h\in P$, and so $P\leq N_G(Q)$ by definition.

Note that since $P\leq N_G(Q) \leq G$ and $p^a$ is the largest power dividing $|G|$, $p^a$ is also the largest power dividing $|N_G(Q)|$, so $P$ and $Q$ are Sylow-$p$ subgroups of $N_G(Q)$, and thus $P = gQg\inv$ for some $g\in N_G(Q)$ by the above. However $Q$ is normal in $N_G(Q)$, so $P=Q$.
