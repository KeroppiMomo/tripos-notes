Check that the inverse works: for $H\mapsto H/N$, $$ \begin{align*} \sb{g\in G}{gN \in H/N} ={}& \sb{g\in G}{gN = hN \text{ for some } h\in H} \\ ={}& \sb{g\in G}{g = hn \text{ for some }h\in H, n\in N} \\ ={}& HN \\ ={}& H \end{align*} $$ since $N\leq H$.

Also if $H\lhd G$, then for all $g\in G$, $h\in H$, we have $ghg\inv\in H$ so $(gN)(hN)(gN)\inv = ghg\inv N\in H/N$ and thus $H/N\lhd G/N$.

Conversely if $G/H\lhd G/N$, then for all $g\in G, h\in H$, there exists some $h'\in H$ such that $ghg\inv N = (gN)(hN)(gN)\inv = h'N$. Then $h'\inv ghg\inv \in N\subseteq H$ and so $ghg\inv \in H$. Hence $H\lhd G$.
