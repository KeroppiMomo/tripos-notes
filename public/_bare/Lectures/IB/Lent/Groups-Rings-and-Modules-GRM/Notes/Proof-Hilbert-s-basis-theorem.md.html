We will show that every ideal of $R[X]$ is finitely generated.

Let $I\lhd R[X]$ be an ideal, and suppose it is not finitely generated. Take $f_1\in I\setminus (0)$ of minimal degree. Since $I\neq (f_1)$, take $f_2 \in I\setminus (f_1)$ of minimal degree. Similarly take $f_3\in I \setminus (f_1, f_2)$ of minimal degree and so on.

Let $a_i$ be the leading coefficient of $f_i$. Since $R$ is Noetherian, the chain $$ \begin{align*} (a_1) \subseteq (a_1, a_2) \subseteq (a_1, a_2, a_3) \subseteq \ldots \end{align*} $$ eventually stabilises, so say $(a_1, a_2, \ldots) = (a_1, \ldots, a_m)$. Then let $a_{m+1} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i b_i$ for some $b_i\in R$.

Now consider the polynomial $$ \begin{align*} g = \sum_{i=1}^{m} b_i \, f_i \, X^{\deg f_{m+1} - \deg f_i} \in (f_1, \ldots, f_m). \end{align*} $$ $g$ has degree $\deg f_{m+1}$ and leading coefficient is $\sum_{i=1}^{m} a_ib_i = a_{m+1}$. Therefore $f_{m+1} - g$ has degree less than $\deg f_{m+1}$, lies in $I$, but does not lie in $(f_1, \ldots, f_m)$. This contradicts with the minimality of $\deg f_{m+1}$.
