The integers $\Z$

  • Euclidean domain with Euclidean function $ϕ(n) = |n|$.

  • Units are $\pm 1$.

  • Primes/irreducibles are the usual prime numbers up to associates.

  • Prime ideals/maximal ideals are $n\Z$ where $n=0$ or prime.

The rationals $\Q$

  • Field of fractions of $\Z$.

$\Z/n\Z$

  • Field if $n$ is prime, not an integral domain otherwise.

Gaussian integers $\Z[i]$

See here.

$\Z[\frac{1+\sqrt{-19}}{2}]$

  • A PID but not an Euclidean domain.

$\Z[\sqrt{-5}]$

  • Units are $\pm 1$.

  • Integral domain as a subring of $\C$.

  • Not a UFD: $$ \begin{align*} 2\times 3 = 6 = (1+\sqrt{-5}) \times (1-\sqrt{-5}). \end{align*} $$

$\Z[X]$

  • UFD since so is $\Z$.

  • Noetherian since so is $\Z$.

  • Not a PID: the ideal $I = (2,X)$ of polynomials with a even constant term is not principal.

$\C[X,Y]$

  • UFD since so is $\C$.

  • Noetherian since so is $\C$.

  • Not a PID: the ideal $I=(X,Y)$ is not principal.

$R[[X]]$

  • $(1-x)$ is a unit: $$ \begin{align*} (1-x)(1+x+x^2 + \ldots) = 1 \end{align*} $$

  • If $R$ is Noetherian, so is $R[[X]]$. (Modification of Hilbert's basis theorem)
