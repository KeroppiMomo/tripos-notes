By the definition of an algebraic integer, $α$ is the root of a monic polynomial $g\in \Z[X]$, so $g\in I$ and thus $I$ is non-zero.

Take $f_α\in I$ to be of minimal degree. By dividing by its content, we can assume $f_α$ is primitive.

Let $h\in I$. In $\Q[X]$, run the Euclidean algorithm to get $$ \begin{align*} h = f_α q + r \qquad (\text{in $\Q[X]$}) \end{align*} $$ where $q,r\in \Q[X]$ and either $r=0$ or $\deg r<\deg f_α$. If $r\neq 0$, "clear denominators" by finding $a,b\in \Z\setminus \set{0}$ such that $aq, br\in \Z[X]$. Then $$ \begin{align*} abh = bf_α\cdot (aq) + a\cdot (br). \end{align*} $$ Plugging in $α$, since $h(α) = 0$ and $f_α(α) = 0$, we have $a\cdot (br)(α) = 0$ and so $(br)(α) = 0$. But then $br\in I$ contradicts with the minimality of $\deg f_α$.

Therefore $r=0$ and hence $f_α \mid h$ in $\Q[X]$. Since $f_α$ is primitive, this implies $f_α\mid h$ in $\Z[X]$. Hence $I = (f_α)$.

To see $f_α$ is irreducible, note by the first isomorphism theorem, $\Z[X]/(f_α) = \Z[X]/\ker(e_α) \isom \im(e_α) \leq \C$. Since $\C$ is an integral domain, so is $\Z[X]/(f_α)$, and thus $(f_α)$ is a prime ideal. Therefore $f_α$ is an irreducible.

Moreover $g\in (f_α)$, so $f_α \mid g$ and so the leading coefficient of $f_α$ is a unit. Hence we can take $f_α$ to be monic.
