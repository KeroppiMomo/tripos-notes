Definition (domain) open_in_new

A domain in $\C$ is an open, non-empty and path-connected subset.

Definition (complex differentiation, holomorphic, entire) open_in_new

Let $f: U\to \C$ be a function on a domain $U$.

  • $f$ is differentiable at $u\in U$ if $$ \begin{align*} f'(u) = \lim_{z\to u} \frac{f(z) - f(u)}{z-u} \end{align*} $$ exists.

  • $f$ is holomorphic at $u\in U$ if there exists $ε>0$ such that $f$ is differentiable at all $z\in B(u,ε)$.

  • $f: \C\to \C$ is entire if it is holomorphic on $\C$.

Proposition (Cauchy-Riemann equation) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq \C$ be a domain and $f: U\to \C$ be a function. Write $f = u + iv$ where $u=\Re f: U\to \R$ and $v=\Im f: U\to \R$.

$f$ is differentiable at $w=c+id$ with $f'(w) = p+iq$ iff $u$ and $v: U\subseteq \R^2\to \R$ are real-differentiable at $(c,d)$ and satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations $$ \begin{align*} u_x = v_y, \qquad -u_y = v_x. \end{align*} $$ In this case, $u_x = v_y = p$ and $-u_y = v_x = q$.

Proof

  • Recall the fact that if $u_x,u_y,v_x,v_y$ are continuous, then $u,v$ are real-differentiable on $U$.

Conformality of holomorphic functions open_in_new

Holomorphic functions are conformal, i.e. angle-preserving:

Suppose $U$ is a domain, $w\in \C$ and $f: U \to \C$ holomorphic with $f'(w)\neq 0$. Take two paths $γ_1, γ_2: [-1, 1] \to U$ with $γ_i(0) = w$, and assume $γ_1,γ_2\in C^1$ with $γ_1'(0),γ_2'(0)\neq 0$.

Note that for $i=1,2$, $$ \begin{align*} \arg\bigl((f\circ γ_i)'(0)\bigr) = \arg\bigl(f'(w) \cdot γ_i'(0)\bigr) = \arg(f'(w)) + \arg(γ_i'(0)) \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \arg\bigl((f\circ γ_2)'(0)\bigr) - \arg\bigl((f\circ γ_1)'(0)\bigr) = \arg(γ_2'(0)) - \arg(γ_1'(0)). \end{align*} $$

Definition (conformal equivalence) open_in_new

Let $U,V$ be domains in $\C$.

A function $f: U\to V$ is called a conformal equivalence if it is bijective, holomorphic and $f'(u)\neq 0$ for all $u\in U$.

$U$ is said to be conformally equivalent to $V$ if such $f$ exists.

  • This is an equivalence relation.

  • In fact if $f$ is injective and holomorphic on $U$, then $f'(z) \neq 0$ on $U$, so that condition is redundant.

Möbius transformations open_in_new

Also see IA Groups.

We can define the Riemann sphere $\hat \C = \C_\infty = \C \cup \set{\infty}$ by identifying $\C$ with $\mathbb{S}^2\setminus \set{0,0,1}\subseteq \R^3$ via the stereographic projection $$ \begin{align*} z\mapsto \left(\frac{z+\overline{z}}{|z|^2 + 1}, \frac{(z-\overline{z})/i}{|z|^2 + 1}, \frac{|z|^2-1}{|z|^2 + 1}\right) \end{align*} $$ and adding the point $\infty$.

A Möbius transformation $$ \begin{align*} z \mapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d}, \qquad ad-bc\neq 0 \end{align*} $$ defines a conformal equivalence $\C\setminus \set{-d/c} \to \C\setminus \set{a/c}$.

We can extend it to a continuous map at $\infty$. By using a Möbius map like $z\mapsto 1/z$ to change coordinate so that $\infty$ is finite, we check that the map is holomorphic and conformal at $\infty$. Thus Möbius transformations are conformal equivalences $\C_\infty \to \C_\infty$.

Consider a sector $S$ bounded by two intersecting lines $L_1$ and $L_2$. The image of $S$ under a Möbius map depends on $μ\inv(\infty)$:

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \in L_1 \cap L_2$, then $f(S)$ is a sector, bounded by lines $f(L_1)$ and $f(L_2)$.

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \in L_1\setminus L_2$, then $f(S)$ is one of the regions bounded by line $f(L_1)$ and circle $f(L_2)$.

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \not\in L_1\cup L_2$, then $f(S)$ is one of the regions bounded by circles $f(L_1)$ and $f(L_2)$.

Definition (simply connected in complex analysis) open_in_new

A subset $U\subseteq \C$ is simply connected if every simple closed curve $U$ is contractible, i.e. for any simple closed curve $γ: \mathbb{S}^1 \to U$, there exists some continuous $\hat γ: \overline{B(0,1)} \to U$ with $\hat γ|_{\mathbb{S}^1} = γ$.

Theorem (Riemann mapping theorem) open_in_new

Let $U$ be a domain in $\C$.

If $U\subset \C$ is proper and $U$ is simply connected, then $U$ is conformally equivalent to the unit disk.

Theorem (complex power series is holomorphic inside radius of convergence) open_in_new

Let $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n(z-a)^n$ be a complex power series with a radius of convergence $R>0$.

$f$ is holomorphic on $B(a,R)$, with $$ \begin{align*} f'(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} nc_n(z-a)^{n-1} \end{align*} $$ which also has a radius of convergence $R$ about $z=a$.

Proof

  • By induction, $f$ has derivatives of all orders on $B(a,R)$ and $f^{(n)}(a) = n!\,c_n$.

  • If $f(z) \equiv 0$ on $B(a,ε)$, then $f(z) \equiv 0$ on $B(a,R)$.

Definition (branch of logarithm) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq C\setminus \set{0}$ be open.

A branch of the logarithm is a continuous function $λ: U\to \C$ such that $\exp$$(λ(z)) = z$ for all $z\in U$.

For $U = \C \setminus \sb{x\in \R}{x\leq 0}$, we have the principal logarithm $\Log: U \to \C$ $$ \begin{align*} \Log(z) = \ln |z| + i \op{Arg}(z) \end{align*} $$ where $\op{Arg}(z) \in (-π, π)$.

Proposition (derivative and power series of principal logarithm) open_in_new

$\Log(z)$ is holomorphic in $C\setminus \sb{x\in \R}{x\leq 0}$ with $$ \begin{align*} \dd{z} \Log(z) = \frac{1}{z}. \end{align*} $$

Moreover if $|z|<1$, then $$ \begin{align*} \Log(1+z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1} z^n}{n}. \end{align*} $$
