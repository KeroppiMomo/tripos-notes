If I walk a dog around a tree while keeping the length of the lead shorter than the distance to the tree, then the dog and I go around the tree the same number of times. Then apply the argument principle.

Define $$ \begin{align*} h(z) = \frac{f(z) + g(z)}{f(z)} = 1+\frac{g(z)}{f(z)}. \end{align*} $$ On $γ$, since $|f(z)| > |g(z)|\geq 0$, $f$ has no zero, so $h$ has no pole. Moreover $|h(z) - 1| = |g(z)|/|f(z)| < 1$, so $h(z) \in B(1,1)$. This implies $h$ has no zero on $γ$, and also $I(h\circ γ; 0) = 0$.

Applying the argument principle, $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \#\text{ zeros of $h$ in $D$} - \#\text{ poles of $h$ in $D$} \\ ={}& \#\text{ zeros of $f+g$ in $D$} - \#\text{ zeros of $f$ in $D$}. \end{align*} $$
