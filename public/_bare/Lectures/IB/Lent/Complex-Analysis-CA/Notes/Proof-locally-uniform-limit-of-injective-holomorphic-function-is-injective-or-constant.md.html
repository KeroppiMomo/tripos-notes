Previously we have shown $f$ is holomorphic.

Suppose towards contradiction $f$ is neither constant nor injective. Let $z_1\neq z_2$ be such that $f(z_1) = f(z_2) = a$.

$U$ is connected and so path-connected, so we can find a path $\tilde γ$ from $z_1$ to $z_2$. Construct a closed curve $γ$ in $U$ which bounds a domain containing $\tilde γ$, so it winds exactly once around $z_1$ and $z_2$. $γ$ can be chosen so that $f(z)\neq a$ for all $z\in γ$ by the principle of isolated zeros.

How exactly? Around each point on $\tilde γ$, choose a disk sufficiently small so that it lies within $U$ and contains at most one preimage of $a$ under $f$. Next, use compactness of $\tilde γ$ to select finitely many such disks that still cover $\tilde γ$. Then it's clear we can construct $γ$ (perhaps using horizontal and vertical segments) that avoids all preimages of $a$ and bounds a domain containing $z_1$ and $z_2$.

Since $f_n\to f$ and $f_n' \to f'$ locally uniformly on $U$, they converge uniformly on the compact set $γ$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_γ \frac{f_n'(z)}{f_n(z) - a} \d z \to \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_γ \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)-a} \d z \qquad \text{as }n\to \infty. \end{align*} $$ By the argument principle, both sides count the number of preimages of $a$ under $f_n$ or $f$ accounting local degrees. Since $f_n$ is injective, it has at most one preimage of $a$, of which the local degree is 1 by the local mapping theorem. Therefore LHS is at most 1. However RHS is at least 2 due to $z_1$ and $z_2$.
