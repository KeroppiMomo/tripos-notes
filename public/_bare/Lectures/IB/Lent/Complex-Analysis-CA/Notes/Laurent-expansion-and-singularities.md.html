Theorem (Laurent expansion) open_in_new

Let $f$ be holomorphic on an annulus $A:= \sb{z\in \C}{r<|z-a|<R}$ for some $a\in \C$ and $0\leq r<R\leq \infty$.

  1. $f$ has a unique convergent expansion about $a$ on $A$ $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n(z-a)^n \end{align*} $$ called the Laurent series of $f$ about $a$.

  2. The Laurent series converges uniformly on some slightly shrunk annulus $\sb{z\in \C}{ρ' < |z-a|<ρ}$ (where $r<ρ'\leq ρ<R$).

  3. The coefficients are given by $$ \begin{align*} c_n = \frac{1}{2πi} \int_{|z| = ρ} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}} \d z \end{align*} $$ for any $r<ρ<R$.

Proof

Definition (isolated singularity) open_in_new

Let $f$ be holomorphic on a domain $U$.

A point $a\in \C\setminus U$ is called an isolated singularity of $f$ if $f$ is holomorphic on a punctured neighbourhood of $a$, i.e. there exists $r>0$ such that $B(a,r)^\times := B(a,r) \setminus \set{a} \subseteq U$.

Definition (removable singularity, pole, essential singularity) open_in_new

Consider a holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ with an isolated singularity at $z=a$. It has a Laurent expansion $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n$ valid on $B(a,r)^\times$.

We say $z=a$ is

  1. a removable singularity if $c_n = 0$ for all $n<0$;

  2. a pole (of order $k$) if there exists $k>0$ such that $c_{-k}\neq 0$ but $c_n=0$ for all $n<-k$; or

  3. an essential singularity if otherwise (i.e. $c_n\neq 0$ for infinitely many $n<0$).

Definition (principal part, residue) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ be holomorphic with an isolated singularity at $z=a$, and write $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n$ as its Laurent series.

  • The principal part of $f$ at $a$ is $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1} c_n(z-a)^n$.

  • The residue of $f$ at $z=a$ is $\Res_{z=a} f(z) := c_{-1}$.

Proposition (limit definition of removable singularity) open_in_new

A holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ has a removable singularity at $z=a$ iff $\lim_{z\to a} (z-a)f(z) = 0$.

Proof

  • This is satisfied if $f$ is continuous at $a$, or is bounded on $B(a,r)$.

Proposition (equivalent definition of pole) open_in_new

Let a holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to\C$ have an isolated singularity at $z=a$. The following are equivalent:

  1. $f$ has a pole of order $k$ at $z=a$;

  2. $f(z) = (z-a)^{-k} g(z)$ on $B(a,r)^\times$ for some holomorphic function $g$ on $B(a,r)$ where $g(a)\neq 0$;

  3. $f(z) = 1/h(z)$ on $B(a,ε)^\times$ for some $0<ε<r$ and holomorphic function $h$ on $B(a,ε)$ having a zero of order $k$ at $z=a$.

Consequently, $f$ has a pole at $z=a$ iff $\lim_{z\to a} |f(z)| = \infty$.

Proof

Proposition (essential singularity iff no limit) open_in_new

A holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ has an essential singularity at $z=a$ iff $|f(z)|$ has no limit (in $\R\cup \set{\infty}$) as $z\to a$.

Proof

Theorem (big Picard theorem) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ be holomorphic with an essential singularity at $a$. Then for all $0<ρ<r$, the image $f(B(a,ρ)^\times) = \C$ or $\C\setminus\set{b}$ for some $b\in \C$.

  • Weaker (but easier to prove) result: the Casorati-Weierstrass theorem.
