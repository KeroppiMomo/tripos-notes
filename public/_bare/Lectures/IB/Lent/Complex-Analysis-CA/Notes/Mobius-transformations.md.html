We can define the Riemann sphere $\hat \C = \C_\infty = \C \cup \set{\infty}$ by identifying $\C$ with $\mathbb{S}^2\setminus \set{0,0,1}\subseteq \R^3$ via the stereographic projection $$ \begin{align*} z\mapsto \left(\frac{z+\overline{z}}{|z|^2 + 1}, \frac{(z-\overline{z})/i}{|z|^2 + 1}, \frac{|z|^2-1}{|z|^2 + 1}\right) \end{align*} $$ and adding the point $\infty$.

A Möbius transformation $$ \begin{align*} z \mapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d}, \qquad ad-bc\neq 0 \end{align*} $$ defines a conformal equivalence $\C\setminus \set{-d/c} \to \C\setminus \set{a/c}$.

We can extend it to a continuous map at $\infty$. By using a Möbius map like $z\mapsto 1/z$ to change coordinate so that $\infty$ is finite, we check that the map is holomorphic and conformal at $\infty$. Thus Möbius transformations are conformal equivalences $\C_\infty \to \C_\infty$.

Consider a sector $S$ bounded by two intersecting lines $L_1$ and $L_2$. The image of $S$ under a Möbius map depends on $μ\inv(\infty)$:

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \in L_1 \cap L_2$, then $f(S)$ is a sector, bounded by lines $f(L_1)$ and $f(L_2)$.

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \in L_1\setminus L_2$, then $f(S)$ is one of the regions bounded by line $f(L_1)$ and circle $f(L_2)$.

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \not\in L_1\cup L_2$, then $f(S)$ is one of the regions bounded by circles $f(L_1)$ and $f(L_2)$.
