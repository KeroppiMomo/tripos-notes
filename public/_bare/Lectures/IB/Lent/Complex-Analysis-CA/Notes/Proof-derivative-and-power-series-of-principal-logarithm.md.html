Derivative

$\Log$ is continuous on $U = \C \setminus \sb{x\in \R}{x\leq 0}$ and $\exp$ is continuous on $\C$. Let $z=\exp(x)$ and $w=\exp(y)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\Log(z) - \Log(w)}{z-w} = \frac{x-y}{\exp(x) - \exp(y)} \to \frac{1}{\exp'(y)} \end{align*} $$ as $x\to y\iff z\to w$. So $\Log$ is holomorphic.

Chain rule gives $$ \begin{align*} \dd{z}(\Log z) = \frac{1}{z}. \end{align*} $$

Power series

Note that $$ \begin{align*} \dd{z}\Log(1+z) = \frac{1}{1+z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n z^n \end{align*} $$ within the radius of convergence $|z|< 1$. By term-by-term differentiation, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n z^n = \dd{z}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}z^n}{n} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \Log(1+z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1} z^n}{n} + \text{const.}. \end{align*} $$ By plugging in $z=0$, we see the constant is 0.
