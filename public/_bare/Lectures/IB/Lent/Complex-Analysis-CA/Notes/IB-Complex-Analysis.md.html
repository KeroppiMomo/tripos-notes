Definition (domain) open_in_new

A domain in $\C$ is an open, non-empty and path-connected subset.

Definition (complex differentiation, holomorphic, entire) open_in_new

Let $f: U\to \C$ be a function on a domain $U$.

  • $f$ is differentiable at $u\in U$ if $$ \begin{align*} f'(u) = \lim_{z\to u} \frac{f(z) - f(u)}{z-u} \end{align*} $$ exists.

  • $f$ is holomorphic at $u\in U$ if there exists $ε>0$ such that $f$ is differentiable at all $z\in B(u,ε)$.

  • $f: \C\to \C$ is entire if it is holomorphic on $\C$.

Proposition (Cauchy-Riemann equation) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq \C$ be a domain and $f: U\to \C$ be a function. Write $f = u + iv$ where $u=\Re f: U\to \R$ and $v=\Im f: U\to \R$.

$f$ is differentiable at $w=c+id$ with $f'(w) = p+iq$ iff $u$ and $v: U\subseteq \R^2\to \R$ are real-differentiable at $(c,d)$ and satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations $$ \begin{align*} u_x = v_y, \qquad -u_y = v_x. \end{align*} $$ In this case, $u_x = v_y = p$ and $-u_y = v_x = q$.

Proof

  • Recall the fact that if $u_x,u_y,v_x,v_y$ are continuous, then $u,v$ are real-differentiable on $U$.

Proposition (vanishing complex derivative implies constant) open_in_new

If $f: U\to \C$ is holomorphic on a domain $U$ and $f'(z) \equiv 0$ on $U$, then $f$ is constant on $U$.

Conformality of holomorphic functions open_in_new

Holomorphic functions are conformal, i.e. angle-preserving:

Suppose $U$ is a domain, $w\in \C$ and $f: U \to \C$ holomorphic with $f'(w)\neq 0$. Take two paths $γ_1, γ_2: [-1, 1] \to U$ with $γ_i(0) = w$, and assume $γ_1,γ_2\in C^1$ with $γ_1'(0),γ_2'(0)\neq 0$.

Note that for $i=1,2$, $$ \begin{align*} \arg\bigl((f\circ γ_i)'(0)\bigr) = \arg\bigl(f'(w) \cdot γ_i'(0)\bigr) = \arg(f'(w)) + \arg(γ_i'(0)) \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \arg\bigl((f\circ γ_2)'(0)\bigr) - \arg\bigl((f\circ γ_1)'(0)\bigr) = \arg(γ_2'(0)) - \arg(γ_1'(0)). \end{align*} $$

Definition (conformal equivalence) open_in_new

Let $U,V$ be domains in $\C$.

A function $f: U\to V$ is called a conformal equivalence if it is bijective, holomorphic and $f'(u)\neq 0$ for all $u\in U$.

$U$ is said to be conformally equivalent to $V$ if such $f$ exists.

  • This is an equivalence relation.

  • We will later show that if $f$ is injective and holomorphic on $U$, then $f'(z) \neq 0$ on $U$, so that condition is redundant.

Möbius transformations open_in_new

Also see IA Groups.

We can define the Riemann sphere $\hat \C = \C_\infty = \C \cup \set{\infty}$ by identifying $\C$ with $\mathbb{S}^2\setminus \set{0,0,1}\subseteq \R^3$ via the stereographic projection $$ \begin{align*} z\mapsto \left(\frac{z+\overline{z}}{|z|^2 + 1}, \frac{(z-\overline{z})/i}{|z|^2 + 1}, \frac{|z|^2-1}{|z|^2 + 1}\right) \end{align*} $$ and adding the point $\infty$.

A Möbius transformation $$ \begin{align*} z \mapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d}, \qquad ad-bc\neq 0 \end{align*} $$ defines a conformal equivalence $\C\setminus \set{-d/c} \to \C\setminus \set{a/c}$.

We can extend it to a continuous map at $\infty$. By using a Möbius map like $z\mapsto 1/z$ to change coordinate so that $\infty$ is finite, we check that the map is holomorphic and conformal at $\infty$. Thus Möbius transformations are conformal equivalences $\C_\infty \to \C_\infty$.

Consider a sector $S$ bounded by two intersecting lines $L_1$ and $L_2$. The image of $S$ under a Möbius map depends on $μ\inv(\infty)$:

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \in L_1 \cap L_2$, then $f(S)$ is a sector, bounded by lines $f(L_1)$ and $f(L_2)$.

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \in L_1\setminus L_2$, then $f(S)$ is one of the regions bounded by line $f(L_1)$ and circle $f(L_2)$.

  • If $μ\inv(\infty) \not\in L_1\cup L_2$, then $f(S)$ is one of the regions bounded by circles $f(L_1)$ and $f(L_2)$.

Definition (simply connected) open_in_new

A subset $U\subseteq \C$ is simply connected if every simple closed curve $U$ is contractible, i.e. for any simple closed curve $γ: \mathbb{S}^1 \to U$, there exists some continuous $\hat γ: \overline{B(0,1)} \to U$ with $\hat γ|_{\mathbb{S}^1} = γ$.

Theorem (Riemann mapping theorem) open_in_new

Let $U$ be a domain in $\C$.

If $U\subset \C$ is proper and $U$ is simply connected, then $U$ is conformally equivalent to the unit disk.

Theorem (complex power series is holomorphic inside radius of convergence) open_in_new

Let $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n(z-a)^n$ be a complex power series with a radius of convergence $R>0$.

$f$ is holomorphic on $B(a,R)$, with $$ \begin{align*} f'(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} nc_n(z-a)^{n-1} \end{align*} $$ which also has a radius of convergence $R$ about $z=a$.

Proof

  • By induction, $f$ has derivatives of all orders on $B(a,R)$ and $f^{(n)}(a) = n!\,c_n$.

  • If $f(z) \equiv 0$ on $B(a,ε)$, then $f(z) \equiv 0$ on $B(a,R)$.

Definition (branch of logarithm) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq C\setminus \set{0}$ be open.

A branch of the logarithm is a continuous function $λ: U\to \C$ such that $\exp$$(λ(z)) = z$ for all $z\in U$.

For $U = \C \setminus \sb{x\in \R}{x\leq 0}$, we have the principal logarithm $\Log: U \to \C$ $$ \begin{align*} \Log(z) = \ln |z| + i \op{Arg}(z) \end{align*} $$ where $\op{Arg}(z) \in (-π, π)$.

Definition (complex integral) open_in_new

Define the integral of a continuous complex-valued function $f: [a,b]\to \C$ as $$ \begin{align*} \int_{a}^{b} f(t) \d t := \int_{a}^{b} \Re(f(t)) \d t + i \int_{a}^{b} \Im(f(t)) \d t. \end{align*} $$

  • $\Re(f(t))$ and $\Im(f(t))$ are integrable since they are continuous.

Proposition (sup-norm bound on complex integral) open_in_new

If $f: [a,b]\to \C$ is continuous, then $$ \begin{align*} \left|\int_{a}^{b} f(t) \d t\right| \overset{(1)}{\leq} \int_{a}^{b} |f(t)| \d t \overset{(2)}{\leq} (b-a) \cdot \sup_{[a,b]} |f|, \end{align*} $$ with equality for (1) iff $\arg(f(t))$ is constant, and equality for (2) iff $f$ is constant.

Proof

In this course, we assume all curves are piecewise $C^1$. We call simple closed curves contours.

Definition (arc length of a complex curve) open_in_new

The arc length of a curve $γ: [a,b]\to \C$ is defined as $$ \begin{align*} \mathrm{length}(γ) = \int_{a}^{b} |γ'(t)| \d t. \end{align*} $$

Definition (integral along curve) open_in_new

Let $U$ be a domain in $\C$, $f: U\to \C$ be a continuous function, and $γ: [a,b]\to U$ be a $C^1$-smooth curve in $U$.

The integral of $f$ along $γ$ is $$ \begin{align*} \int_{γ} f(z) \d z := \int_{a}^{b} f(γ(t))\, γ'(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$

  • Linearity: $\int_{γ} αf(z) + βg(z) \d z = α\int_{γ} f(z) \d z + β\int_{γ} g(z) \d z$.

  • For $a\leq c\leq b$, we have $\int_{γ|_{[a,c]}} f + \int_{γ|_{[c,b]}} f = \int_{γ} f$.

  • $\int_{-γ} f = -\int_{γ} f$ where $(-γ)(t) = γ(-t): [-b,-a]\to U$.

  • Independence of parametrisation: if $ϕ: [a',b'] \to [a,b]$ is $C^1$ with $ϕ(a') = a$ and $ϕ(b') = b$, then by integration by substitution, $$ \begin{align*} \int_{γ\circ ϕ} f = \int_{a'}^{b'} f(γ(ϕ(t))) \, γ'(ϕ(t))\, ϕ'(t) \d t = \int_{a}^{b} f(γ(u))\, γ'(u) \d u = \int_{γ} f. \end{align*} $$

  • If $γ$ is piecewise $C^1$, sum up the integrals of each $C^1$-smooth curve.

Proposition (sup-norm bound on integral along curve) open_in_new

Let $f: U\to \C$ be continuous and $γ: [a,b]\to U$ be a curve. Then $$ \begin{align*} \left|\int_{γ} f(z) \d z\right| \leq \mathrm{length}(γ) \cdot \sup_{γ} |f|. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (starlike domain) open_in_new

A domain $U\subseteq \C$ is said to be starlike if there exists $a_0\in U$ such that for all $w\in U$, the line segment from $a_0$ to $w$ lie inside $U$.

Definition (triangle) open_in_new

A triangle in a domain $U$ is the Euclidean convex hull of three points in $U$, lying wholly in $U$.

Theorem (converse of fundamental theorem of calculus) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq \C$ be a domain and $f$ be continuous on $U$.

If either

  • $\oint_γ f=0$ for every closed curve $γ$ in $U$; or

  • $U$ is starlike and $\oint_{\partial T} f = 0$ for every triangle $T$ in $U$,

then $f$ has an antiderivative, i.e. there exists a holomorphic function $F$ on $U$ such that $F'=f$.

Proof

Theorem (Taylor expansion of holomorphic functions) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)\to \C$ be holomorphic.

$f$ has a power series representation valid on $D(a,r)$ $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n \end{align*} $$ where the coefficients $c_n$ are given by $$ \begin{align*} c_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!} = \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_{|ζ-a|=ρ} \frac{f(ζ)}{(ζ-a)^{n+1}} \d ζ \end{align*} $$ for any $0<ρ<r$.

Proof

  • If $f$ is holomorphic on a domain $U$ and $z\in U$, then for any $B(z,ε)\subseteq U$ we choose, $f$ has a valid power series expansion on the disk. (But this may not be convergent on all of $U$.)

  • Therefore if $f$ is holomorphic, then $f$ is infinitely differentiable and analytic.

Theorem (existence of continuous winding angle) open_in_new

Let $γ: [a,b]\to \C\setminus \set{w}$ be a continuous curve.

Then there exists a continuous function $θ: [a,b]\to \R$ such that $γ(t) = w + r(t) e^{iθ(t)}$ where $r(t) = |γ(t) - w|$.

Moreover $θ$ is unique up to a shift by any multiple of $2π$.

Proof

Definition (winding number) open_in_new

Let $γ: [a,b]\to \C$ be a closed curve and $w\in \C\setminus γ$.

The winding number (or winding index) of $γ$ about $w$ is $$ \begin{align*} I(γ; w) = \frac{θ(b) - θ(a)}{2π}\in \Z \end{align*} $$ where $θ: [a,b]\to \R$ is a continuous polar angle defined here.

Lemma (integral form of the winding number) open_in_new

Let $γ: [a,b]\to γ$ be a closed curve and $w\not\in γ$. Then the winding number is $$ \begin{align*} I(γ; w) = \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_{γ} \frac{\d z}{z - w}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Proposition (winding number is locally constant) open_in_new

If $γ$ is a closed curve and $w\in \C\setminus γ$, then there exists $ε>0$ such that the winding number $I(γ; z)$ is constant on $B(w,ε)$.

Proof

Definition (curve bounding a domain) open_in_new

We say a closed curve $γ$ bounds a domain $U$ if the winding number is $$ \begin{align*} I(γ; w) = \begin{cases} 1, & w\in U, \\ 0, & w\not\in U. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Definition (homologous to zero, simply connected) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq \C$ be a domain.

We say a closed curve $γ$ in $U$ is homologous to zero in $U$ if $γ$ does not wind around any point outside $U$, i.e. $I(γ; w)$ $=0$ for all $w\not\in U$.

We say $U$ is simply connected if every closed curve in $U$ is homologous to zero in $U$.

  • This definition is different to the topological one, but is equivalent due to the Riemann mapping theorem.

Theorem (Laurent expansion) open_in_new

Let $f$ be holomorphic on an annulus $A:= \sb{z\in \C}{r<|z-a|<R}$ for some $a\in \C$ and $0\leq r<R\leq \infty$.

  1. $f$ has a unique convergent expansion about $a$ on $A$ $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n(z-a)^n \end{align*} $$ called the Laurent series of $f$ about $a$.

  2. The Laurent series converges uniformly on some slightly shrunk annulus $\sb{z\in \C}{ρ' < |z-a|<ρ}$ (where $r<ρ'\leq ρ<R$).

  3. The coefficients are given by $$ \begin{align*} c_n = \frac{1}{2πi} \int_{|z| = ρ} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}} \d z \end{align*} $$ for any $r<ρ<R$.

Proof

Definition (isolated singularity) open_in_new

Let $f$ be holomorphic on a domain $U$.

A point $a\in \C\setminus U$ is called an isolated singularity of $f$ if $f$ is holomorphic on a punctured neighbourhood of $a$, i.e. there exists $r>0$ such that $B(a,r)^\times := B(a,r) \setminus \set{a} \subseteq U$.

Definition (removable singularity, pole, essential singularity) open_in_new

Consider a holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ with an isolated singularity at $z=a$. It has a Laurent expansion $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n$ valid on $B(a,r)^\times$.

We say $z=a$ is

  1. a removable singularity if $c_n = 0$ for all $n<0$;

  2. a pole (of order $k$) if there exists $k>0$ such that $c_{-k}\neq 0$ but $c_n=0$ for all $n<-k$; or

  3. an essential singularity if otherwise (i.e. $c_n\neq 0$ for infinitely many $n<0$).

Definition (principal part, residue) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ be holomorphic with an isolated singularity at $z=a$, and write $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n$ as its Laurent series.

  • The principal part of $f$ at $a$ is $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1} c_n(z-a)^n$.

  • The residue of $f$ at $z=a$ is $\Res_{z=a} f(z) := c_{-1}$.

Proposition (limit definition of removable singularity) open_in_new

A holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ has a removable singularity at $z=a$ iff $\lim_{z\to a} (z-a)f(z) = 0$.

Proof

  • This is satisfied if $f$ is continuous at $a$, or is bounded on $B(a,r)$.

Proposition (equivalent definition of pole) open_in_new

Let a holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to\C$ have an isolated singularity at $z=a$. The following are equivalent:

  1. $f$ has a pole of order $k$ at $z=a$;

  2. $f(z) = (z-a)^{-k} g(z)$ on $B(a,r)^\times$ for some holomorphic function $g$ on $B(a,r)$ where $g(a)\neq 0$;

  3. $f(z) = 1/h(z)$ on $B(a,ε)^\times$ for some $0<ε<r$ and holomorphic function $h$ on $B(a,ε)$ having a zero of order $k$ at $z=a$.

Consequently, $f$ has a pole at $z=a$ iff $\lim_{z\to a} |f(z)| = \infty$.

Proof

Proposition (essential singularity iff no limit) open_in_new

A holomorphic function $f: B(a,r)^\times\to \C$ has an essential singularity at $z=a$ iff $|f(z)|$ has no limit (in $\R\cup \set{\infty}$) as $z\to a$.

Proof

Definition (meromorphic) open_in_new

We say $f$ is meromorphic on a domain $U$ if $f$ is holomorphic on $U$ except for a set of isolated singularities which are at worst poles (poles or removable).

Theorem (residue theorem) open_in_new

Let $f$ be meromorphic on a domain $U$ and $γ$ be a closed curve homologous to zero in $U$, not containing any poles of $f$.

Let $a_1, \ldots, a_m$ be the finitely many poles of $f$ that $γ$ winds around (i.e. $I(γ; a_n)$ $\neq 0$). Then $$ \begin{align*} \oint_γ f(z) \d z = 2πi\sum_{n=1}^{m} I(γ; a_n) \Res_{z=a} f(a). \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • This generalises Cauchy's theorem and Cauchy's integral formula.

  • If $γ$ is a closed curve bounding a domain $D$, and $f$ is holomorphic on $D$ and a neighbourhood of $γ$, then $\oint_γ f = 0$ and $f(w) = \frac{1}{2πi}\oint_γ f(z)/(z-w)\d z$ for all $w\in D\setminus γ.$

Applying the residue theorem open_in_new

  • If $f$ has a simple pole at $z=a$, then $\Res_{z=a} f(z) = \lim_{z\to a} (z-a) f(z)$.

  • If $f(z) = g(z)/h(z)$ where $g,h$ are holomorphic at $a$, $g(a)\neq 0$, and $h$ has a simple zero at $a$, then $\Res_{z=a}f(z) = g(a)/h'(a)$.

  • If $f(z) = g(z)/(z-a)^k$ where $g$ is holomorphic at $a$, $g(a)\neq 0$, and $k\in \N$, then $\Res_{z=a} f(z) = g^{(k-1)}(a)/(k-1)!$.

Then $$ \begin{align*} I(f\circ γ; 0) = \frac{1}{2πi}\oint_γ \frac{f'}{f}\d z = (\text{\# zeros of $f$ in $U$}) - (\text{\# poles of $f$ in $U$}). \end{align*} $$ counted with multiplicity (i.e. a zero/pole of order $k$ is counted $k$ times).

Proof

  • $f'/f$ is called the logarithmic derivative, which measures the argument of $f$. The argument principle says $2π(\text{\# zeros} - \text{\# poles})$ is the change in $\arg f(z)$ as $z$ travels $γ$.

  • Apply to $g(z) = f(z) - c$ to see $$ \begin{align*} I(f\circ γ; c) = \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_γ \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)-c} \d z = (\#f\inv(c)) - (\#\text{ poles of $f$}), \end{align*} $$ counted with multiplicity (the local degree for each preimage of $c$).

Definition (local degree) open_in_new

Let $f$ be holomorphic and non-constant on a neighbourhood of $a\in \C$.

The local (mapping) degree of $f$ at $a$ (denoted as $\deg_{z=a} f(a)$) is the order of vanishing of $z\mapsto f(z) - f(a)$ at $z=a$.

Theorem (local mapping theorem) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,R)\to \C$ be holomorphic and non-constant, with a local degree $k$ at $z=a$.

For all $r>0$ sufficiently small, there exists $ε>0$ such that for all $w\in B(f(a),ε)^\times$, the equation $f(z) = w$ has exactly $k$ simple solutions (i.e. each with local degree 1) in $B(a,r)$.

Proof

  • If $k=1$, then $f: B(a,r)\to B(a,ε)$ is a conformal equivalence.

Definition (locally uniform convergence) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq \C$ be open, and $f_n: U\to\C$ be a sequence of functions.

We say $\set{f_n}$ converges locally uniformly on $U$ to a function $f: U\to \C$ if for all $a\in U$, there exists $B(a,r)\subseteq U$ such that $f_n\tto f$ uniformly on $B(a,r)$, i.e. for all $ε>0$, there exists $N\in \N$ such that for all $n>N$, $|f_n(z) - f(z)| < ε$ for all $z\in D(a,r)$.

  • Locally uniform convergence is equivalent to uniform convergence on any compact subset.

Theorem (locally uniform limit preserves holomorphicity) open_in_new

Let $\set{f_n}$ be a sequence on a domain $U$.

If $f_n$ is holomorphic and locally uniformly converges to $f:U\to \C$, then $f$ is holomorphic and $f_n' \to f'$ locally uniformly.
