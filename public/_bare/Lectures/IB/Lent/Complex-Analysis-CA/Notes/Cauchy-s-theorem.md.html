Theorem (fundamental theorem of calculus for holomorphic functions) open_in_new

Let $U$ be a domain and $γ: [a,b]\to U$ be a curve.

If $F: U\to \C$ is holomorphic on $U$, then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{γ} F'(z)\d z = F(γ(b)) - F(γ(a)). \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • Since $F$ is holomorphic, $F'$ is continuous (TODO), so the integral is defined.

  • If $γ$ is closed, then the integral vanishes: $$ \begin{align*} \oint_γ F' = 0. \end{align*} $$

Definition (starlike domain) open_in_new

A domain $U\subseteq \C$ is said to be starlike if there exists $a_0\in U$ such that for all $w\in U$, the line segment from $a_0$ to $w$ lie inside $U$.

Definition (triangle) open_in_new

A triangle in a domain $U$ is the Euclidean convex hull of three points in $U$, lying wholly in $U$.

Theorem (converse of fundamental theorem of calculus) open_in_new

Let $U\subseteq \C$ be a domain and $f$ be continuous on $U$.

If either

  • $\oint_γ f=0$ for every closed curve $γ$ in $U$; or

  • $U$ is starlike and $\oint_{\partial T} f = 0$ for every triangle $T$ in $U$,

then $f$ has an antiderivative, i.e. there exists a holomorphic function $F$ on $U$ such that $F'=f$.

Proof

Theorem (integral of holomorphic function along closed triangle vanishes) open_in_new

Let $U$ be a domain.

If $f: U\to \C$ is either

  • holomorphic on $U$; or

  • continuous on $U$, and holomorphic on $U$ possibly except finitely many points,

then for any triangle $T\subseteq U$, $$ \begin{align*} \oint_{\partial T} f = 0. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Theorem (Cauchy's integral theorem on starlike domain) open_in_new

If $U$ is a starlike domain and $f: U\to \C$ is continuous on $U$ and holomorphic on $U$ except at finitely many points, then for any closed curve $γ$ in $U$, $$ \begin{align*} \oint_γ f = 0. \end{align*} $$

Theorem (Cauchy's integral formula on a disk) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)\to \C$ be holomorphic.

For all $z\in B(a,r)$ and $|z-a|<ρ<r$, $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_{|ζ-a|=ρ} \frac{f(ζ)}{ζ - z} \d ζ. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Corollary (mean value property of holomorphic functions) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)\to \C$ be holomorphic.

For all $0<ρ<r$, $$ \begin{align*} f(a) = \int_{0}^{1} f(a+ρe^{2πit}) \d t, \end{align*} $$ i.e. $f(a)$ takes the average value around the boundary of any disk centred at $a$.

Proof

Corollary (local maximum principle) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)\to \C$ be holomorphic.

If $|f(z)| \leq |f(a)|$ for all $z\in B(a,r)$, then $f$ is constant.

Proof

Theorem (Liouville's theorem) open_in_new

If $f: \C\to \C$ is entire and bounded, then $f$ is constant.

Corollary (fundamental theorem of algebra) open_in_new

Theorem (Taylor expansion of holomorphic functions) open_in_new

Let $f: B(a,r)\to \C$ be holomorphic.

$f$ has a power series representation valid on $D(a,r)$ $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n \end{align*} $$ where the coefficients $c_n$ are given by $$ \begin{align*} c_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!} = \frac{1}{2πi} \oint_{|ζ-a|=ρ} \frac{f(ζ)}{(ζ-a)^{n+1}} \d ζ \end{align*} $$ for any $0<ρ<r$.

Proof

  • If $f$ is holomorphic on a domain $U$ and $z\in U$, then for any $B(z,ε)\subseteq U$ we choose, $f$ has a valid power series expansion on the disk. (But this may not be convergent on all of $U$.)

  • Therefore if $f$ is holomorphic, then $f$ is infinitely differentiable and analytic.

Corollary (Morera's theorem) open_in_new

Let $U$ be a domain.

If $f:U\to \C$ is continuous and $\oint_γ f=0$ for all closed curves $γ$ in $U$, then $f$ is holomorphic on $U$.

Proof
