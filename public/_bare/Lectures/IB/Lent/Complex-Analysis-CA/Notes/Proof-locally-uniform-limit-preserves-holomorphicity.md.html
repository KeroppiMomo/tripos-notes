Fix $a\in U$. By locally uniform convergence, there exists $r>0$ such that $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $\overline{B(a,r)}$.

$f$ is holomorphic

Since $f_n$ is continuous, so is $f$. Given any closed curve $γ$ in $B(a,r)$, using uniform convergence and Cauchy's theorem, $$ \begin{align*} \oint_γ f = \lim_{n\to \infty} \oint_γ f_n = 0. \end{align*} $$ Therefore by Morera's theorem, $f$ is holomorphic on $B(a,r)$.

$f_n'\to f$ locally uniformly

Using the generalised Cauchy's integral formula, $$ \begin{align*} |f'(w) - f_n'(w)| = \frac{1}{2π} \left|\int_{|z-a|=ρ} \frac{f(z) - f_n(z)}{(z-w)^2} \d z\right| \end{align*} $$ for any $0<ρ<r$ and $w\in B(a,ρ)$. For any $ρ<r/2$, we have $$ \begin{align*} |f'(w) - f_n'(w)| \leq \frac{1}{2π} 2πr \frac{1}{(r/2)^2} \sup_{|z-a|=ρ} |f(z) - f_n(z)| \to 0 \qquad \text{as }n\to \infty. \end{align*} $$ This bound does not depend on $w$, so $f_n'\to f$ uniformly on $B(a,r/2)$. Hence $f_n' \to f$ locally uniformly on $U$.
