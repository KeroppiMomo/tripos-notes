Suppose for contradiction that $f(B(a,ρ)) \subseteq \C\setminus B(w,ε)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} g(z) = \frac{1}{f(z) - w} \end{align*} $$ is holomorphic on $B(a,r)^\times$ and is bounded: $|g(z)| < 1/ε$.

To see $z=a$ is also an essential singularity for $g$, as $z\to a$,

  • if $g(z) \to 0$, then $|f(z)|\to \infty$;

  • if $|g(z)|\to \infty$, then $f(z) \to w$;

  • if $g(z)\to L$ finite but non-zero, then $f(z)\to \frac{1}{L}+b$,

but $|f|$ has no limit.

On the other hand, let the Laurent expansion of $g$ be $$ \begin{align*} g(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n. \end{align*} $$ For $n<0$, the coefficient satisfies $$ \begin{align*} |c_n| ={}& \frac{1}{2π}\left|\oint_{|z-a|=δ} g(z) (z-a)^{-n-1} \d z\right| \\ \leq{}& \frac{1}{2π}\cdot 2πδ\cdot \frac{1}{ε}\cdot δ^{-n-1} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{ε} δ^{-n} \end{align*} $$ for any $0<δ<ρ$. Therefore $c_n = 0$ for all $n<0$, which contradicts with $g$ being an essential singularity.
