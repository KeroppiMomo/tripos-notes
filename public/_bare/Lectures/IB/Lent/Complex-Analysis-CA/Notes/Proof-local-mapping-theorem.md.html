Apply the principle of isolated zeros to $f(z) - f(a)$ and $f'(z)$ to see that $f(z) \neq f(a)$ and $f'(z)\neq 0$ on $z\in \overline{B(a,r)}^\times$ for all sufficiently small $r>0$.

Let $γ$ be the curve around $\partial B(a,r)$. Since $f\circ γ$ does not contain $f(a)$ and winding number is locally constant, there exists $ε>0$ such that for all $w\in B(f(a),ε)$, $$ \begin{align*} I(f\circ γ; f(a)) = I(f\circ γ; w). \end{align*} $$ By the argument principle (and noting $f$ is holomorphic), $$ \begin{align*} \text{\# preimages of $f(a)$ under $f$ in $B(a,r)$} = \text{\# preimages of $w$ under $f$ in $B(a,r)$} \end{align*} $$ counted with multiplicity (local degree).

Note $a$ is the only preimage of $f(a)$ in $B(a,r)$ and $f$ has local degree $k$ at $z=a$, so LHS is $k$. Besides all preimages of $w$ are simple because $\dd{z}(f(z) - w)\neq 0$ on $B(a,r)^\times$. Therefore there are $k$ simple preimages of $w$.
