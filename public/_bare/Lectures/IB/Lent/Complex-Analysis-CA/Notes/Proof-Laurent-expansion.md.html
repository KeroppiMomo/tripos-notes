Existence of Laurent series

Fix $w\in A$ and choose $ρ_1$ and $ρ_2$ such that $r<ρ_1<|w-a|<ρ_2<R$.

$f(w)$ expressed in term of integrals

Define two closed curves $γ_0, γ_1$ (positive oriented), bounding two half-annuli defined by $ρ_1$ and $ρ_2$, labelled such that $I(γ_1; w) = 1$ and $I(γ_0; w) = 0$. Note $γ_0$ and $γ_1$ are homologous to zero in $A$, so by the general Cauchy integral formula, $$ \begin{align*} &0 = \frac{1}{2πi}\oint_{γ_0} \frac{f(z)}{z-w} \d z, \qquad f(w) = \frac{1}{2πi}\oint_{γ_1} \frac{f(z)}{z-w} \d z \\ \implies \qquad & f(w) = \frac{1}{2πi}\int_{γ_0 + γ_1} \frac{f(z)}{z-w} \d z. \end{align*} $$

Notice $γ_1 + γ_2 = \partial B(a,ρ_2) - \partial B(a,ρ_1)$, so $$ \begin{align*} f(w) = \frac{1}{2πi}\left(\oint_{|z|=ρ_2} \frac{f(z)}{z-w}\d z - \oint_{|z|=ρ_1} \frac{f(z)}{z-w}\d z\right). \end{align*} $$

Series expansion

On $|z-a|=ρ_1$, we have $|\frac{z-a}{w-a}| < 1$, so $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{z-w} = -\frac{1}{w-a} \frac{1}{1-\frac{z-a}{w-a}} = -\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(z-a)^{n}}{(w-a)^{n+1}}, \tag{1} \end{align*} $$ converging uniformly. Meanwhile on $|z-a|=ρ_2$, we have $|\frac{w-a}{z-a}| < 1$, so $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{z-w} = \frac{1}{z-a} \frac{1}{1-\frac{w-a}{z-a}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(w-a)^{n}}{(z-a)^{n+1}} \tag{2} \end{align*} $$ also uniformly.

Substituting, and swapping $\int$ and $\sum$, $$ \begin{align*} f(w) ={}& \frac{1}{2πi} \left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (w-a)^{n} \oint_{|z|=ρ_2} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}} \d z + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (w-a)^{-(n+1)} \int_{|z|=ρ_1} f(z) \, (z-a)^{n} \d z \right) \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2πi} \left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (w-a)^n \oint_{|z|=ρ_2} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\d z + \sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1} (w-a)^n \oint_{|z|=ρ_1} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}} \d z\right), \end{align*} $$ as required.

Uniform convergence

Pick $r<ρ_1<ρ<ρ'<ρ_2<R$. Then $(1)$ and $(2)$ converges uniformly on $ρ<|z-a|<ρ'$, so the Laurent series converges uniformly also.

Coefficients

Write $$ \begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n(z-a)^n \end{align*} $$ and fix $r<ρ<R$. We have $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2πi} \int_{|z|=ρ} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{m+1}} \d z = \frac{1}{2πi} \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n \int_{|z|=ρ} (z-a)^{n-m-1} \d z. \end{align*} $$ By previous computation, the integral is $2πi$ if $n-m-1=-1$ or zero otherwise. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2πi}\oint_{|z|=ρ} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{m+1}} \d z = c_m. \end{align*} $$
