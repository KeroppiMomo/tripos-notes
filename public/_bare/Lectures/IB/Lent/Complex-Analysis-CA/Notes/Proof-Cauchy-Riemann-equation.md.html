Recall that $u: U\subseteq \R^2\to \R$ is real-differentiable at $(c,d)$ with derivative $Du(c,d)$ if $$ \begin{align*} \lim_{(x,y)\to (c,d)} \frac{u(x,y) - u(c,d) - Du(c,d)(x-c,y-d)}{\sqrt{(x-c)^2 + (y-c)^2}} = 0. \end{align*} $$ Moreover for a function $g: U\to \C$, $$ \begin{align*} \lim_{z\to w} g = 0 \iff \lim_{z\to w} |g| = 0. \end{align*} $$

Now, $f$ is differentiable at $w=c+id$ with $f'(w) = p+iq$ iff $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \lim_{z\to w} \frac{f(z)-f(w)-(p+iq)(z-w)}{z-w} \\ ={}& \lim_{z\to w} \frac{f(z)-f(w)-(p+iq)(z-w)}{|z-w|} \\ ={}& \lim_{(x,y)\to (c,d)} \frac{u(x,y) + iv(x,y) - u(c,d) - iv(c,d) - (p+iq)(x+iy - c-id)}{\sqrt{(x-c)^2 +(y-d)^2}} \\ ={}& \lim_{(x,y)\to (c,d)} \frac{u(x,y) - u(c,d) - \bigl(p(x-c) - q(y-d)\bigr)}{\sqrt{(x-c)^2 + (y-d)^2}} \\ &+ i \lim_{(x,y)\to (c,d)} \frac{v(x,y) - v(c,d) - \bigl(q(x-c) + p(y-d)\bigr)}{\sqrt{(x-c)^2 + (y-d)^2}} \end{align*} $$ which holds iff both real and imaginary parts vanish, i.e. $u,v$ are real-differentiable and the Cauchy-Riemann equations hold.
