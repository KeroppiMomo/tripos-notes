Let $γ$ be a closed curve in $B(a,R)$ and $w\not\in B(a,R)$.

Consider the Möbius map $$ \begin{align*} μ: z \mapsto \frac{z - w}{a-w}. \end{align*} $$ $μ(w)=0$, and $μ(B(a,R)) \subseteq B(1, 1)$ because if $|z-a| < R$, then $|z-a| < |w-a|$. Therefore $μ\circ γ$ lies on the right half plane, so the principal argument $\mathrm{Arg}(μ\circ γ(t)) = \mathrm{Arg}(\frac{γ(t)-w}{a-w})$ is continuous. Since $a-w$ is a constant, we have a continuous definition of argument $θ(t)$ of $γ(t) - w$. Since $γ(0) = γ(1)$, we have $I(γ; w) = \frac{θ(1) - θ(0)}{2π} = 0$.
