Since $X$ is non-empty, pick some $x_0\in X$. Construct a sequence $\set{x_n}$ given by $$ \begin{align*} x_n = f(x_{n-1}). \end{align*} $$ Then for all $n\geq 0$, $$ \begin{align*} d(x_{n}, x_{n-1}) &= d(f(x_{n-1}), f(x_{n-2})) \\ &\leq L\, d(x_{n-1}, x_{n-2}) \\ &= L\, d(f(x_{n-2}), f(x_{n-3})) \\ &\leq L^2 \, d(x_{n-2}, x_{n-3}) \\ &\leq \ldots \\ &\leq L^{n-1}\, d(x_1, x_0). \end{align*} $$ If $m\geq n$, then $$ \begin{align*} d(x_m, x_n) &\leq d(x_{n+1}, x_n) + d(x_{n+2}, x_{n+1}) + \ldots + d(x_{m-1}, x_m) \\ &\leq (L^n + L^{n+1} + \ldots + L^{m-1}) \, d(x_1, x_0) \\ &\leq L^n \left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} L^k\right) \, d(x_1, x_0) \\ &= \frac{L^n}{1-L}d(x_1, x_0) \\ &\to 0 \qquad \text{as }n\to \infty \end{align*} $$ so $\set{x_n}$ is Cauchy. Since $X$ is complete, $x_n$ converges to some $x\in X$. Finally since $f$ is Lipschitz continuous $$ \begin{align*} x = \lim_{n\to \infty} x_{n+1} = \lim_{n\to \infty} f(x_n) = f(x) \end{align*} $$ so $x$ is a fixed point.

Uniqueness

If $x=f(x)$ and $y=f(y)$, then $$ \begin{align*} d(x,y) = d(f(x), f(y)) \leq L\, d(x,y) \end{align*} $$ and since $L<1$, we have $d(x,y) = 0$ so $x=y$.
