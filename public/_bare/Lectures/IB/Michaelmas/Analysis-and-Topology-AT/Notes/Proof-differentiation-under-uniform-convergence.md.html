$f_n$ converges uniformly

We will show that $f_n$ is uniformly Cauchy. Using the mean value inequality, $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{x\in (a,b)^d} \|(f_n-f_m)(x)\| \leq {}& \left\|(f_n-f_m)(x_0)\right\| + \|x-x_0\| \sup_{(a,b)^d} \|Df_n - Df_m\| \\ \leq{} & \|f_n(x_0)-f_m(x_0)\| + \sqrt{n}\,|b-a| \sup_{(a,b)^d} \|Df_n - Df_m\|. \end{align*} $$ Fix some $ε>0$. Since $f_n(x_0)$ converges and $Df_n$ converges uniformly, they are Cauchy, so there exists $N_1, N_2\in \N$ such that $\|f_n(x_0) - f_m(x_0)\| < ε$ for all $n,m>N_1$ and $\sup_{(a,b)^d} \|Df_n - Df_m\| < ε$ for all $n,m>N_2$. Thus for $n,m>\max\set{N_1,N_2}$, $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{(a,b)^d} \|f_n-f_m\| \leq (1+\sqrt{n} \, |b-a|) ε \end{align*} $$ so $f_n$ is uniformly Cauchy and therefore uniformly converges.

$Df_n \tto Df$

Suppose $f_n \tto f$ and $Df_n \tto g$. Fix any $x_1\in (a,b)^d$. We want to show that $g(x_1) = Df(x_1)$.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, for any $j=1,2,\ldots,d$, $$ \begin{align*} f(x_1 + he_j) ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} f_n(x_1 + he_j) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} \left(f_n(x_1) + \int_{x_1^j}^{x_1^j + h} \partial_j f_n(x^1, \ldots, \tilde x^j, \ldots, x^n) \d \tilde x^j \right) \\ ={}& f(x_1) + \int_{x_1^j}^{x_1^j + h} g^j(x^1, \ldots, \tilde x^j, \ldots, x^n) \d \tilde x^j. \end{align*} $$ This first term is given by $f_n\to f$, and the second term is due to $Df_n \tto g$ and the fact that uniform convergence preserves Riemann integration. Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus again, this shows that $\partial_j f(x_1) = g^j(x_1)$.

$Df_n$ is continuous, so $g$ is continuous. Thus $f$ is (continuously) differentiable with $Df(x_1) = g(x_1)$.
