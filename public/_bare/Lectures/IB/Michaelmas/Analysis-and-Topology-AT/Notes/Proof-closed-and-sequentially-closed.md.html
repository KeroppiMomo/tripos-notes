Closed $\implies$ sequentially closed

Use the below fact, or alternatively:

Suppose $A$ is closed but not sequentially closed. Since $A$ is not sequentially closed, there exists a sequence $\set{a_n}_{n=1}^\infty$ where $a_n\in A$ but $a_n \to x\in X\setminus A$.

And since $A$ is closed, $X\setminus A$ is open, so $X\setminus A$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$.

By convergence, $a_n\in X\setminus A$ for sufficiently large $n$, but this contradicts with all $a_n \in A$.

$\scl(A) \subseteq \cl(A)$

Suppose $x\in \scl(A) \setminus \cl(A)$. Then there exists $\set{a_n}$ in $A$ such that $a_n\to x\in X\setminus A$. Hence for all neighbourhood $N$ of $x$, we have $x_n\in N$ for sufficiently large $n$. Therefore $N\cap (A\setminus \set{x})\neq \emptyset$. But then $x$ is an accumulation point and this contradicts with $x\not\in \cl(A)$ $=$ $A \cup A'$.

In metric space: sequentially closed $\implies$ closed

Suppose $A$ is sequentially closed but not closed. Since $A$ is not closed, $X\setminus A$ is not open, so there exists a point $x\in X\setminus A$ such that for any $r>0$, the open ball $B_r(x) \not\subseteq X\setminus A$, which means $B_r(x) \cap A \neq \emptyset$.

Take $r=1/n$ and choose $a_n \in B_r(x) \cap A$. Then $a_n\in A$ and $a_n\to x\in X\setminus A$, which contradicts with sequential closure.
