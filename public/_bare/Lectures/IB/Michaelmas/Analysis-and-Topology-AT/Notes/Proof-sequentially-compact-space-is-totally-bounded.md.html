Let $\epsilon>0$. Suppose $X$ is not totally bounded, i.e. there does not exist a finite $\epsilon$-net of $X$.

Construct a sequence by picking $$ \begin{align*} x_1 &\in X, \\ x_2 &\in X\setminus B_\epsilon(x_1), \\ x_3 &\in X\setminus B_\epsilon(x_1)\setminus B_\epsilon(x_2), \\ \vdots \end{align*} $$ If we cannot pick $x_n$, this means $X = B_\epsilon(x_1) \cup \ldots \cup B_\epsilon(x_{n-1})$, which contradicts with the hypothesis.

Since by construction $d(x_n, x_m) \geq \epsilon$ for all $n\neq m$, no subsequence of $\set{x_n}$ converges. Hence $X$ is not sequentially compact.
