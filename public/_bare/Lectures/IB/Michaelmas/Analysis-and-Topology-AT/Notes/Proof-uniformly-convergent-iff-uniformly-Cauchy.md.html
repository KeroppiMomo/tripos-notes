Uniformly convergent $\implies$ uniformly Cauchy

Let $f: Y\to Z$ such that $f_n \tto f$ as $n\to\infty$. Fix $\epsilon>0$. There exists $N\in\N$ such that $\sup_{x\in Y}d(f_n(x), f(x)) < \epsilon$ for all $n\geq N$.

Therefore for $n, m\geq N$, $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{x\in Y} d(f_n(x), f_m(x)) &\leq \sup_{x\in Y} \Big(d(f_n(x), f(x)) + d(f_m(x), f(x))\Big) \\ &\leq \sup_{x\in Y} d(f_n(x), f(x)) + \sup_{x\in Y} d(f_m(x), f(x)) \\ &< \epsilon + \epsilon = 2\epsilon. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $\set{f_n}$ is uniformly Cauchy.

Uniformly Cauchy $\implies$ uniformly convergent

Suppose $\set{f_n}$ is uniformly Cauchy. For any $\epsilon>0$, there exists $N = N(\epsilon)\in\N$ such that $\sup_{x\in Y} d(f_n(x), f_m(x)) < \epsilon$ for all $n,m\geq N(\epsilon)$.

Then for any $x\in Y$, the sequence $\set{f_n(x)}_{n\geq 1}^\infty$ in $Z$ satisfies that for any $\epsilon>0$, we have $d(f_n(x), f_m(x))$ for all $n,m\geq N(\epsilon)$. Hence $\set{f_n(x)}$ is Cauchy and therefore converges by completeness. Let $f(x)$ be the limit of the sequence $\set{f_n(x)}$.

Fix $\epsilon>0$. Since $\set{f_n}$ is uniformly Cauchy, for $m,n\geq N(\epsilon)$, $$ \begin{align*} d(f_m(x), f_n(x)) < \epsilon. \end{align*} $$ Taking the limit as $n\to\infty$, we obtain for any $m\geq N(\epsilon)$, $$ \begin{align*} d(f_m(x), f(x)) \leq \epsilon < 2\epsilon. \end{align*} $$

Hence $\set{f_n}$ converges uniformly to $f$.
