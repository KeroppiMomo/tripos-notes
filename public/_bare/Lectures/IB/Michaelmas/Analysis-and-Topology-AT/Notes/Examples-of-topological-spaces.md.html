Metric space

Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. $(X,\tau)$ is a topological space with $\tau$ being the metric topology.

  • Clearly it is metrizable.

  • $(\R^n, l^p)$ is second countable: a countable base is $\sb{B_r(x)}{r\in \Q_{>0}, x\in \Q^n}$.

Discrete topology

Let $X$ be any set. $\tau = \mathcal{P}(X) = \set{\text{all subsets of }X}$ is the discrete topology.

  • It is the finest topology.

  • It has a base $\sb{\set{x}}{x\in X}$.

  • If $X$ is uncountable, then $X$ is not second countable.

  • It is metrizable by the discrete metric $$ \begin{align*} d(x,y) = \begin{cases} 1, &x=y, \\ 0, &\text{otherwise} \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ and is therefore Hausdorff. (Derivation)

  • A sequence converges iff it is eventually constant, and the unique limit is the constant.

  • Every function from $X$ to a topological space is continuous.

  • $(X,\tau)$ is compact iff $X$ is finite.

  • If $|X|\geq 2$, then $(X,τ)$ is disconnected and therefore not path-connected.

Indiscrete topology

Let $X$ be any set. $\tau = \set{\emptyset, X}$ is the indiscrete topology.

  • It is the coarsest topology.

  • It is second countable: take the finite base $\mathcal{B}=\tau$.

  • For $|X| \geq 2$, it is not Hausdorff and therefore not metrizable. (Derivation)

  • Every sequence converges to any point.

  • Every function from a topological space to $X$ is continuous, and thus $X$ is path-connected and so connected.

Cofinite topology

Let $X$ be any set. $\tau = \sb{A\subseteq X}{X=\emptyset\text{ or } X\setminus A\text{ is finite}}$ is the cofinite topology.

  • If $X$ is finite, this is the discrete topology.

  • $(X,\tau)$ is second countable $\iff$ $\tau$ is countable $\iff$ $X$ is countable. (Derivation)

  • For $X$ infinite, it is not Hausdorff and therefore not metrizable. (Derivation)
