We will apply the contraction mapping theorem.

  • The solution $y$ that we seek for is in $X=C(I; B_R[y_0])$, which is a complete metric space by the uniform limit theorem.

  • The initial value problem is equivalent to finding a fixed point of the operator $T$ on $X$ given by $$ \begin{align*} Ty(x) = y_0 + \int_{x_0}^{x} f(t,y(t)) \d t. \end{align*} $$

  • To show that $T$ is $X\to X$,

    • first $Ty(x)$ is continuous by the fundamental theorem of calculus.

    • $Ty(x)$ is $I \to B_R[y_0]$ because $$ \begin{align*} \|Ty - y_0\|_\infty ={}& \sup_{x\in I} |Ty(x) - y_0| \\ ={}& \sup_{x\in I} \left|\int_{x_0}^{x} f(t,y(t)) \d t\right| \\ \leq {}& \Big(\sup_{x\in I} |x-x_0|\Big) \left(\sup_{x\in I} \sup_{y\in B_R[y_0]} f(x,y)\right) \\ = {}& \epsilon \|f\|_\infty \\ <{}& R \end{align*} $$ if we pick $\epsilon$ to be sufficiently small ($\epsilon < R/\|f\|_\infty$). To see that $\|f\|_\infty$ is finite, recall that $f(x, y_0)$ is continuous in $x\in [a,b]$ and is therefore bounded, and then $f(x,y)$ is $\tilde L$-Lipschitz in $y\in B_R(y_0)$ and is therefore bounded.

  • To show that $T$ is a contraction, note for any $y,z\in C(I;B_R[y_0])$, $$ \begin{align*} \|Ty - Tz\|_\infty ={}& \sup_{x\in I} |Ty(x) - Tz(x)| \\ ={}& \sup_{x\in I} \left|\int_{x_0}^{x} f(t,y(t)) - f(t,z(t)) \d t\right| \\ \leq {}& \Big(\sup_{x\in I} |x-x_0|\Big)\Big(\sup_{x\in I} \big|f(x,y(x)) - f(x,z(x))\big|\Big) \\ \leq {}& \epsilon \tilde L \sup_{x\in I}\left|y(x) - z(x)\right| \\ ={}& \epsilon \tilde L \|y - z\|_\infty \\ ={}& L\|y-z\|_\infty \end{align*} $$ where $L=\epsilon \tilde L<1$ if $\epsilon$ is sufficiently small ($\epsilon < 1/\tilde L$). Hence $T$ is $L$-Lipschitz with $L<1$.

Therefore we have a unique solution $y\in C(I; B_R[y_0])$.
