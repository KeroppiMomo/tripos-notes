Fix $\epsilon>0$. By uniform continuity of $g$, there exists $\delta_Y>0$ such that for all $y,y_0\in Y$, $$ \begin{align*} d'(y,y_0) < \delta_Y \implies d''(g(y), g(y_0)) < \epsilon. \end{align*} $$ By uniform continuity of $f$, there exists $\delta_X > 0$ such that for all $x,x_0\in X$, $$ \begin{align*} d(x,x_0) < \delta_X &\implies d'(f(x), f(x_0)) < \delta_Y \\ &\implies d''(g(f(x)), g(f(x_0))) < \epsilon. \end{align*} $$
