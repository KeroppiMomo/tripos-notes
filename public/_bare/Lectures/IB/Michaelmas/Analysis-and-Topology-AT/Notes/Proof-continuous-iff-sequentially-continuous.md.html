Continuity $\implies$ sequentially continuous

Suppose $f$ is continuous at $x_0$ and $\set{x_n}$ is a sequence in $X$ where $x_n\to x_0$.

Fix $\epsilon>0$. By continuity, there exists $\delta>0$ such that if $x\in B_\delta(x_0)$, then $f(x)\in B_\epsilon(f(x_0))$. By convergence, there exists $N\in\N$ such that for all $n\geq N$, we have $x_n \in B_{\delta}(x_0)$. Hence for all $n\geq N$, we have $f(x_n) \in B'_\epsilon(f(x_0))$. Thus $f(x_n) \to f(x)$.

Sequentially continuous $\implies$ continuity

Suppose $f$ is not continuous at $x_0$.

This means there exists $\epsilon>0$ such that for all $\delta>0$, there exists $x\in B_\delta(x_0)$ but $f(x) \not\in B'_\epsilon(f(x_0))$.

Take $\delta=1/n$ and let $x_n\in B_{1/n}(x_0)$ such that $f(x_n) \not\in B'_\epsilon(f(x_0))$. Then $x_n \to x_0$ but $f(x_n)\not\to f(x_0)$.

Hence $f$ is not sequentially continuous at $x_0$.
