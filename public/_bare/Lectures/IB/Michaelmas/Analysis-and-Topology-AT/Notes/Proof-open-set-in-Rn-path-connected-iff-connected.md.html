Path-connectedness implies connectedness in general.

Suppose $U$ is non-empty and connected. Pick some $x_0\in U$, and let $S$ be the path-connected component of $x_0$. Further let $E = U \setminus S$.

Note that since $U$ is open, for all $x\in U$ there exists $r>0$ such that $B_r(x) \subseteq U$. Besides, $B_r(x)$ is path-connected.

  • To see $S$ is open, notice for all $x\in S$, any $y\in B_r(x)$ is path-connected to $x$ and $x$ is path-connected to $x_0$, so $y$ is path-connected to $x_0$. Hence $B_r(x) \subseteq S$.

  • To see $E$ is open, notice for all $x\in E$, no $y\in B_r(x)$ is path-connected to $x_0$, since otherwise the fact that $y$ is path-connected to $x$ would imply that $x$ is path-connected to $x_0$. Hence $B_r(x) \subseteq E$.

Thus $S$ and $E$ are open and disjoint with $S\cup E=\emptyset$ and $S\neq \emptyset$. Therefore by connectedness, $E=\emptyset$.
