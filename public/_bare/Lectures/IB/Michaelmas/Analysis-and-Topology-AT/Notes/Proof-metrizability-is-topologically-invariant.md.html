Suppose $(X,\tau)$ is metrizable. This means there exists a metric $d: X\times X \to \R$ such that $\tau$ is induced by $d$. Let $(Y,\rho)$ be homeomorphic to $(X,\tau)$ via $h: X\to Y$. We want to show $\rho$ is also induced by a metric.

Construct a function $d': Y \times Y \to \R$ by $$ \begin{align*} d'(y_1, y_2) = d\big(h\inv(y_1), h\inv(y_2)\big). \end{align*} $$

$d'$ is a metric

  • Symmetry: $d'(y_1, y_2) = d'(y_2, y_1)$ follows from $d(x_1, x_2) = d(x_2, x_1)$.

  • Positive definite:

    • $d'(y_1, y_2) = d(h\inv(y_1), h\inv(y_2)) \geq 0$.

    • $d'(y_1,y_2) = 0$ $\iff$ $d(h\inv(y_1), h\inv(y_2)) = 0$ $\iff$ $h\inv(y_1)=h\inv(y_2)$ $\iff$ $y_1 = y_2$ by bijectivity of $h$.

  • Triangle inequality: $d'(y_1,y_2) + d'(y_2, y_3)$ $=$ $d(h\inv(y_1), h\inv(y_2)) + d(h\inv(y_2), h\inv(y_3))$ $\geq$ $d(h\inv(y_1), h\inv(y_3))$ $=$ $d'(y_1,y_3)$.

$d'$ induces $\rho$

Let $\sigma$ be the topology induced by $d'$. We want to show $\rho = \sigma$.

  • $\sigma\subseteq \rho$: for any ball $B_r'(y_0)$ of $\sigma$, $$ \begin{align*} B_r'(y_0) = h(B_r(h\inv(y_0))). \end{align*} $$ $B_r(h\inv(y_0))$ is open in $\tau$, so by the continuity of $h\inv: (Y,\rho)\to (X,\tau)$, $B_r'(y_0)$ is open in $\rho$. Balls of $\sigma$ form a base, so $\sigma\subseteq \rho$.

  • $\rho\subseteq \sigma$: let $V\in \rho$ be open. $h\inv(V)$ is open in $\tau$ by continuity of $h$ and contains $h\inv(y_0)$, so let $\epsilon>0$ such that $B_\epsilon(h\inv(y_0)) \subseteq h\inv(V)$. Then $B_\epsilon'(y_0) = h(B_\epsilon(h\inv(y_0))) \subseteq V$. Therefore $V$ is open in $\sigma$.
