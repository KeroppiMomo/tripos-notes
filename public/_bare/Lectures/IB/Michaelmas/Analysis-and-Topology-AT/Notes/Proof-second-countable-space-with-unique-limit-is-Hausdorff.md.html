Let $(X,\tau)$ be a second countable topological space which is not Hausdorff. Then there exists $x,y\in X$ such that $x\neq y$ and they cannot be separated by open sets.

Since we have a countable base $\mathcal{B}$, the collections $\sb{B\in \mathcal{B}}{x\in B}$ and $\sb{C\in \mathcal{B}}{y\in C}$ are countable, so enumerate them as $\set{B_1, B_2, \ldots}$ and $\set{C_1, C_2, \ldots}$ respectively.

Define for each $n\in \N$ $$ \begin{align*} X_n = B_1\cap B_2\cap \ldots \cap B_n, \qquad Y_n = C_1 \cap C_2 \cap \ldots \cap C_n, \end{align*} $$ which are finite intersections of open sets and are thus open.

Since $x\in X_n$ and $y\in Y_n$, we have $X_n \cap Y_n \neq \emptyset$, so we can sample a point $z_n\in X_n\cap Y_n$.

Now to see that $z_n\to x$, note that for all neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, there exists some $N\in \N$ such that $x\in X_N \subseteq U$. Then for all $n\geq N$, $$ \begin{align*} z_n \in X_n \subseteq X_N \subseteq U. \end{align*} $$ Thus $z_n\to x$. Similarly $z_n\to y$. Hence $\set{z_n}$ is a sequence with two distinct limits.
