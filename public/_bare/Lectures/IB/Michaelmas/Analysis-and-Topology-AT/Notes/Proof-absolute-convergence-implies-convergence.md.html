For pointwise convergence, use the proof for real sequences on every point on the domain.

We will follow that proof for uniform convergence similarly. Let $g_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i$, and suppose $h_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |f_i|$ converges uniformly.

Fix $ε>0$. Since $h_n$ uniformly converges, it is uniformly Cauchy, so there exists $N\in \N$ such that $\sup_U |h_n - h_m| < ε$ for all $n,m\geq N$. Then for $n>m$, $$ \begin{align*} \sup_{U}|g_n - g_m| = \sup_U \left|\sum_{i=m+1}^{n} f_i\right| \leq \sup_U \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} |f_i| = \sup_U |h_n - h_m| < ε \end{align*} $$ so $g_n$ is uniformly Cauchy and thus uniformly converges as well.
