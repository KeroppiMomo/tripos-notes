It suffices to prove the case for $m=1$.

Let $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(h,k) = f(x_0 + he_i + ke_j) - f(x_0 - he_i) - f(x_0 + ke_j) + f(x_0). \end{align*} $$ Apply the mean value theorem twice, $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(h,k) ={}& k \,\bigl(ϕ(h,0) + \partial_2 ϕ(h,βk)\bigr) \\ ={}& k\,\bigl(\partial_j f(x_0 + he_i + βke_j) - \partial_j f(x_0 + β k e_j)\bigr) \\ ={}& hk\, \partial_{ij}^2 f(x_0 + αhe_i + βke_j) \end{align*} $$ for some $α,β\in (0,1)$.

Then $$ \begin{align*} \partial_{ji}^2 f(x_0) ={}& \lim_{k\to 0} \frac{\partial_i f(x_0 + ke_j) - \partial_i f(x_0)}{k} \\ ={}& \lim_{k\to 0} \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{ϕ(h,k)}{hk} \\ ={}& \lim_{k\to 0} \lim_{h\to 0} \partial_{ij}^2 f(x_0 + αhe_i + βke_j) \\ ={}& \partial_{ij}^2 f(x_0) \end{align*} $$ by continuity.
