If $X$ is countable, then $\tau = \emptyset \cup \sb{X\setminus A}{A\subseteq X\text{ is finite}}$ is countable since the set of finite subsets of a countable set is countable. Therefore we have a countable base $\tau$.

If $X$ is uncountable, then $\tau \supseteq \sb{X\setminus \set{x}}{x\in X}$ is uncountable. Suppose we have a countable base $\mathcal{B}\subset \tau$. Consider $A = \bigcup_{U\in \mathcal{B}} (X\setminus U)$, which is countable since $X\setminus U$ is finite and $\mathcal{B}$ is countable. Therefore $A\subset X$ and let $x\in X\setminus A$. Note that $x\in U$ for all $U\in \mathcal{B}$, which means $X\setminus \set{x}\in \tau$ cannot be written as a union of sets in $\mathcal{B}$.
