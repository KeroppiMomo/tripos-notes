Proof 1: using integration

Using the fundamental theorem of calculus, $$ \begin{align*} \|f(b) - f(a)\| ={}& \left\|\int_{0}^{1} \dd{t} f((1-t)a + tb) \d t\right\| \\ ={}& \left\|\int_{0}^{1} Df((1-t)a + tb)(b-a) \d t\right\| \\ \leq{} & \int_{0}^{1} \|Df((1-t)a + tb) (b-a)\| \d t \\ \leq{} & \int_{0}^{1} \|Df((1-t)a + tb)\|\, \|b-a\| \d t &\text{operator norm}\\ \leq{} & \int_{0}^{1} \sup_U \|Df\| \, \|b-a\| \d t \\ ={}& \sup_U \|Df\| \,\|b-a\|. \end{align*} $$

Proof 2: using mean value theorem

Define $$ \begin{align*} ϕ(t) = f\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr) \cdot \bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr). \end{align*} $$ Then $ϕ = α\circ f\circ β$ where $α: \R^n \to \R$ and $β: [0,1]\to \R^n$ given by $α(x) = x\cdot(f(b) - f(a))$ and $β(t) = (1-t)a + tb$. By the chain rule, $$ \begin{align*} Dϕ|_t(h) ={}& Dα\bigl(f((1-t)a + tb)\bigr) \circ Df\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr) \circ Dβ(t)(h) \\ ={}& \bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr) \cdot Df\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr) (b-a)h \\ \implies ϕ'(t) ={}& \bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr) \cdot Df\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr)(b-a). \end{align*} $$

By the mean value theorem, there exists some $ξ\in (0,1)$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \|f(b) - f(a)\|^2 ={}& f(b) \cdot \bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr) - f(a) \cdot \bigl(f(b) - f(a)\bigr) \\ ={}& ϕ(1) - ϕ(0) \\ ={}& ϕ'(t) \\ ={}& \bigl(f(b)-f(a)\bigr) \cdot Df\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr) (b-a) \\ \leq{} & \|f(b) - f(a)\| \,\|Df\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr) (b-a)\|\| &&\text{Cauchy-Schwarz} \\ \leq{} & \|f(b) - f(a)\| \, \|Df\bigl((1-t)a + tb\bigr)\| \, \|b-a\| \\ \implies \|f(b) - f(a)\| \leq{} & \sup_γ \|Df\|\, \|b-a\|. \end{align*} $$
