Definition (product topology) open_in_new

Let $(X,\tau)$ and $(Y,\rho)$ be topological spaces.

The product topology is the topology on $X\times Y$ which admits $\mathcal{B} = \sb{U\times V}{U\in \tau, V\in \rho}$ as a base.

This can be extended to finite products.

Lemma (projection map is continuous) open_in_new

Let $(X,\tau)$ and $(Y,\rho)$ be topological spaces.

The projection maps $$ \begin{align*} \pi_X: X\times Y&\to X &\pi_Y: X\times Y&\to Y \\ (x,y) &\mapsto x & (x,y) &\mapsto y \end{align*} $$ are continuous relative to the product topology.

Proof

  • The definition of the product topology is the coarsest possible topology such that this lemma is true.

Proposition (continuity in product spaces) open_in_new

Let $(X,\tau)$, $(Y,\rho)$ and $(Z,\sigma)$ be topological spaces.

A function $f: Z\to X\times Y$ is continuous iff both $\pi_X$${}\circ f$ and $\pi_Y \circ f$ are continuous.
