We want to show that $$ \begin{align*} x^5 - x - 1 = 0 \end{align*} $$ has a solution in $\R_{\geq 0}$.

  • Pick some closed interval $I\subseteq \R_{\geq 0}$ with the Euclidean metric. Since $\R_{\geq 0}$ is complete and $I$ is closed, $I$ is complete.

  • Consider the function $$ \begin{align*} f(x) = (x+1)^{1/5}, \quad x\in I. \end{align*} $$ A fixed point of $f$ is the solution to the equation.

  • $f$ is a contraction because $$ \begin{align*} f'(x) = \frac{1}{5(x+1)^{4/5}} \leq \frac{1}{5} \end{align*} $$ so $f$ is $1/5$-Lipschitz on $I$.

  • To have $f: I\to I$, take $I = [0,2]$.

Then applying the contraction mapping theorem, $f$ has a unique fixed point in $[0,2]$.
