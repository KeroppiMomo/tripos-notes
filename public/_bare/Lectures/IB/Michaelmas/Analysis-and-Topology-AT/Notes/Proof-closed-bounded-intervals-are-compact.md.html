Proof for $n=1$

Suppose there exists an open cover $\mathcal{U}$ with no finite subcover for $[a,b]$. Construct sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ iteratively:

Let $a_0 = a, b_0 = b$. By definition, $\mathcal{U}$ has no finite subcover for $[a_0, b_0]$.

Assume inductively that $\mathcal{U}$ has no finite subcover for $[a_n, b_n]$. Let $c_n = (a_n + b_n)/2$. Therefore pick $[a_{n+1}, b_{n+1}]$ be either $[a_n, c_n]$ or $[c_n,b_n]$ such that $\mathcal{U}$ has no finite subcover for $[a_{n+1}, b_{n+1}]$. It cannot be the case that $\mathcal{U}$ has a finite subcover for both since otherwise the union would be a finite subcover for $[a_n, b_n]$.

Since $a_n$ and $b_n$ are monotonic and bounded in $[a,b]$, they have limits, and since $b_{n+1} - a_{n+1} = (b_n - a_n) / 2$, they have a common limit say $x\in [a,b]$.

But $\mathcal{U}$ is a cover for $[a,b]$, so $x\in U$ for some open set $U\in \mathcal{U}$. Let $(x-\epsilon, x+\epsilon) \subseteq U$. For sufficiently large $m$, we have $x-\epsilon < a_m \leq b_m < x+\epsilon$, so $[a_m,b_m] \cap U$ is an open cover for $[a_m,b_m]$, contradicting with the construction.
