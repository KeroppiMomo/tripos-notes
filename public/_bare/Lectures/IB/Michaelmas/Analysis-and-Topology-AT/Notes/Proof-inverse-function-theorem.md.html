Homeomorphism

Let $α = Df(x_0)$. Fix some $y\in B_s(f(x_0))$ for some $s>0$ to be determined. We will apply the Banach fixed-point theorem to seek solution to $y=f(x)$ in $x\in B_r[x_0]$ for some $r>0$ to be determined.

Convert this to finding a fixed point of $$ \begin{align*} ϕ_y(x) = α\inv(y - f(x)) + x. \end{align*} $$

  • To have $ϕ_y$ Lipschitz with $L<1$, we compute its derivative $$ \begin{align*} \|Dϕ_y(x)\| ={}& \|\mathrm{Id} - α\inv \circ Df|_{x}\| \\ ={}& \|α\inv\| \|α - Df|_x\|. \end{align*} $$ Since $Df|_x$ is continuous, $\|α - Df(x)\| \to 0$ as $x\to x_0$, so we can find $r>0$ sufficiently small such that $\|Dϕ_y(x)\| < 1/2$. Then by the mean value inequality, $$ \begin{align*} \|ϕ_y(x_1) - ϕ_y(x_0)\| \leq \sup_{B_r[x_0]}\|Dϕ_y\| \|x_1 - x_0\| < \frac{1}{2}\|x_1 - x_0\| \end{align*} $$ so $ϕ_y: B_r[x_0] \to \R^n$ is $1/2$-Lipschitz.

  • To have $ϕ_y: B_r[x_0] \to B_r[x_0]$, compute $$ \begin{align*} \|ϕ_y(x) - x_0\| ={}& \|ϕ_y(x) - ϕ_y(x_0) + α\inv(y- f(x_0))\| \\ \leq{} & \|ϕ_y(x) - ϕ_y(x_0)\| + \|α\inv\|\, \|y-f(x_0)\| \\ <{}& \frac{1}{2}\|x-x_0\| + \|α\inv\|\, \|y-f(x_0)\| \\ <{}& \frac{r}{2} + \|α\inv\| s. \end{align*} $$ Pick $s < r/(2\|α\inv\|)$ so that $\|ϕ_y(x) - x_0\| < r$.

Since $B_r[x_0]\subseteq \R^n$ is closed, it is complete. Thus there exists a unique solution to $x=ϕ_y(x)$ i.e. $y=f(x)$ on $x\in B_r[x_0]$ as long as $y\in W = B_s(f(x_0))$. Existence implies $f\inv|_W: W \to f\inv(W)$ is well-defined, uniqueness implies it is injective, and the codomain implies it is surjective.

Derivative

First we check that $Df(x)$ is invertible on $x\in f\inv(W)$. Note that $\|Dϕ_y(x)\| < 1/2$, so for any $v\in \R^n$, if $Df(x)(v) = 0$, then $$ \begin{align*} \|v\| = \|v - α\inv\circ Df(x)(v)\| \leq \|\mathrm{Id} - α\inv\circ Df(x)\|\,\|v\| < \frac{1}{2} \|v\| \end{align*} $$ so $v=0$. Hence $Df(x)$ is invertible.

Let $g=f\inv|_W$, $y=f(x)$ and $y+k = f(x+h)$. $$ \begin{align*} k = f(x+h) - f(x) = Df(x)(h) + ε_f(h)\, \|h\| \tag{*} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} g(y+k) - g(y) = h = Df(x)\inv k - \underbrace{\left(Df(x)\inv \frac{ε_f(h)\,\|h\|}{\|k\|}\right)}_{ε_g(k)} \|k\|. \end{align*} $$ To show $ε_g(k)\to 0$ as $k\to 0$, rewrite $(*)$ as $$ \begin{align*} h ={}& Df(x)\inv(k - ε_f(h)\, \|h\|) \\ \|h\| \leq{} & \|Df(x)\inv\| \bigl(\|k\| + \|ε_f(h)\|\, \|h\|\bigr) \\ (\|Df(x)\inv\|\inv - \|ε_f(h)\|)\, \|h\| \leq{} & \|k\|. \end{align*} $$ Since $ε_f(h)\to 0$ as $h\to 0$, $\|ε_f(h)\| < \|Df(x)\inv\|\inv/2$ for sufficiently small $h$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \|ε_g\|(k) \leq \|Df(x)\inv\|\, \|ε_f(h)\|\, (2\|Df(x)\inv\|) \to 0 \end{align*} $$ as $k\to 0$.
