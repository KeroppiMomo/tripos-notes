Let $x_0\in (a,b)$, $y_0 \in \R^n$ and $R>0$. Suppose $$ \begin{align*} f(x,y): [a,b] \times B_R[y_0] \to \R^n \end{align*} $$ be continuous in $x$ and $\tilde L$-Lipschitz in $y$.

Then there exists some $\epsilon>0$ such that the initial value problem $$ \begin{align*} y'(x) = f(x,y(x)), \qquad y(x_0) = y_0 \end{align*} $$ has a unique continuous solution $y: I\to B_R[y_0]$ on the interval $I=[x_0 - \epsilon, x_0 + \epsilon] \subseteq [a,b]$.

Proof

  • This can be strengthened to say if $f$ is smooth, then the solution $y(x)$ depends smoothly on $y_0$ and is smooth in $t$.
