Definition (uniform continuity) open_in_new

Let $(X,d)$ and $(Y,d')$ be metric spaces.

We say a function $f:(X,d)\to (Y,d')$ is uniformly continuous if, equivalently,

  • for all $\epsilon>0$, there exists $\delta=\delta(\epsilon)>0$ such that for all $x,x_0\in X$, if $d(x,x_0) < \delta$, then $d'(f(x), f(x_0)) < \epsilon$;

  • for all $\epsilon>0$, there exists $\delta=\delta(\epsilon)>0$ such that for all $x,x_0\in X$, if $x\in B_\delta(x_0)$, then $f(x) \in B'_\epsilon(f(x_0))$.

Examples of uniform continuity

Lemma (composition preserves uniform continuity) open_in_new

Let $f: (X,d)\to (Y,d')$ and $g: (Y,d')\to (Z,d'')$ be functions between metric spaces.

If $f$ and $g$ are uniformly continuous, then $g\circ f$ is also uniformly continuous.

Definition (uniform equivalence) open_in_new

Let $X$ be a set, and $d$ and $d'$ be metrics on $X$.

We say $(X,d)$ and $(X,d')$ are uniformly equivalent if $\mathrm{id}: (X,d)\to(X,d')$ and $\mathrm{id}': (X,d') \to (X,d)$ are uniformly continuous.
