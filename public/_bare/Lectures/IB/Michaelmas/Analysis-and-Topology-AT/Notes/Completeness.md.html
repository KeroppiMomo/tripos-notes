Definition (Cauchy sequence) open_in_new

Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space.

A sequence $\set{x_n}_{n=1}^\infty$ in $X$ is said to be a Cauchy sequence if for all $\epsilon>0$, there exists $N=N(\epsilon)\in\N$ such that $d(x_n, x_m) < \epsilon$ for all $n,m\geq N$.

Definition (completeness) open_in_new

A metric space $(X,d)$ is said to be complete if every Cauchy sequence on $(X,d)$ converges.

Definition (contraction) open_in_new

Let $(X,d)$ and $(Y,d')$ be metric spaces.

$f: X\to Y$ is said to be a contraction if it is $L$-Lipschitz where $L<1$.

Theorem (Banach fixed-point, contraction mapping) open_in_new

Let $(X,d)$ be a non-empty, complete metric space.

If $f: X\to X$ is a contraction, then it has a unique fixed point, i.e. a point $x\in X$ such that $f(x) = x$.

Proof

  • $X$ has to be complete: $f: \R\setminus \set{0}\to \R\setminus \set{0}$ given by $f(x) = x/2$ has no fixed point.

  • $L$ cannot be $\geq 1$: $f: \R\to\R$ given by $f(x) = x$ has infinitely many fixed points, and $f(x) = x+1$ has no fixed points.
