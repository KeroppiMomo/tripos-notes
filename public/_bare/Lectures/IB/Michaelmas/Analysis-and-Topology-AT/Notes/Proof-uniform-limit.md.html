Fix $x_0\in X$ and $\epsilon>0$. By uniform convergence, there exists some $N\in\N$ such that for all $n\geq N$ and $x\in X$, we have $d(f_n(x), f(x)) < \epsilon$. Thus $d(f_N(x), f(x)) < \epsilon$ for all $x\in X$.

Since $f_N$ is continuous at $x_0$, there exists a neighbourhood $U\subseteq X$ of $x_0$ such that $f_N(U) \subseteq B_ε(f_N(x_0))$.

Therefore, for all $x\in U$, $$ \begin{align*} d(f(x), f(x_0)) &\leq \underbrace{d(f(x), f_N(x))}_{\text{uniform convergence}} + \underbrace{d(f_N(x), f_N(x_0))}_{\text{continuity}} + \underbrace{d(f_N(x_0), f(x_0))}_{\text{uniform convergence}} \\ &< \epsilon + \epsilon + \epsilon = 3\epsilon \end{align*} $$ so $f$ is continuous.
