Define $ϕ^i:[0,1]\to \R$ by $$ \begin{align*} ϕ^i(t) = f^i(x_0 + th). \end{align*} $$ We can compute the derivatives: $$ \begin{align*} & {ϕ^{i}}'(t) k = Dϕ^i(t)(k) = Df^i(x_0+th) \circ h\,\id(k) = k\,Df^i(x_0 + th)(h) \\ \implies & {ϕ^i}'(t) = Df^i(x_0+th)(h), \end{align*} $$ and $$ \begin{align*} &{ϕ^{i}}''(0) k = D\bigl(Df^i(x_0+th)(h)\bigr)(0)(k) = D^2 f^i(x_0)(h) \cdot h\,\id(k) = k\, D^2f^i(x_0)(h)(h) \\ \implies& {ϕ^i}''(0) = D^2 f^i(x_0)(h,h). \end{align*} $$

We have $$ \begin{align*} \lim_{t\to 0} \frac{ϕ^i(t) - ϕ^i(0) - t\,{ϕ^i}'(0) - \frac{1}{2}t^2 \, {ϕ^{i}}''(0)}{t^2} ={}& \lim_{t\to 0} \frac{{ϕ^i}'(t) - {ϕ^i}'(0) - t{ϕ^i}''(0)}{t} &&\text{L'Hopital} \\ ={}& 0 &&\text{definition of derivative}. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \|h\|^2 \lim_{t\to 0} \frac{f^i(x_0+th) - f^i(x_0) - Df^i(x_0)(th) - \frac{1}{2}D^2 f^i(x_0)(th,th)}{\|th\|^2} \\ \implies 0 ={}& \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{f^i(x_0+h) - f^i(x_0) - Df^i(x_0)(h) - \frac{1}{2}D^2 f^i(x_0)(h,h)}{\|h\|^2} \\ \end{align*} $$ as required.
