Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. We say it is

  • bounded if there exists $r>0$ such that $d(x,y) < r$ for all $x,y\in X$; and

  • totally bounded if for every $\epsilon>0$, there exists a finite set $F\subseteq X$ such that $$ \begin{align*} X = \bigcup_{y\in F} B_\epsilon(y), \end{align*} $$ i.e. there is a finite covering of $X$ by open $\epsilon$-balls. We call $F$ an $\epsilon$-net of $X$.
