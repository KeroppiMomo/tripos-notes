We claim that on $X = \R^n$, the $l^p$ norm where $1\leq p\leq \infty$ yield equivalent metrics.

It suffices to show that the topology $\tau_\infty$ generated by $l^\infty$ equals the topology $\tau^p$ generated by $l^p$ for any $1\leq p\leq \infty$. Denote $B^\infty_r(x)$ and $B^p_r(x)$ as open balls centred at $x$ with radius $r$ under $l^\infty$ and $l^p$ metrics respectively.

$\tau_\infty \subseteq \tau_p$

Suppose $U \in \tau_\infty$ and $x \in U$. Since $U$ is open in $l^\infty$, there exists $\epsilon>0$ such that $B_\epsilon^\infty(x) \subseteq U$. For all $y\in B_\epsilon^p(x)$, we have $$ \begin{align*} d_\infty(x,y)^p &= \max_{i\in\set{1,\ldots,n}} |x_i - y_i|^p \\ &\leq \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i-y_i|^p \\ &= d_p(x,y)^p \\ &< \epsilon^p &&y\in B_\epsilon^p(x) \end{align*} $$ so $d_\infty(x,y) < \epsilon$ and therefore $y\in B_\epsilon^\infty(x)$. Hence $B_\epsilon^\infty(x) \subseteq B_\epsilon^p(x) \subseteq U$ so $U\in \tau_p$.

$\tau_p \subseteq \tau_\infty$

Suppose $U\in \tau_p$ and $x\in U$. Let $\epsilon>0$ such that $B_\epsilon^p(x) \subseteq U$. For all $y\in B_{\epsilon'}^\infty(x)$, $$ \begin{align*} d_p(x,y)^p &= \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i - y_i|^p \\ &\leq \sum_{i=1}^n \max_{j\in\set{1,\ldots,n}} |x_j-y_j|^p &&\text{replacing with the maximum} \\ &= n \big(\max_{i\in\set{1,\ldots,n}} |x_i - y_i|\big)^p \\ &= n d_\infty(x,y)^p \\ &< n\epsilon'^p. \end{align*} $$ So by letting $\epsilon' = \epsilon/\sqrt[p]{n} > 0$, we have $d_p(x,y) < \epsilon$, so $B_{\epsilon'}^\infty(x) \subseteq B_\epsilon^p(x) \subseteq U$ and therefore $U\in \tau_\infty$.

You can fit a $B_\epsilon^p$ ball inside a $B_\epsilon^\infty$ ball, or fit a $B_{\epsilon/\sqrt[p]{n}}^\infty$ ball inside a $B_\epsilon^\infty$ ball.

In fact we have showed that $(\R^n, l^p)$ are all Lipschitz equivalent.
