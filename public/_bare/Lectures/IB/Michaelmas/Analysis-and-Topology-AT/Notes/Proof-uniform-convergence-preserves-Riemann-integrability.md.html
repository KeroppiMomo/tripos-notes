Denote $I^\star$ and $I_\star$ as the upper and lower integrals. Let $$ \begin{align*} \Delta_n = \sup_{[a,b]} |f_n - f|. \end{align*} $$ By uniform convergence, $\Delta_n \to 0$ as $n\to \infty$.

Integrability

For any $x\in Y$, we have $|f_n(x) - f(x)| \leq \Delta_n$, so $$ \begin{align*} f_n(x) - \Delta_n \leq f(x) \leq f_n(x) + \Delta_n. \end{align*} $$ Since $\Delta_n\to 0$, there exists an $N\in\N$ such that $\Delta_N < 1$. Then $f_N - 1 \leq f(x) \leq f_N+1$ shows that $f$ is bounded and so $I_\star(f)$ and $I^\star(f)$ exist.

Taking the lower integrals on the first inequality and the upper integrals on the second inequality, $$ \begin{align*} I_\star(f_n - \Delta_n) \leq I_{\star}(f) \leq I^\star(f) \leq I^{\star}(f_n + \Delta_n) \end{align*} $$ but since $f_n \pm \Delta_n$ is Riemann integrable, $$ \begin{align*} \int_{a}^{b} (f_n-\Delta_n) \d x \leq I_\star(f) \leq I^\star(f) \leq \int_{a}^{b} (f_n + \Delta_n) \d x \end{align*} $$ and so $$ \begin{align*} 0 \leq I^\star(f) - I_\star(f) &\leq \int_{a}^{b} (f_n + \Delta_n) \d x - \int_{a}^{b} (f_n-\Delta_n) \d x \\ &= 2(b-a)\Delta_n \\ &\to 0 \qquad \text{as }n\to \infty. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $I^\star(f) = I_\star(f)$ and so $f$ is Riemann integrable.

Integral of limit equals limit of integral

We compute $$ \begin{align*} \left|\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \d x - \int_{a}^{b} f_n(x) \d x\right| &\leq \int_{a}^{b} |f(x) - f_n(x)| \d x \\ &\leq \int_{a}^{b} \Delta_n \d x \\ &= (b-a)\Delta_n \\ &\to 0 \qquad \text{as }n\to \infty. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \int_{a}^{b} f_n(x) \d x \to \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \d x. \end{align*} $$
