Consider the initial value problem $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} Ly = f(t), & t>0, \\ y(0) = \dot y(0) = 0 \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} L = \alpha \dd[2]{t} + \beta \dd{t} + \gamma \end{align*} $$ and $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are constant and $\alpha\neq 0$. WLOG take $\alpha=1$.

Extend $y(t)$ and $f(t)$ into the negative $t$-axis by setting $y(t) = 0 = f(t)$ for $t<0$. $y$ and $\dot y$ are continuous at $t=0$ by the initial conditions, so taking the Fourier transform, $$ \begin{align*} \left((i\omega)^2 + \beta (i\omega) + \gamma\right) \hat y(\omega) = \hat f(\omega). \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \hat y(\omega) ={}& \frac{\hat f(\omega)}{(i\omega)^2 + \beta(i\omega) + \gamma} \\ ={}& \frac{\hat f(\omega)}{(i\omega + \sigma_1) (i\omega + \sigma_2)}. \end{align*} $$ Assume $\sigma_1\neq \sigma_2$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{(i\omega + \sigma_1) (i\omega + \sigma_2)} ={}& \frac{1}{\sigma_2 - \sigma_1}\left(\frac{1}{i\omega+\sigma_1} - \frac{1}{i\omega + \sigma_2}\right) \\ ={}& \mathcal{F}_{t\mapsto \omega}\underbrace{\left[\frac{H(t) \, e^{-\sigma_1 t} - H(t)\, e^{-\sigma_2 t}}{\sigma_2 - \sigma_1}\right]}_{R(t)} \end{align*} $$ where we have used the Fourier transform of the exponential. Hence $$ \begin{align*} \hat y(\omega) ={}& \hat R(\omega) \hat f(\omega). \end{align*} $$ Using the inversion formula, $$ \begin{align*} y(t) ={}& R*f(t) \\ ={}& \int R(t-\tau) \, f(\tau) \d \tau. \end{align*} $$ Since $f(\tau) = 0$ for $\tau<0$ and $R(t) = 0$ for $t<0$, $$ \begin{align*} y(t) ={}& \int_{0}^{t} R(t-\tau)\, f(\tau) \d \tau. \end{align*} $$ We call $R$ the response function for $L$: it is the response from an impulse force $f(t) = \delta(t)$.

Note that $R(t-\tau) \equiv G(t;\tau)$, the Green's function.

Let's investigate the case for $$ \begin{align*} L = \dd[2]{t} + 2\gamma \dd{t} + 1 \end{align*} $$ where $\gamma>0$. The solutions for $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ are $$ \begin{align*} \sigma_1, \sigma_2 = \gamma\pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1}. \end{align*} $$ Note that $\Re(\sigma_i) > 0$.

We have three cases:

  1. If $\gamma>1$, then we have over-damping: the response function is $$ \begin{align*} R(t) = H(t) \, e^{-\gamma t} \, \frac{\sinh (t \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1})}{\sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1}}. \end{align*} $$

  2. If $0<\gamma<1$, then we have under-damping: $$ \begin{align*} R(t) = H(t) \, e^{-\gamma t} \, \frac{\sin(t \sqrt{1-\gamma^2})}{\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}}. \end{align*} $$

  3. If $\gamma=1$, take $\gamma=1+\epsilon$ and set $\epsilon\to 0$. Then we get the critically damped case: $$ \begin{align*} R(t) = H(t) \, t\,e^{-\gamma t}. \end{align*} $$
