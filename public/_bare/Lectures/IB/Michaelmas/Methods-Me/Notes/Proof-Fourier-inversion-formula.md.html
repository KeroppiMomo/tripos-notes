Let $n>0$. Then $$ \begin{align*} & \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-n}^{+n} e^{i\lambda x} \, \hat f(\lambda) \d \lambda \\ ={}&\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-n}^{+n} e^{i\lambda x} \left[\int e^{-i\lambda y} f(y) \d y\right] \d \lambda \\ ={}& \int f(y) \left[\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-n}^{+n} e^{i\lambda (x-y)} \d \lambda\right] \d y &\text{Fubini's theorem} \\ ={}& \int f(y) \times \frac{\sin n(x-y)}{\pi(x-y)} \d y \\ ={}& \int \frac{\sin(ny)}{\pi y} f(x+y) \d y. \end{align*} $$ We use the identity $$ \begin{align*} \int \frac{\sin(ny)}{\pi y} \d y = 1 \end{align*} $$ to see $$ \begin{align*} \left(\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-n}^{+n} e^{i\lambda x} \hat f(\lambda) \d \lambda\right) - f(x) ={}& \int \frac{\sin(n y)}{\pi y}\big(f(x+y) - f(x)\big) \d y \\ ={}& \int \sin(ny) \, F(y; x) \d y \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} F(y; x) = \frac{f(x+y) - f(x)}{\pi y}. \end{align*} $$ If $f$ is smooth, $F$ is also smooth. Hence integrating by parts $$ \begin{align*} \left(\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-n}^{+n} e^{i\lambda x} \hat f(\lambda) \d \lambda\right) - f(x) ={}& \frac{1}{n} \int \cos(ny) \, \dfd{F}{y}(y; x) \d y \\ \to{}& 0 \end{align*} $$ as $n\to\infty$.
