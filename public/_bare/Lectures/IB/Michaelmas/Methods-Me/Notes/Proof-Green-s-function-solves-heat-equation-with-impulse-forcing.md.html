The Fourier transform of the relation we want to show is $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{\hat G}{t} + \kappa |\v \lambda|^2 \hat G = e^{-i\v \lambda\cdot \v y} \, \delta(t-s). \tag{*} \end{align*} $$

The Fourier transform of $G$ is $$ \begin{align*} \hat G(\v \lambda, t; \v y, s) ={}& H(t-s) \, e^{-i\v \lambda\cdot \v y} \hat K_{t-s}(\v \lambda) \\ ={}& H(t-s) \, e^{-i\v \lambda\cdot\v y} \,e^{-\kappa|\v \lambda|^2 (t-s)} \end{align*} $$ so it satisfies $(*)$ for $t\neq s$.

Integrate $(*)$ over $(s-\epsilon, s+\epsilon)$ and set $\epsilon\searrow 0$ to get $$ \begin{align*} \hat G(\v \lambda, t; \v y, s)\big|_{t=s^-}^{t=s^+} = e^{-i\v \lambda\cdot \v y} \end{align*} $$ which is satisfied since $$ \begin{align*} \hat G(\v \lambda, s^+; \v y, s) = e^{-i\v \lambda\cdot \v y}, \qquad \hat G(\v \lambda, s^-; \v y, s) = 0. \end{align*} $$
