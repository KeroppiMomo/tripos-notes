The eigenvalue problem for some fixed $m = 0,1,2,\ldots$ $$ \begin{align*} -\dd{r}\left(r \dfd{y}{r}\right) + \frac{m^2}{r} y &= \lambda r y, &&0<r<1, \\ y(1) &= 0 \end{align*} $$ gives rise under the change of variable $z=r\sqrt{\lambda}$ to Bessel's equation of order $m$ $$ \begin{align*} z^2 R'' + zR' + (z^2 - m^2) R &= 0, &&0 < z < \sqrt{\lambda}, \\ R(\sqrt{\lambda}) &= 0. \end{align*} $$

The only solution which does not blow up as $z\to 0$ is the $m$-th order Bessel function $$ \begin{align*} J_m(z) = \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^m \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n!(n+m)!} \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2n} \end{align*} $$ which has an asymptotic approximation $$ \begin{align*} J_m(z) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi z}} \cos\left(z - \frac{m\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) + O\left(\frac{1}{z^{3/2}}\right) \qquad \text{as }z\to\infty. \end{align*} $$

$J_m(z)$ has infinitely many zeros for $z\in [0,\infty)$, say $j_{m1}, j_{m2}, j_{m3}, \ldots$.

The eigenfunction-eigenvalue pairs are $$ \begin{align*} y_k(r) = J_m(j_{mk} r), \quad \lambda_k = j_{mk}^2 \end{align*} $$ and they are orthogonal: $$ \begin{align*} \int_{0}^{1} J_m(j_{mk} r) J_m(j_{ml} r) r \d r = \frac{\big(J'_m(j_{mk})\big)^2}{2} \delta_{kl}. \end{align*} $$
