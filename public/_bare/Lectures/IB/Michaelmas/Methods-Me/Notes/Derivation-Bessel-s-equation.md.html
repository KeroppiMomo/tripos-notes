$$ \begin{align*} -\dd{r}\left(r\dfd{y}{r}\right) + \frac{m^2}{r} y &= \lambda r y, \quad 0<r<1, \tag{*}\\ \quad y(1)&=0 \end{align*} $$ is a Sturm-Liouville problem with $p = r$, $q=m^2/r$, and $w=r$.

Therefore the eigenfunctions are orthogonal on $V = \sb{y\in C^2[0,1]}{y(1)=0}$ with respsect to $$ \begin{align*} \langle f,g\rangle_w = \int_{0}^{1} f(r)\,g(r)\, r \d r. \end{align*} $$

Note that $\lambda\geq 0$: multiply by $y$ on both sides in $(*)$, and then integrate, $$ \begin{align*} \int_{0}^{1} \Big(\lambda r - \frac{m^2}{r}\Big) y^2 \d r &= -\int_{0}^{1} y \dd{r}(ry') \d r \\ &= -\big[ryy'\big]_0^1 + \int_{0}^{1} r(y')^2 \d r \\ &= \int_{0}^{1} r(y')^2 \d r &y(1)=0 \\ &\geq 0 \end{align*} $$ so $\lambda$ cannot be negative.

Expanding the equation, $$ \begin{align*} -r y'' - y' + \frac{m^2}{r} y - \lambda r y &= 0 \\ r^2 y'' + ry' + (\lambda r^2 - m^2) y &= 0 \end{align*} $$ Taking $z=r\sqrt{\lambda}$ and $R(z) = y(r)$, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} r^2 \frac{R''}{\lambda} + r \frac{R'}{\sqrt{\lambda}} + (z^2-m^2) R &= 0 \\ z^2 R'' + z R' + (z^2 - m^2) R &= 0, \qquad 0<z<\sqrt{\lambda}, \qquad R(\sqrt{\lambda}) = 0. \end{align*} $$

Note that $z=0$ is a regular singular point, so by Fuchs' theorem, we seek for solutions of the form $$ \begin{align*} z\mapsto z^\sigma \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n. \end{align*} $$

Substituting, $$ \begin{align*} 0 &= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left((n+\sigma)(n+\sigma-1)a_n z^{n+\sigma} + (n+\sigma)a_n z^{n+\sigma} + a_n z^{n+\sigma+2} - m^2 a_n z^{n+\sigma}\right) \\ &= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left(\big((n+\sigma)^2 - m^2\big) a_n z^{n+\sigma} + a_n z^{n+\sigma+2}\right) \\ &= (\sigma^2 - m^2) a_0 z^\sigma + ((1+\sigma)^2 - m^2) a_1 z^{\sigma+1} + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \left(\big((n+\sigma)^2 - m^2\big) a_n + a_{n-2}\right) z^{n+\sigma} \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} 0 &= (\sigma^2 - m^2) a_0 &&\implies \sigma=\pm m \\ 0 &= (\sigma+1-m)(\sigma+1+m) a_1 &&\implies a_1 = 0 \\ a_{n-2} &= -((n+\sigma)^2 - m^2) a_n \\ &= -(n+\sigma-m)(n+\sigma+m) a_n. \end{align*} $$ For $\sigma=-m$, setting $n=2m$ gives $a_{2m-2} = 0$, so $a_0=0$ and the whole series vanishes.

Therefore $\sigma=m$, and $$ \begin{align*} a_n &= \frac{(-1)a_{n-2}}{n(n+2m)} \\ &= \frac{(-1)^2 a_{n-4}}{n(n-2)(n+2m)(n-2+2m)} \\ &= \ldots\\ &= \begin{cases} \dfrac{(-1)^{n/2} (m!) a_0}{2^{n} (n/2)! (n/2 + m)!}, & n\text{ even} \\ 0, & n\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Hence the solution is $$ \begin{align*} R(z) = \tilde a_0 \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^m \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!(k+m)!} \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2k} \end{align*} $$
