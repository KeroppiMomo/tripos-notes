Since $f=f(r)$ and $g=g(r)$, we have $\phi = \phi(r,t)$. The wave equation becomes $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{r} \pdd{r}\left(r \pdfd{\phi}{r}\right) = \frac{1}{c^2} \pdfd[2]{\phi}{t}. \end{align*} $$

Try separation of variables $$ \begin{align*} \phi(r,t) = R(r)\, T(t) \end{align*} $$ and we obtain $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{rR} \pdd{r}\left(r R'\right) = \frac{\ddot T}{c^2 T}. \end{align*} $$ $r$ and $t$ are independent, so $$ \begin{align*} -(rR')' = \lambda r R, \qquad \ddot T + c^2 \lambda T = 0. \end{align*} $$

$R$ satisfies $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} -(rR')' = \lambda r R, \qquad 0\leq r < 1, \\ R(1) = 0 \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ which is the Bessel's equation! $R$ has solutions $$ \begin{align*} R_k(r) = J_0(j_{0k}\, r), \qquad \lambda_k = {j_{0k}}^2, \qquad k=1,2,3,\ldots. \end{align*} $$

The equation for $T$ becomes $$ \begin{align*} \ddot T + c^2 {j_{0k}}^2 T = 0 \end{align*} $$ which has solutions $$ \begin{align*} T_{k}(t) = A_k \cos(j_{0k}\, ct) + B_k \sin(j_{0k}\, ct). \end{align*} $$

By superposition $$ \begin{align*} \phi(r,t) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} J_0(j_{0k}\, r) \big(A_k \cos(j_{0k}\, ct) + B_k \sin(j_{0k}\, ct)\big) \end{align*} $$ solves the wave equation and the boundary conditions.

The initial conditions are $$ \begin{align*} f(r) = \phi(r,0) ={}& \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k J_0(j_{0k}\, r) \\ g(r) = \phi_t(r,0) ={}& \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} j_{0k}\, cB_k J_0(j_{0k}\, r) \end{align*} $$ and using the orthogonality relation of Bessel's functions, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} A_k ={}& \frac{2}{J_0'(j_{0k})^2} \int_{0}^{1} f(r)\, J_0(j_{0k}\, r)\, r \d r, \\ B_k ={}& \frac{2}{c\,j_{0k}\,J_0'(j_{0k})^2} \int_{0}^{1} g(r)\, J_0(j_{0k}\, r)\, r \d r. \\ \end{align*} $$
