Consider a smooth function $f: \R\to\C$ where $f(x) = 0$ outside $|x|<L$.

For $|x|<L$, $$ \begin{align*} f(x) = \sum_{n\in \Z} \hat f_n e^{i\pi n x/L} \end{align*} $$ where the Fourier coefficients $\hat f_n$ are given by $$ \begin{align*} \hat f_n ={}& \frac{1}{2L} \int_{-L}^{L} e^{-i\pi nx/L} f(x) \d x \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2L} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\pi nx/L} f(x) \d x \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2L} \hat f\left(\frac{\pi n}{L}\right) \end{align*} $$ and $\hat f$ is the Fourier transform of $f$.

Inverse Fourier transform

Set $\lambda_n = \pi n/L$ and $\delta\lambda = \lambda_{n+1} - \lambda_n = \pi/L$. Then on $|x|<L$, $$ \begin{align*} f(x) ={}& \sum_{n} \frac{1}{2L} \hat f\left(\frac{\pi n}{L}\right) e^{i\pi nx/L} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{n} e^{i\lambda_n x} \,\hat f(\lambda_n)\, \delta\lambda \\ \simeq{}& \frac{1}{2\pi} \int e^{i\lambda x} \hat f(\lambda) \d \lambda &(L\to\infty) \end{align*} $$ which looks like the inverse Fourier transform.

Parseval's theorem

We start from Parseval's theorem for Fourier series $$ \begin{align*} \int_{-L}^{L} |f(x)|^2 \d x ={}& 2L \sum_{n} |\hat f_n|^2 \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2L} \sum_{n} \left|\hat f\left(\frac{\pi n}{L}\right)\right|^2 \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{n} |\hat f(\lambda_n)|^2 \delta\lambda \\ \simeq{}& \frac{1}{2\pi} \int |\hat f(\lambda)|^2 \d \lambda&(L\to\infty) \end{align*} $$ to get Parseval for Fourier transform.
