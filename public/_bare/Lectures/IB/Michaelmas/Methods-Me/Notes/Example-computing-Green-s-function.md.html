Consider the operator $$ \begin{align*} L = \dd[2]{x} + \omega^2. \end{align*} $$ We want to solve, for some fixed $\xi\in (0,1)$, $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} G'' + \omega^2 G = \delta(x-\xi), &0<x<1, \\ G(0,\xi) = 0, \\ G(1,\xi) = 0. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Solving directly

On $x\neq \xi$, $$ \begin{align*} G'' + \omega^2 G = 0, \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} G(x;\xi) = \begin{cases} A(\xi) \sin\omega x + B(\xi) \cos\omega x, &0<x<\xi, \\ C(\xi) \sin\omega(x-1) + D(\xi) \cos\omega (x-1), &\xi<x<1. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ Boundary conditions gives $B(\xi) = D(\xi) = 0$.

Imposing continuity at $x=\xi$, $$ \begin{align*} A(\xi) = C(\xi) \frac{\sin\omega(\xi-1)}{\sin\omega\xi}. \end{align*} $$ The jump condition is $$ \begin{align*} 1 ={}& C(\xi)\, \omega\cos\omega(\xi-1) - A(\xi)\,\omega\cos\omega\xi \\ ={}& \frac{C(\xi)\,\omega}{\sin\omega\xi}\big(\sin\omega\xi \cos\omega(\xi-1) - \cos\omega\xi \sin \omega(\xi-1)\big) \\ ={}&\frac{C(\xi)\,\omega}{\sin\omega\xi} \sin \omega \\ C(\xi) ={}& \frac{\sin\omega\xi}{\omega\sin\omega} \\ A(\xi) ={}& \frac{\sin\omega(\xi-1)}{\omega\sin\omega}. \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} G(x;\xi) = \frac{1}{\omega\sin\omega\xi} \times \begin{cases} \sin\omega x \sin \omega(\xi-1), &0<x<\xi, \\ \sin\omega\xi \sin\omega(x-1), &\xi<x<1. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Using linearly independent solutions

Consider $$ \begin{align*} \dfd[2]{y}{x} + \omega^2 y = 0, \qquad 0<x<1. \end{align*} $$ Under the boundary condition $y(0) = 0$, we have a solution $$ \begin{align*} y_1(x) = \sin\omega x \end{align*} $$ and under $y(1) = 0$ instead, we have a solution $$ \begin{align*} y_2(x) = \sin \omega(x-1). \end{align*} $$ Compute $$ \begin{align*} W(y_1,y_2)(\xi) ={}& y_1(\xi) y_2'(\xi) - y_1'(\xi) y_2(\xi) \\ ={}& \omega \sin \omega x \cos \omega(\xi-1) - \omega\cos \omega x \sin \omega(\xi-1) \\ ={}& \omega\sin\omega. \end{align*} $$

This is independent of $\xi$, which is expected since $L$ is a Sturm-Liouville operator with $p(x) = -1$.

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} G(x;\xi) = \frac{1}{\omega\sin\omega} \begin{cases} \sin\omega x \sin \omega(\xi-1), &0<x<\xi, \\ \sin\omega\xi \sin \omega(x-1), &\xi<x<1. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$
