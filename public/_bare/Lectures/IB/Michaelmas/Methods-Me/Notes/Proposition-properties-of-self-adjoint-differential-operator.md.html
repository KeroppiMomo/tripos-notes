Let $L$ be a self-adjoint differential operator in $(V, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_w)$.

  1. The eigenvalues of $L$ are real.

  2. The eigenfunctions with different eigenvalues are orthogonal ($f$ and $g$ are orthogonal iff $\langle f,g\rangle_w = 0$).

  3. The set of eigenfucntions is countable and complete, i.e. the eigenfunctions form a list $\set{y_n}_{n=1}^\infty$, and for each $f\in V$, we can write $$ \begin{align*} f(x) &= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \hat f_n y_n(x) &&\text{where} & \hat f_n = \frac{\langle f, y_n\rangle_w}{\langle y_n, y_n\rangle_w}. \end{align*} $$ We call $\set{\hat f_n}$ the generalised Fourier coefficients.
