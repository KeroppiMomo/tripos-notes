Throughout this section, write $\int \equiv \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}$. Assume all functions are sufficiently nice.

Definition (Fourier transform) open_in_new

Let $f: \R\to\C$ be an integrable function.

Define its Fourier transform $\hat f: \R\to\C$ by $$ \begin{align*} \hat f(\lambda) = \int_{\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\lambda x} f(x) \d x. \end{align*} $$ We write $\mathcal{F}[f] = \hat f$, or $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}[f(x)] = \hat f(\lambda)$.

Proposition (properties of Fourier transform) open_in_new

Let $f:\R\to\C$.

  1. Derivatives and powers:

    • $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}$$\left[\left(\dd{x}\right)^k f(x)\right] = (i\lambda)^k \hat f(\lambda)$;

    • $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}\left[x^k f(x)\right] = \left(i \dd{\lambda}\right)^k \hat f(\lambda)$.

  2. Translation and exponentials:

    • $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}\left[f(x-a)\right] = e^{-i\lambda a} \hat f(\lambda)$;

    • $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}\left[e^{-iax}f(x)\right] = \hat f(\lambda+a)$.

Proof

Proposition (Fourier inversion formula) open_in_new

Let $f:\R\to\C$. We can recover $f$ from $\hat f$ via the inversion formula $$ \begin{align*} f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int e^{i\lambda x}\hat f(\lambda) \d \lambda. \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • Applying this to $f(x)=\delta(x-y)$ gives $$ \begin{align*} \delta(x-y) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int e^{i\lambda(x-y)} \d \lambda. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (Parseval's theorem for Fourier transform) open_in_new

Let $f,g:\R\to\C$. $$ \begin{align*} \int f(x) \,\overline{g(x)} \d x = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int \hat f(\lambda)\, \overline{\hat g(\lambda)} \d \lambda. \end{align*} $$

In particular, $$ \begin{align*} \int |f(x)|^2 \d x = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int |\hat f(\lambda)|^2 \d \lambda \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (convolution) open_in_new

Let $f,g:\R\to\C$. The convolution of $f$ and $g$ is defined as $$ \begin{align*} (f*g)(x) = \int f(x-y)\,g(y) \d y. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (Fourier transform of convolution) open_in_new

For $f,g:\R\to\C$, $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}\left[f* g(x)\right] = \hat f(\lambda) \,\hat g(\lambda). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Examples of Fourier transform open_in_new

  • Exponential: $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}[H(x) \, e^{-\sigma x}] = \dfrac{1}{\sigma + i\lambda}$ where $H$ is the Heaviside function and $\sigma\in \C$ where $\Re(\sigma) > 0$. (Derivation)

  • Gaussian: $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}\right] = e^{-\lambda^2/2}$. (Derivation)

  • Dirac delta: $\mathcal{F}_{x\mapsto \lambda}[\delta(x)] = 1$. By the inversion formula, $\delta(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int e^{i\lambda x} \d \lambda$(!) (Derivation)

Proposition (Poisson summation formula) open_in_new

For $f: \R\to\C$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n\in \Z} f(x+n) = \sum_{n\in \Z} \hat f(2\pi n) e^{2\pi i n x}. \end{align*} $$ Proof

  • In particular, we get the identity $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n} f(n) = \sum_{n} \hat f(2\pi n). \end{align*} $$

Proposition (Heisenberg uncertainty principle) open_in_new

If $\int |\psi(x)|^2 \d x = 1$, then $$ \begin{align*} \left(\int x^2 |\psi(x)|^2 \d x\right)\left(\int \lambda^2 |\hat \psi(\lambda)|^2 \d \lambda\right) \geq \frac{\pi}{2}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Connection between Fourier series and Fourier transform

Discrete Fourier transform open_in_new

Definition (discrete Fourier transform) open_in_new

For some $N\in \N$ and a sequence $\set{X_n}_{n=0}^{N-1}$, define its discrete Fourier transform $\set{\hat X_k}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ by $$ \begin{align*} \hat X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} X_n e^{-2\pi i nk/N}. \end{align*} $$

Equivalently, writing $\v X = (X_0, \ldots, X_{N-1})^T$ and $\vhat X = (\hat X_0, \ldots, \hat X_{N-1})^T$, $$ \begin{align*} \vhat X = \mathcal{F}\, \v X \quad \text{ where } \mathcal{F}_{kn} = e^{-2\pi ink/N}. \end{align*} $$

Connection between discrete and continuous Fourier transform

Proposition (inverse discrete Fourier transform) open_in_new

We can recover a sequence $\set{X_n}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ from its discrete Fourier transform $\set{\hat X_k}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ via $$ \begin{align*} X_n = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \hat X_k \, e^{2\pi ink/N}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

This implies $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F}^\dagger \mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F} \mathcal{F}^\dagger = NI_N. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (Parseval's theorem for discrete Fourier transform) open_in_new

For two sequences $\set{X_n}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ and $\set{Y_n}_{n=0}^{N-1}$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} X_n \, \overline{Y_n} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \hat X_k \, \overline{\hat Y_k}. \end{align*} $$ In particular, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} |X_n|^2 = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} |\hat X_k|^2. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Higher dimensional Fourier transform open_in_new

Here, we write $\int \equiv \int_{\R^n}$.

Definition (higher dimensional Fourier transform) open_in_new

For a function $f: \R^n \to \C$, define its Fourier transform $\hat f: \R^n \to \C$ by $$ \begin{align*} \hat f(\v \lambda) = \int e^{-i\v \lambda \cdot \v x} f(\v x) \d ^n \v x. \end{align*} $$

We have analogous results from one dimensional Fourier transform:

  • Convolution theorem $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F}_{\v x\mapsto \v \lambda}\left[f*g(\v x)\right] = \hat f(\v \lambda) \hat g(\v \lambda), \qquad \text{where } f*g(\v x) = \int f(\v x-\v y) g(\v y) \d ^n \v y. \end{align*} $$

  • Inverse Fourier transform $$ \begin{align*} f(\v x) ={}& \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n} \int e^{i\v \lambda\cdot \v x} \hat f(\v \lambda) \d^n \v \lambda. \end{align*} $$

  • Delta functions $$ \begin{align*} \hat \delta(\v \lambda) = 1, \qquad \delta(\v x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n} \int e^{i\v \lambda\cdot \v x} \d \v \lambda. \end{align*} $$

  • Laplacian $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F}_{\v x\mapsto \v \lambda}[\Delta f(\v x)] ={}& ((i\lambda_1)^2 + \ldots + (i\lambda_n)^2 ) \hat f(\v \lambda) \\ ={}& - |\v \lambda|^2 \hat f(\v \lambda). \end{align*} $$

Proposition (Fourier transform of powers of radius) open_in_new

In $\R^n$, for $\alpha>0$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{F}_{\v x\mapsto \v \lambda}\big[|\v x|^{-\alpha}\big] = C_{n,\alpha} |\v \lambda|^{\alpha-n} \end{align*} $$ where $C_{n,\alpha}$ is a constant.
