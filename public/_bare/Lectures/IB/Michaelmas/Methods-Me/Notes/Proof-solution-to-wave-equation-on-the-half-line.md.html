Since $F(0,t) = 0 = f(0) = g(0)$, the odd extensions $F_\text{odd}, f_{\text{odd}}, g_{\text{odd}}$ are continuous.

The problem can be split into (zero forcing) + (zero initial conditions).

Zero forcing

The problem on the full line has solution $$ \begin{align*} & w(x,t) = \pdd{t}\Phi_t * f_{\text{odd}}(x) + \Phi_t * g_\text{odd}(x) \\ \iff & \hat w(\lambda,t) = \pdd{t} \hat \Phi_t(\lambda)\, \hat f_\text{odd}(\lambda) + \hat \Phi_t(\lambda) \, \hat g_\text{odd}(\lambda). \end{align*} $$ Since $\hat f_\text{odd}$ and $\hat g_\text{odd}$ are odd, so is $\hat w$ and thus $w$. Hence $w(0,t) = 0$, and so $u(x,t) = w(x,t) |_{x>0,t>0}$ solves $(\dagger)$.

Zero initial conditions

The solution $w$ on the full line has Fourier transform $$ \begin{align*} \hat w(\lambda, t) = \int_{0}^{t} \hat \Phi_{t-s}(\lambda) \, \hat F_\text{odd}(\lambda, s) \d s. \end{align*} $$ $\hat \Phi_{t-s}$ is even and $\hat F_\text{odd}$ is odd, so $\hat w$ is odd and thus so is $w$. Hence $w(0,t) = 0$ and so $u(x,t) = w(x,t)|_{x>0, t>0}$ solves $(\dagger)$.
