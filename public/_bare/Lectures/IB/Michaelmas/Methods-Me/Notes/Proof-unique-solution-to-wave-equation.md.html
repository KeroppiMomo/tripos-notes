Suppose $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ are two solutions. Let $\psi = \phi_1 - \phi_2$. Then by linearity, $\psi$ satisfies $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} \phi_{tt} - c^2 \Delta\psi = 0, &\Omega\times(0,\infty), \\ \psi = 0, &\Omega\times\set{0}, \\ \psi_t = 0, &\Omega\times\set{0}, \\ \psi = 0, &\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty). \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ By the conservation of energy, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega} (\psi_t^2 + c^2|\nabla\psi|^2) \d V = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega} (0^2 + c^20^2) \d V = 0 \end{align*} $$ so we must have $\psi_t = 0$ and $\nabla\psi=0$, i.e. $\psi$ is constant. But $\psi = 0$ on $\partial \Omega$, so $\psi=0$ everywhere, and $\phi_1 = \phi_2$.
