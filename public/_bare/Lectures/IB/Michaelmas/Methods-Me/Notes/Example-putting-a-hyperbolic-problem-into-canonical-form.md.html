Consider the partial differential equation $$ \begin{align*} xy \pdfd[2]{u}{x} - \pdfd[2]{u}{y} = 0. \end{align*} $$

Here $a = xy, b= 0, c= -1$. $b^2 - ac = xy$ is positive in the region $xy>0$. There, we have characteristic curves defined by $$ \begin{align*} \dfd{y}{x} = -\left(\frac{-b\pm \sqrt{b^2-ac}}{a}\right) = \mp \frac{1}{\sqrt{xy}}. \end{align*} $$ Solving, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{3}y^{3/2} \pm x^{1/2} = \text{const.}. \end{align*} $$

We restrict to $x,y>0$ now. By introducing the coordinates $$ \begin{align*} \xi(x,y) ={}& \frac{1}{3} y^{3/2} + x^{1/2}, \\ \eta(x,y) ={}& \frac{1}{3} y^{3/2} - x^{1/2}, \end{align*} $$ the PDE becomes $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& (xy\xi_x \eta_x - \xi_y \eta_y) \pdfxd{U}{\xi}{\eta} + \text{lower order terms} \\ ={}& -\frac{1}{2}y \pdfxd{U}{\xi}{\eta} + \text{lower order terms}. \end{align*} $$
