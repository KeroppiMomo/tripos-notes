Proof of 1 and 2 follows similarly as the proof for Hermitian matrices.

  1. Suppose $Ly = \lambda y$ and $y\neq 0$. $$ \begin{align*} (\lambda-\overline{\lambda}) \|y\|_w^2 &= (\lambda-\overline{\lambda})\langle y,y\rangle_w \\ &= \langle \lambda y, y\rangle_w - \langle y, \lambda y\rangle_w \\ &= \langle Ly, y\rangle_w - \langle y, Ly\rangle_w \\ &= 0 \end{align*} $$ and since $\|y\|_w^2 \neq 0$, we have $\lambda=\overline{\lambda}$ and therefore $\lambda\in \R$.

  2. Suppose $Ly_1=\lambda_1 y_1$, $Ly_2 = \lambda_2 y_2$ and $\lambda_1\neq \lambda_2$. $$ \begin{align*} (\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)\langle y_1,y_2\rangle_w &= \langle \lambda_1 y_1, y_2\rangle_w - \langle y_1, \overline{\lambda_2} y_2\rangle_w \\ &= \langle \lambda_1 y_1, y_2\rangle_w - \langle y_1, \lambda_2 y_2\rangle_w \\ &= \langle Ly_1, y_2\rangle_w - \langle y_1, Ly_2\rangle_w \\ &= 0 \end{align*} $$ and since $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 \neq 0$, we have $\langle y_1,y_2\rangle_w = 0$.
