Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$. $$ \begin{align*} \hat f(\lambda) ={}& \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{-i\lambda x} e^{-x^2/2} \d x. \end{align*} $$ But this is hard to compute. Instead, notice $$ \begin{align*} \dfd{f}{x} = -xf \end{align*} $$ so taking the Fourier transform on both sides, and applying the identities, $$ \begin{align*} i\lambda \hat f(\lambda) = -i \dfd{\hat f}{\lambda} \implies \dfd{\hat f}{\lambda} = -\lambda \hat f. \end{align*} $$ Solving, $$ \begin{align*} \hat f(\lambda) = \hat f(0) \, e^{-\lambda^2/2}. \end{align*} $$ Evaluating $$ \begin{align*} \hat f(0) = \int f(x) \d x = 1, \end{align*} $$ we have $$ \begin{align*} \hat f(\lambda) = e^{-\lambda^2/2}. \end{align*} $$
