Define $$ \begin{align*} Q(t) = \int_{0}^{t} (t-s)\, P(0,s) \d s. \end{align*} $$ Then $Q'(t) = \int_{0}^{t} P(0,s) \d s$ and $Q''(t) = P(0,t)$. In particular $Q(0) = 0 = Q'(0)$.

Set $$ \begin{align*} u(x,t) = v(x,t) - p\Big(t-\frac{x}{c}\Big) - Q(t) + Q\Big(t - \frac{x}{c}\Big). \end{align*} $$ Then $$ \begin{align*} u_{tt} - c^2 u_{x x} ={}& P(x,t) - 0 - Q''(t) + 0 \\ ={}& P(x,t) - P(0,t) \equiv F(x,t) \\ u(0,t) ={}& p(t) - p(t) - Q(t) + Q(t) \\ ={}& 0 \\ u(x,0) ={}& q(t) - p\Big({-\frac{x}{c}}\Big) - 0 + Q\Big({-\frac{x}{c}}\Big) \\ \equiv{}& f(x) \\ u_t(x,0) ={}& r(t) - p'\Big({-\frac{x}{c}}\Big) - 0 + Q'\Big({-\frac{x}{c}}\Big) \\ \equiv{}& g(x). \end{align*} $$ Also $F(0,t) = 0$ and $f(0) = g(0) = 0$.
