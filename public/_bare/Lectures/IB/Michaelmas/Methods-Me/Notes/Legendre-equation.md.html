The eigenvalue problem $$ \begin{align*} -\dd{x}\left((1-x^2) \dfd{y}{x}\right) = \lambda y, \qquad -1<x<1 \end{align*} $$ has eigenvalues and eigenfunctions and eigenfunctions ($n=0,1,2,\ldots$) $$ \begin{align*} \lambda_n &= n(n+1) \\ y_n &= \begin{cases} a_0 + a_2 x^2 + a_4 x^4 + \ldots + a_{2N} x^{2N}, &n = 2N \text{ is even},\\ a_1x + a_3 x^3 + a_5 x^5 + \ldots + a_{2N+1} x^{2N+1}, &n=2N + 1 \text{ is odd}, \end{cases} \\ \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} a_{k+2} = \frac{k(k+1) - \lambda}{(k+1)(k+2)} a_k. \end{align*} $$

The Legendre polynomials $P_n$ are the eigenfunctions normalised so that $y(1)=1$.

  • Rodrigue's formula: $$ \begin{align*} P_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^n n!} \dd[n]{x}(x^2 - 1)^n. \end{align*} $$

  • $P_n$ are orthogonal: $$ \begin{align*} \int_{-1}^{+1} P_n(x) P_m(x) \d x = \frac{2}{2n+1} \delta_{nm}. \end{align*} $$

  • $P_n$ are complete.
