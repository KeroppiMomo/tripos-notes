Definition (discrete Fourier transform) open_in_new

For some $N\in \N$ and a sequence $\set{X_n}_{n=0}^{N-1}$, define its discrete Fourier transform $\set{\hat X_k}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ by $$ \begin{align*} \hat X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} X_n e^{-2\pi i nk/N}. \end{align*} $$

Equivalently, writing $\v X = (X_0, \ldots, X_{N-1})^T$ and $\vhat X = (\hat X_0, \ldots, \hat X_{N-1})^T$, $$ \begin{align*} \vhat X = \mathcal{F}\, \v X \quad \text{ where } \mathcal{F}_{kn} = e^{-2\pi ink/N}. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (inverse discrete Fourier transform) open_in_new

We can recover a sequence $\set{X_n}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ from its discrete Fourier transform $\set{\hat X_k}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ via $$ \begin{align*} X_n = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \hat X_k \, e^{2\pi ink/N}. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (Parseval's theorem for discrete Fourier transform) open_in_new
