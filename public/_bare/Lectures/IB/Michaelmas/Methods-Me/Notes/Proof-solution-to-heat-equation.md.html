Take the Fourier transform of the heat equation in $\v x$ to get $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} \hat u_t + \kappa |\v \lambda|^2 \hat u = \hat F(\v \lambda, t), \\ \hat u(\v \lambda, 0) = \hat f(\v \lambda) \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ where $\hat u(\v \lambda, t) = \int e^{-i\v \lambda\cdot \v x} u(\v x,t) \d ^n \v x$.

Zero forcing

Now we have $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} \hat u_t + \kappa |\v \lambda|^2 \hat u = 0, \\ \hat u(\v \lambda, 0) = \hat f(\v \lambda), \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ which can be solved as $$ \begin{align*} \hat u(\v \lambda, t) = \hat f(\v \lambda) \,e^{-\kappa |\v \lambda|^2 t} = \hat f(\v \lambda) \, \hat K_t(\v \lambda). \end{align*} $$ By the convolution theorem, $$ \begin{align*} u(\v x,t) = (K_t * f)(\v x). \end{align*} $$

Zero initial condition

We use integrating factor to write $$ \begin{align*} \pdd{t}\left(e^{\kappa |\v \lambda|^2 t} \hat u\right) = e^{\kappa |\v \lambda|^2 t} \, \hat F(\v \lambda, t) \end{align*} $$ and with the initial condition $\hat u(\v \lambda,0) = 0$, the solution is $$ \begin{align*} e^{\kappa |\v \lambda|^2 t} \, \hat u(\v \lambda, t) ={}& \int_{0}^{t} e^{\kappa |\v \lambda|^2 s} \hat F(\v \lambda, s) \d s \\ \hat u(\v \lambda, t) ={}& \int_{0}^{t} e^{-\kappa |\v \lambda|^2 (t-s)} \, \hat F(\v \lambda, s) \d s \\ ={}& \int_{0}^{t} \hat K_{t-s}(\v \lambda) \, \hat F(\v \lambda, s) \d s \end{align*} $$ Using the convolution theorem, $$ \begin{align*} u(\v x, t) ={}& \int_{0}^{t} \int K_{t-s}(\v x - \v y) \, F(\v y, s) \d^n \v y \d s. \end{align*} $$
