Motivation for the Fourier series

Definition (complex Fourier series) open_in_new

For an $L$-periodic function $f: \R\to\C$, define the complex Fourier series $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n\in\Z} \hat f_n e^{2\pi in\theta/L} \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} \hat f_n = \frac{1}{L} \int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) e^{-2\pi in\theta/L} \d \theta \end{align*} $$ and we call $\set{\hat f_n}$ the complex Fourier coefficients of $f$.

We write $$ \begin{align*} f(\theta) \sim \sum_{n\in\Z} \hat f_n e^{2\pi in\theta/L} \end{align*} $$ to mean the series on the right is the complex Fourier series of the $L$-periodic function on the left.

Definition (Fourier series) open_in_new

For an $L$-periodic function $f: \R\to\C$, define the Fourier series $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2} a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n\cos \frac{2\pi n \theta}{L} + b_n \sin\frac{2\pi n\theta}{L}\right) \end{align*} $$ where, for each $n\in\N$, $$ \begin{align*} a_n &= \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) \cos \frac{2\pi n \theta}{L} \d \theta \\ b_n &= \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) \sin \frac{2\pi n \theta}{L} \d \theta \end{align*} $$ and we call $\set{a_n, b_n}_{n=1}^\infty$ the Fourier coefficients of $f$.

Derivation (Fourier series from complex Fourier series)

  • If $f$ is even, then all $b_n = 0$.

  • If $f$ is odd, then all $a_n = 0$. (Derivation)

Convergence of Fourier series open_in_new

Definition (partial Fourier series) open_in_new

For an $L$-periodic function $f: \R\to\C$, we define the partial Fourier series $S_N f$ by $$ \begin{align*} (S_N f)(\theta) = \sum_{|n|\leq N} \hat f_n e^{2\pi i n\theta/L} = \frac{1}{2} a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{N} \left(a_n \cos \frac{2\pi n\theta}{L} + b_n \sin \frac{2\pi n \theta}{L}\right). \end{align*} $$

Proposition (convergence of Fourier series) open_in_new

If an $L$-periodic function $f: \R\to\C$ is of bounded variation, i.e.

  1. $f$ is continuous on $[0,L)$ away from a finite number of jump discontinuities, and

  2. $f$ has finitely many maxima/minima on $[0,L)$,

then for each $\theta\in[0,L)$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{f(\theta_+) + f(\theta_-)}{2} = \lim_{N\to\infty} (S_N f)(\theta) = \sum_n \hat f_n e^{2\pi i n\theta/L} \end{align*} $$ where $f(\theta_{\pm}) = \lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0} f(\theta\pm\epsilon)$.

This means the Fourier series converges to the value of $f$ at points of continuity, and converges to the average value of $f$ at points of discontinuity.

Definition (cosine and sine series) open_in_new

Given a function $f: [0,L) \to \C$, define its cosine series as $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2} A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \cos \frac{n\pi\theta}{L} \end{align*} $$ and sine series as $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \sin \frac{n\pi\theta}{L} \end{align*} $$ where the coefficients $\set{A_n}, \set{B_n}$ are given by $$ \begin{align*} A_n &= \frac{2}{L} \int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) \cos \frac{n\pi\theta}{L} \d \theta, \\ B_n &= \frac{2}{L} \int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) \sin \frac{n\pi\theta}{L} \d \theta. \end{align*} $$

The cosine/sine series is the Fourier series of the $2L$-periodic even/odd extension of $f$. (Derivation)

Proposition (decay of Fourier coefficients) open_in_new

Let $f:\R\to\C$ be $L$-periodic.

  • If $f$ is integrable on $[0,L]$, then $\hat f_n$ $\to 0$ as $|n|\to\infty$.

  • If $f$ is $C^k$ (i.e. the $l$-derivative $f^{(l)}: \R\to\C$ are continuous for $l=0,1,\ldots,k$), then $|\hat f_n| =$ $o$$(1/n^k)$.

Proof

The more regular a function is, the more quickly the Fourier coefficients decay.

Proposition (Fourier coefficients of the derivative) open_in_new

Let $f\in \R\to\C$ be $L$-periodic. Suppose $f$ restricted to $(0,L)$ is continuously differentiable and the derivative is bounded.

Then the complex Fourier coefficients of $g:=f'$ are $$ \begin{align*} \hat g_n = \frac{f(L_-) - f(0_+)}{L} + \frac{2\pi i n}{L} \hat f_n. \end{align*} $$ In particular, if $f$ is continuous, then $\hat g_n = (2\pi i n/L) \hat f_n$, which is what you obtain from term-wise differentiation of the Fourier series.

Proof

Theorem (Parseval's theorem) open_in_new

Suppose $f,g: \R\to\C$ are $L$-periodic and square integrable with Fourier series $$ \begin{align*} f(\theta) = \sum_n \hat f_n e^{2\pi in\theta/L}, \qquad g(\theta) = \sum_n \hat g_m e^{2\pi im\theta/L}. \end{align*} $$

Then $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) \,\overline{g(\theta)} \d \theta = \sum_n \hat f_n \, \overline{\hat g_n}. \end{align*} $$ And in particular, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{L} \int_{0}^{L} |f|^2 \d \theta = \sum_n |\hat f_n|^2. \end{align*} $$
