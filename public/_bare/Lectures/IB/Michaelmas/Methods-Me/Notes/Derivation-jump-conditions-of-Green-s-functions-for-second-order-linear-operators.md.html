$G(x;\xi)$ satisfies $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(x) \dfd[2]{G}{x} + \beta(x) \dfd{G}{x} + \gamma(x) G(x) = \delta(x-\xi). \tag{*} \end{align*} $$

If $x<\xi$ or $x>\xi$, then $LG = 0$, so we expect $x \mapsto G(x;\xi)$ to be nice.

For $x=\xi$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(x) \dd{x}\left(\dfd{G}{x}\right) \sim \delta(x-\xi). \end{align*} $$ We interpret statements involving $\delta(x)$ as limits of smooth function $x\mapsto \delta_n(x)$. We look at the sequence $$ \begin{align*} H_n' = \delta_n \iff H_n(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} \delta_n(t) \d t. \end{align*} $$ We see that $$ \begin{align*} H_n(x) \to H(x) = \begin{cases} 1, &x>0, \\ 0, &x<0. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ So we expect $\dfd{G}{x}(x;\xi)$ to look like $H(x-\xi)$ near $x=\xi$, i.e. $\dfd{G}{x}$ should have a jump discontinuity at $x=\xi$.

And $$ \begin{align*} G(x;\xi) \sim \int_{a}^{x} H(t-\xi) \d t = \begin{cases} x-\xi, &x>\xi, \\ 0, &x<\xi \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ so we expect $x\mapsto G(x;\xi)$ to be continuous at $x=\xi$.

To calculate the jump, divide $(*)$ by $\alpha(x)$ and integrate over $(\xi-\epsilon, \xi+\epsilon)$ for some $\epsilon>0$ to get $$ \begin{align*} \left[\dfd{G}{x}\right]_{\xi-\epsilon}^{\xi+\epsilon} + \int_{\xi-\epsilon}^{\xi+\epsilon} \frac{\beta}{\alpha} \dfd{G}{x} + \frac{\gamma}{\alpha}G \d x ={}& \int_{x-\epsilon}^{x+\epsilon} \frac{1}{\alpha(x)}\delta(x-\xi) \d x \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\alpha(\xi)} \end{align*} $$ By the regularity of $G(x;\xi)$, the integral tends to zero as $\epsilon\searrow 0$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \dfd{G}{x}(\xi^+; \xi) - \dfd{G}{x}(\xi^-; \xi) = \frac{1}{\alpha(\xi)}. \end{align*} $$
