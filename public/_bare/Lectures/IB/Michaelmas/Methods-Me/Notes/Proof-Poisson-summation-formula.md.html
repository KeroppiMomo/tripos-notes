Both sides are periodic with period 1, so we can take the Fourier series. Let $F(x) = \sum_{n\in \Z} f(x+n)$. The Fourier coefficients are $$ \begin{align*} \hat F_n ={}& \int_{0}^{1} e^{-2\pi inx} F(x) \d x \\ ={}& \sum_{m\in \Z} \int_{0}^{1} e^{-2\pi in x} f(x+m) \d x \\ ={}& \sum_{m\in \Z} \int_{m}^{m+1} e^{-2\pi i n(y-m)} f(y) \d y &y=x+m \\ ={}& \sum_{m\in \Z} \int_{m}^{m+1} e^{-2\pi in y} f(y) \d y \\ ={}& \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2\pi iny} f(y) \d y \\ ={}& \hat f(2\pi n). \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n\in \Z} f(x+n) = F(x) = \sum_{n\in \Z} \hat f(2\pi n) e^{2\pi inx}. \end{align*} $$
