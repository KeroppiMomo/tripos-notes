$f$ is integrable

(non-examinable)

This follows directly from the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma: if $g$ is integrable on $[a,b]$, then $$ \begin{align*} \int_{a}^{b} g(\theta) e^{-i\lambda\theta} \d \theta \to 0 \quad \text{as }|\lambda|\to\infty. \end{align*} $$ Take $\lambda = 2\pi n/L$ to get $\hat f_n \to 0$ as $|n|\to \infty$.

$f$ is $C^k$

We apply integration by parts: $$ \begin{align*} \hat f_n &= \frac{1}{L} \int_{0}^{L} f(\theta) e^{-2\pi in\theta/L} \d \theta \\ &= -\frac{L}{2\pi in} \frac{1}{L} \underbrace{\left[ f(\theta) e^{-2\pi in\theta/L}\right]_{0^-}^{L^+}}_{\text{$0$ since $f$ is continuous}} + \frac{L}{2\pi i n} \frac{1}{L} \int_{0}^{L} f'(\theta) e^{-2\pi i n\theta/L} \d \theta \\ &= -\left(\frac{L}{2\pi i n}\right)^2 \frac{1}{L} \underbrace{\left[f'(\theta) e^{-2\pi in\theta/L}\right]_{0^-}^{L^+}}_{\text{$0$ since $f'$ is continuous}} + \left(\frac{L}{2\pi i n}\right)^2 \frac{1}{L} \int_{0}^{L} f''(\theta) e^{-2\pi i n\theta/L} \d \theta \\ &= \ldots \\ &= \left(\frac{L}{2\pi i n}\right)^k \frac{1}{L} \underbrace{\int_{0}^{L} f^{(k)}(\theta) e^{-2\pi i n\theta/L} \d \theta}_{\to 0 \text{ as } |n|\to\infty \text{ by Riemann-Lebesgue}} \end{align*} $$ and therefore $$ \begin{align*} \hat f_n = o\left(\frac{1}{n^k}\right). \end{align*} $$
