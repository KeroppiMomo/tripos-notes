From here, we want to solve $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} \Delta \psi + \mu \psi = 0, &\Omega, \\ \psi = 0, &\partial \Omega. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

We use $\psi(\rho, z) = P(\rho) Z(z)$, and in spherical polars, the heat equation becomes $$ \begin{align*} \frac{(\rho P')'}{\rho P} + \frac{Z''}{Z} + \mu = 0 \end{align*} $$ and since $\rho$ and $z$ are independent, there is a constant $\lambda$ such that $$ \begin{align*} -\dd{\rho}(\rho P') &= \lambda \rho P, & Z'' + (\mu - \lambda) Z ={}& 0. \end{align*} $$ With the boundary condition $P(1) = 0$, the equation for $P$ is Bessel's equation with $m=0$, so we have $$ \begin{align*} P(\rho) = P_k(\rho) &= J_0(j_{0k} \, \rho), & \lambda = \lambda_k &= {j_{0k}}^2, & k&=1,2,3,\ldots \end{align*} $$ where $J_0$ is the 0-th order Bessel's function and $j_{0k}$ is the $k$-th positive root of $J_0$.

The equation for $Z$ has the solution $$ \begin{align*} Z(z) = Z_n(z) &= \sin \frac{n\pi z}{L}, & \mu - \lambda_k = \left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2. \end{align*} $$

Therefore we have eigenvalues $$ \begin{align*} \mu_{k,n} = \left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2 + {j_{0k}}^2. \end{align*} $$

By superposition, $$ \begin{align*} \phi(\rho,z,t) = \sum_{k,n=1}^{\infty} A_{k,n} e^{-\kappa\mu_{k,n} t} J_0(j_{0k}\, \rho) \sin \frac{n\pi x}{L} \end{align*} $$ is a solution to the heat equation satisfying $\phi=0$ on $\partial \Omega$.

The initial condition is $$ \begin{align*} f(\rho, z) = \sum_{k,n=1}^{\infty} A_{k,n} J_0(j_{0k}\, \rho) \sin \frac{n\pi x}{L}. \end{align*} $$

We use the orthogonality relations to obtain $$ \begin{align*} &\int_{0}^{1} \left(\int_{0}^{L} f(\rho,z) \sin \frac{m\pi x}{L}\d x\right) J_0(j_{0l}\, \rho) \, \rho \d \rho \\ ={}& \sum_{k,n=1}^{\infty} A_{k,n} \left(\int_{0}^{1} J_{0}(j_{0k}\, \rho) J_0(j_{0l}\,\rho)\, \rho \d \rho\right) \left(\int_{0}^{L} \sin \frac{n\pi x}{L} \sin \frac{m\pi x}{L} \d x\right) \\ ={}& \sum_{k,n=1}^{\infty} A_{k,n} \frac{J_0'(j_{0k})^2}{2} \delta_{kl} \frac{L}{2} \delta_{nm} \\ ={}& \frac{L\,J_0'(j_{0l})^2}{4} A_{l,m}. \end{align*} $$
