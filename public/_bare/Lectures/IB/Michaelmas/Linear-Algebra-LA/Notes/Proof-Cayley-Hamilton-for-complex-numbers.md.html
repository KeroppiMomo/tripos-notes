For $\mathbb{F} = \C$, every linear map $\alpha$ is triangularisable, so there exists a basis $B = \set{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n}$ for $V$ such that $$ \begin{align*} [\alpha]^B_B = \pmat{\lambda_1 & * & \ldots & * \\ 0 & \lambda_2 & \ldots & * \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \ldots & \lambda_n} \end{align*} $$ is upper-triangular. Then $$ \begin{align*} \chi_\alpha(t) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} (t-\lambda_i). \end{align*} $$

Define $U_i = \langle v_1, \ldots, v_i\rangle \leq V$. Note that $U_n = V$, $$ \begin{align*} (\alpha - \lambda_1\, \id_V)(U_1) = \set{\v 0}, \end{align*} $$ and for $i\geq 2$, $$ \begin{align*} (\alpha - \lambda_i\, \id_V)(U_i) \leq U_{i-1}. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \chi_\alpha(\alpha)(V) = (\alpha-\lambda_1\,\id_V) \circ (\alpha - \lambda_2\,\id_V) \circ \ldots \circ (\alpha - \lambda_n\,\id_V) (U_n) = \set{\v 0}. \end{align*} $$ Hence $\chi_\alpha(\alpha) = 0$.
