$U^0 \isom W^\star$

Note that $U^0 \subseteq V^\star$. Consider the function $$ \begin{align*} \phi : U^0 &\to W^\star \\ \theta &\mapsto \theta|_W \end{align*} $$ which restricts $\theta$ to the domain $W$.

Clearly $\phi$ is linear. It has an inverse $$ \begin{align*} \phi\inv: W^\star &\to U^0 \\ \bar\theta &\mapsto ((u+w) \mapsto \bar\theta(w)), \quad u\in U, w\in W. \end{align*} $$ Since every element in $V$ can be written uniquely as a sum of an element in $U$ and an element in $W$, $\phi\inv(\bar\theta): V\to\mathbb{F}$ is well-defined. Also $\phi\inv(\bar\theta)\in U^0$ since $\phi\inv(\bar\theta)(u) = \phi\inv(\bar\theta)(u+\v 0) = \bar\theta(\v 0) = 0$ for any $u\in U$.

Therefore $\phi$ is bijective.

$\dim(V) = \dim(U) + \dim(U^0)$

Suppose $V$ is finite-dimensional.

Non-constructive proof

There exists a direct complement $W$ of $U$ in $V$. Then by the above, $U^0 \isom W^\star$. Then by the sum-intersection formula and $W\isom W^\star$, $\dim(V) = \dim(U) + \dim(W) = \dim(U) + \dim(W^\star) = \dim(U) + \dim(U^0)$.

Constructive proof

Let $n=\dim(V)$ and $k=\dim(U)$. Let $B_U = \set{v_1, \ldots, v_k}$ be a basis for $U$. Extend to $B_V = \set{v_1, \ldots, v_k, v_{k+1}, \ldots, v_n}$ a basis for $V$.

Let $B_{V^\star} = \set{v_1^\star, \ldots, v_k^\star, v_{k+1}^\star, \ldots, v_n^\star}$ be the basis for $V^\star$ dual to $B_V$. We claim that $\set{v_{k+1}^\star, \ldots, v_n^\star}$ is a basis for $U^0$.

Subset of $U^0$

For each $i\leq k$ and $j\geq k+1$, $$ \begin{align*} v_j^\star (v_i) = \delta_{ij} = 0 \end{align*} $$ so $v_j^\star\in B_U^0$ $=$ $\langle B_U\rangle^0 = U^0.$

Linear independence

$\set{v_{k+1}^\star, \ldots, v_n^\star} \subseteq B_{V^\star}$ which is linearly independent.

Spanning set

Let $\theta\in U^0$. Using the basis, write $$ \begin{align*} \theta = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \mu_j v_j^\star. \end{align*} $$ Then for each $i=1,2,\ldots,k$, $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \theta(v_i) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \mu_j v_j^\star(v_i) = \mu_i \end{align*} $$ and hence $$ \begin{align*} \theta = \sum_{j=k+1}^{n} \mu_j v_j^\star \in \langle v_{k+1}^\star, \ldots, v_n^\star\rangle. \end{align*} $$
