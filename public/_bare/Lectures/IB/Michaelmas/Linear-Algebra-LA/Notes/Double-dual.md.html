Definition (double dual) open_in_new

For a vector space $V$, the double dual of $V$ is the dual space of the dual space of $V$: $$ \begin{align*} V^{\star\star} = (V^\star)^\star. \end{align*} $$

For $V$ finite-dimensional, it is common to identify $V^{\star\star}=V$: see here.

Theorem (double dual and evaluation) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space.

The evaluation map $\mathcal{E}: V\to V^{\star\star}$ given by $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E}(v)(\theta) = \theta(v) \end{align*} $$ is a linear map.

If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $\mathcal{E}$ is an isomorphism.

Proof

  • $\mathcal{E}$ is also called the natural isomorphism, because it does not require a basis.

  • It is common to identify $\mathcal{E}(V) = V$, so we write $V^{\star\star}=V$.

Proposition (double annihilator) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and $U\leq V$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E}(U) = U^{00}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • Identifying $\mathcal{E}(U) = U$, we write $U = U^{00}$.

  • For $X\subseteq V^\star$, $X^0 \leq V^{\star\star}$ is identified with $$ \begin{align*} X_0 = \mathcal{E}\inv(X^0) = \sb{v\in V}{\theta(v) = 0 \text{ for all }\theta\in X} = \bigcap_{\theta\in X} \ker(\theta). \end{align*} $$ Then every $U\leq V$ can be written as $U = (U^0)_0$.
