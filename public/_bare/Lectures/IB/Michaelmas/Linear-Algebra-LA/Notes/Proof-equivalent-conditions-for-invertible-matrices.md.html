Full rank $\implies$ product of elementary matrices

Let $E_1, \ldots, E_l$ be elementary matrices such that $A' = E_1 \ldots E_l A$ is in row-reduced echelon form with $\op{rk}(A')$ $=$ $\op{rk}(A) = n$. This means $A'$ has no zero rows, and therefore $A' = I_n$. Hence $A = E_l\inv \ldots E_1\inv$ where $E_i\inv$ are elementary.

Product of elementary matrices $\implies$ invertible

Elementary matrices are invertible, so products of elementary matrices are invertible also.

Invertible $\implies$ $\op{rk}(A) = n$

Let $A,B\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F})$ and $AB = I_n$. Using the rank of matrix products, $$ \begin{align*} n = \op{rk}(I_n) = \op{rk}(AB) \leq \op{rk}(A) \leq n. \end{align*} $$

Product of elementary matrices $\implies$ non-zero determinant

If $A$ is a product of elementary matrices $E_1, \ldots, E_l$, then from the properties of volume forms, $$ \begin{align*} \det(A) = \det(E_1 \ldots E_l) = \det(E_1) \ldots \det(E_l) \neq 0. \end{align*} $$
