Sum is direct sum

Suppose not. There exists $v_i\in V_i$ which is not all zero such that $v_1 + v_2 + \ldots + v_k = \v 0 \in V$. But this contradicts with eigenvectors with distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent.

Diagonalisable $\implies$ direct sum

If $\alpha$ is diagonalisable, then $V$ has a basis of eigenvectors. Then $V$ is spanned by the eigenspaces of $\alpha$.

Direct sum $\implies$ diagonalisable

Let $B_i$ be the basis for $V_{\lambda_i}$ and let $|B_i| = n_i$. Then $B = \bigcup_{i} B_i$ is a basis for $V$ and $$ \begin{align*} [\alpha]^B_B = \pmat{\lambda_1 I_{n_1} \\ & \ddots \\ && \lambda_k I_{n_k}}. \end{align*} $$
