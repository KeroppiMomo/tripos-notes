Let $E = \set{e_1, \ldots, e_n}$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb{F}^n$. Furthermore let $f: E\to B$ by $f(e_i) = b_i$ and note that $f$ is bijective.

There exist unique linear maps

  • $F_B: \mathbb{F}^n \to V$ extending $f: E\to B$, and

  • $\overline{F_B}: V \to \mathbb{F}^n$ extending $f\inv: B\to E$.

Since $(\overline{F_B} \circ F_B)(e_i) = e_i$ and $\overline{F_B}\circ F_B$ is a linear map, $\overline{F_B} \circ F_B$ is a linear extension of the identity map $\mathrm{id}_E: E\to E$. But $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{F}^n}: \mathbb{F}^n \to \mathbb{F}^n$ is also a linear extension of $\mathrm{id}_E$, so by uniqueness, $\overline{F_B} \circ F_B = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{F}^n}$. Hence $F_B$ is bijective.
