Linearity

Let $v,w\in V$ and $\lambda\in \mathbb{F}$. For all $\theta\in V^\star$, $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E}(v+\lambda w)(\theta) ={}& \theta(v+\lambda w) \\ ={}& \theta(v) + \lambda\theta(w) \\ ={}& \mathcal{E}(v)(\theta) + \lambda \mathcal{E}(w)(\theta) \\ ={}& \big(\mathcal{E}(v) + \lambda \mathcal{E}(w)\big) (\theta) \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{E}(v + \lambda w) = \mathcal{E}(v) + \lambda \mathcal{E}(w). \end{align*} $$

Injectivity for finite dimensions

Suppose not. Then there exists $v\in V\setminus \set{\v 0}$ such that $\mathcal{E}(v) = 0\in V^{\star\star}$, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \theta(v) = \v 0&&&\text{for all } \theta\in V^\star. \end{align*} $$

Since $v\neq \v 0$, we can extend $\set{v}$ to a basis $B=\set{v, w_2, w_3, \ldots, w_n}$. We have a dual basis $B^\star = \set{v^\star, w_2^\star, \ldots, w_n^\star}$. Setting $\theta=v_1^\star$, we have $$ \begin{align*} \theta(v) = v_1^\star(v_1) = 1 \neq 0 \end{align*} $$ which is a contradiction.

(This holds in infinite dimensions if we assume the axiom of choice.)

Surjectivity for finite dimensions

Follows from $\dim(V) = \dim(V^\star) = \dim(V^{\star\star})$.

(This is false in infinite dimensions.)
