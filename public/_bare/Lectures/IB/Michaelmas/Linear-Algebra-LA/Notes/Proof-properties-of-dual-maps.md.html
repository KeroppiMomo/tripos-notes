$\alpha^\star$ is linear

Let $\theta,\tau\in W^\star$ and $\lambda\in \mathbb{F}$. For every $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} \alpha^\star(\theta + \lambda\tau)(v) ={}& ((\theta+\lambda\tau)\circ\alpha)(v) \\ ={}& (\theta+\lambda\tau)(\alpha(v)) \\ ={}& (\theta\circ\alpha)(v) + \lambda (\tau\circ\alpha)(v) \\ ={}& \alpha^\star(\theta)(v) + \lambda\alpha^\star(\tau)(v) \\ ={}& (\alpha^\star(\theta) + \lambda\alpha^\star(\tau))(v) \end{align*} $$ so $$ \begin{align*} \alpha^\star(\theta + \lambda\tau) = \alpha^\star(\theta) + \lambda\alpha^\star(\tau) \end{align*} $$ and therefore $\alpha^\star$ is linear.

$\gamma\mapsto \gamma^\star$ is linear

Let $\alpha,\beta\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ and $\lambda\in \mathbb{F}$. For each $\theta\in W^\star$ and $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} (\alpha+\lambda\beta)^\star(\theta)(v) ={}& (\theta\circ(\alpha+\lambda\beta))(v) \\ ={}& \theta((\alpha+\lambda\beta)(v)) \\ ={}& \theta(\alpha(v) + \lambda\beta(v)) \\ ={}& \theta(\alpha(v)) + \lambda\theta(\beta(v)) \\ ={}& \alpha^\star(\theta)(v) + \lambda\beta^\star(\theta)(v) \\ ={}& (\alpha^\star(\theta) + \lambda\beta^\star(\theta))(v) \\ ={}& (\alpha^\star + \lambda\beta^\star)(\theta)(v) \\ \implies (\alpha+\lambda\beta)^\star(\theta) ={}& (\alpha^\star + \lambda\beta^\star)(\theta) \\ \implies (\alpha+\lambda\beta)^\star ={}& \alpha^\star + \lambda\beta^\star. \end{align*} $$

$(\alpha\circ\beta)^\star = \beta^\star \circ \alpha^\star$

For all $\theta\in W^\star$, $$ \begin{align*} (\alpha\circ\beta)^\star(\theta) ={}& \theta\circ\alpha\circ\beta \\ ={}&\beta^\star(\theta\circ\alpha) \\ ={}&\beta^\star(\alpha^\star(\theta)) \\ ={}&(\beta^\star\circ\alpha^\star) (\theta) \end{align*} $$ so $(\alpha\circ\beta)^\star = \beta^\star \circ \alpha^\star$.

$(\alpha^\star)\inv = (\alpha\inv)^\star$

Suppose $\alpha$ is an isomorphism. Then by 3., $$ \begin{align*} \alpha^\star\circ(\alpha\inv)^\star = (\alpha\inv\circ\alpha)^\star = {\mathrm{id}_V}^\star =\mathrm{id}_{V^\star} \end{align*} $$ so $(\alpha\inv)^\star = (\alpha^\star)\inv$.
