Note that $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(\v 0_V) = \alpha(\v 0_V + \v 0_V) = \alpha(\v 0_V) + \alpha(\v 0_V) \end{align*} $$ so subtracting $\alpha(\v 0_V)$ on both sides gives $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(\v 0_V) = \v 0_W. \end{align*} $$

  1. From the above, $\v 0_V\in\ker(\alpha)$ so $\ker(\alpha)\neq\emptyset$.

    Besides let $k,k'\in \ker(\alpha)$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$. Since $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(k + \lambda k') = \alpha(k) + \lambda\alpha(k') = \v 0_{W} + \lambda\v 0_{W} = \v 0_{W}, \end{align*} $$ we have $k + \lambda k' \in \ker(\alpha)$. Therefore by the subspace test, $\ker(\alpha) \leq V$.

  2. Again from the above, $\v 0_W \in \im(\alpha)$.

    Besides let $w,w'\in \im(\alpha)$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$. By definition of $\im(\alpha)$, let $v,v'\in V$ such that $\alpha(v) = w$ and $\alpha(v') = w'$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(v + \lambda v') = \alpha(v) + \lambda\alpha(v') = w + \lambda w' \end{align*} $$ so $w+\lambda w'\in\im(\alpha)$. Therefore by the subspace test, $\im(\alpha) \leq W$.
