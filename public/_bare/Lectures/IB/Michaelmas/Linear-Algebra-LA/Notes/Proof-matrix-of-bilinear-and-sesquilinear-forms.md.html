Existence

For any $u\in U$ and $v\in V$, write $u = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i b_i$ and $v = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \mu_j c_j$. For a bilinear form, by linearity, $$ \begin{align*} \phi(u,v) ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} \lambda_i \mu_j \phi(b_i, c_j) \\ ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} ([u]_B)_i\, ([v]_C)_j\, ([\phi]_{B,C})_{i,j} \\ ={}& ([u]_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \, [v]_C. \end{align*} $$ And for a sesquilinear form, $$ \begin{align*} \phi(u,v) ={}& \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} \lambda_i \overline{\mu_j} \phi(b_i, c_j) = ([u]_B)^T\, [\phi]_{B,C} \, \overline{[v]_C}. \end{align*} $$

Uniqueness

Suppose a matrix $A\in M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{F})$ also satisfies $\phi(u,v) = ([u]_B)^T \, A \, [v]_C$ or $([u]_B)^T \, A \, \overline{[v]_C}$. Taking $u = b_i$ and $v = c_j$, $$ \begin{align*} \phi(b_i, c_j) = \v e_i^T A \v e_j = A_{ij}. \end{align*} $$
