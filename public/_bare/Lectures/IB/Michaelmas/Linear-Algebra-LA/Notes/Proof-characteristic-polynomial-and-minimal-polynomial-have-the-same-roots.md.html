Root of $m_\alpha$ $\implies$ root of $\chi_\alpha$

If $\lambda$ is a root of $m_\alpha$, by Cayley-Hamilton, $t-\lambda \mid m_\alpha(t) \mid \chi_\alpha(t)$, so $\lambda$ is a root of $\chi_\alpha$.

Root of $\chi_\alpha$ $\implies$ root of $m_\alpha$

Let $\lambda$ be a root of $\chi_\alpha$. Then there exists an eigenvector $v\in V\setminus \set{\v 0}$ with the eigenvalue $\lambda$.

Then $m_\alpha(\lambda)\cdot v$ $=$ $m_\alpha(\alpha)(v) = 0(v) = \v 0$.

Since $v\neq \v 0$, we have $m_\alpha(\lambda) = 0$ as required.
