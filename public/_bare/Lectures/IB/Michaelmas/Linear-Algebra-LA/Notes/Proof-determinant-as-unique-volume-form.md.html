Uniqueness

Let $F$ be an $n$-dimensional volume form and suppose $F(I_n) = 1$. By the properties of volume form, $$ \left.\begin{align*} F(T_{ij}) &= F(I_n T_{ij}) = -1, \\ F(M_{i,\lambda}) &= F(I_n M_{i,\lambda}) = \lambda, \\ F(C_{i,j,\lambda}) &= F(I_n C_{i,j,\lambda}) = 1, \end{align*}\qquad \right\} (*) $$ and this shows that $F(AE) = F(A)F(E)$ for an elementary matrix $E$.

Let $A\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F})$ and let $E_1, \ldots, E_l$ be elementary matrices such that $A' = A E_1 E_2 \ldots E_l$ is in column-reduced echelon form. Then $$ \begin{align*} F(A') = F(A) F(E_1) \ldots F(E_l). \end{align*} $$

If $\op{rk}(A) = n$, then $\op{rk}(A') = n$ also and so $A' = I_n$. Therefore $F(A) = F(E_1)\inv \ldots F(E_l)\inv$ uniquely determines $F(A)$ as the values of $F$ of elementary matrices are fixed by $(*)$.

If $\op{rk}(A) < n$, then $\op{rk}(A') < n$ and so $A'$ has a zero column. Hence $F(A') = 0$ and thus $F(A) = 0$, which is uniquely determined.

Existence

Consider $$ \begin{align*} \det(A) = \sum_{\sigma\in S_n} \sign(\sigma) \prod_{i=1}^n a_{\sigma(i),i}. \end{align*} $$

$\det(I_n) = 1$ since only $\sigma = \mathrm{id}$ contributes to the sum.

To show multilinearity, let $\v c_1, \ldots, \v c_n \in \mathbb{F}^n$ and $\v c_j, \v c_j' \in \mathbb{F}$ with $\lambda\in \mathbb{F}$ for some $j$. Then $$ \begin{align*} &\sum_{\sigma\in S_n} \sign(\sigma) (\v c_1)_{\sigma(1)} (\v c_2)_{\sigma(2)} \ldots (\v c_j + \lambda \v c_j')_{\sigma(j)} \ldots (\v c_n)_{\sigma(n)} \\ ={}& \sum_{\sigma\in S_n} \sign(\sigma) (\v c_1)_{\sigma(1)} \ldots (\v c_j)_{\sigma(j)} \ldots (\v c_n)_{\sigma(n)} \\ &+\lambda \sum_{\sigma\in S_n} \sign(\sigma) (\v c_1)_{\sigma(1)} \ldots (\v c_j')_{\sigma(j)} \ldots (\v c_n)_{\sigma(n)} \end{align*} $$

Suppose $\v c_k = \v c_l$ where $k\neq l$. Let $\tau = \pmat{k&l}\in S_n \setminus$ $A_n$. We have $a_{i,j} = a_{i,\tau(j)}$ (because $\tau(j) = j$ when $j\neq k,l$, and when $j=k$, the columns are the same). Therefore splitting $S_n = A_n \sqcup A_n \tau$, $$ \begin{align*} \det(A) ={}& \sum_{\sigma\in A_n} \underbrace{\sign(\sigma)}_{1} \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{\sigma(i),i} + \sum_{\sigma\in A_n} \underbrace{\sign(\sigma\tau)}_{-1} \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{\sigma\tau(i),i} \\ ={}& \sum_{\sigma\in A_n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{\sigma(i),i} - \sum_{\sigma\in A_n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{\sigma\tau(i),\tau(i)} &&a_{i,j}=a_{i,\tau(j)} \\ ={}& \sum_{\sigma\in A_n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{\sigma(i),i} - \sum_{\sigma\in A_n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{\sigma(i),i} &&\text{rearranging order} \\ ={}& 0. \end{align*} $$
