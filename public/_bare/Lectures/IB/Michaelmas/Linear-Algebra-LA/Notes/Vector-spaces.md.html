Definition (vector space) open_in_new

Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a field.

An $\mathbb{F}$-vector space (or a vector space over $\mathbb{F}$) is a abelian group $(V, +, \v 0)$, equipped with a function (scalar multiplication) $$ \begin{align*} \mathbb{F}\times V &\to V \\ (\lambda, v) & \mapsto \lambda v \end{align*} $$ satisfying, for all $v,w\in V$ and $\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{F}$,

  • Distributive law:

    1. $(\lambda+\mu)v = \lambda v + \mu v$;

    2. $\lambda(v + w) = \lambda v + \lambda w$;

  1. Associative law: $(\lambda\mu) v = \lambda(\mu v)$;

  2. $1 v = v$.


  • A vector space over $\mathbb{F}$ is a $\mathbb{F}$-module.

  • $0v = \v 0$ for all $v\in V$. (Derivation)

  • $(-1)v = -v$ for all $v\in V$. (Derivation)

Definition (vector subspace) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a $\mathbb{F}$-vector space.

A subset $U\subseteq V$ is said to be a subspace of $V$ (denoted as $U\leq V$) if it is a $\mathbb{F}$-vector space under the same operations as $V$, i.e. $(U, +)$ is a subgroup of $(V, +)$, and $\lambda u\in U$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$ and $u\in U$.

Examples of vector subspace

Proposition (subspace test) open_in_new

A subset $U\subseteq V$ is a subspace of a vector space $V$ iff

  1. $U$ is non-empty;

  2. for all $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$ and $u,w\in U$, we have $u+\lambda w \in U$.

Proof

  • Intersection of subspaces is also a subspace: if $U,W\leq V$, then $U\cap W\leq V$. (Derivation)

  • Union of subspaces is almost never a subspace.

Definition (sum of vector subspaces) open_in_new

Let $U,W$ $\leq$ $V$ where $V$ is a vector space.

Define the sum of $U$ and $W$ in $V$ as $$ \begin{align*} U + W = \sb{u+w}{u\in U, w\in W} \end{align*} $$

  • Sums are also subspaces. (Derivation)

  • $U+W$ is the smallest subspace of $V$ containing both $U$ and $W$.

Linear maps open_in_new

Definition (linear map) open_in_new

Let $V, W$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces.

A function $\alpha: V\to W$ is said to be a linear map if it preserves vector addition and scalar multiplication, i.e. for all $u,v\in V$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$, $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(u+v) = \alpha(u) + \alpha(v) \quad \text{and} \quad \alpha(\lambda u) = \lambda \alpha(u). \end{align*} $$

Equivalently, if for all $u,v\in V$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$, $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(u+\lambda v) = \alpha(u) + \lambda \alpha(v). \end{align*} $$

Examples of linear maps

Composition of linear maps is also linear.

Definition (isomorphism of vector spaces) open_in_new

A bijective linear map $\alpha: V\to W$ is called an isomorphism.

We say $V$ are $W$ are isomorphic (denoted as $V\cong W$) if there exists an isomorphism from $V$ to $W$.

Proposition (inverse of isomorphism is an isomorphism) open_in_new

If $\alpha: V\to W$ is an isomorphism, then so is $\alpha\inv: W\to V$.

Proof

Definition (kernel and image of a linear map) open_in_new

Let $\alpha: V\to W$ be a linear map. Define

  • $\ker(\alpha) = \sb{v\in V}{\alpha(v) = \v 0_W}$ as the kernel of $\alpha$;

  • $\im(\alpha) = \sb{\alpha(v)}{v\in V}$ as the image of $\alpha$.

  • $\ker(\alpha) \leq V$ and $\im(\alpha) \leq W$. (Derivation)

  • $\alpha$ is surjective iff $\im(\alpha) = W$.

  • $\alpha$ is injective iff $\ker(\alpha) = \set{\v 0_V}$. (Derivation)

Definition (quotient space) open_in_new

Let $V$ be an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space and $U\leq V$.

The quotient space of $V$ by $U$ is defined as $$ \begin{align*} V/U := \sb{v + U}{v\in V}, \end{align*} $$ the set of left cosets of $(U,+)$ in $(V,+)$.

$V/U$ is an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space under the operations $$ \begin{align*} (v_1 + U) + (v_2 + U) &= (v_1+v_2) + U, \\ \lambda(u_1 + U) &= (\lambda u_1) + U. \end{align*} $$ Proof

Proposition (map to quotient space) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space and $U\leq V$.

There exists a surjective linear map $$ \begin{align*} \pi_U : V &\to V/U \\ v &\mapsto v + U \end{align*} $$ with a kernel $\ker(\pi_U) = U$.

Proof
