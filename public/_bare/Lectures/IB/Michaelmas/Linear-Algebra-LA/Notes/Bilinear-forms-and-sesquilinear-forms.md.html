Definition (bilinear form) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces.

A function $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ is called a bilinear form if for all $u_0\in U$ and $v_0\in V$, both $$ \begin{align*} V &\to \mathbb{F} &&& U &\to \mathbb{F} \\ v &\mapsto \phi(u_0, v) &&\text{and} & u &\mapsto \phi(u, v_0) \end{align*} $$ are linear maps.

Definition (sesquilinear form) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be $\mathbb{C}$-vector spaces.

A function $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{C}$ is called a sesquilinear form if it is

  • linear in the first argument: $\phi(\lambda u + u', v) = \lambda\phi(u, v) + \phi(u', v)$ for all $\lambda\in \C, u,u'\in U, v\in V$; and

  • conjugate-linear in the second argument: $\phi(u, \mu v + v') = \overline{\mu}\phi(u,v) + \phi(u,v')$ for all $\mu\in \C, u\in U, v,v'\in V$.

Definition (symmetric bilinear form) open_in_new

A bilinear form $\phi:V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ is said to be symmetric if for all $u,v\in V$, $\phi(u,v) = \phi(v,u)$.

Definition (Hermitian sesquilinear form) open_in_new

A sesquilinear form $\phi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ is said to be Hermitian if for all $u,v\in V$, $\phi(u,v) = \overline{\phi(v,u)}$.

Proposition (matrix of bilinear and sesquilinear forms) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces with bases $B = \set{b_1, \ldots, b_n} \subseteq U$ and $C = \set{c_1, \ldots, c_m} \subseteq V$.

  • For any bilinear form $\phi$, the matrix of $\phi$ with respect to $B$ and $C$ $$ \begin{align*} [\phi]_{B,C} = (\phi(b_i, c_j))_{i,j} \in M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{F}) \end{align*} $$ is the unique matrix satisfying $$ \begin{align*} ([u]_B)^T\, [\phi]_{B,C} \,[v]_C = \phi(u, v) \qquad \text{for all }u\in U, v\in V. \end{align*} $$

  • For any sesquilinear form $\phi$, $[\phi]_{B,C}$ defined the same way is the unique matrix satisfyiing $$ \begin{align*} ([u]_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \, \overline{[v]_C} = \phi(u,v) \qquad \text{for all } u\in U, v\in V. \end{align*} $$

Proof

  • For $U=V$ and $B=C$, we write $[\phi]_B \equiv [\phi]_{B,B}$.

Proposition (change of bases of bilinear and sesquilinear form) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces. Suppose $B,B'$ are bases for $U$ and $C,C'$ are bases for $V$.

  • For a bilinear form $\phi$, $$ \begin{align*} [\phi]_{B',C'} = ([\id_U]^{B'}_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \, [\id_V]^{C'}_C. \end{align*} $$

  • For a sesquilinear form $\phi$, $$ \begin{align*} [\phi]_{B',C'} = ([\id_U]^{B'}_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \, \overline{[\id_V]^{C'}_C}. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Proposition (matrix of symmetric bilinear and Hermitian sesquilinear form) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space.

  • A bilinear form $\phi$ is symmetric iff $[\phi]_{B}$ is symmetric (i.e. $[\phi]_{B} = ([\phi]_B)^T$) for some (equivalently all) bases $B$ for $V$.

  • A sesquilinear form $\phi$ is Hermitian iff $[\phi]_{B}$ is Hermitian (i.e. $[\phi]_{B} = \overline{([\phi]_B)^T}$) for some (equivalently all) bases $B$ for $V$.

Proof

Definition (functionals of bilinear form, kernels) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ be a bilinear form. Define the maps $$ \begin{align*} \phi_L: U &\to V^\star & \phi_R: V&\to U^\star \\ u &\mapsto (v'\mapsto \phi(u,v')), & v&\mapsto (u'\mapsto \phi(u',v)). \end{align*} $$

  • $\phi_L$ and $\phi_R$ are linear. (Derivation)

Furthermore, we call

  • $\ker(\phi_L) \leq U$ the left-kernel of $\phi$; and

  • $\ker(\phi_R) \leq V$ the right-kernel of $\phi$.

If $\phi$ is symmetric, then the left and right-kernels equal, and we call this the kernel of $\phi$.

Proposition (matrices of functionals of bilinear form) open_in_new

Let $U,V$ be finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces with bases $B\subseteq U$ and $C\subseteq V$. Also let $B^\star\subseteq U^\star$ and $C^\star\subseteq V^\star$ be the dual bases. Then

  1. $[$$\phi_L$$]^B_{C^\star}$ $= ($$[\phi]_{B,C}$$)^T$;

  2. $[\phi_R]^C_{B^\star} = [\phi]_{B,C}$.

Proof

Definition (rank of bilinear form) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be finite-dimensional.

Define the rank of a bilinear form $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ as $\rk(\phi) = \rk($$[\phi]_{B,C}$$)$ for any basis $B$ for $U$ and $C$ for $V$.

  • This is well-defined, since rank is unchanged when multiplying invertible matrices during base changes.

Corollary (ranks of bilinear form and its functionals) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces.

For any bilinear maps $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$, we have $\rk$$(\phi) =$ $\rk$$(\phi_L) = \rk(\phi_R)$

Proof

Definition (orthogonal to set) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ be a bilinear form.

For subsets $S\subseteq U$ and $T\subseteq V$, define

  • $S^\perp = \sb{v\in V}{\phi(s,v)=0 \, \forall s\in S} \leq V$, and

  • $^\perp T = \sb{u\in U}{\phi(u, t)=0 \, \forall t\in T} \leq U$.


  • Inclusion reversing: $S_1 \subseteq S_2 \subseteq U \implies S_2^\perp \leq S_1^\perp$. Similarly in $V$.

  • $U^\perp = \ker($$\phi_R$$)$ and $^\perp V = \ker(\phi_L)$.

Definition (degenerate bilinear form) open_in_new

Following the notation here, $\phi$ is said to be degenerate if either $U^\perp$ or $^\perp V$ is non-trivial.

Proposition (non-degenerate bilinear form iff invertible matrix) open_in_new

Let $U$ and $V$ be finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ be a bilinear form.

$\phi$ is non-degenerate iff $\dim U = \dim V$ and $[\phi]_{B,C}$ is invertible for some (equivalently any) bases $B,C$ for $U,V$.

Proof

Definition (quadratic form) open_in_new

Let $V$ be an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space.

A function $Q: V\to \mathbb{F}$ is said to be a quadratic form if there exists a bilinear form $\phi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ such that $Q(v) = \phi(v,v)$ for all $v\in V$.

Proposition (polarisation identity) open_in_new

  • Let $V$ be an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space and $Q: V\to \mathbb{F}$ be a quadratic form. Suppose $1+1\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}$.

    The function $\psi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ given by the polarisation identity $$ \begin{align*} \psi(u,v) = \frac{1}{2}\big(Q(u+v) - Q(u) - Q(v)\big) \end{align*} $$ is the unique symmetric bilinear form such that $Q(v) = \psi(v,v)$ for all $v\in V$.

  • Let $V$ be a $\C$-vector space and $\phi$ be a Hermitian sesquilinear form on $V$.

    $\phi$ is uniquely determined by $Q: V\to\R$ given by $Q(v) = \phi(v,v)\in \R$ via $$ \begin{align*} \phi(u,v) = \frac{1}{4}\big(Q(u+v) - Q(u-v) + iQ(u+iv) - iQ(u-iv)\big). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (congruent matrix) open_in_new

Two matrices $A,A' \in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F})$ are said to be congruent if there exists $P\in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$ such that $A' = P^T A P$.

But over $\C$, $A,A'\in M_{n\times n}(\C)$ are said to be congruent if there exists $P\in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ such that $A' = P^T A \overline{P}$.

Theorem (matrix of symmetric bilinear map can be diagonal) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector space. Suppose $1+1\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}$.

  • For any bilinear form $\phi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$, $\phi$ is symmetric iff there exists a basis $B$ for $V$ such that $[\phi]_B$ is diagonal.

  • For any sesquilinear form $\phi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$, $\phi$ is Hermitian iff there exists a basis $B$ for $V$ such that $[\phi]_B$ is real and diagonal.

Proof

Corollary (symmetric matrix is congruent to diagonal matrix) open_in_new

Suppose $1+1\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}$.

A square matrix over $\mathbb{F}$ is symmetric iff it is congruent to a diagonal matrix.

Proof: apply this and the fact that $[\phi]_{B'} = ([\id_V]^{B'}_{B})^T \, [\phi]_B \, [\id_V]^{B'}_{B}$ gives a congruence relation.

Corollary (normalised diagonal matrix of bilinear form) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector space.

  • For any symmetric bilinear form $\phi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$, there exists a basis $B$ for $V$ such that

    1. for $\mathbb{F}=\C$: $[\phi]_B$ $= \pmat{I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0}$ where $r = \rk(\phi)$;

    2. for $\mathbb{F}=\R$: $[\phi]_B = \pmat{I_p & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_q & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0}$ where $p+q = \rk(\phi)$.

  • For any Hermitian sesquilinear form $\phi: V\times V\to \C$, there exists a basis $B$ for $V$ such that $[\phi]_B = \pmat{I_p & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_q & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0}$.

Proof

Definition (definite bilinear, sesquilinear and quadratic form) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space over $\R$, and $\phi:V\times V\to \mathbb{R}$ be a symmetric bilinear form. We say $\phi$ is

  • positive definite if $\phi(v,v)>0$ for all $v\neq \v 0$;

  • negative definite if $\phi(v,v)<0$ for all $v\neq \v 0$;

  • positive semidefinite if $\phi(v,v)\geq 0$ for all $v\neq \v 0$;

  • negative semidefinite if $\phi(v,v)\leq 0$ for all $v\neq \v 0$;

  • indefinite if otherwise.

Similarly for quadratic form and Hermitian sesquilinear form ($\phi(v,v)$ is real because $\phi(v,v) = \overline{\phi(v,v)}$).

Theorem (inertia of a matrix) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\R$ and $\phi: V\times V\to \R$ be a symmetric bilinear form.

If $B$ is a basis for $V$ such that $[\phi]_B$ $= \pmat{I_p \\ & -I_q \\ && 0}$, then

  • $p$ is the largest dimension of a subspace $U\leq V$ such that $\phi|_U$ is positive definite; and

  • $q$ is the largest dimension of a subspace $U\leq V$ such that $\phi|_U$ is negative definite.

This also holds for $V$ a finite-dimensional vector space over $\C$ and $\phi: V\times V\to \C$ a Hermitian sesquilinear form.

Proof

  • The fact that $p$ and $q$ are invariant under different bases is known as Sylvester's law of inertia.

  • $\sigma(\phi) \equiv p-q$ is called the signature of $\phi$.

Proposition (maximal dimension of totally isotropic subspace) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\R$ and $\phi: V\times V\to \R$ be a symmetric bilinear form.

The maximal dimension of a totally isotropic subspace ($T\leq V$ such that $\phi|_T\equiv 0$) is $n-\max\set{p,q}$, where $p,q$ are defined here.

This also holds for $V$ a finite-dimensional vector space over $\C$ and $\phi: V\times V\to \C$ a Hermitian sesquilinear form.
