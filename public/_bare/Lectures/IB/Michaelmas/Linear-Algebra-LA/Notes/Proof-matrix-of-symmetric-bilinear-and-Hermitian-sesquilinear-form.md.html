I'll just prove the case for a Hermitian sesquilinear form. For bilinear form, remove all complex conjugates.

Let $B = \set{b_1, \ldots, b_n}$.

Hermitian form $\implies$ Hermitian matrix

From how we define the matrix of a sesquilinear form, $$ \begin{align*} ([\phi]_{B})_{i,j} = \phi(b_i, b_j) = \overline{\phi(b_j, b_i)} = \overline{([\phi]_B)_{j,i}}. \end{align*} $$

Hermitian matrix $\implies$ Hermitian form

For any $u,v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} \phi(u,v) ={}& ([u]_B)^T \, [\phi]_B \, \overline{[v]_B} \\ ={}& \overline{\big(([v]_B)^T \, \overline{([\phi]_B)^T} \, \overline{[u]_B}\big)^T} \\ ={}& \overline{([v]_B)^T \, [\phi]_B \overline{[u]_B}} \\ ={}& \overline{\phi(v,u)}. \end{align*} $$
