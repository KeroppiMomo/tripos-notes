Let $T = \set{t_1, \ldots, t_n}$ and $S = \set{s_1, \ldots, s_m}$, and define $$ \begin{align*} T_k = \set{s_1, \ldots, s_k, t_{k+1}, \ldots, t_n}. \end{align*} $$

If $S=\emptyset$, then we are done.

$T_0 = T$ spans $V$ by hypothesis.

Suppose $T_k$ spans $V$ for some $0\leq k<m$. Then $$ \begin{align*} s_{k+1} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i s_i + \sum_{j=k+1}^{n} \mu_j t_j \end{align*} $$ for some $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_{k}, \mu_{k+1}, \ldots, \mu_{n} \in \mathbb{F}$.

If all $\mu_i = 0$ or if $n = k < m$, then $$ \begin{align*} s_{k+1} - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i s_i = \v 0 \end{align*} $$ but this contradicts with $S$ being linearly independent.

Thus $n<k$ and WLOG assume $\mu_{k+1} \neq 0$. Then we have $$ \begin{align*} t_{k+1} &= \frac{1}{\mu_{k+1}}\left(s_{k+1} - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i s_i - \sum_{j=k+2}^{n} \mu_j t_j\right) \\ &\in \langle T_{k+1}\rangle \end{align*} $$ so $T_{k+1}$ spans $V$ as $T_k$ spans $V$.

By induction, $m\leq n$, and $T_m = S \cup \set{t_{m+1}, \ldots, t_n}$ spans $V$. Set $T' = \set{t_{m+1}, \ldots, t_n}$ and we are done.
