Bilinear form

Existence

By definition, there exists $\phi:V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ such that $Q(v) = \phi(v,v)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \psi(u,v) = \frac{1}{2}\big(\phi(u,v) + \phi(v,u)\big) \end{align*} $$ is a symmetric bilinear map also satisfying $Q(v) = \psi(v,v)$.

Uniqueness

If $\psi: V\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ is a symmetric bilinear map satisfying $Q(v) = \psi(v,v)$, then $$ \begin{align*} Q(u+v) ={}& \psi(u+v, u+v) \\ ={}& \psi(u,u) + \psi(u,v) + \psi(v,u) + \psi(v,v) \\ ={}& Q(u) + Q(v) + 2\psi(u,v) \end{align*} $$ so $\psi$ satisfies the polarisation identity.

Sesquilinear form

Just expand: $$ \begin{align*} & Q(u+v) - Q(u-v) + iQ(u+iv) - iQ(u-iv) \\ ={}& \|u\|^2 + \langle u,v\rangle + \overline{\langle u,v\rangle} + \|v\|^2 \\ &- \big(\|u\|^2 - \langle u,v\rangle - \overline{\langle u,v\rangle} + \|v\|^2\big) \\ &+ i\big(\|u\|^2 - i\langle u,v\rangle + i\overline{\langle u,v\rangle} + \|v\|^2\big) \\ &- i\big(\|u\|^2 + i\langle u,v\rangle - i\overline{\langle u,v\rangle} + \|v\|^2\big) \\ ={}& 4\langle u,v\rangle. \end{align*} $$
