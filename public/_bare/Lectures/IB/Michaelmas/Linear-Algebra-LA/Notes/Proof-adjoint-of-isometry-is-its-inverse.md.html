Note the identity $$ \begin{align*} \langle \alpha(v), \alpha(w)\rangle = \langle v, \alpha^\star(\alpha(w))\rangle. \end{align*} $$

$\alpha^\star = \alpha\inv$ $\implies$ isometry

$\alpha^\star\circ\alpha = \id_V$, so $\langle \alpha(v), \alpha(w)\rangle = \langle v,w\rangle$.

Isometry $\implies$ $\alpha^\star = \alpha\inv$

Now $\langle \alpha(v), \alpha(w)\rangle = \langle v,w\rangle$, so $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \langle v, \alpha^\star(\alpha(w)) - w\rangle \end{align*} $$ for all $v,w\in V$.

Take $v = \alpha^\star(\alpha(w)) - w$ to see $$ \begin{align*} \alpha^\star(\alpha(w)) - w = 0 \end{align*} $$ for all $w\in V$. Thus $\alpha^\star\circ\alpha = \id_V$.
