Let $C = \set{\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_n}$ be a basis for $V^\star$.

Let $C^\star = \set{\hat \theta_1, \ldots, \hat \theta_n}$ be the dual basis. Set $v_i =$ $\mathcal{E}$$\inv(\hat \theta_i)$ and then $B=\set{v_1, \ldots, v_n}$ is a basis for $V$ since it is the image under an isomorphism $\mathcal{E}\inv$ of basis $C^\star$.

Note that $$ \begin{align*} \theta_i(v_j) = \mathcal{E}(v_j)(\theta_i) = \hat \theta_j(\theta_i) = \delta_{ij} \end{align*} $$ so $B$ and $C$ satisfy the defining property of dual basis. Hence $C = B^\star$.
