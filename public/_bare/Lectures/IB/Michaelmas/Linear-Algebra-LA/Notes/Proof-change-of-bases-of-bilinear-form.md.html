Let $\phi: U\times V\to \mathbb{F}$ be a bilinear form. For any $u\in U$ and $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} & ([u]_{B'})^T\, \big( ([\id_U]^{B'}_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \,[\id_V]^{C'}_C \big) \, [v]_{C'} \\ ={}& ([\id_U]^{B'}_B \, [u]_{B'})^T\, [\phi]_{B,C} \, ([\id_V]^{C'}_C \, [v]_{C'}) \\ ={}& ([u]_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \, [v]_{C} \\ ={}& \phi(u,v). \end{align*} $$ Since $([\id_U]^{B'}_B)^T \, [\phi]_{B,C} \,[\id_V]^{C'}_C$ satisfies the defining property of $[\phi]_{B',C'}$, we are done.

Similar for a sesquilinear form.
