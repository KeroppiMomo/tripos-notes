Linear independence

Suppose for some $\lambda_b \in \mathbb{F}$, $$ \begin{align*} \v 0 = \sum_{b\in B} ' \lambda_b b^\star. \end{align*} $$ Then for any $c\in B$, evaluating the functions on both sides at $c$ yields $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \left(\sum_{b\in B} '\lambda_b b^\star\right)(c) \\ ={}& \sum_{b\in B} ' \lambda_b b^\star(c) \\ ={}& \sum_{b\in B} '\lambda_b \delta_{b,c} \\ ={}& \lambda_c \end{align*} $$

Spanning in finite-dimensional vector space

Non-constructive proof

We know that $\dim(\mathcal{L}(V,\mathbb{F})) = \dim(V)$. And since $B^\star \subseteq V^\star$ is a linearly independent set with size $|B^\star| = |B| = \dim(V)$, $B^\star$ is a basis for $V^\star$.

Constructive proof

For any $\theta\in V^\star$, let $\mu_b = \theta(b) \in \mathbb{F}$ for each $b\in B$. $$ \begin{align*} \bar\theta = \sum_{b\in B} ' \mu_b \,b^\star \in \langle B^\star\rangle. \end{align*} $$ Then for any $c\in B$, $$ \begin{align*} \bar\theta(c) = \sum_{b\in B} \mu_b\,b^\star(c) = \sum_{b\in B} \mu_b \, \delta_{b,c} = \mu_c = \theta(c) \end{align*} $$ so $\theta$ and $\bar \theta$ are linear maps that agree on the basis $B$. By uniqueness of linear extensions, $\theta = \bar\theta$.

$V \isom V^\star$

This follows from $\dim(V) = |B| = |B^\star| = \dim(V^\star)$
