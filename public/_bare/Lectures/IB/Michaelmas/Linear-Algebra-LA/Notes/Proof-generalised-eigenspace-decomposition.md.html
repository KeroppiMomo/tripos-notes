Since $V$ is over $\C$, $\chi_\alpha$ is a product of linear factors, so the minimal polynomial $m_\alpha$ is also a product of linear factors. Let $$ \begin{align*} m_\alpha(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (t-\lambda_i)^{c_i} \end{align*} $$ where $\lambda_i$ are distinct.

Define for each $i=1,2,\ldots,n$, $$ \begin{align*} p_i(t) = \sum_{\substack{i=1 \\ i\neq j}}^n (t-\lambda_i)^{c_i}. \end{align*} $$ Since the $p_i$'s have no common factor of degree at least 1, by polynomial Bézout, there exists $q_i$'s such that $$ \begin{align*} 1 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i(t) q_i(t). \tag{*} \end{align*} $$

Set $$ \begin{align*} \pi_i = p_i(\alpha) \circ q_i(\alpha) \in \mathcal{L}(V,V). \end{align*} $$

$V = \im(\pi_1) \oplus \im(\pi_2) \oplus \ldots \oplus \im(\pi_n)$

Firstly, evaluating $(*)$ at $\alpha$, $$ \begin{align*} \id_V = \pi_1 + \pi_2 + \ldots + \pi_n \end{align*} $$ so for all $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} v = \pi_1(v) + \ldots + \pi_n(v) \in \im(\pi_1) + \ldots + \im(\pi_n) \end{align*} $$ and therefore $V = \im(\pi_1) + \ldots + \im(\pi_n)$.

Next, to show $\pi_i\circ\pi_j = 0$ for $i\neq j$, note that $(t-\lambda_j)^{c_j} \mid p_i(t)$. Let $p_i(t) = r(t) \, (t-\lambda_j)^{c_j}$. So $p_i(t) p_j(t) = r(t) \,(t-\lambda_j)^{c_j} p_j(t) = r(t) m_\alpha(t)$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \pi_i\circ\pi_j = r(\alpha)\circ q_i(\alpha)\circ q_j(\alpha) \circ m_\alpha(\alpha) = 0. \end{align*} $$

This gives us the identity $\pi_i = \pi_i\circ\pi_i$, since $$ \begin{align*} \pi_i ={}& \pi_i\circ\id_V \\ ={}& \pi_i \circ (\pi_1 + \ldots + \pi_n) \\ ={}& \pi_i \circ \pi_i. \end{align*} $$

Finally to show the direct sum, suppose for some $u_1, \ldots, u_n, v_1, \ldots, v_n \in V$, $$ \begin{align*} \pi_1(u_1) + \ldots + \pi_n(u_n) = \pi_1(v_1) + \ldots + \pi_n(v_n). \end{align*} $$ For each $i$, apply $\pi_i$ on both sides to get $$ \begin{align*} \pi_i\circ\pi_i(u_i) = \pi_i\circ\pi_i(v_i) \implies \pi_i(u_i) = \pi_i(v_i). \end{align*} $$ Hence $V = \im(\pi_1) \oplus \ldots \oplus \im(\pi_n)$.

$\im(\pi_i) = V_i$

To show $\im(\pi_i) \leq V_i$, note that for all $v\in V$, since $m_\alpha = (t - \lambda_i)^{c_i} p_i(t)$, $$ \begin{align*} 0 = m_\alpha(\alpha) \circ q_i(\alpha) = (\alpha-\lambda_i\, \id_V)^{c_i} \circ p_i(\alpha) \circ q_i(\alpha) = (\alpha - \lambda_i \, \id_V)^{c_i} \circ \pi_i(\alpha) \end{align*} $$ so $\im(\pi_i) \leq \ker((\alpha - \lambda_i \, \id_V)^{c_i}) = V_i$.

Finally to show $V_i \leq \im(\pi_i)$, for $j\neq i$, since $(t-\lambda_i)^{c_i} \mid p_j(t) q_j(t)$, let $p_j(t) q_j(t) = s(t) \,(t-\lambda_i)^{c_i}$. For $v\in V_i = \ker((\alpha - \lambda_i \, \id_V)^{c_i})$, $$ \begin{align*} \pi_j (v) = s(\alpha) \circ (\alpha-\lambda_i \, \id_V)^{c_i} (v) = 0. \end{align*} $$
