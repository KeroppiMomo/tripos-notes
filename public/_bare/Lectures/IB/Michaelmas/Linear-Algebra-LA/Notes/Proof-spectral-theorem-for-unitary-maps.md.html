We will use:

Lemma (properties of eigenvectors of isometries) open_in_new

Let $V$ be an inner product space and $\alpha\in \mathcal{L}(V,V)$ be an isometry.

  1. Every eigenvalue of $\alpha$ has modulus 1.

  2. Eigenvectors of distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.

Proof

Induct on $\dim(V)$. The cases for 0 and 1 are clear.

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, the characteristic polynomial of $\alpha$ in any basis has a root $\lambda$, which is an eigenvalue of $\alpha$. From 1, $|\lambda|=1$. Let $v_1\in V\setminus \set{\v 0}$ be a $\lambda$-eigenvector. By replacing $v_1$ by $v_1/\|v_1\|$, we can assume $\set{v_1}$ is orthonormal.

For all $w\in \langle v_1\rangle^\perp$, $$ \begin{align*} \langle \alpha(w), v_1\rangle ={}& \langle \alpha(w), \lambda\inv \alpha(v_1)\rangle \\ ={}& \overline{\lambda}\inv \langle w, v_1\rangle \\ ={}& 0 \end{align*} $$ so $\alpha(\langle v_1\rangle^\perp) \leq \langle v_1\rangle^\perp$. Therefore we can define the restriction $$ \begin{align*} \alpha': \langle v_1\rangle^\perp &\to \langle v_1\rangle^\perp \\ v &\mapsto \alpha(v) \end{align*} $$ which is still an isometry. Hence by induction hypothesis, there is an orthonormal basis $B'$ for $\langle v_1\rangle^\perp$ consisting of eigenvectors of $\alpha'$ and therefore $\alpha$. Since $V = \langle v_1\rangle \operp \langle v_1\rangle^\perp$, $\set{v_1} \cup B'$ is our desired basis.
