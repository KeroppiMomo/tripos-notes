Uniqueness

Suppose $m_\alpha, \overline{m_\alpha} \neq 0$ are monic polynomials of minimal degree among all non-zero polynomials in $I_\alpha$.

We have $\overline{m_\alpha} \mid m_\alpha$. Therefore there is $q\in \mathbb{F}[t]$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \overline{m_\alpha}(t) = q(t)\cdot m_\alpha(t). \end{align*} $$

But since $\deg(m_\alpha) = \deg(\overline{m_\alpha}) = \deg(q) + \deg(m_\alpha)$, we have $\deg(q) = 0$ and so $q(t) = \lambda$ a constant.

However $m_\alpha$ and $\overline{m_\alpha}$ are monic, so $\lambda=1$. Therefore $\overline{m_\alpha} = m_\alpha$.

Existence in finite-dimensional vector space

Let $n=\dim(V)$. Since $\dim(\mathcal{L}(V,V)) = n^2$, the set $\set{\id_V, \alpha, \alpha^2, \ldots, \alpha^{n^2}}$ is linearly independent. So there exists $\lambda_i\in \mathbb{F}$ not all zero such that $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \sum_{i=0}^{n^2} \lambda_i \alpha^i \in \mathcal{L}(V,V). \end{align*} $$ Therefore $p(\alpha) = 0$ where $p(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n^2} \lambda_i t^i$ is non-zero.
