Definition (linear combination, span) open_in_new

Let $V$ be an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space and $S\subseteq V$.

  • A linear combination of $S$ is an expression of the form $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s s. \end{align*} $$ We assume $\lambda_s = 0$ for all but finitely many $s\in S$ ($\sum'$ denotes finite sum).

  • Define the span of $S$ in $V$ be the set of all linear combinations of $S$ $$ \begin{align*} \langle S\rangle = \Big\{\sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s s \ \Big|\ \lambda_s \in \mathbb{F}\Big\}. \end{align*} $$ We define $\langle \emptyset\rangle = \set{\v 0_V}$ by convention.

  • If $\langle S\rangle = V$, then we say $S$ spans $V$, or $S$ is a spanning set for $V$.

Definition (finite-dimensional vector space) open_in_new

A vector space $V$ is said to be finite-dimensional if $V$ has a finite spanning set.

  • $\langle S\rangle$ is the smallest subspace of $V$ that contains $S$. (Derivation)

  • $\langle S\cup T\rangle = \langle S\rangle + \langle T\rangle$.

Definition (linear independence) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a $\mathbb{F}$-vector space and $S\subseteq V$.

$S$ is said to be linearly independent if for all $\lambda_s \in \mathbb{F}$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s s = \v 0 \quad \implies \quad \lambda_s = 0 \text{ for all } s\in S. \end{align*} $$

$S$ is said to be linearly dependent if it is not linearly independent.

  • If $\v 0\in S$, then $S$ is linearly dependent.

  • If $S$ is linearly independent, then any subset of $S$ is linearly independent.

  • If $S$ is infinite, $S$ is linearly independent iff every finite subset of $S$ is linearly independent.

Definition (basis) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space and $S\subseteq V$.

$S$ is said to be a basis for $V$ if

  1. $S$ is linearly independent; and

  2. $S$ spans $V$.

Theorem (Steinitz exchange lemma) open_in_new

Let $S, T \subseteq V$ be finite subsets in a vector space $V$. Suppose $S$ is linearly independent and $T$ spans $V$. Then

  1. $|S| \leq |T|$; and

  2. there exists a subset $T'\subseteq T$ such that $|T'| = |T| - |S|$ and $S\cup T'$ spans $V$. (This means you can remove $|S|$ elements from $T$, replace them with $S$, and obtain a set that still spans $V$.)

Proof

The picture is $$ \begin{align*} \text{linearly independent set} \quad \subseteq \quad \text{basis} \quad \subseteq \quad \text{spanning set}. \end{align*} $$

Proposition (finite spanning set contains basis) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a vector space, $S\subseteq V$ is finite and $S$ spans $V$.

Then there exists a subset $S' \subseteq S$ such that $S'$ is a basis of $V$.

Proof

Definition (dimension of a finite-dimensional vector space) open_in_new

The dimension $\dim(V)$ of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ is defined as the size of a basis for $V$.

For this to be well-defined, we rely on the fact that

  1. $V$ has a finite basis (Proof);

  2. every basis for $V$ is finite (Proof); and

  3. if $B_1, B_2 \subseteq V$ are bases, then $|B_1| = |B_2|$ (Proof).

Corollary (size of linearly independent set, basis and spanning set) open_in_new

Let $S, T \subseteq V$ be finite subsets in a vector space $V$. Suppose $S$ is linearly independent and $T$ spans $V$. Then $$ \begin{align*} |S| \leq \dim(V) \leq |T| \end{align*} $$ with $|S| = \dim(V)$ iff $S$ is a basis, and $|T| = \dim(V)$ iff $T$ is a basis.

Proof

Proposition (dimension of subspace) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and $U$ $\leq$ $V$.

Then $U$ is also finite-dimensional and $\dim(U) \leq \dim(V)$ with equality iff $U=V$.

Proof

Proposition (extending a basis) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and $U$ $\leq$ $V$.

For any basis $B_U$ of $U$, there exists a basis $B_V$ for $V$ with $B_U \subseteq B_V$.

Proof

Lemma (dimension of quotient spaces) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and $U$ $\leq$ $V$.

Then $V/U$ is finite-dimensional and $$ \begin{align*} \dim(V/U) = \dim(V) - \dim(U). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (rank, nullity) open_in_new

Let $V$ and $W$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $\alpha: V\to W$ is a linear map.

  • $\op{rk}(\alpha) := \dim($$\im(\alpha)$$)$ is the rank of $\alpha$.

  • $\op{n}(\alpha) := \dim($$\ker(\alpha)$$)$ is the kernel of $\alpha$.

Theorem (rank-nullity) open_in_new

Let $V$ and $W$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $\alpha: V\to W$ be a linear map. Suppose $V$ is finite-dimensional.

Then $\ker(\alpha)$ and $\im(\alpha)$ are also finite-dimensional and $$ \begin{align*} \dim(V) = \dim(\ker(\alpha)) + \dim(\im(\alpha)). \end{align*} $$

Proof

Corollary (linear pigeonhole) open_in_new

Let $V$ and $W$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $\alpha: V\to W$ be a linear map.

If $\dim$$(V) = \dim(W)$, then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\alpha$ is injective;

  2. $\alpha$ is surjective;

  3. $\alpha$ is bjective.

Proof

Proposition (linear map is uniquely determined by action on basis) open_in_new

Let $V,W$ be $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces and $B$ be a basis of $V$.

For any function $f: B\to W$, there exists a unique linear map $F: V\to W$ extending $f$.

Proof

Corollary (isomorphism between Fn and n-dimensional vector space) open_in_new

Let $V$ be an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space and $\dim$$(V) = n$.

For any basis $B = \set{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n}$ for $V$, there exists an isomorphism $F_B : \mathbb{F}^n \to V$ given by $$ \begin{align*} \pmat{\lambda_1 \\ \lambda_2 \\ \vdots \\ \lambda_n} \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i. \end{align*} $$

Proof

Definition (coordinate vector) open_in_new

Following the notation here, for $v\in V$, if $v = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i$, then we write $$ \begin{align*} [v]_B \equiv \mathcal{E}_B(v) \equiv F_B\inv(v) = \pmat{\lambda_1 \\ \lambda_2 \\ \vdots \\ \lambda_n} \end{align*} $$ called the coordinate vector of $v$ with respect to $B$.
