Triangularisable $\implies$ $\chi_A$ is a product of linear factors

Let $B$ be a basis of $V$ such that $$ \begin{align*} [\alpha]^B_B = \pmat{a_1 & * & * & \ldots & * \\ 0 & a_2 & * & \ldots & * \\ 0 & 0 & a_3 & \ldots & * \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \ldots & a_n}. \end{align*} $$ Then $$ \begin{align*} \xi_\alpha(t) = \det \pmat{t-a_1 & * & \ldots & * \\ 0 & t-a_2 & \ldots & * \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \ldots & t-a_n} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} (t-a_i) \end{align*} $$ where we have used the determinant of triangular matrix.

$\chi_A$ is a product of linear factors $\implies$ triangularisable

We will show by induction on $n$. True for $n=1$.

Let $\lambda$ be a root of $\chi_\alpha$. $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $\alpha$, so there exists $v_1\in V\setminus \set{\v 0}$ such that $\alpha(v_1) = \lambda v_1$. Extend $\set{v_1}$ to a basis $B = \set{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n}$ for $V$. Then $$ \begin{align*} [\alpha]^B_B = \left(\begin{array}{c|ccc} \lambda & * & \ldots & * \\\hline 0 \\ \vdots & & C \\ 0 \end{array}\right) \end{align*} $$ Note that $\chi_A(t) = (t-\lambda) \chi_C(t)$ by expanding along the first column. So $\chi_C$ is a product of linear factors.

By induction hypothesis, $C$ is triangularisable, i.e. there exists $P\in \mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F})$ such that $PCP\inv$ is upper-triangular.

Set $$ \begin{align*} \tilde P = \left(\begin{array}{c|ccc} 1 & 0 & \ldots & 0 \\\hline 0 \\ \vdots & & P \\ 0 \end{array}\right) \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}). \end{align*} $$ Then $$ \begin{align*} \tilde P [\alpha]^B_B \tilde P\inv = \left(\begin{array}{c|ccc} \lambda & * & \ldots & * \\\hline 0 \\ \vdots && PCP\inv \\ 0 \end{array}\right) \end{align*} $$ is upper-triangular.
