Consider $P(\R)$, the set of all polynomials functions over $\R$, i.e. functions $\R\to\R$ of the form $x\mapsto a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \ldots + a_d x^d$.

$P(\R)^\star\isom \R^\N$

(Example sheet 2 Q10)

$P(\R)$ has a basis $B = \set{b_0, b_1, b_2, \ldots}$ given by $b_k(x) = x^k$.

Consider the function $$ \begin{align*} \phi : P(\R)^\star &\to \R^\N \\ \xi &\mapsto (\xi(b_0), \xi(b_1), \xi(b_2), \ldots). \end{align*} $$

Clearly $\phi$ is linear.

For any sequence $(c_0, c_1, c_2, \ldots) \in \R^\N$, let $$ \begin{align*} \eta: B &\to \R \\ b_k &\mapsto c_k. \end{align*} $$ Then there exists a unique linear extension $\xi: P(\R) \to \R$ of $\eta$. Existence proves surjectivity, and uniqueness proves injectivity. Hence $\phi$ is an isomorphism.

$P(\R) \not\isom \R^\N$
