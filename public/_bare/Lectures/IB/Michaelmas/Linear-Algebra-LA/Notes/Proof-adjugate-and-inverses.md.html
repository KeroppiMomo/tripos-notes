2 follows from 1 since inverses are unique.

To show 1, note that $$ \begin{align*} (\op{adj}(A)\, A)_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^{i+j} \det(A_{\hat{ki}}) a_{kj}. \end{align*} $$

If $i=j$, then this is the expansion of $A$ along the $i$-th column, so $$ \begin{align*} (\op{adj}(A)\, A)_{ii} = \det(A). \end{align*} $$

If $i\neq j$, let $\bar A$ be obtained from $A$ by replacing the $i$-th column by the $j$-th column: $$ \begin{align*} A ={}& \pmat{\v c_1 & \ldots & \v c_i & \ldots & \v c_j & \ldots & \v c_n}, \\ \bar A ={}& \pmat{\v c_1 & \ldots & \v c_j & \ldots & \v c_j & \ldots & \v c_n}. \end{align*} $$

Then expanding about the $i$-th column of $\bar A$, $$ \begin{align*} 0 = \det(\bar A) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^{i+j} \det(\bar A_{ki}) \bar a_{ki} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^{i+j} \det(A_{ki}) a_{kj}. \end{align*} $$
