2 follows from 1 using $\det(A^T) = \det(A)$.

To show 1, fix $j$. Write $$ \begin{align*} A = \pmat{\v c_1 & \ldots & \v c_{j-1} & \v c_j & \v c_{j+1} & \v c_n}. \end{align*} $$ Let $$ \begin{align*} A^{(i)} = \pmat{\v c_1 & \ldots & \v c_{j-1} & \v e_i & \v c_{j+1} & \ldots & \v c_n}. \end{align*} $$ By linearity of $\det(A)$ in the $j$-th column, $$ \begin{align*} \det(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij} \det(A^{(i)}). \end{align*} $$

It suffices to show that $\det(A^{(i)}) = (-1)^{i+j} \det(A_{\hat{ij}})$. We apply elementary operations to $A^{(i)}$:

  1. Apply elementary column operations $T_{kl}$'s to move $\v e_i$ to the left and preserve the order the other $\v c_k$. This takes $j-1$ swaps, so $$ \begin{align*} \bar A^{(i)} ={}& \pmat{\v e_i & \v c_1 & \ldots & \v c_{j-1} & \v c_{j+1} & \ldots & \v c_n}, \\ \det(A^{(i)}) ={}& (-1)^{j-1} \det\bar A^{(i)} \end{align*} $$

  2. Apply elementary column operations $C_{k,l,\lambda}$ to purge all non-zero entries in row $i$ and column $2$ to $n$.

  3. Apply elementary row operations $T_{kj}$ to move row $i$ to row $1$. This takes $i-1$ swaps, so we obtain $$ \begin{align*} \det(\bar A^{(i)}) = (-1)^{i-1} \det\left(\begin{array}{c|ccc} 1 & 0 & \ldots & 0 \\\hline 0 \\ \vdots & & A_{\hat{ij}} \\ 0 \end{array}\right) \end{align*} $$ By the determinant of block-triangular matrices, $$ \begin{align*} \det(\bar A^{(i)}) = (-1)^{i-1} \det(A_{\hat{ij}}). \end{align*} $$

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \det(A^{(i)}) = (-1)^{i+j-2} \det(A_{\hat{ij}}) = (-1)^{i+j} \det(A_{\hat{ij}}) . \end{align*} $$
