Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F}$, and $\alpha\in \mathcal{L}(V,V)$.

$\alpha$ is triangularisable iff $\chi_\alpha(t)$ is a product of linear factors.

Proof

  • If $\chi_\alpha$ is degree $n$ but does not have $n$ roots, then $\chi_\alpha$ is not a product of linear factors and so $\alpha$ is not triangularisable.

  • If $\mathbb{F}$ is $\mathbb{C}$, then $\chi_\alpha(t)$ is a product of linear factors so all $\alpha\in \mathcal{L}(\C^n, \C^n)$ are triangularisable.
