Let $B$ be a basis for a vector space $V$.

Define the basis set of $B$ is defined as $B^\star = \sb{b^\star}{b\in B} \subseteq$ $V^\star$, where $b^\star: V\to \mathbb{F}$ is a linear map given by $$ \begin{align*} b^\star\left(\sum_{c\in B} ' \lambda_c c\right) = \lambda_b, \end{align*} $$ or equivalently, $b^\star$ is the unique linear map defined by $b^\star(c) = \delta_{b,c}$ for all $c\in B$.

  1. $B^\star$ is linearly independent.

  2. If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $B^\star$ is a basis for $V^\star$, called the dual basis of $B$. As a result $V\isom V^\star$.

Proof

  • There exist (infinite-dimensional) vectors spaces $V$ where $V\not\isom V^\star$. (Example)
