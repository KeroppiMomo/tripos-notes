Matrix satisfies equation

Let $v = \sum_j \lambda_j v_j$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(v) &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} \lambda_j \alpha(v_j) &&\text{linearity}\\ &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} \lambda_j \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij} w_i &&\text{definition of $a_{ij}$}\\ &= \sum_{i=1}^{m} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} \lambda_j\right) w_i \\ \end{align*} $$ and therefore $$ \begin{align*} ([\alpha(v)]_C)_i &= \sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} \lambda_j &&\text{coordinate vector}\\ &= \sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} ([v]_B)_j &&\text{coordinate vector}\\ &= ([\alpha]^B_C [v]_B)_i &&\text{matrix multiplication}. \end{align*} $$

Uniqueness

Note that $[v_i]_B = e_i$, the $i$-th standard basis vector of $\mathbb{F}^n$. So for any matrix $A\in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F})$ that satisfies $A[v]_B = [\alpha(v)]_C$, $$ \begin{align*} i\text{-th column of } A &= Ae_i &&\text{matrix multiplication}\\ &= A[v_i]_B \\ &= [\alpha(v_i)]_C &&\text{hypothesis}\\ &= [\alpha]^B_C [v_i]_B &&\text{proven last section}\\ &= [\alpha]^B_C e_i \\ &= i\text{-th column of } [\alpha]^B_C &&\text{matrix multiplication}. \end{align*} $$ Since all columns equal, $A = [\alpha]^B_C$.

Isomorphism

Linearity

Suppose $\alpha, \beta \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ and $\lambda\in \mathbb{F}$. For any vector $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} \big([\alpha]^B_C + \lambda[\beta]^B_C\big)\, [v]_B &= [\alpha]^B_C [v]_B + \lambda[\beta]^B_C [v]_B &&\text{linearity of matrix multiplication} \\ &= [\alpha(v)]_C + \lambda[\beta(v)]_C &&\text{proven} \\ &= [\alpha(v) + \lambda \beta(v)]_C &&\text{linearity of } \mathcal{E}_C \\ &= [(\alpha+\lambda\beta)(v)]_C &&\text{operations in } \mathcal{L}(V,W) \\ &= [\alpha+\lambda\beta]^B_C \,[v]^B &&\text{proven}. \end{align*} $$ (The $\mathcal{E}_C$ mentioned refers to here.)

Since this holds for every $v\in V$, by uniqueness, $$ \begin{align*} [\alpha]^B_C + \lambda[\beta]^B_C = [\alpha+\lambda\beta]^B_C \end{align*} $$ so $\mathcal{E}^B_C$ is linear.

Surjectivity

For any $A' = (a'_{ij}) \in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F})$, set $f: B\to W$ by $$ \begin{align*} f(v_j) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij}' w_i. \end{align*} $$ $f$ has a unique linear extension $F: V\to W$, and by construction $A' = [F]^B_C$. Therefore $\mathcal{E}^B_C$ is surjective.

Injectivity

Continuing from the proof for surjectivity, suppose $\bar F\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ also satisfies $A' = [\bar F]^B_C$. Then by definition, $$ \begin{align*} \bar F(v_j) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij}' w_i \end{align*} $$ so $\bar F$ extends $f$. By uniqueness of linear extension, $F = \bar F$. This proves injectivity.
