Let $K = \ker(\alpha)$.

Well-defined and injective

Let $u,v\in V$. $$ \begin{align*} u + K = v + K &\iff v-u \in K \\ &\iff \alpha(v-u) = \v 0_W \\ &\iff \alpha(u) = \alpha(v) \\ &\iff \bar\alpha(u + K) = \bar\alpha(v + K). \end{align*} $$ "$\Rightarrow$" shows $\bar\alpha$ is well-defined. "$\Leftarrow$" shows $\bar\alpha$ is injective.

Surjective

Let $w\in\im(\alpha)$. There exists $v\in V$ such that $w = \alpha(v)$. Then $w = \alpha(v) = \bar\alpha(v + K) \in \im(\bar\alpha)$.

Linearity

Let $u+K, v+K\in V/K$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \bar\alpha((u+K) + \lambda(v+K)) &= \bar\alpha((u+\lambda v) + K) \\ &= \alpha(u + \lambda v) \\ &= \alpha(u) + \lambda\alpha(v) \\ &= \bar\alpha(u+K) + \lambda\bar\alpha(v + K). \end{align*} $$
