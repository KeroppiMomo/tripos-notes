Clearly $\alpha\inv$ is a bijection.

To show $\alpha\inv$ is a linear map, let $w_1, w_2\in W$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$. Further let $v_1 = \alpha\inv(w_1)$ and $v_2 = \alpha\inv(w_2)$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \alpha\inv(w_1 + \lambda w_2) &= \alpha\inv(\alpha(v_1) + \lambda \alpha(v_2)) \\ &= \alpha\inv(\alpha(v_1 + \lambda v_2)) &&\text{linearity of }\alpha \\ &= v_1 + \lambda v_2 \\ &= \alpha\inv(w_1) + \lambda\alpha\inv(w_2) \end{align*} $$ as required.
