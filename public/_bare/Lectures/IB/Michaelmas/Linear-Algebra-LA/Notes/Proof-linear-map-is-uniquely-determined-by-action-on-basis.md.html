Existence

Since $B$ is a basis, every $v\in V$ can be written as $$ \begin{align*} v = \sum_{b\in B}' \lambda_b b, \qquad \lambda_b\in \mathbb{F}, \end{align*} $$ so define $$ \begin{align*} F(v) = \sum_{b\in B}' \lambda_b f(b). \end{align*} $$

$F$ is well-defined, since if $v = \sum_{b\in B}' \lambda_b b = \sum_{b\in B}' \mu_b b$, then $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{b\in B} (\lambda_b - \mu_b) b = \v 0 \end{align*} $$ so $\lambda_b = \mu_b$ for all $b\in B$ as $B$ is linearly independent.

$f$ is linear, since for all $v, u\in V$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$, write $v = \sum_{b\in B}' \lambda_b b$ and $u = \sum_{b\in B}' \mu_b b$, and then $$ \begin{align*} F(u + \lambda v) &= F\left(\sum_{b\in B} (\mu_b + \lambda \lambda_b) b\right) \\ &= \sum_{b\in B}(\mu_b + \lambda \lambda_b) f(b) \\ &= \sum_{b\in B}\mu_b f(b) + \lambda\sum_{b\in B}\lambda_b f(b) \\ &= F(u) + \lambda F(v). \end{align*} $$

Uniqueness

If $\bar F$ is another linear extension of $f$ on $V$, then for every $v\in V$, writing $v = \sum_{b\in B}' \lambda_b b$, $$ \begin{align*} \bar F(v) &= \bar F\left(\sum_{b\in B} \lambda_b b\right) \\ &= \sum_{b\in B} \lambda_b \bar F(b) \\ &= \sum_{b\in B} \lambda_b f(b) \\ &= F(v) \end{align*} $$ so $\bar F = F$.
