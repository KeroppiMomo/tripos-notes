Let $v\neq \v 0$ be a $\lambda$-eigenvector and $w\neq \v 0$ be a $\mu$-eigenvector. Note that $$ \begin{align*} \langle v,w\rangle ={}& \langle \alpha(v), \alpha(w)\rangle \\ ={}& \langle \lambda v, \mu w\rangle \\ ={}& \lambda \overline{\mu}\, \langle v, w\rangle. \end{align*} $$

  1. Take $v=w$ and $\lambda=\mu$ to see that since $\langle v,v\rangle\neq 0$, $\lambda\overline{\lambda} = 1$ so $|\lambda| = 1$.

  2. For $\lambda\neq \mu$, since $\lambda\overline{\mu} = \lambda|\mu|^2\mu\inv = \lambda\mu\inv \neq 1$, we have $\langle v,w\rangle = 0$.
