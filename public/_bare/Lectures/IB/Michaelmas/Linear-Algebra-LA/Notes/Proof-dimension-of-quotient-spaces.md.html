Let $B_U = \set{u_1, u_2, \ldots, u_m}$ be a basis for $U$ where $m=\dim(U)$. Extend to a basis for $V$: $B_V = \set{u_1, \ldots, u_m, v_{m+1}, \ldots, v_n}$ where $n=\dim(V)$. We claim that $B_{V/U} = \set{v_{m+1} + U, \ldots, v_n + U}$ is a basis for $V/U$ of size $n-m$.

To show $B_{V/U}$ is linearly independent, suppose $$ \begin{align*} \v 0 + U = \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i (v_{i} + U). \end{align*} $$ Then $$ \begin{align*} \v 0 + U = \Big(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i\Big) + U \implies \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i \in U. \end{align*} $$ Using the basis $B_U$, let $\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_m\in\mathbb{F}$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} \mu_j u_j \\ \implies \sum_{j=1}^{n} \mu_j u_j - \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i &= \v 0. \end{align*} $$ Therefore since $B_V$ is linearly independent, all $\lambda_i = \mu_j = 0$.

This also shows that $v_{m+1} + U, \ldots, v_n + U$ are distinct.

To show $B_{V/U}$ spans $V/U$, let $v + U \in V/U$ for some $v\in V$. As $B_V$ spans $V$, there exists $\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_m, \lambda_{m+1}, \ldots, \lambda_n\in\mathbb{F}$ such that $$ \begin{align*} v &= \sum_{j=1}^{m} \mu_j u_j + \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i \\ v+U &= \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{m} \mu_j u_j}_{\in U} + \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i + U \\ &= \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i v_i + U \\ &= \sum_{i=m+1}^{n} \lambda_i (v_i + U). \end{align*} $$
