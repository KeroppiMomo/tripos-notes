$\ker(\alpha^\star) = \im(\alpha)^0$

For any $\theta\in W^\star$, $$ \begin{align*} \theta\in \ker(\alpha^\star) &\iff \alpha^\star(\theta) = 0 \in V^\star \\ &\iff \theta\circ\alpha = 0 \in V^\star \\ &\iff \theta(\alpha(v)) = 0 \text{ for all } v\in V \\ &\iff \theta(w) = 0 \text{ for all } w\in \im(V) \\ &\iff \theta\in \im(\alpha)^0. \end{align*} $$

$\im(\alpha^\star) \leq \ker(\alpha)^0$

For each $\tau\in \im(\alpha^\star) \subseteq V^\star$, write for some $\theta \in W^\star$. $$ \begin{align*} \tau = \alpha^\star(\theta) = \theta\circ\alpha. \end{align*} $$ Then for all $k\in \ker(\alpha)$, $$ \begin{align*} \tau(k) = \theta(\alpha(k)) = \theta(0) = 0 \end{align*} $$ so $\tau\in \ker(\alpha)^0$.

$\im(\alpha^\star) = \ker(\alpha)^0$ if $V$ and $W$ are finite-dimensional

We compute the dimensions: $$ \begin{align*} \dim(\im(\alpha^\star)) ={}& \op{rk}(\alpha^\star) \\ ={}& \op{rk}(\alpha) \tag{1}\\ ={}& \dim(V) - \dim(\ker(\alpha)) \tag{2}\\ ={}& \dim(\ker(\alpha)^0). \tag{3} \end{align*} $$

(1) (2) (3)
