A matrix $A\in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F})$ is said to be in row-reduced echelon form if

  1. every non-zero row of $A$ appears above every zero row;

  2. in a non-zero row, the leftmost non-zero entry (the pivot entry) is 1;

  3. for two non-zero rows $\v r_i$ and $\v r_j$ where $i<j$, the pivot entry in $\v r_j$ is strictly to the right of that of $\v r_i$; and

  4. each pivot entry is the only non-zero entry in its column.

$A$ is said to be in column-reduced echelon form if $A^T$ is in row-reduced echelon form.
