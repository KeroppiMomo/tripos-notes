Equivalence with $\pmat{I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0}$

Define the linear map $$ \begin{align*} \alpha: \mathbb{F}^n &\to \mathbb{F}^m \\ v &\mapsto A v. \end{align*} $$ Note that $[\alpha]^{E_n}_{E_m} = A$ where $E_n$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{F}^n$.

There exists bases $B$ for $\mathbb{F}^n$ and $C$ for $\mathbb{F}^m$ such that $[\alpha]^B_C = \pmat{I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0}$ where $r = \op{rk}(\alpha) = \op{c-rk}(A)$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} A = [\alpha]^{E_n}_{E_m} = [\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{F}^m}]^C_{E_m} \, [\alpha]^B_C \, [\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{F}^n}]^{E_n}_{B} = P \pmat{I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0} Q \end{align*} $$ where $P \in \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{F})$ and $Q \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$.

Column rank equals $\implies$ matrix equivalence

If $\op{c-rk}(A) = \op{c-rk}(A') = r$, then by the above, both are equivalent to $\pmat{I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0}$. Therefore $A$ and $A'$ are equivalent by transitivity of matrix equivalence.

Matrix equivalence $\implies$ column rank equals.

Suppose $A' = PAQ$. Since column rank of matrix products is at most the column rank of each matrix, we have $\op{c-rk}(A') \leq \op{c-rk}(A)$. But then $A = P\inv A' Q\inv$, so $\op{c-rk}(A) \leq \op{c-rk}(A')$.
