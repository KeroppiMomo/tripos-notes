Orthogonal projection is idempotent and self-adjoint

Let $$ \begin{align*} \alpha: \qquad V &\to V \\ w + u &\mapsto w, &&w\in W, u\in W^\perp \end{align*} $$ where $W\leq V$ is a subspace. Recall $V = W\operp W^\perp$.

For all $w,w'\in W$ and $u,u'\in W^\perp$, $$ \begin{align*} \langle \alpha(w+u), w'+u'\rangle ={}& \langle w, w'+u'\rangle \\ ={}& \langle w,w'\rangle \\ ={}& \langle w+u, w'\rangle \\ ={}& \langle w+u, \alpha(w' + u')\rangle \end{align*} $$ so $\alpha$ is self-adjoint.

Also clearly $\alpha^2 = \alpha$.

Idempotent self-adjoint linear map is orthogonal projection

For all $v\in V$, $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(\alpha(v)) = \alpha(v) \implies \alpha(v - \alpha(v)) = 0 \implies v - \underbrace{\alpha(v)}_{\in \im(\alpha)} \in \ker(\alpha) \end{align*} $$ so $V = \ker(\alpha) + \im(\alpha)$.

Moreover, for all $w=\alpha(v) \in \im(\alpha)$ and $u\in \ker(\alpha)$, $$ \begin{align*} \langle w,u\rangle ={}& \langle \alpha(v), u\rangle \\ ={}& \langle v, \alpha(u)\rangle &&\text{self-adjoint} \\ ={}& \langle v, \v 0\rangle \\ ={}& 0 \end{align*} $$ so $V = \ker(\alpha) \operp \im(\alpha)$.

Finally for $w=\alpha(v) \in \im(\alpha)$ and $u\in \ker(\alpha)$, $$ \begin{align*} \alpha(w + u) = \alpha^2(v) + \alpha(u) = \alpha(v) = w \end{align*} $$ so $\alpha = \pi_{\im(\alpha)}$.
