$\langle S\rangle$ is a subspace

$\v 0_V = \sum_{s\in S}' 0s \in \langle S\rangle$ so $\langle S\rangle \neq \emptyset$.

Let $v,v'\in \langle S\rangle$ and $\mu\in\mathbb{F}$. By definition we can let $v = \sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s s$ and $v' = \sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s' s$. So $$ \begin{align*} v + \mu v' &= \sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s s + \mu \sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s' s \\ &= \sum_{s\in S}' (\lambda_s + \mu\lambda_s') s \\ &\in \langle S\rangle. \end{align*} $$

Hence by the subspace test, $\langle S\rangle\leq V$.

$\langle S\rangle$ is contained by any subspace containing $S$

Suppose $W\leq V$ and $S\subseteq W$. For any $\lambda_s\in\mathbb{F}$ for each $s\in S$, since $s\in W$, $\sum_{s\in S}' \lambda_s s \in W$. Therefore $\langle S\rangle\subseteq W$.
