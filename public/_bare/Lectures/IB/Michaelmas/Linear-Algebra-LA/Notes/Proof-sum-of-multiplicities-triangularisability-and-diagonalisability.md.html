Geometric multiplicity and diagonalisability

Since $V_{\lambda_1} \oplus V_{\lambda_2} \oplus \ldots \oplus V_{\lambda_k} \leq V$, $$ \begin{align*} \dim(V) \geq \dim(V_{\lambda_1} \oplus \ldots \oplus V_{\lambda_k}) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \dim(V_{\lambda_i}) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} g_{\lambda_i} \end{align*} $$ where we have used the dimension of direct sums.

Equality occurs iff $V = V_{\lambda_1} \oplus \ldots \oplus V_{\lambda_k}$, which is equivalent to saying $\alpha$ is diagonalisable.

Algebraic multiplicity and triangularisability

Roots of the characteristic polynomial $\chi_\alpha$ are all the eigenvalues of $\alpha$, so $$ \begin{align*} \chi_\alpha(t) = p(t) \prod_{i=1}^{k} (t-\lambda_i)^{a_{\lambda_i}} \end{align*} $$ where $p\in \mathbb{F}[t]$ has no linear factor. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \dim(V) = \deg(\chi_\alpha) = \deg(p) + \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_{\lambda_i} \geq \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_{\lambda_i} \end{align*} $$ as required.

Equality occurs iff $\deg(p) = 0$, i.e. $\chi_\alpha$ is a product of linear factors. This is equivalent to $\alpha$ being triangularisable.
