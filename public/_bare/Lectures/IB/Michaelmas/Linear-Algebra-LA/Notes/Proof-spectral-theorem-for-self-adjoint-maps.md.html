We will use the following properties:

Lemma (properties of eigenvectors of self-adjoint maps) open_in_new

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space, and $\alpha\in \mathcal{L}(V,V)$ be self-adjoint.

  1. All eigenvalues of $\alpha$ are real.

  2. Eigenvectors of distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.

  3. $\alpha$ has an eigenvector.

Proof

Induct on $n=\dim(V)$. It is clear for $n=0$ or $1$.

Using 2, let $v_1\in V\setminus \set{\v 0}$ be an eigenvector of $\alpha$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$, which is real. By replacing $v_1$ by $v_1/\|v_1\|$, we can assume $\set{v_1}$ is orthonormal.

$\alpha(\langle v_1\rangle) \leq \langle v_1\rangle$, so $\alpha(\langle v_1\rangle^\perp) \leq \langle v_1\rangle^\perp$ since $\alpha$ is self-adjoint. Consider the restriction $$ \begin{align*} \alpha': \langle v_1\rangle^\perp &\to \langle v_1\rangle^\perp \\ u &\mapsto \alpha(u). \end{align*} $$ $\alpha'$ is still self-adjoint, and $\dim(\langle v_1\rangle^\perp) = n-1$, so by induction hypothesis, there exists an orthonormal basis $B'$ for $\langle v_1\rangle^\perp$ consisting of eigenvectors of $\alpha'$ and therefore $\alpha$ with real eigenvalues.

Finally, set $B = \set{v_1} \cup B'$. This is a basis for $V = \langle v_1\rangle \operp \langle v_1\rangle^\perp$.
