$\lambda\geq \nu_k$

Iterate the invariance property of $\lambda$: $$ \begin{align*} \lambda_i ={}& \sum_{j_1\in I} \lambda_{j_1} P(j_1, i) \\ ={}& P(k,i) + \sum_{j_1\neq k} \lambda_{j_1} P(j_1, i) \\ ={}& P(k,i) + \sum_{j_1\neq k} P(k,j_1) P(j_1,i) + \sum_{j_1, j_2\neq k} \lambda_{j_2} P(j_2, j_1) P(j_1,i) \\ ={}& \ldots \\ ={}& P(k,i) + \sum_{j_1 \neq k} P(k,j_1) P(j_1,i) + \ldots + \sum_{j_1,j_2,\ldots,j_{n-1}\neq k} P(k,j_{n-1}) \ldots P(j_2, j_1) P(j_1,i) \\ &+ \underbrace{\sum_{j_1,j_2,\ldots,j_n\neq k} \lambda_{j_n} P(j_n, j_{n-1}) \ldots P(j_2, j_1) P(j_1, i)}_{\geq 0} \\ \geq{} & \prob(X_1 = i, T_k\geq 1|X_0 = k) + \prob(X_2 = i, T_k\geq 2|X_0 = k) + \ldots + \prob(X_{n} = i, T_k\geq n|X_0 = k) \\ ={}& \sum_{t=1}^{n} \prob(X_t = i, T_k \geq t|X_0 = k). \end{align*} $$ Since this is true for all $n$, take $n\to \infty$ to get $$ \begin{align*} \lambda_i &\geq \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \prob(X_t = i, T_k \geq t|X_0 = k) \\ &= \expect\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{1}(X_t = i, T_k \geq t) \,\Big|\, X_0 = k\Big) \\ &= \expect\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T_k} \mathbb{1}(X_t = i) \Big| X_0 = k\Big) \\ &= \nu_k(i). \end{align*} $$

$\lambda=\nu_k$ if $P$ is recurrent

Since $P$ is irreducible and recurrent, $\nu_k$ is an invariant measure. Therefore $\rho \equiv \lambda - \nu_k$ is also an invariant measure (since $\lambda\geq \nu_k$).

For each $i\in I$, by irreducibility, there exists $m$ such that $p_{ik}(m)>0$. So $$ \begin{align*} 0 ={}& \underbrace{\lambda_k}_{1} - \underbrace{\nu_k}_{1} \\ ={}& \rho_k \\ ={}& (\rho P^m)_k \\ ={}& \sum_{j\in I} \rho_j\, p_{jk}(m) \\ \geq{} &\rho_i\, p_{ik}(m) \end{align*} $$ Therefore $\lambda_i - \nu_k(i) = \rho_i = 0$ as required.
