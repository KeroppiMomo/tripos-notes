Consider a simple symmetric random walk on $\Z_+ = \set{0,1,2,\ldots}$ with $$ \begin{align*} P(0,1) = 1, \qquad P(i,i+1) = P(i,i-1) = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{for }i\geq 1. \end{align*} $$

Let $T_0 = \inf\sb{n\geq 0}{X_n = 0}$ and $T_1 = \inf\sb{n\geq 0}{X_n=1}$. Write $h_i = \prob(T_0< \infty | X_0 = i)$. We want to find $h_1$. We have the linear equations $$ \begin{align*} h_0 = 1, \qquad h_1 = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}h_2. \end{align*} $$

Write $T_0 = T_1+\tilde T_0$. By the strong Markov property, $\tilde T_0$ given $X_0=2$ is identically distributed as $T_0$ given $X_0 = 1$ and is independent of $T_1$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} h_2 &= \prob(T_0 < \infty | X_0 = 2) \\ &= \prob(T_0 < \infty, T_1 < \infty | X_0 = 2) \\ &= \prob(T_1 < \infty, \tilde T_0 < \infty | X_0 = 2) \\ &= \prob(T_1 < \infty | X_0 = 2) \times \prob(\tilde T_0 < \infty | T_1 < \infty, X_0 = 2) \\ &= \prob(T_0 < \infty | X_0 = 1) \times \prob(T_0<\infty|X_0 = 1) \\ &= h_1^2 \end{align*} $$

Solving $$ \begin{align*} \begin{cases} h_1 = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}h_2 \\ h_2 = h_1^2 \end{cases} \end{align*} $$ gives $h_1 = 1$.
