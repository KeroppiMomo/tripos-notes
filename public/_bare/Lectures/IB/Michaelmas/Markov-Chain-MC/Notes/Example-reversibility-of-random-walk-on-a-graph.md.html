Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite, undirected, finite, connected graph. Let $d(x)$ be the degree of $x\in V$.

Consider a random walk on $G$ where at any time, the chain moves to one of its neighbours uniformly: $$ \begin{align*} P(x,y) = \begin{cases} 1/d(x), &\text{if } (x,y)\in E, \\ 0, &\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

Since $G$ is connected, the chain is irreducible. The detailed balance equations read $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\pi(x)}{d(x)} = \frac{\pi(y)}{d(y)}. \end{align*} $$ Since $G$ is finite, we can normalise this: $$ \begin{align*} \pi(x) = \frac{d(x)}{\sum_{y\in V} d(y)} = \frac{d(x)}{2|E|}. \end{align*} $$

Therefore the chain is positive recurrent and reversible.
