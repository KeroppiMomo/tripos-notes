Since $x\leftrightarrow y$, there exists $m$ such that $p_{yx}(m) > 0$. Since $y$ in recurrent, $$ \begin{align*} 1 ={}& \prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often} | X_0 = y) \\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} \prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often}, X_m = z | X_0 = y) \\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) \prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often}|X_m = z, X_0 = y) \\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) \prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often}|X_0 = z) &&\text{simple Markov}\\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) \prob(T_y < \infty, X_n = y\text{ infinitely often} | X_0 = z) \\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) \prob(T_y < \infty|X_0 = z) \\ &\qquad \qquad \times\prob(X_n = y\text{ infinitely often}|T_y < \infty,X_0 = z) \\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) \prob(T_y < \infty|X_0 = z) \\ &\qquad \qquad \times\prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often} | X_0 = y) &&\text{strong Markov} \\ ={}& \sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) \prob(T_y < \infty|X_0 = z). \end{align*} $$

Since $\sum_{z\in I} p_{yz}(m) = 1$ and all quantities are non-negative, we must have $\prob(T_y < \infty|X_0 = x)$.
