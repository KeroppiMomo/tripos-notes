To show the new process is a Markov chain, for all $x_m, x_{m+1}, \ldots, x_{m+n}\in I$, using the probabilities in a Markov chain, $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(X_m = x_m, X_{m+1} = x_{m+1}, \ldots, X_{m+n}) \\ ={}& \sum_{x_0, \ldots, x_{m-1}\in I} \prob(X_0 = x_0, \ldots, X_{m+n}) \\ ={}& \sum_{x_0, \ldots, x_{m-1}\in I} \lambda_{x_0} P(x_0, x_1) \ldots P(x_{m-1}, x_m) P(x_m, x_{m+1}) \ldots P(x_{m+n-1}, x_{m+n}) \\ ={}& \prob(X_m = x_m) P(x_m, x_{m+1}) \ldots P(x_{m+n-1}, x_{m+n}). \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(X_m=x_m, \ldots, X_{m+n} = x_{m+n} | X_m = i) \\ ={}& \frac{\prob(X_m = x_m, \ldots, X_{m+n} = x_{m+n})}{\prob(X_m = x_m)} \mathbb{1}(X_m = i) \\ ={}& \mathbb{1}(X_m = i) P(x_m, x_{m+1}) \ldots P(x_{m+n-1}, x_{m+n}). \end{align*} $$ Therefore, $(X_{m+n})_{n\geq 0} \sim \mathrm{Markov}(\delta_{i}, P)$ conditional on $X_m = i$.
