Using the probabilities in a Markov chain, $$ \begin{align*} \prob(X_n = x) &= \sum_{x_0, \ldots, x_{n-1}\in I} \prob(X_0 = x_0, \ldots, X_{n-1} = x_{n-1}, X_n = x_n) \\ &= \sum_{x_0, \ldots, x_{n-1} \in I} \lambda_{x_0} P(x_0, x_1) \ldots P(x_{n-2}, x_{n-1}) P(x_{n-1}, x) \\ &= (\lambda P^n)_x \end{align*} $$ which can be seen from how matrices multiply.

  2. By the simple Markov property, conditional on $\set{X_m = x}$, the process $(X_{m+n})_{n\geq 0} \sim \mathrm{Markov}(\delta_x, P)$, so by 1., $$ \begin{align*} \prob(X_{m+n} = y|X_m = x) = (\delta_x P^n)_y = (P^n)_{xy}. \end{align*} $$
