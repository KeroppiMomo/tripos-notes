Given $X_0 = 0$, since $X_n = 0$ if $n$ is even and there are $n/2$ steps to the right and $n/2$ steps to the left, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} p_{00}(2n) ={}& \pmat{2n \\ n} p^{n} (1-p)^n \\ ={}& \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2} p^n (1-p)^n \\ \sim{}& \frac{\sqrt{4\pi n}\, (2n)^{2n} e^{-2n}}{\big(\sqrt{2\pi n}\, n^n e^{-n}\big)} p^n(1-p)^n \\ ={}& \frac{(4p(1-p))^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}. \end{align*} $$ Since $p\neq 1/2$, we have $4p(1-p) < 1$, so $$ \begin{align*} \lim_{n\to \infty} \frac{p_{00}(2(n+1))}{p_{00}(2n)} ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} \frac{(4p(1-p))^{n+1} / \sqrt{\pi(n+1)}}{(4p(1-p))^n \sqrt{\pi n}} \\ ={}& 4p(1-p) \\ <{}& 1 \end{align*} $$ so by the ratio test, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n\geq 0} p_{00}(n) = \sum_{n\geq 0} p_{00}(2n) < \infty. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $0$ is transient. Since the chain is irreducible, the chain is transient.
