As with $d=1$, it suffices to show that $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n\geq 0} p_{00}(n) = \infty. \end{align*} $$

Create a map $$ \begin{align*} f: \Z^2 &\to \R^2 \\ (x,y) &\mapsto \left(\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \end{align*} $$ and write $(X_n^+, X_n^-) = f(X_n)$.

Claim: both $(X_n^+)$ and $(X_n^-)$ are simple random walks on $\Z/\sqrt{2}$ and they are independent.

Proof. We can write $X_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \xi_i$ where $\xi_i$ are independent and identically distributed random variables, with $$ \begin{align*} \prob(\xi_1 = (1,0)) = \prob(\xi_1 = (-1,0)) = \prob(\xi_1 = (0,1)) = \prob(\xi_1 = (0,-1)) = \frac{1}{4}. \end{align*} $$ Then writing $\xi_i = (\xi_i^1, \xi_i^2)$, we have $$ \begin{align*} X_n^+ = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\xi_i^1 + \xi_i^2}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad X_n^- = \sum_{n=1}^{n} \frac{\xi_i^1 - \xi_i^2}{\sqrt{2}}. \end{align*} $$ Then both $(X_n^+)$ and $(X_n^-)$ are simple random walks in $\Z/\sqrt{2}$.

To show that they are independent, it suffices to show that for each $i$, $\xi_i^1 + \xi_i^2$ is independent of $\xi_1^1 - \xi_1^2$. This follows from, for each $a,b\in\set{-1,1}$, $$ \begin{align*} \prob(\xi_i^1 + \xi_i^2 = a, \xi_i^1 - \xi_i^2 = b) ={}& \prob\Big(\xi_i = \big(\frac{a+b}{2}, \frac{a-b}{2}\big)\Big) \\ ={}& \frac{1}{4} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \\ ={}& \prob(\xi_i^1 + \xi_i^2 = a) \times \prob(\xi_{i}^1 + \xi_i^2 = b). \end{align*} $$

Now observe that $X_n = 0$ iff $X_n^+ = X_n^- = 0$. So $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(X_{2n} = 0|X_0 = 0) \\ ={}& \prob(X_{2n}^{+} = X_{2n}^- = 0 | X_0^+ = X_0^- = 0) \\ ={}& \prob(X_{2n}^+ = 0|X_0^+ = 0) \times \prob(X_{2n}^- = 0 | X_0^- = 0) &&\text{independence} \\ \sim{}& \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\pi n} \end{align*} $$ where we have used the asymptotic relation found in the $d=1$ case.

Therefore, for $n\geq n_0$ sufficiently large, we have $\prob(X_{2n} = 0|X_0 = 0) \geq 1/2\pi n$, so $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{n\geq 0} p_{00}(n) ={}& \sum_{n\geq 0} p_{00}(2n) \\ \geq {}& \sum_{n\geq n_0} \frac{1}{2\pi n} \\ ={}& \infty. \end{align*} $$
