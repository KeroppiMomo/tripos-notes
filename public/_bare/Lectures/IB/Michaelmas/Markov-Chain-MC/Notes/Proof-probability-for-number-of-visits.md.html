Let $f_i = \prob($$T_i$ $< \infty|X_0 = i)$. For $r=0$, from definition $\prob(V_i > 0|X_0 = i) = 1 = f_i^0$. Suppose this equation holds for $r\leq k$. Then $$ \begin{align*} \prob(V_i>k+1|X_0 = i) &= \prob(T_i^{(k+1)} < \infty | X_0 = i) \\ &= \prob(T_i^{(k+1)} < \infty, T_i^{(k)} < \infty | X_0 = i) \\ &= \prob(T_i^{(k+1)} < \infty|T_i^{(k)} < \infty, X_0 = i) \times \prob(T_i^{(k)}< \infty | X_0=i) \\ &= \prob(T_i^{(1)} < \infty|X_0 = i) \times \prob(V_i > k|X_0 = i) \tag{*} \\ &= f_i \times f_i^k = f_i^{k+1}. \end{align*} $$ (*) uses the strong Markov property.
