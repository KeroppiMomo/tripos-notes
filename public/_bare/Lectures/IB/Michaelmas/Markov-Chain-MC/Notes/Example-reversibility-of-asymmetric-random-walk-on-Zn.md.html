Consider the random walk on $\Z_n = \set{0,1,\ldots,n-1}$ with transition probabilities $$ \begin{align*} P(i,\ (i+1)\bmod n) &= p, \\ P(i,\ (i-1)\bmod n) &= q \equiv 1-p. \end{align*} $$ for each $i\in \Z_n$, where $p\in (0,1)$ and $p\neq \frac{1}{2}$.

The chain is irreducible, and the uniform distribution $\pi_i = 1/n$ is the unique invariant distribution. But the detailed balance equations are not satisfied: $$ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{n} \times p \neq \frac{1}{n} \times q \end{align*} $$ so the chain is not reversible.

This makes sense since if we start the chain from $\pi$ and run the chain forward in time, we observe a rightward (or leftward) drift, but if we run it backward in time, we observe a leftward (or rightward) drift.
