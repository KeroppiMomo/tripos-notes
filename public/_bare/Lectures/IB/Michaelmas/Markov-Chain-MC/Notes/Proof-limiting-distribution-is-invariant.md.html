$\pi$ is a distribution

We check $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{j\in I} \pi_j ={}& \sum_{j\in I} \lim_{n\to \infty} p_{ij}(n) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} \sum_{j\in I} p_{ij}(n) &&\text{exchanging limit and finite sum}\\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} 1 \\ ={}& 1. \end{align*} $$

$\pi$ is invariant

For any $j\in I$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{k\in I} \pi_k P(k,j) ={}& \sum_{k\in I} \lim_{n\to \infty} p_{ik}(n) P(k,j) \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} \sum_{k\in I} p_{ik}(n) P(k,j) &&\text{exchanging limit and finite sum} \\ ={}& \lim_{n\to \infty} p_{ij}(n+1) \\ ={}& \pi_j. \end{align*} $$
