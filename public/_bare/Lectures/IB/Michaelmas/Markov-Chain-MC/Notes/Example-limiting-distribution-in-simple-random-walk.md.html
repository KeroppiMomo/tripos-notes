Consider the limit of $p_{0x}(n)$ as $n\to \infty$ for all $x$ in a simple symmetric random walk on $\Z$.

To get from 0 to $x$ after $n$ steps, we need to take $(n+x)/2$ steps to the right and $(n-x)/2$ steps to the left. So, assuming $n\equiv x\mod 2$, $$ \begin{align*} p_{0x}(n) ={}& \pmat{n \\ (n-x)/2} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n \\ ={}& \frac{n!}{(\frac{n-x}{2})! (\frac{n+x}{2})!} \frac{1}{2^n} \\ \sim{}& \frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}\, n^n e^{-n}}{2^n\sqrt{\pi(n-x)}\, (\frac{n-x}{2})^{(n-x)/2} e^{-(n-x)/2} \sqrt{\pi(n+x)} (\frac{n+x}{2})^{(n+x)/2} e^{-(n+x)/2}} \\ ={}& \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}} \frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{(n-x)(n+x)}} \frac{n^n}{(n-x)^{(n-x)/2} (n+x)^{(n+x)/2}} \\ \sim{}& \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}} \\ \to{}& 0 \end{align*} $$ where we have used Stirling's formula.

Therefore $$ \begin{align*} 1 = \lim_{n\to \infty} \sum_{x\in I} p_{0x}(n) \neq \sum_{x\in I} \lim_{n\to \infty} p_{0x}(n) = 0 \end{align*} $$
