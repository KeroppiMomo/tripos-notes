Recurrent $\implies$ closed

Suppose $x\in C$, $y\not\in C$ and $x\to y$. Then there exists $m$ such that $p_{xy}(m) > 0$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \prob(V_x < \infty | X_0 = x) \geq \prob(X_m = y|X_0 = x) > 0 \end{align*} $$ so $x$ is transient.

Closed and finite $\implies$ recurrent

Suppose $C$ is closed and finite. Let $x\in C$. There exists some $y\in C$ such that $$ \begin{align*} \prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often} | X_0 = x) > 0. \end{align*} $$ This is because it is impossible to have $X_n = y$ finitely often for all $y\in C$ as $C$ is finite.

Since $x\leftrightarrow y$, there exists $m$ such that $p_{yx}(m) > 0$. Then $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often} | X_0 = y) \\ \geq{}& \prob(X_m = x, X_n = y \text{ infinitely often for } n\geq m| X_0 = y) \\ ={}& \prob(X_m = x|X_0 = y) \prob(X_n = y\text{ infinitely often for } n\geq m| X_0= y, X_m = x) \\ ={}& p_{yx}(m) \prob(X_n = y \text{ infinitely often} | X_0 = x) \qquad \qquad \text{by simple Markov}\\ >{}& 0. \end{align*} $$ So $y$ is recurrent, and therefore $C$ is recurrent.
