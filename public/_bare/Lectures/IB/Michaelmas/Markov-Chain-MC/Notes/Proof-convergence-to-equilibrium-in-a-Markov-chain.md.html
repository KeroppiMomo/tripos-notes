We will use the technique of coupling: fix some $a\in I$, and let

  • $\ouline{X}\sim \mathrm{Markov}(\lambda, P)$;

  • $\ouline{Y}\sim \mathrm{Markov}(\pi, P)$, independent of $\ouline{X}$;

  • $T = \inf\sb{n\geq 1}{X_n = a, Y_n=a}$;

  • $\ouline{Z} = (Z_n)_{n\geq 0}$ given by $$ \begin{align*} Z_n = \begin{cases} X_n, &\text{if }n < T, \\ Y_n, &\text{if }n > T. \end{cases} \end{align*} $$

$\prob(T<\infty) = 1$

To show $\prob(T<\infty) = 1$, we define $\ouline{W} = (W_n)_{n\geq 0}$ by $$ \begin{align*} W_n = (X_n, Y_n). \end{align*} $$ Then $T$ can be expressed as $$ \begin{align*} T = \inf\sb{n\geq 1}{W_n=(a,a)}. \end{align*} $$

$\ouline{W}$ is a Markov chain

For any $x_0, \ldots, x_n, y_0, \ldots, y_n\in I$, using the independence of $\ouline{X}$ and $\ouline{Y}$, $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(W_n=(x_n,y_n)\, |\,W_{n-1}=(x_{n-1}, y_{n-1}), \ldots, W_0 = (x_0, y_0)) \\ ={}& \prob(X_n = x_n, Y_n = y_n \, | \, X_{n-1} = x_{n-1}, Y_{n-1} = y_{n-1}, \ldots, X_0 = x_0, Y_0 = y_0) \\ ={}& \prob(X_n = x_n \,|\, X_{n-1} = x_{n-1}, \ldots, X_{0} = x_0) \times \prob(Y_n = y_n \, | \, Y_{n-1} = y_{n-1}, \ldots, Y_0 = y_0) \\ ={}& P(x_{n-1}, x_n) \times P(y_{n-1}, y_n) \end{align*} $$ so $\ouline{W}$ has the transition matrix $\tilde P$ given by $$ \begin{align*} \tilde P\big((x,y), (x',y')\big) = P(x,x') \, P(y,y'). \end{align*} $$

$\ouline{W}$ is irreducible

Since $\ouline{X}$ is irreducible, there exists $m$ and $n$ such that $p_{xx'}(m) > 0$ and $p_{yy'}(n) > 0$. Using the aperiodicity of $\ouline{X}$, $p_{xx}(l) > 0$ and $p_{yy}(l)>0$ for sufficiently large $l$. Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \tilde p_{(x,y),(x',y')}(l) ={}& p_{xx'}(l) \, p_{yy'}(l) \\ ={}& p_{xx}(l-n) \, p_{xx'}(n) \, p_{yy}(l-m) \, p_{yy'}(m) \\ >{}& 0 \end{align*} $$ for sufficiently large $l$.

$\ouline{W}$ is positive recurrent

Note that $\ouline{W}$ has an invariant distribution $\tilde \pi(x,y) = \pi(x)\, \pi(y)$: $\tilde \pi$ is a distribution since $\sum_{x,y} \tilde p(x,y) = \sum_{x} \pi(x) \times \sum_{y} \pi(y) = 1$, and it is invariant since $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{x,y} \tilde \pi(x,y)\, \tilde P((x,y), (x',y')) ={}& \sum_{x} \pi(x) P(x,x') \times \sum_{y} \pi(y) P(y,y') \\ ={}& \pi(x')\pi(y') \\ ={}& \tilde \pi(x',y'). \end{align*} $$ Therefore $\ouline{W}$ is positive recurrent.

Hence, $\ouline{W}$ is recurrent, and thus $\prob(T<\infty) = 1$.

$\ouline{Z} \sim \mathrm{Markov}(\lambda, P)$

Since $T\geq 1$, $Z_0 = X_0 \sim \lambda$.

To show that $\ouline{Z}$ is a Markov chain with transition matrix $P$, let $z_0, \ldots, z_{n-1}, x, y\in I$, and write $A = \set{Z_0 = z_0, \ldots, Z_{n-1} = z_{n-1}}$. We want to show $\prob(Z_{n+1} = y | Z_n = x, A) = P(x,y)$.

We split into time before $T$ and after $T$: $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(Z_{n+1} = y | Z_n = x, A) \\ ={}& \prob(Z_{n+1} = y, T > n|Z_n = x, A) + \prob(Z_{n+1} = y, T\leq n | Z_n = x, A) \\ ={}& \prob(X_{n+1} = y | T>n, Z_n = x, A) \times \prob(T>n | Z_n = x, A) \\ &+\prob(Y_{n+1} = y | T\leq n, Z_n = x, A) \times \prob(T\leq n|Z_n = x, A). \end{align*} $$ TODO

$\prob(X_n = y) \to \pi(y)$

Finally for all $y\in I$, $$ \begin{align*} &|\prob(X_n = y) - \pi(y)| \\ ={}& |\prob(Z_n = y) - \prob(Y_n = y)| &X_n\sim Z_n, Y_n\sim\pi \\ ={}& |\prob(Z_n = y, n<T) - \prob(Z_n=y, n\geq T) \\ &- \prob(Y_n = y, n<T) + \prob(Y_n = y, n\geq T)| \\ ={}& |\prob(X_n = y, n<T) - \prob(Y_n=y, n\geq T) \\ &- \prob(Y_n = y, n<T) + \prob(Y_n = y, n\geq T)| \\ ={}& |\prob(X_n = y, n<T) - \prob(Y_n = y, n<T)| \\ ={}& \big|\prob(n<T) \cdot \big(\prob(X_n = y|n<T) - \prob(Y_n=y | n<T)\big)\big| \\ \leq {}& \prob(n<T) \\ \to {}& 0 \end{align*} $$ as $n\to \infty$, since $\prob(T<\infty)= 1$.
