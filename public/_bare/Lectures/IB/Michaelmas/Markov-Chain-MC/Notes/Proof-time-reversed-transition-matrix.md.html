Let $\ouline{X}$ be a Markov chain on $I$.

Transition matrix

$\hat P$ is a transition matrix, since for any $x\in I$, $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{y\in I} \hat P(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi(x)} \underbrace{\sum_{y\in I} \pi(y) P(y,x)}_{\pi(x)} = 1 \end{align*} $$ by the invariance of $\pi$.

Markov

For any path $y_0,y_1,\ldots, y_N\in I$, $$ \begin{align*} &\prob(Y_0 = y_0, \ldots, Y_N = y_N) \\ ={}& \prob(X_0 = y_N, X_1 = y_{N-1}, \ldots, X_N = y_0) \\ ={}& \pi(y_N) \, P(y_N,y_{N-1}) \, P(y_{N-1}, y_{N-2}) \ldots P(y_1, y_0) \\ ={}& \pi(y_N) \left(\frac{\pi(y_{N-1})}{\pi(y_N)} \hat P(y_{N-1}, y_{N})\right) \left(\frac{\pi(y_{N-2})}{\pi(y_{N-1})} \hat P(y_{N-2}, y_{N-1})\right) \ldots \left(\frac{\pi(y_0)}{\pi(y_1)} \hat P(y_0, y_1)\right) \\ ={}& \pi(y_0) \, \hat P(y_{0}, y_1) \ldots \hat P(y_{N-2}, y_{N-1}) \, \hat P(y_{N-1}, y_{N}). \end{align*} $$ Therefore $\ouline{Y} \sim \mathrm{Markov}(\pi, \hat P)$.

Invariance

$\pi$ is invariant for $\hat P$, since $$ \begin{align*} \sum_{x\in I} \pi(x) \hat P(x,y) ={}& \sum_{x\in I} \pi(x) \,\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi(x)} P(y,x) \\ ={}& \pi(y) \underbrace{\sum_{x\in I} P(y,x)}_{1} \\ ={}& \pi(y). \end{align*} $$

Irreducibility

For any $x,y\in I$, since $\ouline{X}$ is irreducible, there exists a path $x=x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{k-1}, x_k=y$ such that $$ \begin{align*} P(x_0, x_1) \, P(x_1, x_2) \ldots P(x_{k-1}, x_k) > 0. \end{align*} $$ Multiplying $(\pi(x_0)/\pi(x_1))\times (\pi(x_1)/\pi(x_2)) \times \ldots \times (\pi(x_{k-1})/\pi(x_k))$ (which is non-zero) on both sides to get $$ \begin{align*} \hat P(x_k,x_{k-1}) \, \hat P(x_{k-1}, x_{k-2}) \ldots \hat P(x_{1}, x_0) > 0. \end{align*} $$ Hence $\ouline{Y}$ is irreducible.
