Note that from Schrodinger's equation, $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{\psi}{t} = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\nabla^2 \psi - \frac{i}{\hbar} U(\v x) \psi. \tag{*} \end{align*} $$ We take the time derivative of the probability density $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{\rho}{t} &= \pdd{t}(\psi^\star \psi) \\ &= \psi \pdfd{\psi^\star}{t} + \psi^\star \pdfd{\psi}{t} \\ &= \psi\left(-\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\nabla^2 \psi^\star + \frac{i}{\hbar}U(\v x)\psi^\star\right) + \psi^\star \left(\frac{i\hbar}{2m} \nabla^2 \psi - \frac{i}{\hbar}U(\v x)\psi\right) &&\text{using (*)} \\ &= \frac{i\hbar}{2m} (\psi^\star\nabla^2\psi - \psi\nabla^2\psi^\star) \\ &= \nabla\cdot \underbrace{\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\left(\psi^\star \nabla \psi - \psi\nabla\psi^\star\right)}_{-\v j} \end{align*} $$ Therefore $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{\rho}{t} + \nabla\cdot\v j = 0. \end{align*} $$
