Definition (probability density for a wavefunction) open_in_new

We interpret the probability density of finding a particle at a particular position $$ \begin{align*} \rho(x,t) = |\psi(\v x,t)|^2 \end{align*} $$ where $\psi$ is the wavefunction.

Therefore we require

Normalisation condition

$$ \begin{align*} \int_{\R^3} |\psi(\v x)|^2 \d V = 1. \end{align*} $$

Starting from Schrodinger's equation, we define the probability current $$ \begin{align*} \v j(\v x,t) = -\frac{i\hbar}{2m}(\psi^\star \nabla\psi - \psi\nabla\psi^\star) \end{align*} $$ which satisfies the continuity equation $$ \begin{align*} \pdfd{\rho}{t} + \nabla\cdot \v j = 0. \end{align*} $$

(Derivation)

By the divergence theorem, $$ \begin{align*} \dd{t}\int_{V} \rho(\v x,t) \d V = -\int_{\partial V} \v j\cdot \d \v S \implies \dd{t}\int_{\R^3} \rho(\v x,t) \d V = 0. \end{align*} $$ So if $\psi$ is normalised, all subsequent states are normalised.
