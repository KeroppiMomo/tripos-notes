Momentum operator open_in_new

The momentum operator is defined as the vector of operators $\vhat p = (\hat p_1, \hat p_2, \hat p_3)$, given by $$ \begin{align*} \hat p_j = -i\hbar \pdd{x_j}, \qquad \vhat p = -i\hbar \nabla. \end{align*} $$

  • $\vhat p$ is Hermitian, and so are $\hat p_i$. (Derivation)

The eigenfunction of definite momentum $\v p$ is $$ \begin{align*} \psi_\v p(\v x) = \exp\left(i \frac{\v p}{\hbar}\cdot \v x\right), \end{align*} $$

  • This is the plane-wave solution with wave vector $\v k = \v p/\hbar$ (de Broglie relation).

  • This is non-normalisable.

  • Completeness of $\set{\psi_\v p(\v x)}$ is equivalent to the existence of the Fourier transform of $\psi(\v x)$.

  • $\vhat p$ has a continuous spectrum, because the eigenvalues $\v p$ are continuous.

Position operator open_in_new

The position operator is a vector of operators $\vhat x = (\hat x_1, \hat x_2, \hat x_3)$ given by $$ \begin{align*} \hat x_i f(\v x) = x_i f(\v x), \qquad \vhat x f(\v x) = \v x f(\v x). \end{align*} $$

  • For any function $U$, the operator $U(\vhat x)$ behaves as $U(\vhat x) f(\v x) = U(\v x) f(\v x)$.

  • $\vhat x$ and $U(\vhat x)$ are Hermitian.

The eigenfunction in one dimension of definite position $x_0$ is $$ \begin{align*} \psi_{x_0}(x) = \delta(x - x_0). \end{align*} $$ And in three dimensions, $$ \begin{align*} \psi_\v X(\v x) = \delta^{(3)} (\v x - \v X) = \delta(x_1 - X_1) \delta(x_2 - X_2) \delta(x_3 - X_3). \end{align*} $$

  • These are non-normalisable, since $\int \delta^2(x-x_0)\d x$ does not exist.

Hamiltonian operator open_in_new

The Hamiltonian operator is defined as $$ \begin{align*} \hat H = - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + U(\v x). \end{align*} $$

  • $\hat H$ $= (\vhat p\cdot \vhat p)/2m + U(\v x)$, which is the same in classical mechanics.

  • $\hat H$ is Hermitian, because $\vhat p$ and $\vhat x$ are Hermitian.

Stationary states are eigenfunction of $\hat H$, i.e. $\hat H \psi = E\psi$.

Angular momentum operator open_in_new

The angular momentum operator $\vhat L = (\hat L_1, \hat L_2, \hat L_3)$ is given by $$ \begin{align*} \vhat L ={}& \vhat x\times \vhat p = -i\hbar \v x\times \nabla, \\ \hat L_i ={}& -i\hbar \epsilon_{ijk} x_j \pdd{x_k}. \end{align*} $$ The total angular momentum operator is $$ \begin{align*} \hat L^2 = \hat L_1^2 + \hat L_2^2 + \hat L_3^2. \end{align*} $$
