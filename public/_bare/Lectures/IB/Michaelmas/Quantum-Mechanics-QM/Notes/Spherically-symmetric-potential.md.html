Spherically symmetric stationary state open_in_new

In spherical polars, the time-independent Schrodinger's equation is $$ \begin{align*} E\psi = U(\v x) \psi - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Big(\frac{1}{r} \pdd[2]{r}\big(r\psi\big) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta} \pdd{\theta}\big(\sin\theta \pdfd{\psi}{\theta}\big) + \frac{1}{r^2 \sin^2\theta} \pdfd[2]{\psi}{\phi}\Big). \end{align*} $$

For a spherically symmetric potential $U(\v x) = U(r)$, we look for a spherically symmetric stationary state $\psi(\v x) = \psi(r)$. Then $$ \begin{cases} Er\psi = U(r) r\psi - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dd[2]{r}(r\psi), \\ \int_{\R^3} |\psi(\v x)|^2 \d V < \infty \iff \int_{0}^{\infty} |\psi(r)|^2 r^2 \d r < \infty. \end{cases} $$

Using $\sigma(r) = r\psi(r)$, this is equivalent to solving $$ \begin{cases} E\sigma = U(r) \sigma - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \dfd[2]{\sigma}{r}, \\ \sigma(0) = 0, \\ \int_{0}^{\infty} |\sigma(r)|^2 \d r <\infty, \end{cases} $$ which is the Schrodinger equation on the half line $r>0$. Equivalently, this is the odd-parity solution of the Schrodinger equation with the symmetric potential $U(-r) = U(r)$.

Maximal set of commuting observables open_in_new

If the potential is spherically symmetric ($U(\v x) = U(r)$), then $$ \begin{align*} \set{\hat H, \hat L^2, \hat L_3} \end{align*} $$ form a set of mutually commuting operators. (Derivation)

In fact this set is maximal, meaning we cannot construct another independent operator (other than the unit operator) that commutes with $\hat H$, $\hat L^2$ and $\hat L_3$.

Spherical harmonics open_in_new

In spherical polars, $$ \begin{align*} \hat L^2 ={}& -\frac{\hbar^2}{\sin^2 \theta} \left(\sin\theta \pdd{\theta}\left(\sin\theta \pdd{\theta}\right) + \pdd[2]{\phi}\right), \\ \hat L_1 ={}& i\hbar\left(\sin\phi \pdd{\theta} + \cos\phi \cot\theta \pdd{\phi}\right), \\ \hat L_2 ={}& i\hbar\left(-\cos\phi \pdd{\theta} + \sin\phi \cot\theta \pdd{\phi}\right), \\ \hat L_3 ={}& -i\hbar \pdd{\phi}. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

We look for a simultaneous eigenfunction of $\hat L^2$ and $\hat L_3$ of the form $\psi(\theta, \phi) = Y(\theta) Z(\phi)$. The solutions are $$ \begin{align*} Y_{l,m}(\theta, \phi) ={}& P_{l,m}(\cos\theta) \exp(im\phi), \\ ={}& \sin^{|m|}(\theta) \left(\dd[|m|]{(\cos\theta)} P_l(\cos\theta)\right) \exp(im\phi),& l,m\in \Z, \quad -l\leq m\leq l \end{align*} $$ where $P_{l,m}$ are the associated Legendre functions and $P_l$ is the Legendre polynomial, and the eigenvalues are $$ \begin{align*} \hat L^2 Y_{l,m} ={}& l(l+1) \hbar^2 Y_{l,m}, &&l=0,1,2,\ldots\\ \hat L_3 Y_{l,m} ={}& m\hbar Y_{l,m}, && m=-l,-l+1,\ldots,l. \end{align*} $$ $l$ is called the total angular momentum quantum number and $m$ is called the azimuthal quantum number. Hydrogen atom open_in_new

For the hydrogen atom, we have the Coulomb potential $$ \begin{align*} U(\v x) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}. \end{align*} $$ We seek for a simultaneous eigenstate of $\set{\hat H, \hat L^2, \hat L_3}$ in the form $\psi(\v x) = \chi(r)\, Y_{l,m}(\theta,\phi)$, The solutions are $$ \begin{align*} \psi_{N,l,m}(r,\theta,\phi) = \xi^l L_{N,l}(\xi) \exp(-\xi) \, Y_{l,m}(\theta, \phi) \end{align*} $$ where $L_{N,l}$ is the generalised Laguerre polynomial and $\xi = (e^2 m_e r)/(4N\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar^2)$, and the eigenvalues are $$ \begin{align*} E ={}& -\frac{e^4 m_e}{32\pi^2 \epsilon_0^2 \hbar^2} \frac{1}{N^2}, &&N=1,2,3,\ldots, \\ L^2 ={}& l(l+1)\hbar^2, &&l = 0,1,,\ldots,N-1, \\ L_3 ={}& m\hbar, &&m=-l, -l+1, \ldots, l. \end{align*} $$ (Derivation)

In particular, the ground state wavefunction $N=1, l=m=0$ are given by $$ \begin{align*} \psi(r) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi r_B^3}} e^{-r/r_B}, \qquad \text{ where } r_B = \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar^2}{e^2m_e}. \end{align*} $$ $r_B$ is the Bohr radius, the radius of the lowest orbit in a Bohr atom.
