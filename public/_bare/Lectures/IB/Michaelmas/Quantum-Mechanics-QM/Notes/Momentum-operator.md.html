The momentum operator is defined as the vector of operators $\vhat p = (\hat p_1, \hat p_2, \hat p_3)$, given by $$ \begin{align*} \hat p_j = -i\hbar \pdd{x_j}, \qquad \vhat p = -i\hbar \nabla. \end{align*} $$

  • $\vhat p$ is Hermitian, and so are $\hat p_i$. (Derivation)

The eigenfunction of definite momentum $\v p$ is $$ \begin{align*} \psi_\v p(\v x) = \exp\left(i \frac{\v p}{\hbar}\cdot \v x\right), \end{align*} $$

  • This is the plane-wave solution with wave vector $\v k = \v p/\hbar$ (de Broglie relation).

  • This is non-normalisable.

  • Completeness of $\set{\psi_\v p(\v x)}$ is equivalent to the existence of the Fourier transform of $\psi(\v x)$.

  • $\vhat p$ has a continuous spectrum, because the eigenvalues $\v p$ are continuous.
