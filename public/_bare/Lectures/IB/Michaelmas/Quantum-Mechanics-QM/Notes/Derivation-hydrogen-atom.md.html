The Hamiltonian can be written as $$ \begin{align*} \hat H\psi = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}\psi - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m_er} \pdd[2]{r}(r\psi) + \frac{\hat L^2\psi}{2m_er^2}. \end{align*} $$ Substituting $\psi = \chi(r)\, Y_{m,l}(\theta, \phi)$ gives $$ \begin{align*} E\chi = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} \chi - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\left(\dfd[2]{\chi}{r} + \frac{2}{r} \dfd{\chi}{r}\right) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2m_er^2} \chi. \end{align*} $$ Defining constants $$ \begin{align*} \nu^2 = \frac{-2m_eE}{\hbar^2} > 0, \qquad \beta = \frac{e^2 m_e}{2\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar^2}, \end{align*} $$ we have $$ \begin{align*} \chi'' + \frac{2}{r}\chi' - \frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}\chi + \frac{\beta}{r}\chi - \nu^2 \chi = 0. \end{align*} $$

Make the substitution $$ \begin{align*} \chi(r) = f(r) \,\exp(-\nu r) \end{align*} $$ which gives $$ \begin{align*} \chi'(r) ={}& (f'(r) - \nu f(r))\exp(-\nu r), \\ \chi''(r) ={}& (f''(r) - 2\nu f'(r) + \nu^2 f(r)) \exp(-\nu r) \end{align*} $$ and we get $$ \begin{align*} f'' + \frac{2}{r}(1 - \nu r)f' - \frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}f + \frac{1}{r}(\beta - 2\nu)f = 0. \end{align*} $$

Since this has a regular singular point at $r=0$, we look for a Frobenius series solution $$ \begin{align*} f(r) = r^\sigma \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nr^n. \end{align*} $$

Setting the coefficient of $r^{\sigma-2}$ to zero, $$ \begin{align*} a_0 \big(\sigma(\sigma-1) + 2\sigma - l(l+1) \big) = 0 \implies \sigma = l, \,-l-1. \end{align*} $$ For the root $\sigma=-l-1$, $\chi(r) \sim 1/r^{l+1}$ near $r=0$, which leads to a singular wavefunction. Hence $\sigma = l$.

Now compare the coefficient of $r^{l+n-2}$, $$ \begin{align*} & (n+l)(n+l-1)a_{n} + 2(n+l)a_n - 2\nu(n+l-1)a_{n-1} - l(l+1)a_{n} + (\beta - 2\nu) a_{n-1} = 0 \\ \implies & a_n = \frac{2\nu(n+l) - \beta}{n(n+2l+1)} a_{n-1}. \end{align*} $$

If the series does not terminate, then $a_n/a_{n-1} \sim 2\nu/n$ as $n\to \infty$, so $$ \begin{align*} &f(r) \sim a_0 r^l \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(2\nu)^n}{n!} r^n = a_0 r^l \exp(2\nu r) \\ \implies& \chi(r) \sim a_0 r^l \exp(\nu r) \to \infty \end{align*} $$ which results in a non-normalisable wavefunction.

Hence we need the series to terminate, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} \nu = \frac{\beta}{2N} \end{align*} $$ for some integer $N > l$. Substituting the definition of $\nu$ and $\beta$, $$ \begin{align*} E = E_N = -\frac{e^4 m_e}{32\pi^2 \epsilon_0^2 \hbar^2} \frac{1}{N^2}. \end{align*} $$
