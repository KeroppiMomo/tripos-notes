From here, we have shown that $[\hat L^2, \hat L_3] = 0$.

For a spherically symmetric potential $U$, the Hamiltonian is $$ \begin{align*} \hat H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 + U(r) = \frac{|\vhat p|^2}{2m} + U(\hat r) \end{align*} $$ where $\hat r f(\v x) = rf(\v x)$. Moreover $$ \begin{align*} [\hat L_i, |\vhat p|^2] ={}& [\hat L_i, \hat p_j\hat p_j] \\ ={}& \hat p_j [\hat L_i, \hat p_j] + [\hat L_i, \hat p_j] \hat p_j \\ ={}& i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}(\hat p_j \hat p_k + \hat p_k \hat p_j) \\ ={}& 0, \end{align*} $$ and similarly $$ \begin{align*} [\hat L_i, \hat r^2] = [\hat L_i, \hat x_j\hat x_j] = 0. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $[\hat L_i, \hat H] = 0$ and therefore $[\hat L^2, \hat H] = 0$.
