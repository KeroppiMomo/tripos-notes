Lagrange method "works" when we can find $\v \lambda^\ast\in\R^m$ and $\v x^\ast\in\mathcal{X}$ such that $\v x^\ast$ minimises $L(\v x,\v \lambda^\ast)$ and $\v x^\ast$ is feasible, i.e. $\v x^\ast\in\mathcal{X}(\v \lambda)$. Then we can apply Lagrange sufficiency theorem to insist that $\v x$ is optimal.

If Lagrange method works, we can observe using weak duality that $$ \begin{align*} f(\v x^\ast) &\geq \min_{\v x\in\mathcal{X}(\v b)} f(\v x) &&\text{since }\v x^\ast\in\mathcal{X}(\v b) \\ &\geq \max_{\v \lambda\in\Lambda} g(\v \lambda) &&\text{by weak duality} \\ &\geq g(\v \lambda^\ast) &&\text{since }\v \lambda^\ast\in \Lambda \\ &= f(\v x^\ast) - {\v\lambda^\ast}^T(h(\v x^\ast) - \v b) &&\text{since $\v x^\ast$ minimises $L(\v x,\v \lambda^\ast)$} \\ &= f(\v x^\ast) &&\text{since } \v x^\ast \in \mathcal{X}(\v b) \end{align*} $$ so strong duality holds: $$ \begin{align*} \min_{\v x\in\mathcal{X}(\v b)} f(\v x) = \max_{\v\lambda\in\Lambda} g(\v \lambda) = f(\v x^\ast). \end{align*} $$

If strong duality holds, it means $$ \begin{align*} \min_{\v x\in\mathcal{X}(\v b)} f(\v x) = \max_{\v \lambda\in\Lambda} g(\v \lambda) \end{align*} $$ so there exists $\v x^\ast\in\mathcal{X}(\v b)$ and $\v \lambda^\ast\in\Lambda$ such that $f(\v x^\ast) = g(\v \lambda^\ast)$. Then Lagrange method will succeed using this choice of $\v \lambda^\ast$.

The "almost" caveat corresponds to when we define strong duality as $\inf_{\v x\in\mathcal{X}(\v b)} f(\v x) = \sup_{\v \lambda\in\Lambda}g(\v \lambda)$. In this case the extrema may not be attained, but we don't care about this here.
