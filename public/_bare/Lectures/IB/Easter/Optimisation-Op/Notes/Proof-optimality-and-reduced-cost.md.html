Let $\v x$ be a basic feasible solution with basic variables $B(1), B(2), \ldots, B(m)$.

$\v x$ is optimal iff there exists $\v \lambda$ such that it is feasible (i.e. $A^T\v \lambda \leq \v c$) and satisfies complementary slackness (i.e. $x_i(\v c - A^T\v \lambda)_i = 0$ for all $i$).

Under assumption C, $x_i = 0$ iff $i=B(1), B(2), \ldots, B(m)$, so complementary slackness holds iff $$ \begin{align*} \v c_B = B^T \v \lambda \iff \v \lambda = (B^T)\inv \v c_B. \end{align*} $$

Therefore $\v x$ is optimal iff this $\v \lambda$ is feasible, i.e. $$ \begin{align*} A^T(B^T)\inv \v c_B \leq \v c \iff \v c_B^T B\inv A \leq \v c^T \iff \bar{\v c} \geq \v 0. \end{align*} $$
