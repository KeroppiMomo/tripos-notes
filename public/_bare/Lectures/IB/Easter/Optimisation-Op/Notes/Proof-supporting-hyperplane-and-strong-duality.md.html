The crucial observation is to split up $\mathcal{X}$ into $\mathcal{X}(\v c)$: $$ \begin{align*} g(\v \lambda) &= \min_{\v x\in\mathcal{X}} \left[f(\v x) - \v \lambda^T(h(\v x) - \v b)\right] &&\text{definition of $g$}\\ &= \min_{\v c} \min_{\v x\in \mathcal{X}(\v c)} \left[f(\v x) - \v \lambda^T(h(\v x) - \v b)\right] &&\mathcal{X} = \bigcup_{\v c} \mathcal{X}(\v c)\\ &= \min_{\v c} \min_{\v x\in \mathcal{X}(\v c)} \left[f(\v x) - \v \lambda^T(\v c-\v b)\right] &&\v x\in\mathcal{X}(\v c)\\ &= \min_{\v c}\left[\left(\min_{\v x\in\mathcal{X}(\v c)} f(\v x)\right) -\v \lambda^T(\v c - \v b)\right] \\ &= \min_{\v c}\left[\phi(\v c) - \v \lambda^T(\v c - \v b)\right] &&\text{definition of $\phi$}. \tag{*} \end{align*} $$

Note that when $\v c = \v b$, we have $\phi(\v c) - \v \lambda^T(\v c - \v b) = \phi(\v b)$.

Supporting hyperplane exists $\implies$ strong duality

Suppose $\phi$ has a supporting hyperplane $\v \lambda$ at $\v b$. By definition $$ \begin{align*} \phi(\v c) \geq \phi(\v b) + \v \lambda^T (\v c - \v b). \end{align*} $$ Note that $$\phi(\v c) - \v \lambda^T(\v c - \v b) \geq \phi(\v b)$$ so by (*), $$ \begin{align*} g(\v \lambda) = \min_{\v c} \left[\phi(\v c) - \v \lambda^T (\v c - \v b)\right] = \phi(\v b) \end{align*} $$ so strong duality holds.

Strong duality $\implies$ supporting hyperplane exists

Suppose strong duality holds for some $\v \lambda$, i.e. $g(\v \lambda) = \phi(\v b)$.

(*) implies for any $\v c$, $$ \begin{align*} \phi(\v b) = g(\v \lambda) \leq \phi(\v c) - \v \lambda^T(\v c - \v b) \end{align*} $$ so $\phi$ has a supporting hyperplane $\v \lambda$ at $\v b$.
