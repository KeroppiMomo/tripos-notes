We will show this is equivalent to the first-order condition.

First-order condition implies increasing directional gradient

Apply the first-order condition with $(\v x,\v y)$ and $(\v y,\v x)$: $$ \begin{align*} f(\v y) &\geq f(\v x) + (\v y - \v x)\cdot \nabla f(\v x), \\ f(\v x) &\geq f(\v y) + (\v x - \v y)\cdot \nabla f(\v y). \end{align*} $$ Adding yields $$ \begin{align*} 0 \geq (\v y-\v x) \cdot (\nabla f(\v x) - \nabla f(\v y)) \end{align*} $$ as required.

Increasing directional gradient implies first-order condition

Let $\v z(t) = (1-t)\v x + t \v y$. Since $f$ has increasing directional gradient, $$ \begin{align*} (\v z - \v x) \cdot (\nabla f(\v z) - \nabla f(\v x)) \geq 0 \implies (\v z-\v x)\cdot \nabla f(\v z) \geq (\v z-\v x)\cdot \nabla f(\v x). \end{align*} $$ We then use the fundamental theorem of calculus $$ \begin{align*} f(\v y) - f(\v x) &= \int_0^1 \dfd{f(\v z(t))}{t}\, dt \\ &= \int_{0}^{1} \v z'(t)\cdot \nabla f(\v z(t))\, dt \\ &= \int_{0}^{1} (\v y-\v x)\cdot \nabla f(\v z)\, dt \\ &\geq \int_{0}^{1} (\v y-\v x)\cdot \nabla f(\v x)\, dt &&\v z - \v x = t(\v y-\v x) \\ &= (\v y-\v x)\cdot \nabla f(\v x), \end{align*} $$ which is the first-order condition.
