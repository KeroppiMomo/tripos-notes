Convexity implies first-order condition

Suppose $f$ is convex.

First consider $n=1$. Then $$ \begin{align*} f\big(x+t(y-x)\big) &\leq (1-t)f(x) + tf(y) \\ f(y) &\geq f(x) + \frac{f(x+t(y-x)) - f(x)}{t}. \end{align*} $$ Taking limit as $t\to 0$, we obtain $$ \begin{align*} f(y) &\geq f(x) + f'(x)(y-x) \end{align*} $$ as required.

Now consider any $n$. Define, for $t\in[0,1]$, $$ \begin{align*} g(t) = f((1-t)\v x + t\v y). \end{align*} $$ $g$ is convex since $$ \begin{align*} g(\lambda t_1 + (1-\lambda)t_2) &= f\big((1-\lambda t_1 -(1-\lambda)t_2)\v x + (\lambda t_1 + (1-\lambda)t_2)\v y\big) \\ &= f\Big(\lambda\big((1-t_1)\v x + t_1\v y)\big) + (1-\lambda)\big((1-t_2)\v x + t_2\v y\big)\Big)\\ &\geq \lambda f((1-t_1)\v x + t_1 \v y) + (1-\lambda) f((1-t_2)\v x + t_2\v y) &&\text{convexity of $f$}\\ &= \lambda g(t_1) + (1-\lambda) g(t_2). \end{align*} $$ Using the $n=1$ result, $$ \begin{align*} f(\v y) &= g(1) \\ &\geq g(0) + g'(0) \\ &= f(\v x) + \nabla f(\v x)^T (\v y - \v x) &&\text{chain rule} \end{align*} $$ as required.

First-order condition implies convexity

Assume the first-order condition holds for $f$. Let $\v z = \lambda \v x + (1-\lambda) \v y$. We have $$ \begin{align*} f(\v x) &\geq f(\v z) + \nabla f(\v z)^T (\v x-\v z), \tag{1} \\ f(\v y) &\geq f(\v z) + \nabla f(\v z)^T (\v y-\v z). \tag{2}\\ \end{align*} $$ Multiplying (1) by $\lambda$ and (2) by $(1-\lambda)$ and add them, $$ \begin{align*} \lambda f(\v x) + (1-\lambda) f(\v y) &\leq f(\v z) + \nabla f(\v z)^T \underbrace{(\lambda\v x + (1-\lambda)\v y - \v z)}_{0} \\ &= f(\lambda \v x + (1-\lambda)\v y) \end{align*} $$ which shows that $f$ is convex.
