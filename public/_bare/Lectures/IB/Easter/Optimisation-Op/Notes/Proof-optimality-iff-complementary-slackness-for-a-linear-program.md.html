Let $u_i = \lambda_i(\v a_i^T \v x - b_i)$ and $v_j = (c_j - \v \lambda^T \v A_j) x_j$. Note that $$ \begin{align*} \sum_i u_i &= \v \lambda^T A\v x - \v \lambda^T \v b, \\ \sum_j v_j &= \v c^T \v x - \v \lambda^T A\v x \\ \implies \sum_i u_i + \sum_j v_j &= \v c^T \v x - \v \lambda^T \v b. \end{align*} $$

If complementary slackness holds, i.e. $u_i = 0 = v_j$ for all $i$ and $j$, then $\v c^T \v x = \v \lambda^T \v b$. Hence by weak duality, $\v x$ and $\v \lambda$ are optimal.

Now conversely suppose $\v x$ and $\v \lambda$ are optimal. Linear programs satisfy strong duality, so $\v c^T \v x = \v \lambda^T \v b$ and therefore $\sum_i u_i + \sum_j v_j = 0$.

But note from the conditions of the primal and dual problems that $u_i \geq 0$ and $v_i\geq 0$ for all $i,j$. Hence $u_i = 0 = v_j$ for all $i,j$.
