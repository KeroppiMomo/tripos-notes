Take $$ \begin{align*} T = \frac{1}{2} m|\dv x|^2, \qquad V = V(\v x). \end{align*} $$ The Lagrangian is $$ \begin{align*} L = \frac{1}{2} m|\dv x|^2 - V(\v x). \end{align*} $$ Applying Euler-Lagrange to the least action principle, $$ \begin{align*} 0 &= \dd{x}\left(\pdfd{L}{\dot x_i}\right) - \pdfd{L}{x_i} &&i=1,2 \\ &= \dd{x}(m\dot x_i) + \pdfd{V}{x_i} \\ m\ddot x_i &= -\pdfd{V}{x_i} \\ m\ddv x &= -\nabla V \end{align*} $$ which is Newton's second law.

Also, since $L$ does not depend on $t$, we can use the first integral to get $$ \begin{align*} \text{const.} &= L - \dot x_1\pdfd{L}{\dot x_1} - \dot x_2 \pdfd{L}{\dot x_2} \\ &= \frac{1}{2}m|\dv x|^2 - V(\v x) - m\dot x_1^2 - m\dot x_2^2 \\ &= - T - V \end{align*} $$ so the total energy $T+V$ is conserved. This demonstrates Noether's theorem.
