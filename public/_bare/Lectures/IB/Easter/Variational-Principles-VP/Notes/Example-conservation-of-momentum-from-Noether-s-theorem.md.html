Take $$ \begin{align*} \v x=(y,z), \qquad L = \frac{1}{2}\dot y^2 + \frac{1}{2}\dot z^2 - V(y-z). \end{align*} $$ The potential energy of the two particles depend only on the distance between them.

Let $$ \begin{align*} \v X \equiv (Y,Z) = (y+s, z+s). \end{align*} $$ $f$ is independent of $s$ (translational invariance).

Noether's theorem gives $$ \begin{align*} \text{const.} = \pdfd{f}{\dot y} \dfd{Y}{s} + \pdfd{f}{\dot z} \dfd{Z}{s} = \dot y + \dot z \end{align*} $$ which is conservation of momentum (in one direction).
