Take $$ \begin{align*} T = \frac{1}{2}m (\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2), \qquad V=V(r), \qquad L = T-V. \end{align*} $$ Applying Euler-Lagrange, $$ \begin{align*} 0 &= \dd{t}\left(\pdfd{L}{\dot r}\right) - \pdfd{L}{r} \\ &= \dd{t}(m\dot r) - mr\dot\theta^2 + \pdfd{V}{r} \\ m\ddot r - mr\dot\theta^2 &= -\pdfd{V}{r}, \end{align*} $$ which is Newton's second law in polars, and $$ \begin{align*} 0 &= \dd{t}\left(\pdfd{L}{\dot\theta}\right) - \pdfd{L}{\theta} \\ &= \dd{t}(mr^2\dot \theta) \\ mr^2\dot\theta &= \text{const.}, \end{align*} $$ which is conservation of angular momentum.

Also, since $L$ does not depend on $t$, we can use the first integral to get $$ \begin{align*} \text{const.} &= L - \dot r\pdfd{L}{\dot r} - \dot \theta \pdfd{L}{\dot \theta} \\ &= \frac{1}{2}m(\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2) - V(r) - m\dot r^2 - r^2\dot\theta^2 \\ &= -T-V \end{align*} $$ which is the conservation of total energy $T+V$. This demonstrates Noether's theorem.
