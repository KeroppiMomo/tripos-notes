We use summation notation here.

Starting from the definition of Hamiltonian $$ \begin{align*} H = p_i \dot q_i - L(\v q, \dv q, t), \quad \text{with $\dv q$ such that } p_i = \pdfd{L}{\dot q_i}, \end{align*} $$ we take the differential $$ \begin{align*} dH &= \dot q_i \, dp_i + p_i\,d\dot q_i - \pdfd{L}{q_i}\, dq_i - \pdfd{L}{\dot q_i}\,d\dot q_i - \pdfd{L}{t}\,dt \\ &= \dot q_i \, dp_i - \pdfd{L}{q_i}\,dq_i - \pdfd{L}{t}\,dt &&p_i - \pdfd{L}{\dot q_i} = 0 \\ &= \dot q_i \, dp_i - \dd{t}\left(\pdfd{L}{\dot q_i}\right)\,dq_i - \pdfd{L}{t}\,dt &&\text{Euler-Lagrange} \\ &= \dot q_i \, dp_i - \dot p_i\, dq_i - \pdfd{L}{t}\,dt &&\pdfd{L}{\dot q_i} = p_i. \end{align*} $$

Comparing with the chain rule applied to $H(\v q, \v p, t)$ $$ \begin{align*} dH = \pdfd{H}{p_i}\,dp_i + \pdfd{H}{q_i}\,dq_i + \pdfd{H}{t}\,dt, \end{align*} $$ we obtain the Hamilton's equations $$ \begin{align*} \dot q_i = \pdfd{H}{p_i}, \qquad \dot p_i = -\pdfd{H}{q_i}, \qquad \pdfd{H}{t}=-\pdfd{L}{t}. \end{align*} $$
